# Content-Adaptive Retrieval Gates: Improving Long-Context Needle Retrieval in Transformers

## Abstract

Transformers with long context windows often fail to retrieve sparse, relevant information buried in irrelevant surrounding text — the "lost in the middle" phenomenon. We propose **Retrieval Gating (RG)**, a lightweight, learned gating mechanism inserted between attention heads and the residual stream that suppresses contributions from tokens whose content is estimated to be irrelevant to the current query, before they are aggregated into the layer output. Unlike sparse-attention methods that hard-prune the KV cache, RG is a soft, differentiable multiplicative gate conditioned jointly on the query representation and each key's content, trained end-to-end with the rest of the model on a mixture of language modeling and synthetic retrieval objectives. We evaluate RG on synthetic needle-in-a-haystack (NIAH) retrieval, multi-key retrieval, and RULER-style aggregation tasks at context lengths from 4K to 128K tokens, using a 1.3B-parameter decoder-only backbone. RG improves single-needle retrieval accuracy at 128K from 61.4% to 89.7% over a matched no-gate baseline, improves multi-needle exact-match F1 by 14.2 points, and reduces effective attention entropy over distractor spans by 38% relative, all with <0.5% additional parameters and <3% wall-clock overhead. Ablations show the gain is driven primarily by the content-conditioning term rather than position information, and that gating benefits compound with (but do not require) positional interpolation methods such as YaRN.

## 1. Introduction

Modern transformers routinely advertise context windows of 128K–1M tokens, but empirical studies (Liu et al., 2023; Hsieh et al., 2024) show that effective utilization of that context is far lower than nominal length. Two failure modes recur across model families: (1) attention mass diffuses across long irrelevant spans, diluting the signal for the few tokens that actually answer a query, and (2) information positioned in the middle of the context is retrieved less reliably than information near the beginning or end.

Existing remedies fall into three broad categories. **Positional methods** (RoPE scaling, ALiBi, YaRN, NTK-aware interpolation) address length extrapolation but do not directly address content relevance — a model can attend correctly in position-space and still fail to identify which tokens matter. **Architectural sparsity** methods (Longformer, BigBird, H2O, StreamingLLM) reduce the set of tokens attended to via fixed patterns or heuristic cache eviction, which improves efficiency but can permanently discard information needed later in a multi-turn or multi-query setting. **Retrieval-augmented methods** offload long context to an external index, sidestepping in-context retrieval entirely but introducing a separate retrieval system with its own failure modes and losing gradient-based end-to-end optimization.

We take a different approach: keep the full context in the attention computation, but insert a learned, content-conditioned **gate** that down-weights the contribution of low-relevance tokens *after* attention scores are computed but *before* they are mixed into the residual stream. This is closer in spirit to gated linear attention and highway-network gating than to sparsification — nothing is discarded from the KV cache, so the mechanism degrades gracefully to standard attention when gates are near 1, and it remains fully differentiable, allowing the model to learn what "relevant" means directly from training signal rather than from a fixed heuristic (recency, position, or attention-score magnitude alone).

Our contributions:
1. **Retrieval Gating (RG)**, a per-head, per-token multiplicative gate conditioned on query–key content, added to a standard transformer block with minimal parameter and compute overhead.
2. A **training recipe** that mixes standard language modeling with synthetic retrieval tasks so gates learn a general notion of relevance rather than overfitting to a narrow synthetic distribution.
3. An **evaluation suite** spanning single-needle, multi-needle, and aggregation retrieval at lengths up to 128K, with ablations isolating the source of the improvement.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Background

In standard multi-head attention, layer output for head $h$ is $O_h = \text{softmax}(Q_hK_h^\top/\sqrt{d})V_h$. Long-context failures are typically diagnosed as attention scores that are not sufficiently peaked on the correct key tokens — either because softmax over tens of thousands of keys produces low absolute probability mass even on the "correct" key, or because positional bias systematically down-weights middle-context tokens regardless of content.

### 2.2 Retrieval Gating

We introduce a gate $g_i \in (0,1)$ per key position $i$ per head, computed from a lightweight content-relevance score, and apply it multiplicatively to that key's attention output contribution:

$$
g_i = \sigma\big(w^\top \tanh(W_q q + W_k k_i + b)\big)
$$

$$
\tilde{O}_h = \sum_i g_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot v_i, \qquad \alpha_i = \text{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d})_i
$$

where $q$ is the (per-query-token) query vector, $k_i$ the key vector at position $i$, and $W_q, W_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times r}$ project into a small bottleneck ($r=32$) to keep the gate cheap. Critically, $g_i$ is computed from **content only** — no explicit positional features are fed to the gate network — so any observed benefit cannot be attributed to the gate simply re-deriving a recency or position prior.

The gate is applied per-head but shared across the batch dimension trivially (it is a function of $q_i, k_i$ already materialized during attention), so it adds one extra small MLP forward pass ($O(n^2 \cdot r)$, negligible next to the $O(n^2 \cdot d)$ attention cost since $r \ll d$) and reuses the existing $QK^\top$ computation.

We apply RG to every layer but only to a subset of heads (the top 50% by a learned or fixed schedule; see ablations) — we found applying it to all heads over-constrains early layers that need diffuse attention for syntactic/local processing.

### 2.3 Training

We initialize $w$ near zero so that $g_i \approx \sigma(0) = 0.5$ at initialization, then warm up toward a regime where gates are informative, rather than initializing at $g_i\approx 1$ (which risks the gate collapsing to a no-op) or $g_i \approx 0$ (which risks vanishing gradients through attention). We fine-tune from a pretrained checkpoint using a mixed objective:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{LM}} + \lambda \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{retrieval}} + \beta \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{sparsity}}
$$

- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{LM}}$: standard next-token cross-entropy on a long-document pretraining mixture (books, code, web) truncated/packed to lengths sampled uniformly in $[4K, 128K]$.
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{retrieval}}$: cross-entropy on synthetic retrieval tasks — single needle-in-haystack, multi-needle (2–4 needles), and key-value lookup tasks — inserted at random depths and random context lengths, following the RULER task generator.
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{sparsity}}$: an $L_1$ penalty on mean gate activation, $\beta=0.01$, to discourage the trivial solution $g_i \to 1$ everywhere and encourage the model to actually use the gate's capacity.

We fine-tune for 5B tokens at $\lambda=0.3$, roughly 2% of original pretraining compute.

### 2.4 Baselines and Ablations

**Baselines:**
- **No-Gate**: identical backbone, no gating, fine-tuned on the same data mixture for the same token budget (controls for the extra fine-tuning stage itself).
- **H2O** (heavy-hitter KV cache eviction): a strong sparsity baseline that discards low-score tokens from the cache.
- **StreamingLLM**: attention-sink + sliding-window baseline.
- **YaRN-only**: positional interpolation without gating.
- **RG + YaRN**: our method combined with positional interpolation, to test complementarity.

**Ablations:**
- **Position-conditioned gate**: replace content features with RoPE position embeddings, to isolate whether gains come from content or from an implicitly learned recency prior.
- **Gate on attention weights only** ($g_i$ a function of $\alpha_i$ rather than raw $q,k_i$): tests whether the gate needs to see content directly or can bootstrap purely from attention scores.
- **All-heads vs. half-heads gating.**
- **No sparsity penalty** ($\beta=0$).
- **Gate bottleneck size** $r \in \{8, 32, 128\}$.

## 3. Results

All experiments use a 1.3B-parameter decoder-only transformer (24 layers, $d=2048$, 16 heads), RoPE positional encoding, pretrained on 300B tokens, then fine-tuned as described above. Evaluation uses held-out synthetic tasks generated with the same generator family as training but disjoint templates/entities, plus RULER's standard suite.

### 3.1 Single-Needle Retrieval (NIAH)

Accuracy (%) at retrieving a single inserted fact, averaged over needle depths (0–100%) and 5 seeds per (length, depth) cell.

| Context Length | No-Gate | StreamingLLM | H2O | YaRN-only | **RG (ours)** | RG + YaRN |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4K | 97.8 | 96.1 | 95.4 | 97.9 | 98.2 | 98.4 |
| 16K | 89.3 | 74.2 | 81.6 | 90.7 | 96.5 | 97.1 |
| 32K | 78.6 | 58.9 | 70.3 | 82.1 | 93.8 | 95.0 |
| 64K | 68.9 | 41.3 | 61.7 | 74.5 | 91.2 | 93.6 |
| 128K | 61.4 | 29.8 | 53.2 | 68.0 | 89.7 | 92.1 |

RG substantially outperforms both the no-gate baseline and eviction-based sparsity baselines at all lengths ≥16K, with the gap widening as context grows — consistent with the hypothesis that gating specifically mitigates the dilution of relevant signal among distractors, an effect that compounds with length. RG combined with YaRN gives the best results overall, indicating the two mechanisms address largely orthogonal failure modes (position extrapolation vs. content relevance).

### 3.2 Multi-Needle Retrieval and Aggregation

Exact-match F1 for retrieving 2–4 facts simultaneously, and accuracy on RULER's variable-tracking and common-word-extraction aggregation tasks, at 64K context.

| Task | No-Gate | H2O | YaRN-only | **RG (ours)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-needle (2) F1 | 71.5 | 63.0 | 76.9 | 88.4 |
| Multi-needle (4) F1 | 52.1 | 38.7 | 58.3 | 74.9 |
| Variable tracking | 44.0 | 31.2 | 49.8 | 66.7 |
| Common-word extraction | 60.3 | 49.6 | 63.1 | 75.2 |

Gains are largest on tasks requiring the model to hold and combine multiple relevant spans (variable tracking), suggesting the soft, non-destructive nature of gating — as opposed to H2O's hard eviction — matters more as the number of relevant-but-scattered tokens increases.

### 3.3 Attention Concentration

We measure attention entropy restricted to distractor (non-needle) spans as a proxy for wasted attention mass. RG reduces mean distractor-span entropy by 38% relative to No-Gate at 64K (4.9 vs. 7.9 bits), and increases the fraction of total attention mass on the correct needle token from 3.1% (No-Gate) to 21.6% (RG), averaged across heads and layers where gating is applied.

### 3.4 Ablations

| Variant | 128K NIAH Accuracy |
|---|---|
| **RG (full)** | 89.7 |
| Position-conditioned gate (no content) | 64.2 |
| Gate on attention weights only | 79.5 |
| All-heads gating | 85.1 |
| $\beta=0$ (no sparsity penalty) | 82.9 |
| $r=8$ | 86.0 |
| $r=128$ | 90.1 |

The position-conditioned ablation collapses nearly to the No-Gate baseline (64.2 vs. 61.4), confirming that content conditioning — not an implicit position/recency prior — drives the improvement. Applying the gate to all heads rather than half slightly *hurts* performance (85.1 vs. 89.7), supporting the design choice of leaving some heads ungated for local/syntactic processing. The sparsity penalty contributes a meaningful but secondary effect (82.9 → 89.7); without it, gates tend toward a less discriminative, closer-to-uniform distribution. Bottleneck size beyond $r=32$ yields diminishing returns, supporting our default choice on compute/quality grounds.

### 3.5 Efficiency

RG adds 0.44% parameters (bottleneck projections + gate vector) and increases wall-clock training and inference time by 2.7% at 128K context on our hardware, well within the noise floor of run-to-run FLOP variance for the baselines. Unlike H2O and StreamingLLM, RG requires no change to KV cache size or memory footprint, since no tokens are evicted.

## 4. Conclusion

We presented Retrieval Gating, a simple, differentiable, content-conditioned gating mechanism that substantially improves long-context retrieval in transformers without modifying the KV cache or requiring architectural changes beyond a small per-head MLP. Across single-needle, multi-needle, and aggregation tasks up to 128K tokens, RG outperforms both a matched no-gate baseline and established sparsity-based baselines (H2O, StreamingLLM), with the largest gains at the longest contexts and on tasks requiring the model to combine multiple scattered relevant spans. Ablations confirm the mechanism's benefit comes from learned content relevance rather than an implicit positional prior, and that it composes well with orthogonal positional-extrapolation methods like YaRN. Limitations include our reliance on synthetic retrieval tasks during fine-tuning, which may not fully capture the distribution of real-world long-document QA relevance signals, and our evaluation at a single model scale (1.3B); future work should examine scaling behavior, transfer to naturalistic long-document benchmarks (e.g., long-document QA, code repository navigation), and whether gate sparsity can be exploited for inference-time compute savings via dynamic pruning of near-zero-gated computation.
