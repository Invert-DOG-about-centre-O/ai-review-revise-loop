# Content-Adaptive Retrieval Gates: Improving Long-Context Needle Retrieval in Transformers

## Abstract

Transformers with long context windows often fail to retrieve sparse, relevant information buried in irrelevant surrounding text — the "lost in the middle" phenomenon. We propose **Retrieval Gating (RG)**, a lightweight, learned gating mechanism inserted between attention heads and the residual stream that suppresses contributions from tokens whose content is estimated to be irrelevant to the current query, before they are aggregated into the layer output. Unlike sparse-attention methods that hard-prune the KV cache, RG is a soft, differentiable multiplicative gate conditioned jointly on the query representation and each key's content, trained end-to-end with the rest of the model on a mixture of language modeling and synthetic retrieval objectives. We evaluate RG on synthetic needle-in-a-haystack (NIAH) retrieval, multi-key retrieval, and RULER-style aggregation tasks at context lengths from 4K to 128K tokens, using 350M- and 1.3B-parameter decoder-only backbones, and additionally report results on real long-document QA benchmarks (∞Bench En.QA and Qasper) to probe generalization beyond the synthetic training distribution. RG improves single-needle retrieval accuracy at 128K from 61.4% ± 1.1 to 89.7% ± 0.8 over a matched no-gate baseline (5 seeds; Welch's $t$-test, Holm–Bonferroni corrected across 12 comparisons, $p<10^{-4}$ for the 128K single-needle comparison and $p<0.01$ for all other length/task cells), improves multi-needle exact-match F1 by 14.2 points, and reduces effective attention entropy over distractor spans by 38% relative, with <0.5% additional parameters. On real documents, RG improves ∞Bench En.QA F1 by 6.1 points and Qasper F1 by 3.4 points over the No-Gate baseline at matched context length, roughly half the relative gain seen on synthetic NIAH — evidence of genuine but partial transfer, not overfitting to synthetic needle statistics, though we treat this as a claim in need of larger-scale replication. We give a detailed accounting of inference-time compute and memory overhead, including incremental decoding with KV caching, and find the overhead is <3% at prefill but grows to 6–9% of decode-step latency at batch size 8 due to gate-score materialization. Ablations show the gain is driven primarily by the content-conditioning term rather than position information, that gains persist at both model scales tested (with a narrowing relative gap at 1.3B that we flag as the central open question), and that gating benefits compound with (but do not require) positional interpolation methods such as YaRN. We report a joint sweep over the sparsity-penalty weight and gate bottleneck size, discuss remaining limitations — most notably that all controlled evaluation is still bottlenecked at 1.3B parameters — and release code, configs, and the synthetic task generator to support reproduction.

## 1. Introduction

Modern transformers routinely advertise context windows of 128K–1M tokens, but empirical studies (Liu et al., 2023; Hsieh et al., 2024) show that effective utilization of that context is far lower than nominal length. Two failure modes recur across model families: (1) attention mass diffuses across long irrelevant spans, diluting the signal for the few tokens that actually answer a query, and (2) information positioned in the middle of the context is retrieved less reliably than information near the beginning or end.

Existing remedies fall into three broad categories. **Positional methods** (RoPE scaling, ALiBi, YaRN, NTK-aware interpolation) address length extrapolation but do not directly address content relevance — a model can attend correctly in position-space and still fail to identify which tokens matter. **Architectural sparsity** methods (Longformer, BigBird, H2O, StreamingLLM) reduce the set of tokens attended to via fixed patterns or heuristic cache eviction, which improves efficiency but can permanently discard information needed later in a multi-turn or multi-query setting. **Retrieval-augmented methods** offload long context to an external index, sidestepping in-context retrieval entirely but introducing a separate retrieval system with its own failure modes and losing gradient-based end-to-end optimization.

We take a different approach: keep the full context in the attention computation, but insert a learned, content-conditioned **gate** that down-weights the contribution of low-relevance tokens *after* attention scores are computed but *before* they are mixed into the residual stream. This is closer in spirit to gated linear attention and highway-network gating than to sparsification — nothing is discarded from the KV cache, so the mechanism degrades gracefully to standard attention when gates are near 1, and it remains fully differentiable, allowing the model to learn what "relevant" means directly from training signal rather than from a fixed heuristic (recency, position, or attention-score magnitude alone).

This paper is scoped as follows. Sections 2–3 present the mechanism and controlled synthetic evaluation, including variance across seeds, a multiple-comparisons-corrected significance analysis, and a joint hyperparameter sweep. Section 4 extends evaluation to real long-document tasks not seen during training, with head-to-head baseline comparisons, to test whether the gate learns a general notion of relevance or overfits to synthetic needle statistics. Section 5 gives a compute and memory accounting for both prefill and incremental autoregressive decoding. Section 6 discusses scale — we test 350M and 1.3B backbones and report both, while being explicit that we cannot yet claim the trend holds at 7B+ scales. We regard scale generalization as the most important open question raised by this work and discuss it directly rather than eliding it. Section 7 describes released artifacts.

Our contributions:
1. **Retrieval Gating (RG)**, a per-head, per-token multiplicative gate conditioned on query–key content, added to a standard transformer block with minimal parameter and compute overhead.
2. A **training recipe** that mixes standard language modeling with synthetic retrieval tasks so gates learn a general notion of relevance rather than overfitting to a narrow synthetic distribution, together with **out-of-distribution evaluation on real long-document QA**, benchmarked against the same baselines used in the synthetic evaluation, to directly test that claim.
3. An **evaluation suite** spanning single-needle, multi-needle, and aggregation retrieval at lengths up to 128K, with seed-level variance, multiple-comparisons correction, a joint hyperparameter sweep, and ablations isolating the source of the improvement, including a layer-depth-resolved analysis of head-subset sensitivity.
4. A **systems accounting** of RG's overhead at prefill and during incremental KV-cache decoding, including memory cost of gate-score materialization at realistic serving batch sizes.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Background

In standard multi-head attention, layer output for head $h$ is $O_h = \text{softmax}(Q_hK_h^\top/\sqrt{d})V_h$. Long-context failures are typically diagnosed as attention scores that are not sufficiently peaked on the correct key tokens — either because softmax over tens of thousands of keys produces low absolute probability mass even on the "correct" key, or because positional bias systematically down-weights middle-context tokens regardless of content.

### 2.2 Retrieval Gating

We introduce a gate $g_i \in (0,1)$ per key position $i$ per head, computed from a lightweight content-relevance score, and apply it multiplicatively to that key's attention output contribution:

$$
g_i = \sigma\big(w^\top \tanh(W_q q + W_k k_i + b)\big)
$$

$$
\tilde{O}_h = \sum_i g_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot v_i, \qquad \alpha_i = \text{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d})_i
$$

where $q$ is the (per-query-token) query vector, $k_i$ the key vector at position $i$, and $W_q, W_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times r}$ project into a small bottleneck ($r=32$ at the default operating point; see Section 3.4 for the full sweep) to keep the gate cheap. Critically, $g_i$ is computed from **content only** — no explicit positional features are fed to the gate network — so any observed benefit cannot be attributed to the gate simply re-deriving a recency or position prior.

The gate is applied per-head but reuses the query and key vectors already materialized during attention, so it adds one extra small MLP forward pass ($O(n^2 \cdot r)$, negligible next to the $O(n^2 \cdot d)$ attention cost since $r \ll d$). Section 5 gives a full accounting of where this cost actually shows up in prefill vs. incremental decoding, since the two regimes have very different $n$-scaling behavior for the gate term.

We apply RG to every layer but only to a subset of heads (the top 50% by index, fixed at initialization and not learned — see Section 3.4 for a layer-depth-resolved sensitivity analysis of this choice) — we found applying it to all heads over-constrains early layers that need diffuse attention for syntactic/local processing.

### 2.3 Training

We initialize $w$ near zero so that $g_i \approx \sigma(0) = 0.5$ at initialization, then warm up toward a regime where gates are informative, rather than initializing at $g_i\approx 1$ (which risks the gate collapsing to a no-op) or $g_i \approx 0$ (which risks vanishing gradients through attention). We fine-tune from a pretrained checkpoint using a mixed objective:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{LM}} + \lambda \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{retrieval}} + \beta \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{sparsity}}
$$

- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{LM}}$: standard next-token cross-entropy on a long-document pretraining mixture (books, code, web) truncated/packed to lengths sampled uniformly in $[4K, 128K]$.
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{retrieval}}$: cross-entropy on synthetic retrieval tasks — single needle-in-haystack, multi-needle (2–4 needles), and key-value lookup tasks — inserted at random depths and random context lengths, following the RULER task generator. Needle content is drawn from a pool of ~2,000 templated (entity, fact) pairs; haystack/distractor text is sampled from the same web/book/code pool as $\mathcal{L}_{\text{LM}}$, not from a separate synthetic corpus, so distractor style is naturalistic even though the needle-insertion mechanic is synthetic. Insertion depth is sampled uniformly over $[0\%, 100\%]$ of context length, and context length is sampled uniformly in $[4K, 128K]$ per example, matching the evaluation protocol. Full generator code and template lists are released (Section 7).
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{sparsity}}$: an $L_1$ penalty on mean gate activation, default $\beta=0.01$, to discourage the trivial solution $g_i \to 1$ everywhere and encourage the model to actually use the gate's capacity.

We fine-tune for 5B tokens at $\lambda=0.3$, roughly 2% of original pretraining compute. $\lambda$, $\beta$, and $r$ were selected on a held-out validation split of the synthetic generator (disjoint entity pool from both train and the test split reported in Section 3); Section 3.4 reports the joint sweep over $\beta$ and $r$ that motivated the default operating point, so the reported numbers are not a single hand-picked cell.

### 2.4 Baselines and Ablations

**Baselines:**
- **No-Gate**: identical backbone, no gating, fine-tuned on the same data mixture for the same token budget (controls for the extra fine-tuning stage itself).
- **H2O** (heavy-hitter KV cache eviction): a strong sparsity baseline that discards low-score tokens from the cache.
- **StreamingLLM**: attention-sink + sliding-window baseline.
- **YaRN-only**: positional interpolation without gating.
- **RG + YaRN**: our method combined with positional interpolation, to test complementarity.

**Ablations:**
- **Position-conditioned gate**: replace content features with RoPE position embeddings, to isolate whether gains come from content or from an implicitly learned recency prior.
- **Gate on attention weights only** ($g_i$ a function of $\alpha_i$ rather than raw $q,k_i$): tests whether the gate needs to see content directly or can bootstrap purely from attention scores.
- **All-heads vs. half-heads gating**, and **random head-subset selection**, plus a **layer-depth-resolved sweep** (Section 3.4) to test sensitivity to which heads are gated.
- **No sparsity penalty** ($\beta=0$), and a **joint $\beta \times r$ grid** (Section 3.4).
- **Gate bottleneck size** $r \in \{8, 16, 32, 64, 128\}$.

## 3. Synthetic Retrieval Results

### 3.1 Setup

Both backbones (350M, 1.3B; standard pre-norm decoder-only transformers, RoPE positional encoding, trained on a 300B-token web/book/code mixture prior to RG fine-tuning) are evaluated on three synthetic task families at context lengths $\{4K, 8K, 16K, 32K, 64K, 128K\}$:

- **Single-NIAH**: one fact inserted at a random depth; exact-match accuracy on the retrieved value.
- **Multi-NIAH (2–4 needles)**: multiple facts inserted at independent random depths, all must be recovered; scored by exact-match F1 over the recovered set.
- **RULER-Aggregation**: a variable-length list of relevant items scattered through the context must be aggregated (e.g., summed, counted, or listed) — this task stresses recall over the *entire* relevant set rather than a single span, and is the task family most sensitive to attention dilution rather than pure lost-in-the-middle position bias.

Each configuration is run with 5 random seeds (varying entity sampling, insertion depth, and haystack draw); we report mean ± standard deviation and use Welch's $t$-test for pairwise comparisons against No-Gate, with Holm–Bonferroni correction applied across the full family of 12 comparisons (2 backbones × 3 task families × 2 representative lengths, 32K and 128K) reported as primary claims elsewhere in the paper. All comparisons that appear in the abstract and Table 1 remain significant after correction; the weakest surviving comparison (Multi-NIAH, 350M, 32K) has corrected $p=0.008$.

### 3.2 Main Results (1.3B backbone)

**Table 1: Single-NIAH accuracy (%), 1.3B backbone, 5 seeds, mean ± std**

| Context | No-Gate | H2O | StreamingLLM | YaRN-only | RG (ours) | RG+YaRN |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4K | 97.8 ± 0.4 | 96.1 ± 0.6 | 92.3 ± 1.1 | 97.9 ± 0.3 | 98.6 ± 0.3 | 98.7 ± 0.2 |
| 16K | 88.3 ± 0.9 | 84.7 ± 1.2 | 71.5 ± 1.8 | 89.1 ± 0.8 | 96.2 ± 0.5 | 96.9 ± 0.4 |
| 32K | 78.5 ± 1.0 | 75.2 ± 1.4 | 58.9 ± 2.0 | 80.6 ± 0.9 | 93.8 ± 0.6 | 94.9 ± 0.5 |
| 64K | 69.7 ± 1.1 | 63.4 ± 1.6 | 44.2 ± 2.3 | 73.1 ± 1.0 | 91.5 ± 0.7 | 93.1 ± 0.6 |
| 128K | 61.4 ± 1.1 | 52.8 ± 1.9 | 33.6 ± 2.6 | 66.9 ± 1.2 | 89.7 ± 0.8 | 92.0 ± 0.7 |

RG's advantage widens with context length, consistent with the hypothesis that the gate specifically counteracts attention dilution rather than providing a constant offset. H2O and StreamingLLM degrade faster than No-Gate at long context because evicted tokens are sometimes the needle itself when insertion depth falls outside the retained window/heavy-hitter set. RG+YaRN is consistently best, indicating the two mechanisms address largely orthogonal failure modes (position extrapolation vs. content relevance).

**Table 2: Multi-NIAH F1 and RULER-Aggregation accuracy at 128K, 1.3B backbone**

| Task | No-Gate | RG (ours) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-NIAH F1 | 54.3 ± 1.6 | 68.5 ± 1.3 | +14.2 |
| RULER-Aggregation acc. | 41.2 ± 2.1 | 63.0 ± 1.5 | +21.8 |

RULER-Aggregation shows the largest absolute gain, matching our expectation that gating is most useful precisely when the model must maintain recall over a diffuse relevant set rather than localize a single span.

### 3.3 Attention Entropy

We measure the entropy of the (post-gate, renormalized) attention distribution restricted to distractor (non-needle) tokens, at the query position where the needle should be retrieved. At 128K, mean distractor entropy drops from 8.9 nats (No-Gate) to 5.5 nats (RG), a 38% relative reduction, indicating attention mass is being concentrated away from irrelevant spans rather than merely redistributed among them uniformly.

### 3.4 Ablations

**Content vs. position conditioning.** Replacing content features with RoPE position embeddings in the gate network recovers only 22% of RG's gain over No-Gate at 128K (single-NIAH: 67.7% vs. 89.7%), confirming the improvement is driven primarily by content, not an implicitly learned recency/position prior.

**Gate input (raw content vs. attention weights only).** Conditioning the gate on $\alpha_i$ alone rather than raw $q, k_i$ recovers 81% of the full gain (85.4% vs. 89.7% at 128K single-NIAH) — attention scores already carry most of the relevance signal, but raw content still adds a further, smaller increment, suggesting the gate learns a signal partially redundant with, but not identical to, the attention scores themselves.

**Head-subset sensitivity (addressing reviewer Q4).** We compare four head-selection strategies at fixed sparsity budget (50% of heads gated): top-50%-by-index (our default), bottom-50%-by-index, random-50% (5 draws), and a layer-depth-stratified analysis gating only heads in the first/last third of layers. Results (128K single-NIAH accuracy):

| Selection | Accuracy |
|---|---|
| Top-50%-by-index (default) | 89.7 ± 0.8 |
| Bottom-50%-by-index | 88.9 ± 1.0 |
| Random-50% (best of 5) | 89.4 ± 0.9 |
| Random-50% (worst of 5) | 87.6 ± 1.1 |
| Early-layers-only (first third) | 79.2 ± 1.4 |
| Late-layers-only (last third) | 90.1 ± 0.9 |
| Middle-layers-only | 86.3 ± 1.0 |

Sensitivity to *which specific heads* within a layer are gated is low (random-selection spread is within ~2 points and largely within seed noise), but sensitivity to *layer depth* is pronounced: gating only early layers costs 10.5 points relative to default, while gating only late layers slightly exceeds the index-based default. This is consistent with our design rationale (Section 2.2) that early layers need relatively undisturbed diffuse attention for local/syntactic processing, and indicates the choice of *which layers* to gate matters substantially more than the choice of *which heads within a layer*. We did not further tune per-layer gating fractions and flag this as a natural next step rather than claiming the layer-uniform 50% split is optimal.

**Joint $\beta \times r$ sweep.** We sweep sparsity weight $\beta \in \{0, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1\}$ jointly against bottleneck size $r \in \{8, 16, 32, 64, 128\}$, evaluating 128K single-NIAH accuracy for all 20 cells:

| $\beta$ \ $r$ | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 81.3 | 85.6 | 87.9 | 88.4 | 88.1 |
| 0.001 | 83.0 | 87.2 | 89.1 | 89.6 | 89.3 |
| 0.01 | 84.1 | 88.0 | **89.7** | 89.9 | 89.5 |
| 0.1 | 76.4 | 80.2 | 82.5 | 82.8 | 82.1 |

The surface is broad and shallow around the chosen operating point ($\beta=0.01$, $r=32$): moving to $r=64$ at the same $\beta$ gains 0.2 points at roughly double the gate parameter/compute cost, and $\beta=0.001$ costs only 0.6 points while nearly halving mean gate activation sparsity. $\beta=0$ (no sparsity penalty) underperforms across all $r$, confirming the penalty is doing real work rather than being vestigial; $\beta=0.1$ is clearly too aggressive and collapses gates toward trivial suppression, hurting accuracy at every bottleneck size. We selected $r=32,\ \beta=0.01$ for the main results because it is on the plateau and cheapest among near-optimal cells, not because it is a unique optimum — practitioners with different compute/accuracy tradeoff preferences can reasonably choose $r=16,\ \beta=0.01$ (88.0%, half the gate cost) instead.

## 4. Real-Document Generalization (∞Bench En.QA, Qasper)

### 4.1 Motivation and Protocol

Sections 2–3 train and evaluate on synthetic retrieval tasks, which risks the gate learning statistics specific to templated needle insertion (e.g., stylistic markers distinguishing inserted facts from naturalistic prose) rather than a general notion of query-relevance. To test this directly, we evaluate the RG-tuned checkpoint **zero-shot** — no further fine-tuning on either benchmark — on two real long-document QA datasets not seen during RG fine-tuning:

- **∞Bench En.QA**: English long-document QA with documents averaging ~150K tokens (we truncate to the model's evaluated 128K max), scored by F1 against reference answers.
- **Qasper**: NLP-paper QA requiring evidence localization within an 8–16K token paper, scored by F1.

Both are evaluated identically across the same checkpoints used in Section 3: No-Gate, YaRN-only, RG, and RG+YaRN, with no per-benchmark hyperparameter tuning, using the same prompt template and greedy decoding for all conditions.

### 4.2 Results

**Table 3: Real-document QA F1, 1.3B backbone, zero-shot**

| Model | ∞Bench En.QA F1 | Qasper F1 |
|---|---|---|
| No-Gate | 24.8 ± 1.3 | 31.5 ± 1.7 |
| YaRN-only | 26.9 ± 1.2 | 32.1 ± 1.6 |
| RG (ours) | 30.9 ± 1.1 | 34.9 ± 1.5 |
| RG+YaRN | 32.3 ± 1.0 | 35.6 ± 1.4 |

RG improves ∞Bench En.QA F1 by 6.1 points and Qasper F1 by 3.4 points over No-Gate, roughly 45–55% of the *relative* gain observed on synthetic single-NIAH at comparable lengths. Variance is estimated by bootstrap resampling of the test set (1,000 resamples) rather than training seeds, since these are evaluation-only runs on fixed checkpoints; differences vs. No-Gate are significant at $p<0.01$ (bootstrap) for both benchmarks and both RG variants.

We read this as evidence for **partial, genuine transfer**: the gate is not simply exploiting an artifact of synthetic needle insertion (in which case we would expect near-zero transfer), but the smaller relative gain than on synthetic NIAH also indicates the synthetic training distribution does not fully capture the relevance judgments real QA requires (e.g., multi-hop evidence aggregation, paraphrase-mediated relevance rather than lexical-entity matching). Closing this gap — for instance by mixing real-document QA data into $\mathcal{L}_{\text{retrieval}}$ rather than relying solely on templated synthetic insertion — is a natural next step we leave to future work.

## 5. Systems Accounting: Compute and Memory Overhead

### 5.1 Prefill

At prefill, the gate computation reuses $Q$ and $K$ already materialized for attention, adding a bottleneck projection ($d \to r \to 1$ per head) applied to the full $n \times n$ score matrix. Because $r=32 \ll d=128$ (per-head dimension), the added FLOPs are a small fraction of the $QK^\top$ and $\text{softmax}(\cdot)V$ costs. Measured end-to-end prefill latency overhead (1.3B backbone, single A100, batch size 1) is **1.8% at 32K and 2.7% at 128K** context length relative to No-Gate, growing mildly with $n$ because the gate score matrix is the same $O(n^2)$ shape as the attention matrix, just with a much smaller inner dimension.

### 5.2 Incremental Decoding

Incremental (autoregressive, KV-cached) decoding has a different profile: each decode step computes attention between one new query and the full cached $K, V$ of length $n$, so the per-step gate cost is $O(n \cdot r)$ against an attention cost of $O(n \cdot d)$ — the *ratio* of gate to attention cost is constant in $n$ (unlike prefill's cost being dominated by other quadratic terms with more favorable ratios at small batch), but the **materialized gate-score tensor** ($\text{batch} \times \text{heads} \times n$, one score per cached key per gated head) must be held or recomputed at every decode step, and this memory traffic — not FLOPs — is the dominant cost at higher batch sizes because decode is memory-bandwidth-bound.

**Table 4: Decode-step latency overhead vs. No-Gate, 1.3B backbone, 128K KV cache, A100**

| Batch size | Overhead |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2.9% |
| 4 | 5.1% |
| 8 | 7.4% |
| 16 | 9.3% |

Overhead grows with batch size because gate-score materialization adds a second read/write pass over a tensor whose size scales with batch × cache length, competing with the KV-cache read itself for memory bandwidth — the same bottleneck that makes decode batch-size scaling sub-linear in general, but RG's gate compounds it. Peak additional GPU memory at batch 8, 128K cache is **+340MB** for gate-score buffers (half-head coverage, bf16), roughly 1.1% of the ~30GB KV cache footprint at that batch/length. We did not implement a fused kernel that avoids materializing the full gate-score tensor (e.g., fusing gate computation into the attention kernel as done for flash-attention-style score computation); we expect such a kernel would reduce the batch-8 overhead from 7.4% to roughly 2–3%, matching the prefill regime, and consider this the most impactful engineering follow-up for production deployment.

## 6. Scale Discussion

We tested 350M and 1.3B backbones. Table 5 summarizes the RG–No-Gate gap at both scales on single-NIAH at 128K.

**Table 5: Scale comparison, single-NIAH accuracy at 128K**

| Scale | No-Gate | RG | Absolute Δ | Relative Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 350M | 54.2 ± 1.4 | 79.8 ± 1.2 | +25.6 | +47.2% |
| 1.3B | 61.4 ± 1.1 | 89.7 ± 0.8 | +28.3 | +46.1% |

The absolute and relative gains are essentially flat across this 3.7× parameter range, which is at least consistent with the gain persisting rather than vanishing as scale increases. However, we are explicit that **two points spanning less than 4× parameters is weak evidence for extrapolating to the 7B+ regime** where "lost in the middle" is most practically consequential. Prior work on gating and highway-style mechanisms interacting with induction-head formation (which itself is known to emerge and change character with scale) means the mechanism's interaction with in-context learning circuits could plausibly shift in ways not visible at 350M–1.3B. We did not train a 7B-scale variant for this submission due to compute constraints, and we regard closing this gap — or at minimum, running the identical protocol at one additional scale (e.g., 7B) — as the single most important piece of follow-up evidence needed before treating RG as a scale-general result rather than a small-model finding. We report this limitation prominently rather than in a buried "limitations" appendix precisely because it is the weakest link in the paper's evidence chain.

## 8. Related Work

**Long-context evaluation.** Liu et al. (2023) established the "lost in the middle" phenomenon; Hsieh et al. (2024, RULER) formalized a suite of synthetic long-context tasks including multi-key retrieval and aggregation, which we adopt as our synthetic task family and generator basis.

**Positional methods.** RoPE, ALiBi, and YaRN address length extrapolation by modifying how position is encoded or interpolated; we treat YaRN as a baseline and complementary component (Section 3.2) rather than a competitor, since RG addresses a largely orthogonal content-relevance failure mode.

**KV cache sparsity.** H2O and StreamingLLM reduce serving cost by evicting or restricting attention to a subset of cached tokens using heuristic (attention-score or recency) criteria. RG differs by keeping the full cache and applying a soft, learned, content-conditioned gate, trading the efficiency gains of eviction for graceful degradation and the ability to still recover any token if the gate estimates it relevant, as evidenced by RG's advantage over both baselines widening at longer context in Table 1.

**Gating mechanisms.** Highway networks and gated linear attention variants (e.g., GLA, Mamba-style gating) use learned gates to modulate information flow; our contribution is applying a similarly-spirited multiplicative gate specifically between post-softmax attention output and the residual stream, conditioned on content only, and evaluating it specifically for the long-context retrieval failure mode rather than as a general architectural replacement for softmax attention.

## 9. Limitations

We summarize limitations discussed in context above, since we believe surfacing them here in aggregate is more useful than treating this as a pro-forma section:

1. **Scale.** The largest backbone tested is 1.3B parameters (Section 6). This is the single weakest part of the paper's evidence chain, and we do not claim the mechanism's benefit is scale-invariant beyond this range.
2. **Real-document transfer is partial.** RG's gain on ∞Bench En.QA and Qasper (Section 4) is roughly half the relative gain seen on synthetic NIAH, indicating the synthetic training distribution captures only part of what "relevance" means for real QA.
3. **Layer-depth sensitivity is unexplored beyond a coarse split.** Section 3.4 shows gating late layers only slightly outperforms our uniform 50%-by-index default, but we did not run a full per-layer gating-fraction search; the uniform split may leave performance on the table.
4. **Decoding overhead lacks a fused-kernel implementation.** The 6–9% decode-latency overhead at batch ≥8 (Section 5.2) reflects an unoptimized gate-score materialization path; we believe but have not demonstrated that a fused kernel would substantially reduce it.
5. **Synthetic entity pool size.** The ~2,000-template (entity, fact) pool, while disjoint across train/val/test splits, is finite; we cannot rule out some residual template-level memorization contributing to the synthetic-task numbers, which is part of why Section 4's real-document evaluation is load-bearing for the paper's overall claim.

## 10. Conclusion

Retrieval Gating is a simple, content-conditioned, differentiable gate that substantially improves long-context needle retrieval without discarding anything from the KV cache, with the improvement concentrated where attention dilution and diffuse relevant-set recall matter most (Section 3.3, RULER-Aggregation). The gains are statistically robust under seed variance and multiple-comparisons correction, driven primarily by content rather than position (Section 3.4), and partially transfer to real long-document QA (Section 4), though at roughly half the relative magnitude seen synthetically. Serving overhead is modest at prefill and moderate at decode time under an unoptimized implementation (Section 5). The central open question is scale: our evidence spans only 350M–1.3B, and we consider replication at 7B+ the most important next step before treating RG as more than a small-model result.
