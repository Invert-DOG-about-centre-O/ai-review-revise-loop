# When Calibration Fights Fidelity: Temperature Scaling Can Move Language Model Predictions Away From the True Generative Distribution (in a Controlled Testbed)

## Abstract

Calibration methods for large language models (LLMs) — temperature scaling, conformal prediction, verbalized confidence — are almost always evaluated against proxies for truth: sampled correctness labels, human judgments, or downstream accuracy. We never get to see whether a calibrated model's *full predictive distribution* is actually closer to the process that generated the data, because for real text that process is unknown. We build a synthetic testbed where it is known: a small causal Transformer is trained from scratch to predict the next token of sequences generated by a latent-mode Markov mixture, whose exact Bayesian posterior predictive distribution we can compute analytically at every position. This lets us measure calibration error and distributional fidelity (KL divergence to the true generative distribution) simultaneously and exactly. Across 5 seeds at our main 600-step budget, temperature scaling fit by validation NLL — the standard LLM calibration recipe — activates ($T^*\neq1$) in 2/5 seeds; whenever active it increases mean KL by 0.005–0.006 while cutting oracle-ECE 2–6×, and never once improves KL when active. A paired test across all 5 seeds (including the 3 where it does nothing) does *not* reach significance ($p=0.18$) — the aggregate effect is real but seed-conditional, not a guaranteed per-seed property, and we report this rather than the more flattering conditional-only framing. The KL-minimizing scalar temperature is on the opposite side of $T{=}1$ from the NLL-minimizing one ($T^*_{\text{KL}}{=}0.92$ vs. $T^*_{\text{NLL}}{=}1.02$, both differences significant at $p<0.03$), and optimizing for KL fidelity makes oracle-ECE more than double. Split conformal prediction hits its target coverage regardless of temperature, since rank-based nonconformity scores are invariant to monotonic logit rescaling. Two new checks in this revision temper the story: a 3-seed training-budget ablation shows the dissociation is *not* a monotonically-strengthening function of steps — at 1200 steps only 1/3 seeds still trigger a correction, down from 2/3 at 600 — so "more training" is not a reliable trigger, only "the specific trained model happens to be overconfident" is. And a richer two-parameter affine recalibration (scale + per-token additive bias, fit by the same NLL objective) partially escapes the trade-off in one of two seeds tested (ECE improves 2× at a KL cost near zero), suggesting the conflict is sharpest for pure scalar multiplication and not fully fundamental to any post-hoc rescaling — though it does not disappear either.

## 1. Introduction

Modern LLMs are frequently wrong while sounding certain, and a large body of work tries to fix this by producing better confidence estimates: recalibrating token probabilities, sampling multiple generations to detect disagreement, or asking the model to state a confidence verbally. Nearly all of this work is evaluated the same way: bin predictions by confidence and check whether confidence matches *empirical* accuracy on held-out data (ECE), or check whether a prediction set covers the *observed* answer at a target rate (conformal coverage). Both criteria are proxies — they use the single realized outcome as a stand-in for the model's true target distribution, because for real language the data-generating process is not known and cannot be queried.

This matters because a calibration method could, in principle, satisfy a proxy metric while moving the model's full predictive distribution *further* from the true generative process — e.g., by sharpening or flattening probabilities in a way that happens to align average confidence with average accuracy without making the distribution itself more accurate. Standard benchmarks cannot detect this because they have no access to ground truth beyond the single sampled outcome per example.

**Related work.** LLM uncertainty quantification is an active area with recent surveys (KDD 2025; arXiv:2412.05563). Verbalized confidence partially aligns with internal probabilities but is prompt-sensitive. Semantic entropy probes detect hallucination via multi-sample disagreement but miss confident, consistent hallucinations. Temperature scaling remains the standard post-hoc recipe inherited from classification (Guo et al., ICML 2017) and is applied to LLM logits with little scrutiny of what it trades off. Conformal prediction gives finite-sample, distribution-free coverage for LLM token/generation sets (Quach et al., ICLR 2024; ConU, ACL Findings 2024; CoVeR, arXiv:2509.04733), attractive because its guarantee does not depend on ECE-style calibration. None of this literature checks calibration methods against a *known* ground-truth predictive distribution, since real LLMs are trained on text whose generative process is inaccessible. Our contribution is a minimal synthetic environment that removes this obstacle.

## 2. Method

**Generative process.** A latent-mode Markov mixture over vocabulary size $V=10$, $K=3$ hidden modes each with a row-stochastic transition matrix (rows ~ Dirichlet($\alpha=0.3$)). Per sequence: draw a mode uniformly (never observed by the learner), draw the first token uniformly, then emit each subsequent token via the mode's transition row. Sequence length $T=40$.

**Exact ground truth.** Because all transition matrices and the mode prior are known, the true Bayesian posterior predictive $p_{\text{true}}(x_{t+1}\mid x_{1:t})$ is computed exactly by HMM-style forward filtering: maintain a posterior over modes, update by Bayes' rule after each observed transition, predict via the posterior-weighted mixture of transition rows. This is the exact ceiling any predictor could achieve.

**Learner.** A small causal Transformer (2 layers, 4 heads, $d_{\text{model}}=48$, ~50K params) trained from scratch with next-token cross-entropy on 4,000 sequences, AdamW (lr $3\times10^{-3}$), CPU-only. Evaluated against $p_{\text{true}}$ on 1,200 held-out sequences with two exact metrics: **KL$(p_{\text{true}}\|p_{\text{model}})$** and **oracle-ECE** (standard ECE machinery with empirical accuracy replaced by $p_{\text{true}}$'s probability on the model's top-1 token, removing finite-sample noise).

**Calibration methods.** (1) *Raw* softmax. (2) *Temperature scaling by NLL*: scalar $T^*_{\text{NLL}}$ from a grid search over $[0.5,2.5]$ minimizing validation NLL — the standard recipe. (3) *Temperature scaling by KL*: same grid, but selecting $T^*_{\text{KL}}$ to minimize mean test KL directly — not a fittable real-world recipe (needs ground truth), but characterizes the best any scalar temperature could do for fidelity. (4) *Split conformal prediction*: nonconformity $s=1-p_{\text{model}}$, calibrated to 90% coverage. (5) *Affine (scale+bias) recalibration, new in this revision*: logits$'=a\cdot\text{logits}+b$ with scalar $a$ and a $V$-dimensional additive bias $b$, both fit jointly by gradient descent on validation NLL (300 Adam steps) — a minimal two-parameter family that keeps the same fitting objective as (2) but adds one bias degree of freedom per token, addressing whether the fidelity/calibration conflict is specific to pure multiplicative rescaling.

**Seeds and robustness.** The main run uses 5 seeds (0–4) with mean±std reported throughout, and we now add paired significance tests (paired $t$ and Wilcoxon) across seeds. New in this revision: a 3-seed (0–2) training-budget ablation (300/600/1200 steps, vs. the original single seed) and a 2-seed affine-recalibration check at 600 steps.

## 3. Results

**Main run, 5 seeds at 600 steps.** KL raw $=0.0661\pm0.0070$, KL scaled (NLL) $=0.0683\pm0.0054$; oracle-ECE raw $=0.0204\pm0.0046$, scaled (NLL) $=0.0156\pm0.0079$. $T^*_{\text{NLL}}\neq1$ in only 2/5 seeds (1.05 in both; the rest exactly 1.00). In both activating seeds the direction replicated: KL rose (0.0589→0.0637; 0.0610→0.0671) while ECE fell sharply (0.0221→0.0092; 0.0165→0.0056). **Significance (new):** a paired test over all 5 seeds — including the 3 seeds with zero effect — does *not* reach significance for either metric (KL: $t=-1.61$, $p=0.18$; ECE: $t=1.62$, $p=0.18$; Wilcoxon $p=0.50$ both). This is not a null result to hide: it means the *average* effect at $n=5$ is not statistically distinguishable from noise once non-activating seeds are included, even though the effect is perfectly consistent in direction *whenever it activates* (2/2). We report both facts rather than only the flattering conditional one — the honest claim is "when NLL-scaling does something, it always trades fidelity for calibration" (mechanism-level, well supported), not "NLL-scaling reliably degrades fidelity by a precise average amount" (population-level, underpowered at $n=5$).

**KL-optimal vs. NLL-optimal temperature.** $T^*_{\text{KL}}=0.92\pm0.02$ (sharpening) vs. $T^*_{\text{NLL}}=1.02\pm0.02$ (flat/near-1) — opposite sides of $T{=}1$. Using $T^*_{\text{KL}}$ improves KL (0.0661→0.0617) but oracle-ECE more than doubles (0.0204→0.0447). Unlike the NLL-scaling comparison, both of these paired differences *are* significant across the 5 seeds (KL: $t=3.43$, $p=0.026$; ECE: $t=-6.61$, $p=0.003$), because $T^*_{\text{KL}}$ is always active (grid search always finds a non-trivial minimum) — a useful contrast: the always-on KL-optimal comparison is well powered, the sometimes-on NLL comparison is not.

**Bin-count sensitivity.** For the two activating seeds, the ECE improvement ratio shrinks with more bins (seed 0: 2.84×→1.81×, 5→20 bins; seed 1: 5.91×→2.19×). Direction is robust; magnitude is not — we report "roughly 2–6×," not a single precise number.

**Split conformal prediction** hit its 90% target almost exactly regardless of scaling (0.900 both, set size 4.37/10), consistent with rank-invariance of positive scalar rescaling — a deterministic argument that does not depend on seed.

**Multi-seed training-budget ablation (new — 3 seeds, was 1 in the prior revision).** Re-running seeds 0–2 at 300/600/1200 steps: at 300 steps, $T^*=1$ in 3/3 seeds (no gap at all — confirms undertrained models give the grid search nothing to correct, robustly). At 600 steps, $T^*\neq1$ in 2/3 seeds (0, 2), each showing the same trade (seed 0: KL 0.0612→0.0730, ECE 0.0245→0.0107; seed 2: KL 0.0597→0.0709, ECE 0.0289→0.0064); seed 1 shows no scalar correction at all ($T^*=1$). At 1200 steps, only 1/3 seeds (seed 0) still triggers a correction (KL 0.0457→0.0522, ECE 0.0298→0.0063); seeds 1 and 2 both land back at $T^*=1$. This corrects an overclaim in the previous version: training longer does *not* monotonically increase the chance of triggering the dissociation — the single-seed 50→1200-step trajectory we originally reported (which did trigger at both 600 and 1200) was not representative. The correct, weaker claim is: activation requires the specific trained model to be measurably overconfident on its validation split, which is necessary-but-not-sufficient with more training, not a guaranteed consequence of it.

**Affine (scale+bias) recalibration (new — 2 seeds, addressing whether richer rescaling escapes the conflict).** At 600 steps: seed 1, whose *scalar* $T^*$ found no correction ($T^*=1$), still had a fittable affine correction ($a=0.97$, $\|b\|=0.28$) that cut ECE roughly in half (0.0168→0.0084) while leaving KL essentially unchanged (0.0619→0.0623, +0.6%) — a near-free win the pure scalar family could not find. Seed 2, whose scalar correction already cost KL +18.8% for a 4.5× ECE gain, saw the affine version achieve a similar ECE gain (2.7×, 0.0289→0.0106) at roughly half the KL cost (+9.1% vs. +18.8%). So a two-parameter family does not eliminate the conflict, but it markedly softens it in both seeds tested — evidence that the sharpest form of the trade-off is specific to pure multiplicative (scalar) rescaling rather than an unavoidable property of any post-hoc logit adjustment fit by NLL. This is a 2-seed check and should be read as suggestive, not conclusive.

**Limitations.** Small, fully synthetic testbed (10-token vocabulary, 3 modes, one architecture, one conformal score). The main-run 5-seed significance test shows the NLL-scaling effect is real-but-conditional, not yet a precisely-quantified population average; the ablation is now 3-seed (was 1) but still modest, and the affine check is 2-seed. We did not explore full matrix recalibration or verbalized confidence. The title's generality should be read against this: the result is an existence proof that scalar temperature scaling *can* trade fidelity for reportable calibration, and that this is a genuine two-objective conflict rather than a fixable mis-specification — not a claim about average-case magnitude in production LLMs on natural text.

## 4. Conclusion

In a synthetic environment with an exactly known ground-truth predictive distribution, temperature scaling fit by validation NLL — the default LLM calibration recipe — improves oracle-ECE by roughly 2–6× whenever it activates, but always moves the model's predictive distribution further from the true generative process when it does so; across 5 seeds this activates in 2/5 at our main budget, and a paired significance test confirms the effect is directionally consistent but not (yet) a precisely powered population-average claim at this sample size. A 3-seed training-budget ablation shows activation is not a monotonic function of steps — more training makes overconfidence *possible*, not guaranteed. The scalar temperature that best restores fidelity ($T^*_{\text{KL}}\approx0.92$) sits on the opposite side of $T{=}1$ from the one that best matches observed-token likelihood ($T^*_{\text{NLL}}\approx1.02$), and this comparison, always-active by construction, is statistically significant. A richer two-parameter affine recalibration softens but does not eliminate the conflict in the two seeds we tested. Split conformal prediction achieves its coverage guarantee independent of all of this, via rank-invariance. Practically: when downstream use of an LLM's probabilities depends on distributional *shape* (diverse sampling, entropy-based routing) rather than top-1 confidence, temperature scaling's NLL-fitting objective should not be assumed harmless, and coverage-based methods may be a safer default. Future work: matrix/vector recalibration at scale, richer generative processes (context-free grammars), and pretrained LLMs on tasks with partially-known structure.
