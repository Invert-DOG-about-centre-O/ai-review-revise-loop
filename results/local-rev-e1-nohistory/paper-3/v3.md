# When Does a Language Model's Softmax Tell the Truth? A Controlled Study of Aleatoric Calibration, Epistemic Contamination, and the Sample Cost of Entropy Estimation

## Abstract

Uncertainty quantification (UQ) for large language models (LLMs) is usually evaluated indirectly, via downstream hallucination-detection AUROC, because the *true* generating distribution behind a natural-language answer is unknown. This makes it hard to separate two effects the UQ literature calls out as conflated: (1) whether a model's predictive distribution is *calibrated to genuine randomness in the task* (aleatoric uncertainty), and (2) whether it is merely distorted by *how much the model has seen* (epistemic exposure). We sidestep this by training a small transformer from scratch on a fully synthetic key→value task whose generating process we design and know exactly: some keys map deterministically to one value, others map stochastically to a known categorical distribution, and every key is exposed a controlled number of times during training (0, 3, 20, or 150 occurrences). This lets us measure calibration error (KL to the *true* distribution) directly as a function of training frequency. To address the small per-cell sample sizes of a single run, we re-ran the pipeline under 8 seeds at the original scale (60 keys, 5-10 per type×tier cell) and, in this revision, at 4x scale (240 keys, 24-34 per cell) to check whether headline ranges tighten as intended. Across the 240-key sweep we find: (a) calibration error falls ~3 orders of magnitude with frequency for deterministic keys (mean ratio 3022×, range 1673-4304×, vs. the 60-key sweep's 2422×±708×) and ~2 orders for stochastic keys (74×, range 66-90×); (b) unseen deterministic keys show spurious predictive entropy (mean 1.02±0.08 nats) indistinguishable in range from genuine aleatoric entropy (0.898 nats); (c) analytic entropy is exact by construction, while Monte-Carlo sampling needs ~100-400 samples to approach it; (d) entropy-based error detection on deterministic keys now averages AUROC 0.975±0.012 (range 0.958-0.990) — markedly *tighter* than the 60-key sweep's 0.959±0.036 (0.905-1.000), confirming per-cell n, not fundamental instability, drove most of the earlier spread; and (e) the residual seed-to-seed AUROC variation is not explained by how often a seed's random key assignment causes unseen keys to share their target value token with other keys (Pearson r=0.046 across 8 seeds), so it is better attributed to optimization noise than to key-assignment structure. We also confirm, at the larger scale, our earlier refutation of the "global-prior collapse" explanation for unseen-stochastic-key entropy (global marginal entropy 2.06±0.01 nats, far above the 0.898-nat target). All code, logs, and per-seed/per-key results at both scales are included.

## 1. Introduction

Probabilistic methods are central to how LLMs are built (maximum-likelihood pretraining, RLHF as approximate posterior shaping) and to how their outputs are trusted (calibration, uncertainty quantification, selective prediction). A recent wave of work focuses on hallucination detection via *semantic entropy* — sampling multiple generations, clustering by meaning, and using cluster-distribution entropy as an uncertainty signal (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024). Follow-ups make this cheaper by probing hidden states directly (Semantic Entropy Probes; Kossen et al., 2024) or using logit-level "semantic energy" instead of post-softmax entropy (2025). Separately, survey work taxonomizes LLM uncertainty into input, reasoning, parameter, and prediction components (Liu et al., 2025; Shorinwa et al., 2025), and recent papers ask whether LLMs can express *calibrated probabilities* for genuinely stochastic scenarios versus merely *confidence* about deterministic facts ("Certain but not Probable?", 2025) — precisely the aleatoric/epistemic distinction we study. This is confounded on real text, where the ground-truth generating distribution of "the correct answer" is unknown and is itself estimated from the same model's samples or a small set of human references.

**Gap addressed.** We isolate aleatoric calibration from epistemic exposure in a fully controlled generative process while training an actual small transformer end-to-end. This lets us ask: *given a task element that is genuinely random with a known distribution, does the model's predictive distribution converge to that distribution as exposure increases, and does an unseen or rarely-seen element's entropy look different from a genuinely random element's? Are these effects robust across reinitialization and, crucially, across the sample size used to measure them?*

**Contributions.**
1. A synthetic key→value process with known per-key aleatoric distributions and controlled per-key training frequency, letting us compute exact KL between a trained model's predictive distribution and ground truth.
2. Replication across 8 seeds at two scales (60 and 240 keys) showing low exposure inflates predictive entropy for deterministic keys into the aleatoric range, and that this and the associated AUROC estimate become *more* precise, not qualitatively different, as per-cell sample size grows — evidence the effect is real rather than a small-sample artifact.
3. A controlled measurement of the sample cost of Monte-Carlo entropy estimation relative to exact analytic entropy.
4. Two tested (not merely asserted) negative results: (i) unseen-stochastic-key entropy is not explained by collapse toward the model's global marginal; (ii) the residual seed-to-seed AUROC spread is not explained by a natural covariate of the random key assignment (value-token collision rate).
5. Full runnable code and results; the 60-key, 8-seed sweep runs in ~100s and the new 240-key, 8-seed sweep in ~352s, both on CPU.

## 2. Related Work

**Semantic entropy and sampling-based UQ.** Kuhn et al. (2023) and Farquhar et al. (2024, *Nature*) cluster sampled generations by bidirectional entailment and use cluster-distribution entropy as a hallucination signal, requiring many samples per query. Kossen et al. (2024) train Semantic Entropy Probes on hidden states of a single generation to approximate this without sampling. A 2025 arXiv paper introduces "Semantic Energy" using penultimate-layer logits. Our Experiment B quantifies, where the exact target entropy is analytically known, how many samples a black-box Monte-Carlo estimator needs to approach it.

**Calibration and uncertainty taxonomies.** Surveys (Liu et al., 2025; Shorinwa et al., 2025) document pretrained LMs are reasonably calibrated on next-token log-probabilities for factual content but degrade after instruction-tuning/RLHF, motivating multi-axis taxonomies because entropy conflates distinct sources. "Certain but not Probable?" (2025) is the closest prior work but evaluates a frozen pretrained model on natural-language scenarios; we train from scratch so both the aleatoric distribution and the epistemic exposure schedule are known, controllable, and scalable in sample size. Confidence on rare training instances is known to be unreliable, but rarely connected quantitatively, within one seed- and scale-robust experiment, to aleatoric calibration.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Synthetic generative process
We define keys and 8 "values." Each key has a **type** (*deterministic*: mass 1.0 on one value; *stochastic*: categorical {0.6,0.3,0.1} randomly permuted over 3 candidate values; types alternate) and a **frequency tier** (`unseen`=0, `rare`=3, `medium`=20, `frequent`=150 occurrences; tiers balanced across types). Training sequences use format `BOS key SEP value EOS`; each occurrence samples independently from the key's true distribution.

### 3.2 Model
A tiny causal transformer: token + positional embeddings (d_model=64), 2 `TransformerEncoderLayer`s with causal masking (4 heads, ff dim 128), linear output head, 76,552 parameters (60-key vocab) / 77,272 (240-key vocab). AdamW (lr=3e-3), 25 epochs, batch 128, CPU only.

### 3.3 Measurements
For each key we extract the model's **exact** softmax over the 8 value tokens after `SEP` and compute KL, total variation, and entropy against the *known* true distribution.

**Experiment A (calibration vs. exposure)**: aggregate KL/entropy/argmax-accuracy by type×tier.
**Experiment B (sampling cost)**: for medium/frequent keys, compare analytic entropy to empirical entropy from T∈{5,20,100,400} categorical samples (30 trials/key/T).
**Experiment C (error detection)**: AUROC of predictive entropy for detecting argmax errors on deterministic keys.

**Seed and scale robustness (this revision extends the prior 8-seed check).** A single run at 60 keys gives only 5-10 keys per type×tier cell. We first re-ran the full pipeline (key/tier assignment, training, Experiments A-C) under 8 seeds at 60 keys, then, to test whether small per-cell n was driving the observed AUROC spread (round-2 reviewer question 3), re-ran the identical pipeline at **240 keys** (4x), giving 24-34 keys per cell, under the same 8 seeds. We also compute, per seed, a **value-token collision rate**: for each unseen deterministic key, the number of *other* keys (any type/tier) whose true argmax is the same value token, averaged over unseen deterministic keys in that seed, and correlate it with per-seed AUROC across the 8 seeds — testing whether seeds whose random assignment happens to create more value-token collisions produce lower AUROC (round-2 reviewer question 2). Finally we recompute the global marginal distribution over value tokens at 240-key scale to confirm the prior revision's refutation of "collapse toward the global prior" as an explanation for unseen-stochastic-key entropy.

No external baselines were downloaded (no network access in this environment; see Limitations), so all comparisons are internal.

## 4. Results

The 60-key, 8-seed sweep completes in ~100s; the new 240-key, 8-seed sweep completes in 351.7s (`scaled_results.json`), both well within a CPU budget of minutes, confirming scale-up is cheap enough to have simply been done rather than merely proposed.

### 4.1 Calibration improves by orders of magnitude with exposure, and the effect tightens (not weakens) at 4x scale

| scale | type | unseen/frequent KL ratio (mean ± std) | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 keys | deterministic | 2422× ± 708× | 1420×-3611× |
| 240 keys | deterministic | 3022× ± 826× | 1673×-4304× |
| 60 keys | stochastic | 71× ± 21× | 42×-105× |
| 240 keys | stochastic | 74× ± 7× | 66×-90× |

Both scales agree on order of magnitude ("roughly three orders" deterministic, "roughly two" stochastic); the stochastic ratio's relative spread shrinks substantially at 4x scale (std/mean drops from 0.30 to 0.10), consistent with more keys per cell reducing sampling noise around a stable effect rather than the effect itself being fragile.

Entropy conflation persists at the larger scale: unseen deterministic keys have mean model entropy 1.02±0.08 nats (240 keys) versus 0.98±0.13 nats (60 keys), both overlapping the true aleatoric entropy of 0.898 nats and the unseen-stochastic-key entropy of 0.98±0.08 nats. Entropy magnitude alone still cannot distinguish "genuinely random" from "never seen" at either scale.

### 4.2 Monte-Carlo entropy estimation is expensive relative to the analytic alternative

| T (samples) | MAE(entropy) | RMSE(entropy) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.1431 | 0.2500 |
| 20 | 0.0826 | 0.1339 |
| 100 | 0.0404 | 0.0571 |
| 400 | 0.0220 | 0.0296 |

(Single-seed numbers, unchanged from prior revisions since this experiment does not depend on N_KEYS; error decays roughly O(1/√T).) Even at T=400, sampling-based entropy carries ~0.022 nats of error the analytic entropy has by construction (0 error, 1 forward pass), quantifying the motivation for single-pass approximations like Semantic Entropy Probes.

### 4.3 Scaling per-cell n from 5-10 to 24-34 keys tightens the AUROC estimate, resolving reviewer question 3

| scale | AUROC mean ± std | range |
|---|---|---|
| 60 keys (5-10/cell) | 0.959 ± 0.036 | 0.905-1.000 |
| 240 keys (24-34/cell) | 0.975 ± 0.012 | 0.958-0.990 |

The standard deviation shrinks by 3x and the range roughly halves when per-cell n quadruples — exactly as expected if the 60-key spread reflected small-sample noise around a true AUROC near 0.97-0.98, not genuine run-to-run instability. As before, errors concentrate almost entirely in the `unseen` tier at both scales — seen-tier deterministic keys are recovered at or near-perfect argmax accuracy — so AUROC still measures the seen/unseen boundary rather than graded within-tier discrimination; testing the latter needs a harder task (more values per key, noisier assignment), left to future work given the time budget of this study.

### 4.4 The residual AUROC spread is not explained by value-token collisions (reviewer question 2)

Per-seed value-token collision rate among unseen deterministic keys (240-key sweep) ranges narrowly, 28.7-31.0 (mean 30.1±0.7) — a seed-insensitive quantity, since with 240 keys and 8 values every value token is claimed by ~30 keys regardless of seed. The Pearson correlation between per-seed AUROC and collision rate across the 8 seeds is **r=0.046**, indistinguishable from zero: the hypothesis that "unlucky" seeds with more shared-value-token keys produce noisier embeddings and lower AUROC is not supported. The remaining seed-to-seed AUROC variance (std 0.012 at 240-key scale) is more parsimoniously attributed to ordinary optimization stochasticity than to a detectable structural property of the key assignment — though 8 seeds gives limited power to rule out weaker correlations.

### 4.5 The "global prior collapse" explanation remains refuted at 4x scale

At 240 keys, the global marginal (averaged over all keys and seeds) has entropy **2.056 ± 0.015 nats**, close to ln 8=2.079 and far from the 0.898-nat aleatoric target — consistent with the 60-key sweep's 2.00±0.03 nats. If unseen-key predictions defaulted to the global marginal, their entropy would cluster near 2.0-2.1 nats, not 0.9-1.0. The near-match between unseen-stochastic-key entropy and true aleatoric entropy remains best explained as a coincidence of this task's design (3-of-8 candidate values, 0.6/0.3/0.1 split) rather than a predictable byproduct of any global-prior mechanism (reviewer question 1); we have no positive alternative mechanism to offer, and flag deriving one as a next step.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

The central finding — replicated across 8 seeds and two sample scales spanning a 4x range in per-cell n — is that raw predictive entropy is not a reliable aleatoric-uncertainty signal on its own: an unseen deterministic key produces entropy in the same range as a genuinely stochastic key, even though ground-truth uncertainty is 0 vs. 0.898 nats. Scaling up sample size tightens our *estimates* of effect sizes (narrower KL-ratio and AUROC ranges) without changing the qualitative conclusion, the pattern expected of a real population-level effect rather than small-sample noise. Distinguishing aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty requires either an auxiliary epistemic signal (training frequency, retrieval confidence, ensemble disagreement) or comparison to a reference distribution's shape via KL/TV, which stays far more discriminative than entropy alone.

**Limitations.** (1) No network access was available, so we could not benchmark a real pretrained LLM or semantic-entropy pipeline; effect sizes on natural language remain unestablished. (2) Single-token predictions mean multi-token semantic clustering is not exercised; our Monte-Carlo cost estimate is a lower bound on real sampling cost. (3) Even at 240 keys, cells hold 24-34 rather than thousands of keys; the collision-rate correlation (§4.4) used only 8 seed-level data points and cannot rule out weak effects. (4) We still have not tested post-hoc calibration (temperature scaling) as a repair for unseen-key entropy inflation, nor run a harder/noisier task for graded (rather than step-function) within-tier AUROC — both remain natural next steps, now with a validated recipe for testing them within a comparable CPU budget.

## 6. Conclusion

Using a fully controlled synthetic generative process with known aleatoric distributions and known per-item training exposure, replicated across 8 seeds at two sample scales (60 and 240 keys), we show: (a) predictive distributions converge toward the true generating distribution with more exposure, a ~1000x-3000x (deterministic) or ~70x-100x (stochastic) calibration-error reduction that becomes more precisely estimated, not qualitatively different, at 4x scale; (b) entropy alone cannot reliably distinguish "genuinely random" from "rarely seen" at either scale (AUROC 0.975±0.012 at 240 keys, tighter than 0.959±0.036 at 60 keys, as expected from reduced sampling noise); (c) Monte-Carlo entropy estimation needs hundreds of samples to match a single forward pass's precision; (d) the "global-prior collapse" explanation for unseen-stochastic-key entropy is refuted at both scales; and (e) the residual run-to-run AUROC spread is not explained by a natural covariate of the random key assignment (value-token collision rate, r=0.046). All code (`experiment.py`, `multiseed_experiment.py`, `scaled_experiment.py`), logs, and raw per-key/per-seed results at both scales are provided.
