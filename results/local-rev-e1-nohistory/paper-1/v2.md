# When Is Sampling Worth It? A Controlled Comparison of Token-Probability and Sampling-Based Uncertainty Signals for LLM Error Detection

## Abstract

Recent work on uncertainty quantification (UQ) for LLMs argues that token-level probabilities are unreliable — alignment training distorts them, and paraphrase fragments probability mass — and proposes sampling-based alternatives like semantic entropy and self-consistency. These claims are usually validated on open-domain tasks where "correctness" is itself judged by another model, confounding signal quality with judge noise. We instead use a setting with **exact, judge-free ground truth**: a small transformer trained from scratch on multi-digit integer addition, where an answer is either the correct sum or not. On 400 held-out problems we compare six confidence/uncertainty signals — mean/min token log-probability, perplexity, first-token entropy, semantic entropy, and self-consistency agreement — as error detectors (AUROC) and calibrated estimates (ECE). Token-probability signals (AUROC 0.955) *outperform* semantic entropy (0.930) and self-consistency (0.933), and self-consistency majority voting gives no accuracy lift over greedy decoding (92.75% both). To check this isn't a single-run artifact, we rerun the full pipeline on 3 independent seeds with widely varying error rates (21–80 errors out of 150 problems, accuracy 46.7–86.0%): the token-probability advantage is positive in every seed and tightly clustered (mean ΔAUROC = +0.040 vs. semantic entropy, +0.036 vs. self-consistency; std ≈ 0.006–0.007), indicating the ranking is not a fluke of one evaluation draw. This suggests sampling-based UQ's advantage over token probabilities depends substantially on properties of open-ended generation (paraphrase, divergent reasoning paths) absent in single-canonical-answer tasks, and practitioners should verify their task has those properties before assuming sampling is a strict upgrade.

## 1. Introduction

LLMs frequently produce fluent but incorrect output, and detecting this requires some signal about the model's confidence. The default tool is token-level probability, but recent literature argues this signal is compromised: alignment training rewards confident-sounding text regardless of correctness, and a single meaning can be expressed by many token sequences, so probability mass on any one surface form understates true confidence.

This motivated **sampling-based** UQ methods. Semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024) samples multiple generations, clusters them by semantic equivalence, and computes entropy over clusters rather than token sequences. Self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) samples multiple reasoning chains and majority-votes over final answers. Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024) approximate this signal from a single forward pass's hidden states; we do not evaluate probing here since it requires a pretrained LLM's internal representations, but flag it as a natural next comparison (Section 5).

### Related work and the gap we address

Surveys of LLM UQ report that sampling/consistency-based methods generally dominate raw token probability on open-domain QA and reasoning benchmarks. A recurring limitation is evaluation methodology: because ground-truth correctness for open-ended generation is itself uncertain, most benchmarks use an LLM judge or human annotation, which is noisy and makes it hard to isolate whether a signal fails because of the signal itself or judge noise correlated with difficulty. Most evaluations also mix tasks where sampling should help for principled reasons (paraphrase-heavy QA, multi-hop reasoning with divergent valid paths) with a blanket "prefer sampling" recommendation, without isolating *when* token-probability specifically fails.

We complement this with a small, controlled study: integer addition, where correctness is a deterministic function of the output and the answer space is a single canonical string (no paraphrase fragmentation) — letting us cleanly ask whether sampling-based signals still beat token-probability when the theoretical reason sampling should help is absent by construction.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Task and model

We generate `"a+b="` → `"c"` examples with `a, b ~ Uniform[0, 999]`, tokenized at the character level. We train a small GPT-style decoder-only transformer from scratch (3 layers, 4 heads, d_model=128, d_ff=256; ≈0.6M params) with loss masked to answer/EOS tokens only, using AdamW (lr 3e-4, batch 128). This sandboxed environment had no network access to model hubs, hence training from scratch rather than probing a pretrained LLM (Section 5); the controlled ground truth is independently valuable regardless of model provenance.

**Main run** (Section 3): fixed 240s CPU training budget, 3,937 steps, final loss 0.046, evaluated on 400 held-out problems.

**Multi-seed robustness check** (Section 3.1): to test whether the main-run ranking survives re-randomization, we train 3 additional independent models (different init, training stream, and 150-problem eval set), each under a reduced 130s budget (~1,740 steps) so 3 full train+eval cycles fit the compute available for this revision. This yields far more varied accuracy across seeds (46.7–86.0%) than the main run — a feature, not a confound, since it stress-tests whether the ranking holds as error rate and composition shift substantially.

### 2.2 Confidence/uncertainty signals

From a single **greedy** decode: **mean/min token log-probability**, **perplexity** (`exp(-mean log-prob)`), and **first-token predictive entropy**. From **K=8 temperature samples** (T=0.9): **semantic entropy** over the empirical distribution of sampled integer values (equivalence is exact integer equality here, not NLI-based, since this task has no paraphrase ambiguity), and **self-consistency agreement** (fraction of samples matching the greedy answer).

### 2.3 Evaluation

We report **AUROC** for detecting greedy-answer errors, **ECE** (10 bins) for two derived confidence scores, and **self-consistency majority-vote accuracy vs. greedy accuracy**. For the multi-seed check we report the per-seed AUROC gap between the strongest token-probability signal and each sampling-based signal, and its mean/std — a paired comparison controlling for the fact that absolute AUROC moves with the error rate.

## 3. Results

Training reached 92.75% greedy accuracy on 400 held-out problems (29 errors, 371 correct).

| Signal | Type | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| −mean log-prob | token-probability | **0.955** |
| perplexity | token-probability | **0.955** |
| −min log-prob | token-probability | 0.952 |
| −sample agreement | sampling-based | 0.933 |
| semantic entropy | sampling-based | 0.930 |
| first-token entropy | token-probability | 0.809 |

Whole-answer token-probability aggregates (mean/min log-prob, perplexity) are the strongest error detectors, edging out both sampling-based signals. First-token entropy alone is far weaker — it only reflects uncertainty at the first digit — showing "token probability" is not monolithic: *which* aggregation is used matters as much as token- vs. sample-based framing.

### 3.1 Is this ranking stable across seeds?

The original review of this paper correctly noted that a single run with 29 errors cannot establish that a ~0.02 AUROC gap is real rather than noise. We reran the full pipeline on 3 independent seeds with their own init, training stream, and 150-problem eval set. Accuracy varied substantially (46.7%, 86.0%, 78.7%; 80, 21, 32 errors) — a much wider spread than the main run.

| Seed | Accuracy | Errors | −mean log-prob AUROC | Semantic entropy AUROC | Self-consistency AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 46.7% | 80 | 0.893 | 0.858 | 0.859 |
| 1 | 86.0% | 21 | 0.968 | 0.918 | 0.923 |
| 2 | 78.7% | 32 | 0.941 | 0.904 | 0.910 |
| **mean ± std** | — | — | **0.934 ± 0.031** | **0.893 ± 0.026** | **0.898 ± 0.028** |

Token-probability beats both sampling-based signals in all 3 seeds. The paired gap (computed per seed, so not confounded by differing error rates) is consistently positive and tightly clustered: **+0.040 ± 0.007** vs. semantic entropy, **+0.036 ± 0.006** vs. self-consistency, despite accuracy swinging by almost 40 points. This is the central new evidence in this revision: the direction of the effect is robust to re-randomization, even as absolute AUROC moves with the error rate. We do not overclaim: 3 seeds show the ranking isn't a fluke of one draw, but do not support a formal significance test — "consistent across all tested seeds, low variance," not "significant at n=3."

**Calibration.** ECE is 0.041 (token-probability) and 0.035 (sample-agreement); both are reasonably calibrated where confidence > 0.9 (97.9% and 99.3% empirical accuracy respectively) but markedly overconfident in the low-confidence tail (e.g., the [0.5, 0.6) token-probability bin: 8 examples, ~0.57 mean confidence, 0% accuracy) — exactly where error detection matters. This shows ECE and AUROC answer different questions: a signal can look calibrated in aggregate while being a poor error detector at the decision-relevant threshold.

**Self-consistency vs. greedy accuracy.** Majority-vote accuracy over K=8 was 92.75%, identical to greedy. Unlike multi-step reasoning (e.g., GSM8K-style CoT) where divergent sampled reasoning paths can land on different, sometimes better, answers, single-forward-pass arithmetic has no analogous alternate-path structure: resampling mostly reproduces the greedy answer or its error mode. This is a direct instance of the paper's thesis — self-consistency's benefit is tied to a structural property this task lacks by construction.

## 4. Discussion

Token probabilities are not secretly better than sampling-based UQ in general — the *mechanism* by which sampling helps (resolving paraphrase ambiguity, aggregating divergent reasoning paths) is absent in a single-canonical-answer task, so sampling adds only variance and compute cost without added signal. Section 3.1 rules out the obvious alternative explanation (a coincidental evaluation set) without overclaiming rigor from 3 seeds. Whole-answer token-probability remains a strong, cheap baseline whenever a task has (i) a small/canonical answer space and (ii) no meaningful multiple-paths-to-the-same-answer structure — a concrete, testable criterion before paying the K× cost of sampling-based UQ: does correctness admit multiple structurally different correct generations? If not, whole-answer aggregation (not just first-token entropy) is a reasonable, possibly stronger, default.

## 5. Limitations

(1) Model trained from scratch on a synthetic task rather than a pretrained LLM on natural language, due to no network access in this environment; the qualitative claim is consistent with the semantic-entropy literature's mechanism but unverified on real alignment-induced miscalibration, and we have not evaluated Semantic Entropy Probes. (2) The multi-seed check uses only 3 seeds, a reduced training budget, and smaller (150-problem) eval sets due to this revision's compute budget — enough to show the sign of the gap is consistent, not enough for a formal significance test, and these models are less accurate than the main run's 92.75%. (3) We did not complete a K-sample ablation (K=8/16/32): an initial attempt using undertrained models gave uninterpretable results and was discarded rather than reported, and there was no remaining budget to redo it properly; whether larger K closes the gap remains open. (4) Exact integer equality is a best-case semantic-equivalence oracle (no NLI-judge noise); with model-based equivalence checking, semantic entropy's relative disadvantage here may not transfer. (5) We have not built a second controlled task with genuine multiple-valid-derivation structure to show the same pipeline recovers a sampling advantage when semantic multiplicity is present by construction — the most valuable direction for follow-up work.

## 6. Conclusion

In a controlled, judge-free setting built to remove the paraphrase and multi-path-reasoning effects motivating sampling-based UQ, whole-answer token-probability signals matched or exceeded semantic entropy and self-consistency, and self-consistency gave zero accuracy lift over greedy decoding. A 3-seed robustness check with widely varying error rates (21–80 errors) confirms this ranking is not a single-run artifact: the token-probability advantage is positive and low-variance (+0.04 AUROC, std ≈ 0.007) in every seed tested. This sharpens rather than contradicts the sampling-based UQ literature's scope: reported gains likely come specifically from tasks with semantic multiplicity or divergent reasoning paths, and the field would benefit from evaluations that isolate this variable directly — including, per our own unresolved limitations, K-sample ablations and a matched "semantic multiplicity present" control task.
