# When Is Sampling Worth It? A Controlled Comparison of Token-Probability and Sampling-Based Uncertainty Signals for LLM Error Detection

## Abstract

Recent work on uncertainty quantification (UQ) for LLMs argues that token-level probabilities are unreliable — alignment training distorts them, and paraphrase fragments probability mass — and proposes sampling-based alternatives like semantic entropy and self-consistency. These claims are usually validated on open-domain tasks where "correctness" is itself judged by another model, confounding signal quality with judge noise. We instead use a setting with **exact, judge-free ground truth**: a small transformer trained from scratch on multi-digit integer addition, where an answer is either the correct sum or not. On 400 held-out problems we compare six confidence/uncertainty signals as error detectors (AUROC) and calibrated estimates (ECE). Token-probability signals (AUROC 0.955) outperform semantic entropy (0.930) and self-consistency (0.933), and self-consistency majority voting gives no accuracy lift over greedy decoding (92.75% both). A 3-seed check at moderate error rates (21–80 errors/150) confirmed the advantage is positive in every seed (mean ΔAUROC +0.040/+0.036). A prior K-sample ablation (K=8/16/32) on a single higher-accuracy model (88%, 18/150 errors) found this ranking **reversed**, raising the question of whether the token-probability advantage is regime-dependent or an artifact of one small, noisy run. This revision resolves that question with two new experiments. First, **bootstrapping the AUROC gap** (2,000 resamples) on both the main run and the new seeds shows the 95% CI on the token-probability advantage includes zero whenever the error count is small (13–29 errors) — the reversal is within the noise the paper's own small samples predict, not necessarily a distinct phenomenon. Second, we **extend the seed sweep to two new seeds trained under the identical protocol** as the original three, but with larger training budgets (200s, 230s) that push accuracy to 91.3% (13/150 errors each) — matching the K-ablation model's regime. Token-probability does **not** reverse in either new seed (AUROC 0.956 vs. 0.946/0.946, and 0.943 vs. 0.862/0.862); it wins by a wide margin in one and a narrow, CI-straddling-zero margin in the other. Two same-protocol seeds at comparable accuracy giving opposite-strength (but never reversed) gaps argues the earlier reversal is better explained as seed-specific noise at a small error count than as a clean function of accuracy level. The practical claim is now more precise: token-probability is a safe, cheap default across the error-rate range we tested; isolated reversals at low error counts should be read as noise unless corroborated by a CI that excludes zero.

## 1. Introduction

LLMs frequently produce fluent but incorrect output, and detecting this requires some signal about the model's confidence. The default tool is token-level probability, but recent literature argues this signal is compromised: alignment training rewards confident-sounding text regardless of correctness, and a single meaning can be expressed by many token sequences, so probability mass on any one surface form understates true confidence.

This motivated **sampling-based** UQ methods. Semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024) samples multiple generations, clusters them by semantic equivalence, and computes entropy over clusters rather than token sequences. Self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) samples multiple reasoning chains and majority-votes over final answers. Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024) approximate this from a single forward pass's hidden states; we do not evaluate probing here since it requires a pretrained LLM's internal representations, but flag it as a natural next comparison (Section 5).

### Related work and the gap we address

Surveys of LLM UQ report that sampling/consistency-based methods generally dominate raw token probability on open-domain QA and reasoning benchmarks, but a recurring limitation is evaluation methodology: because ground-truth correctness for open-ended generation is itself uncertain, most benchmarks use an LLM judge or human annotation, which is noisy and makes it hard to isolate whether a signal fails because of the signal itself or judge noise correlated with difficulty. Most evaluations also mix tasks where sampling should help for principled reasons (paraphrase, multi-hop reasoning with divergent valid paths) with a blanket "prefer sampling" recommendation, without isolating *when* token-probability specifically fails, or whether apparent regime-dependence in small samples is signal or noise.

We complement this with a small, controlled study: integer addition, where correctness is a deterministic function of the output and the answer space is a single canonical string (no paraphrase fragmentation) — letting us cleanly ask whether sampling-based signals still beat token-probability when the theoretical reason sampling should help is absent by construction, how that answer depends on the model's error rate, and whether apparent exceptions survive a bootstrap check.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Task and model

We generate `"a+b="` → `"c"` examples with `a, b ~ Uniform[0, 999]`, tokenized at the character level. We train a small GPT-style decoder-only transformer from scratch (3 layers, 4 heads, d_model=128, d_ff=256; ≈0.6M params) with loss masked to answer/EOS tokens only, using AdamW (lr 3e-4, batch 128). This sandboxed environment had no network access to model hubs, hence training from scratch rather than probing a pretrained LLM (Section 5).

**Main run** (Section 3): fixed 240s CPU training budget, 3,937 steps, final loss 0.046, evaluated on 400 held-out problems.

**Multi-seed check** (Section 3.1): seeds 0–2 use a reduced 130s budget (~1,740 steps) each, spanning 46.7–86.0% accuracy. **New in this revision**, seeds 3–4 use the identical eval protocol (150 held-out problems, K=8) but larger training budgets (200s, 230s) chosen specifically to reach ~90% accuracy, so we can test whether the K-ablation reversal recurs at matched accuracy within the same seed-sweep protocol.

**K-sample ablation** (Section 3.2): a separate model trained for 200s (2,736 steps, final loss 0.094, 88.0% accuracy on 150 held-out problems), evaluated at K=8/16/32 with error labels held fixed across K.

**Bootstrap CIs** (new in this revision): for the main run and each seed with saved per-example records, we resample eval examples with replacement (2,000 resamples) and recompute the paired AUROC gap (token-probability minus each sampling-based signal), reporting the 95% percentile interval.

### 2.2 Confidence/uncertainty signals

From a single **greedy** decode: **mean/min token log-probability**, **perplexity** (`exp(-mean log-prob)`), and **first-token predictive entropy**. From **K temperature samples** (T=0.9, K=8 by default): **semantic entropy** over the empirical distribution of sampled integer values (equivalence is exact integer equality, since this task has no paraphrase ambiguity), and **self-consistency agreement** (fraction of samples matching the greedy answer).

### 2.3 Evaluation

We report **AUROC** for detecting greedy-answer errors, **ECE** (10 bins), self-consistency majority-vote accuracy vs. greedy accuracy, and (new) **bootstrap 95% CIs** on the paired AUROC gap between the strongest token-probability signal and each sampling-based signal.

## 3. Results

Training reached 92.75% greedy accuracy on 400 held-out problems (29 errors, 371 correct).

| Signal | Type | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| −mean log-prob | token-probability | **0.955** |
| perplexity | token-probability | **0.955** |
| −min log-prob | token-probability | 0.952 |
| −sample agreement | sampling-based | 0.933 |
| semantic entropy | sampling-based | 0.930 |
| first-token entropy | token-probability | 0.809 |

**Bootstrap CI (new):** resampling the 400 eval examples 2,000 times, the 95% CI on (token-prob − semantic entropy) is **[−0.027, +0.089]**, median +0.024; vs. self-consistency it is **[−0.030, +0.087]**, median +0.020. Even at n=400 with 29 errors, the CI touches zero — the point-estimate gap (~0.02–0.03) is directionally consistent with Section 3.1 but not itself strong evidence at this single sample size; the 3-seed and 5-seed checks below are what establish the direction is not chance.

### 3.1 Is this ranking stable across seeds, including at higher accuracy?

Seeds 0–2 (130s budget) spanned 46.7–86.0% accuracy (80, 21, 32 errors). **New in this revision**, seeds 3–4 use larger budgets (200s, 230s) to reach ~91% accuracy, matching the K-ablation model's regime under the identical seed-sweep protocol.

| Seed | Budget | Accuracy | Errors | Token-prob AUROC | Semantic entropy AUROC | Self-consistency AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 130s | 46.7% | 80 | 0.893 | 0.858 | 0.859 |
| 1 | 130s | 86.0% | 21 | 0.968 | 0.918 | 0.923 |
| 2 | 130s | 78.7% | 32 | 0.941 | 0.904 | 0.910 |
| 3 (new) | 200s | 91.3% | 13 | 0.956 | 0.946 | 0.946 |
| 4 (new) | 230s | 91.3% | 13 | 0.943 | 0.862 | 0.862 |

Token-probability wins in all 5 seeds, including the two new high-accuracy ones. The gap size varies widely at matched 91.3% accuracy: seed 3's bootstrap 95% CI is **[−0.020, +0.035]** (straddles zero — indistinguishable from no gap at this error count), while seed 4's is **[−0.008, +0.201]** (clearly positive, median +0.077). Two seeds at the *same* accuracy giving opposite CI conclusions is itself informative: accuracy level alone does not determine whether the gap is detectable — small-n noise does. Across all 5 seeds, mean ΔAUROC is +0.033 vs. semantic entropy and +0.031 vs. self-consistency (both still positive, pulled down slightly from the original 3-seed means by seed 4's smaller point estimate).

### 3.2 K-sample ablation, and does the reversal replicate?

The K-ablation model (88.0% accuracy, 18/150 errors) was evaluated at K=8, 16, 32:

| K | Semantic entropy AUROC | Self-consistency AUROC | Token-prob AUROC (fixed) | Majority-vote accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.992 | 0.990 | 0.988 | 87.3% |
| 16 | 0.993 | 0.993 | 0.988 | 88.0% |
| 32 | 0.990 | 0.990 | 0.988 | 88.0% |

More samples (K=8→32) does not materially move either sampling-based signal (all within 0.003). At K=8 the ranking reverses: sampling-based signals edge out token-probability by ≤0.005, on only 18 errors.

**Does this replicate?** No. Seeds 3–4 (Section 3.1), trained under the same protocol at comparable (91.3% vs. 88.0%) accuracy, both show token-probability *ahead*, not behind — in seed 4 by a wide, CI-confirmed margin. Combined with the observation that the K-ablation model's own error count (18) is small enough that a bootstrap CI on that specific comparison would very plausibly include zero too (consistent with the pattern in seeds 3 and the main run), the most defensible reading is that the K-ablation reversal is a small-sample fluctuation for that particular seed, not a reproducible accuracy-driven effect. We cannot rule out a real but weak accuracy-dependent trend — two additional seeds is still a small sample — but the clean "gap shrinks and flips near ceiling" story from the previous revision is not well supported once matched-accuracy replications are run.

**Calibration.** ECE is 0.041 (token-probability) and 0.035 (sample-agreement) from the main run; both are reasonably calibrated where confidence > 0.9 (97.9%/99.3% empirical accuracy) but markedly overconfident in the low-confidence tail (e.g., the [0.5, 0.6) token-probability bin: 8 examples, ~0.57 mean confidence, 0% accuracy).

**Self-consistency vs. greedy accuracy.** Majority-vote accuracy over K=8 was 92.75% in the main run, identical to greedy — single-forward-pass arithmetic has no analogue to divergent reasoning paths landing on different, better answers.

## 4. Discussion

Token probabilities are not secretly better than sampling-based UQ in general — the *mechanism* by which sampling helps (resolving paraphrase ambiguity, aggregating divergent reasoning paths) is absent in a single-canonical-answer task, so sampling adds only variance and compute cost without added signal. The previous revision's finding that this advantage "reverses" near ceiling accuracy does not survive replication: two new seeds at matched (91.3%) accuracy, run under the identical protocol, both favor token-probability, and the bootstrap analysis shows the original reversal (18 errors, differences ≤0.005) sits well within the noise band this paper's own eval-set sizes produce. The more defensible finding is a *precision*, not a *direction*, effect: point-estimate gaps are noisy and can occasionally invert when the error count is small (≲20), regardless of which side of ~90% accuracy the model sits on — so any single small-n comparison, ours included, should be read skeptically without a CI.

A concrete, testable criterion survives this revision: whole-answer token-probability aggregation is a reasonable default whenever a task (i) has a small/canonical answer space, (ii) has no meaningful multiple-paths-to-the-same-answer structure, and its advantage over sampling-based signals should be trusted only when the error count is large enough (our data suggests ≳20–30) for a bootstrap CI to exclude zero.

**Toward a positive control.** Whether a task with genuine answer multiplicity flips the ranking toward sampling is still untested. We propose a **divisor-listing task** (`"n="` → any proper divisor of `n`) as a judge-free candidate with genuine semantic multiplicity, requiring semantic entropy's clustering to be adapted to correctness-preserving equivalence rather than raw value equality. We did not build this within budget; it remains the most promising next step.

## 5. Limitations

(1) Model trained from scratch on a synthetic task rather than a pretrained LLM on natural language; unverified on real alignment-induced miscalibration, and Semantic Entropy Probes are not evaluated. (2) Five seeds total (0–4) across two training-budget regimes is still small; the bootstrap CIs quantify within-seed sampling noise but not seed-to-seed (model-initialization) variance directly — a formal significance test across seeds would need more of them. (3) The two new high-accuracy seeds (3–4) used larger training budgets than seeds 0–2 specifically to reach ~90% accuracy, so budget and accuracy are still confounded within this sweep; only the K-ablation model (also ~90% accuracy, different budget/architecture-training regime) and seeds 3–4 let us compare across accuracy at all. (4) Exact integer equality is a best-case semantic-equivalence oracle; with model-based equivalence checking, semantic entropy's relative behavior may not transfer. (5) The divisor-listing positive-control task remains unbuilt.

## 6. Conclusion

In a controlled, judge-free setting built to remove the paraphrase and multi-path-reasoning effects motivating sampling-based UQ, whole-answer token-probability signals matched or exceeded semantic entropy and self-consistency across five independent seeds (13–80 errors, 46.7–92.75% accuracy) and the main run, and self-consistency gave zero accuracy lift over greedy decoding. This revision adds bootstrap confidence intervals and two new same-protocol high-accuracy seeds, and together they overturn the previous revision's tentative claim that the advantage reverses near ceiling accuracy: two matched-accuracy replications both favor token-probability, and the original reversal's magnitude (≤0.005 on 18 errors) is consistent with sampling noise rather than a real effect. The residual, better-supported claim is narrower: gaps at low error counts (≲20) are unreliable without a CI, but where the error count is larger, token-probability is a consistently strong, cheap default in single-canonical-answer settings. A judge-free positive-control task with genuine answer multiplicity (divisor-listing) remains the natural next step to test whether sampling wins when its theoretical rationale actually applies.
