# Does Semantic Clustering Actually Beat Lexical Entropy? A Controlled Toy Study of Uncertainty Signals in a Small Transformer

## Abstract

Semantic entropy — clustering sampled generations by meaning before computing
entropy — is widely presented as an improvement over naive lexical (surface-form)
entropy for detecting hallucinations in LLMs, on the intuition that surface-form
diversity inflates apparent uncertainty for questions that are actually
semantically determinate. We test this in a fully controlled synthetic
environment where ground-truth ambiguity is known by construction. We train a
small (≈0.4M-parameter) causal Transformer from scratch on a synthetic
"capital city" QA task with three question classes — deterministic (one true
answer, two surface aliases), genuinely ambiguous (2–3 true answers), and
out-of-distribution (OOD, unseen at training) — and compare lexical vs.
oracle-semantic sampling entropy as predictors of ground-truth ambiguity,
**across 5 random seeds** to give the comparison a variance estimate (a gap in
our earlier single-seed version). Lexical entropy is a perfect ambiguity
predictor in every seed (AUROC 1.000 ± 0.000), while oracle-semantic entropy is
slightly but consistently lower (0.961 ± 0.018) — a small, seed-stable gap, not
noise. We also directly probe *why*: an alias-count ablation (2, 4, 8 aliases
per city) shows the lexical-entropy edge shrinks as surface diversity grows
(AUROC 1.000 → 0.997 → 0.986), confirming the review's hypothesis that the null
result is a designed-in consequence of our default 2-alias world rather than a
general claim. The length-entropy confound we originally reported as absent is
in fact seed-dependent and non-negligible (Pearson r ranges from −0.53 to +0.41
across seeds for semantic entropy) — we retract the "no confound" framing and
replace it with the honest, noisier picture. Finally, we quantify the
out-of-distribution result with AUROC (previously only means/rates): semantic
entropy separates OOD from in-distribution questions at AUROC 0.899 ± 0.027,
confirming entropy-based UQ correctly flags epistemic uncertainty for unseen
entities. All code, logs, and raw results (including the new multi-seed and
ablation runs) are included for inspection and re-run.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect or unsupported
statements ("hallucinations"), and a growing line of work uses **sampling-based
uncertainty quantification (UQ)** — generating multiple completions and
measuring their diversity — as a training-free detector. The best-known method,
**semantic entropy** (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*),
clusters sampled answers into equivalence classes using an NLI model before
computing entropy, on the argument that naive token- or string-level entropy
conflates *how an answer is phrased* with *whether the model knows the answer*.
Follow-up work proposes cheaper approximations — **Semantic Entropy Probes**
(Kossen et al., 2024), **Kernel Language Entropy** (NeurIPS 2024), **Semantic
Energy** (2025), and **Semantic Reformulation Entropy** (2025).

A recent evaluation paper — *"Revisiting Uncertainty Quantification Evaluation
in Language Models: Spurious Interactions with Response Length"* (2025) — warns
that reported UQ benchmark gains may partly reflect a length confound rather
than genuine discrimination. Survey work (KDD 2025; ACM CSUR 2025) notes that
evaluating semantic vs. lexical clustering cleanly is hard: real QA datasets
have unknown ground-truth ambiguity, clustering depends on an imperfect
NLI/judge model, and answer length is entangled with topic and difficulty.

**Gap.** We are not aware of small, fully controlled studies isolating the
lexical-vs-semantic comparison from these confounds via a synthetic world where
true ambiguity and semantic equivalence classes are known exactly.

**Contribution.** We build such an environment, train a small Transformer on it
from scratch, and — following round-1 review — extend the study with (1) a
5-seed replication giving every headline number a variance estimate, (2) an
alias-count ablation that directly tests the mechanism we proposed for the null
result, and (3) a quantitative (AUROC) treatment of the OOD claim, previously
reported only as raw means. The revised picture: lexical entropy's edge is real
but modest and shrinks with more surface variation; the length confound is not
absent, it is noisy and seed-dependent; and OOD detection is solid but
imperfect (AUROC ≈0.90, not the near-ceiling implied by the raw mean deltas
alone).

## 2. Related Work

- **Semantic entropy family.** Kuhn, Gal & Farquhar (ICLR 2023) cluster sampled
  generations via NLI entailment before computing entropy; Farquhar et al.
  (*Nature*, 2024) scale this to detect confabulations. Kossen et al. (2024)
  show semantic uncertainty is largely linearly decodable from hidden states.
  Kernel Language Entropy (NeurIPS 2024) generalizes hard clustering to a
  continuous similarity kernel. Semantic Energy (2025) clusters penultimate
  logits instead of post-softmax probabilities. Semantic Reformulation Entropy
  (2025) perturbs the input rather than clustering outputs.
- **Confound / evaluation critiques.** The length-bias paper (arXiv:2504.13677,
  2025) shows several UQ benchmarks' apparent gains correlate with answer
  length rather than calibration — a theme echoed in the KDD 2025 and ACM CSUR
  survey literature.
- **This paper's relation to that work.** We contribute a controlled instrument
  for testing these claims (semantic > lexical; length confounds exist) at
  small scale with exact ground truth, and — per review — now report the
  instrument's own seed-to-seed variance rather than a single draw.

## 3. Method

**Synthetic world.** 65 "countries," split into 25 deterministic (one true
city), 25 ambiguous (2–3 valid cities), and 15 OOD (never seen in training).
15 distinct "cities" each have (by default) 2 surface **aliases** — random
token sequences referring to the same entity, analogous to paraphrase variation
("NYC" vs. "New York City"). This gives an *oracle* semantic map (alias → city)
that is exact, unlike real NLI clustering.

**Model.** A causal Transformer (2 layers, d_model=64, 4 heads, dff=128,
≈0.4M parameters), trained with teacher forcing for 1200 steps (batch 64, Adam,
lr=3e-3), CPU only.

**Sampling & metrics.** K=40 samples per question. **Lexical entropy**: Shannon
entropy over raw output token sequences. **Semantic entropy**: entropy over the
oracle-mapped city id ("UNK" if unmapped, tracked as *unmapped rate*). Mean
output length and correctness rate are also recorded.

**Evaluation questions, revised.** (a) *Ambiguity discrimination*: AUROC of
each entropy metric, **repeated over 5 seeds** (0–4; each seed reshuffles which
countries are det/amb/OOD, which aliases each city gets, and retrains the model
from scratch) to give the AUROC gap a variance estimate. (b) *Length confound*:
within deterministic countries, Pearson r between mean answer length and
entropy, **also over the same 5 seeds**. (c) *Temperature ablation* (T=0.6,
1.0, 1.5), seed 0. (d) *OOD behavior*: now scored with **AUROC** (OOD vs.
det+amb) for semantic entropy, lexical entropy, and unmapped rate, over the
same 5 seeds. (e) **New: alias-count ablation** — rerun the full pipeline
(seed 0) with 2, 4, and 8 aliases per city, directly testing whether the
lexical-entropy edge is an artifact of the 2-alias default, as the review
hypothesized.

## 4. Results

Training converges in ~20–25s per run (final loss 0.87–1.13 depending on
seed/alias count, higher with more aliases — expected, since more aliases
raise the achievable label-noise floor). All numbers below are reproducible via
`experiment.py` (original single-seed pipeline) and `experiment_v2.py`
(5-seed + ablation runs); raw output in `results.json` and `results_v2.json`.

**(a) Ambiguity discrimination, 5 seeds (T=1.0, K=40, 2 aliases/city).**

| Metric | AUROC (mean ± std, n=5) |
|---|---|
| Lexical entropy | **1.000 ± 0.000** |
| Oracle semantic entropy | 0.961 ± 0.018 (range 0.930–0.987) |

The lexical edge over semantic entropy is real and consistent — lexical hits
the AUROC ceiling in all 5 seeds, semantic never does — but the *margin* is
small (≈0.04) and the semantic AUROC itself is still very high. This is a
modest, seed-stable effect, not the dramatic separation a single seed's
1.00-vs-0.96 might suggest in isolation.

**(b) Length confound, 5 seeds — revised finding.**

| Metric | Pearson r across seeds (min, mean, max) |
|---|---|
| Lexical entropy | −0.178, 0.122, 0.412 |
| Semantic entropy | −0.531, −0.090, 0.341 |

Our original single-seed report (r ≈ −0.01 / −0.06) understated the true
picture: correlation magnitude varies substantially by seed, reaching
|r| > 0.4–0.5 for both metrics in some draws, with sign flipping across seeds.
Averaged over seeds the correlation is near zero (mean ≈ 0.12 lexical, −0.09
semantic), consistent with *no systematic* confound — but the per-seed spread
means a single run cannot rule one out, and this design (independent alias
length draws, single-shot per-position sampling) is still not a good proxy for
the compounding, multi-step length confound reported in real autoregressive
generation by the length-bias literature. We revise our claim from "no
confound emerges" to "no confound emerges *on average*, but per-seed noise is
large enough that this mechanism is not cleanly separable from a small
systematic effect at this sample size (25 deterministic countries/seed)."

**(c) Temperature ablation (seed 0).**

| Temperature | AUROC lexical | AUROC semantic |
|---|---|---|
| 0.6 | 0.966 | 0.948 |
| 1.0 | 1.000 | 0.967 |
| 1.5 | 0.992 | 0.968 |

Consistent with the 5-seed result: a small, positive lexical edge at all three
temperatures.

**(d) Out-of-distribution detection — now quantified with AUROC, 5 seeds.**

| Signal | AUROC (OOD vs. in-distribution, mean ± std) |
|---|---|
| Semantic entropy | 0.899 ± 0.027 |
| Lexical entropy | 0.841 ± 0.047 |
| Unmapped ("novel token") rate | 0.656 ± 0.078 |

This directly answers the review's request for a quantitative OOD metric.
Entropy-based signals are good but *not* near-ceiling OOD detectors here
(semantic AUROC ≈0.90, well below the 1.00 lexical AUROC seen for
ambiguity detection); unmapped rate alone is a substantially weaker, noisier
signal (AUROC 0.66) than entropy, so the >10× mean-rate increase reported in
our original write-up overstated how discriminative that particular signal is
in isolation.

**(e) Alias-count ablation (seed 0): does the lexical edge survive more
surface diversity?**

| Aliases/city | AUROC lexical | AUROC semantic | corr(len, lex) | corr(len, sem) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 (default) | 1.000 | 0.965 | −0.009 | −0.058 |
| 4 | 0.997 | 0.976 | 0.229 | 0.232 |
| 8 | 0.986 | 0.955 | 0.066 | 0.370 |

This is the direct test the review asked for. As alias count grows from 2 to 8,
lexical entropy's AUROC drops monotonically (1.000 → 0.997 → 0.986) while
semantic entropy's does not track it consistently (0.965 → 0.976 → 0.955) —
i.e., more surface diversity per meaning erodes exactly the lexical-entropy
advantage we report, as the "additive offset" mechanism predicts once alias
diversity grows. The length confound also becomes non-trivial at 4 and 8
aliases (up to r = 0.37 for semantic entropy), suggesting more alias diversity
begins to entangle length with entropy even though alias length is still drawn
independently of city identity. We only ran this ablation at a single seed
given the compute budget, so these are trend-level, not statistically
confirmed, observations — a natural next step is multi-seed replication of
each alias-count condition.

## 5. Discussion

The headline finding survives revision but is now better calibrated: lexical
entropy has a small, real, seed-stable edge over oracle semantic entropy at 2
aliases/city, and that edge — as the alias-count ablation shows — visibly
shrinks as surface diversity increases, consistent with our mechanistic
account (alias-choice entropy as a roughly class-independent additive offset
that erodes once alias diversity is large enough to itself vary with class or
context). This confirms the review's concern that the original null result was
partly a designed-in property of the 2-alias default, while also showing the
effect is not purely an artifact — it degrades gradually rather than vanishing
at 4 aliases. The length-confound story is now honestly noisier: we no longer
claim "no confound," only that no systematic confound survives averaging
across seeds at our sample size, while acknowledging within-seed variance large
enough that a real small effect could not be ruled out. The OOD result holds up
under AUROC scrutiny (0.90 semantic) but is less pristine than the raw mean
deltas implied, and unmapped rate specifically is a weak standalone signal.

**Limitations.** 5 seeds for the main comparison and OOD analysis; only 1 seed
per alias-count condition in the ablation (compute-budget trade-off — full
multi-seed × multi-alias would be the natural extension); a 0.4M-parameter
model trained for 1200 steps is not representative of frontier LLM scale; the
alias mechanism remains a simplified stand-in for real, context-dependent
paraphrase diversity; K=40 samples bounds entropy-estimate precision. These
results should still be read as a controlled probe of a mechanism, not a claim
about production LLM behavior.

## 6. Conclusion

Across 5 seeds, lexical sampling entropy is a slightly but consistently better
ambiguity detector than oracle semantic entropy in our synthetic 2-alias world
(AUROC 1.000 ± 0.000 vs. 0.961 ± 0.018), and this edge shrinks as we increase
alias diversity per city (2→4→8 aliases: 1.000→0.997→0.986), confirming the
edge is partly an artifact of low surface diversity rather than a
mechanism-free fact about lexical vs. semantic entropy. The length-entropy
confound is not clearly absent, as we originally claimed, but noisy and
seed-dependent, averaging near zero with individual seeds reaching |r|>0.4.
Out-of-distribution detection is quantitatively solid (semantic-entropy AUROC
0.899 ± 0.027) though not at ceiling, and unmapped-token rate alone is a
markedly weaker signal than entropy. We see this revised study as a more
honestly calibrated data point — with variance estimates and a direct
mechanism test where the original had neither — supporting further controlled
probing of *when* semantic clustering helps, rather than treating either
"semantic > lexical" or its negation as a mechanism-free given. Code
(`experiment.py`, `experiment_v2.py`), logs, and raw results (`results.json`,
`results_v2.json`) are provided for verification and extension.
