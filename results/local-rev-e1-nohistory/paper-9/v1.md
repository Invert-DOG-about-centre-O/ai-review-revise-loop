# Does Semantic Clustering Actually Beat Lexical Entropy? A Controlled Toy Study of Uncertainty Signals in a Small Transformer

## Abstract

Semantic entropy — clustering sampled generations by meaning before computing
entropy — is widely presented as an improvement over naive lexical (surface-form)
entropy for detecting hallucinations in large language models, on the intuition
that surface-form diversity inflates apparent uncertainty for questions that are
actually semantically determinate. We test this intuition in a fully controlled
synthetic environment where the ground-truth ambiguity of every question is known
by construction, removing the confounds (unknown ground truth, imperfect
paraphrase clustering, uncontrolled answer length) that make this comparison hard
to isolate on real LLMs and real datasets. We train a small (≈0.4M-parameter)
causal Transformer from scratch on a synthetic "capital city" QA task with three
question classes — deterministic (one true answer, but two surface aliases),
genuinely ambiguous (2–3 true answers), and out-of-distribution (no true answer,
unseen at training time) — and compare lexical vs. oracle-semantic sampling
entropy as predictors of ground-truth ambiguity. Contrary to the standard
narrative, we find lexical entropy is an equally good or marginally *better*
predictor of ambiguity than semantic entropy in this controlled setting
(AUROC 1.00 vs. 0.96 at T=1.0, and lexical ≥ semantic across three temperatures),
because alias-choice entropy acts as a near-constant additive offset rather than
a class-correlated confound. We also directly test — and, in this design, do not
find — a length-entropy confound that prior work has flagged as a spurious driver
of apparent UQ performance on real LLMs. We report both results as an honest,
small-scale data point: the case for semantic over lexical entropy may rest more
on real-world paraphrase diversity (which our alias model only partially
recreates) than on ambiguity-detection power per se. All code, logs, and raw
results are included for inspection and re-run.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect or unsupported
statements ("hallucinations"), and a growing line of work uses **sampling-based
uncertainty quantification (UQ)** — generating multiple completions and
measuring their diversity — as a training-free detector. The best-known method,
**semantic entropy** (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*),
clusters sampled answers into equivalence classes using an NLI model before
computing entropy, on the argument that naive token- or string-level entropy
conflates *how an answer is phrased* with *whether the model knows the answer*.
Follow-up work has proposed cheaper approximations — **Semantic Entropy Probes**
that read semantic uncertainty off hidden states in a single forward pass
(Kossen et al., 2024), **Kernel Language Entropy** for finer-grained similarity
structure (NeurIPS 2024), **Semantic Energy** operating on penultimate-layer
logits instead of post-softmax probabilities (2025), and **Semantic
Reformulation Entropy**, which perturbs the *input* with paraphrases rather than
just clustering outputs (2025).

At the same time, a recent evaluation paper — *"Revisiting Uncertainty
Quantification Evaluation in Language Models: Spurious Interactions with
Response Length"* (2025) — raises a methodological concern: reported UQ
benchmark gains may partly reflect a confound between answer length and both the
uncertainty metric and correctness, rather than genuine uncertainty
discrimination. A 2025 KDD survey and a 2024 taxonomy survey (*A Survey on
Uncertainty Quantification of Large Language Models*, ACM Computing Surveys)
both note that evaluating semantic vs. lexical clustering cleanly is hard in
practice: real QA datasets have unknown or debatable ground-truth ambiguity,
clustering quality depends on an auxiliary NLI/judge model that itself has
errors, and answer length is entangled with topic and difficulty in ways that
are hard to strip out post hoc.

**Gap.** We are not aware of small, fully controlled studies that isolate the
lexical-vs-semantic comparison from these confounds by constructing a synthetic
world where (a) true ambiguity is known exactly, (b) semantic equivalence
classes are known exactly (an "oracle" clustering, sidestepping NLI-model
error), and (c) answer length can be examined for confounding independently of
ambiguity class. This lets us ask a narrow, answerable question: *holding
clustering quality and ground truth fixed and known, does semantic
clustering entropy in fact separate ambiguous from deterministic questions
better than raw lexical entropy, and is the length-entropy confound real
outside of the studies that reported it?*

**Contribution.** We build such a synthetic environment, train a small
Transformer on it from scratch (CPU, no pretrained weights, no internet
access required), and report what we actually observe: (1) an unexpected null
result — lexical entropy is *not* inflated relative to oracle-semantic entropy
in our setting, and if anything is a slightly cleaner ambiguity signal; (2) a
second null result — no length-entropy confound emerges under our controlled
sampling procedure; and (3) a positive result — entropy and "unmapped" (novel,
off-manifold) token rate both rise sharply on out-of-distribution questions,
consistent with entropy-based UQ correctly flagging epistemic uncertainty when
the model has no learned answer at all.

## 2. Related Work

- **Semantic entropy family.** Kuhn, Gal & Farquhar (ICLR 2023) introduced
  clustering sampled generations via bidirectional NLI entailment before
  computing entropy; Farquhar et al. (*Nature*, 2024) scaled this to detect
  confabulations across QA benchmarks. Kossen et al. (2024, *Semantic Entropy
  Probes*) show semantic uncertainty is largely linearly decodable from hidden
  states, avoiding expensive multi-sample clustering at inference time. Kernel
  Language Entropy (NeurIPS 2024) generalizes hard clustering to a continuous
  semantic-similarity kernel. Semantic Energy (2025) argues clustering
  post-softmax probabilities discards information already present in raw
  logits and proposes an energy-based alternative. Semantic Reformulation
  Entropy (2025) targets brittleness in output-only clustering by perturbing
  the question instead.
- **Confound / evaluation critiques.** The length-bias paper (arXiv:2504.13677,
  2025) shows that several UQ benchmarks' apparent gains correlate with answer
  length rather than genuine calibration, echoing a broader theme in the survey
  literature (KDD 2025 tutorial/survey; ACM CSUR taxonomy survey, 2025) that UQ
  evaluation methodology itself is an open problem, not just method design.
- **This paper's relation to that work.** Rather than proposing a new UQ
  method, we contribute a controlled instrument for testing claims from that
  literature (semantic > lexical; length confounds exist) at small scale, where
  ground truth is exactly known — a complement to, not a replacement for,
  large-scale benchmark evaluation.

## 3. Method

**Synthetic world.** We define 65 "countries" (tokens), split into 25
deterministic (exactly one true "city" answer), 25 ambiguous (2–3 valid city
answers, modeling genuine aleatoric uncertainty), and 15 out-of-distribution
(OOD) countries that never appear during training. There are 15 distinct
"cities" (semantic answer classes); each city has exactly two surface
**aliases** — different token sequences (length 1–3 subtokens, drawn
independently at random) referring to the same semantic entity, analogous to
paraphrase variation in real answers ("NYC" vs. "New York City"). Training
examples are sequences `BOS Q <country> SEP <alias-tokens> EOS`, where for each
example a valid city is sampled uniformly from the country's true-answer set
and then one of its two aliases is sampled uniformly. This gives us an *oracle*
semantic map (alias → city) that is exact, unlike real NLI-based clustering.

**Model.** A causal Transformer encoder used as a decoder (2 layers, d_model=64,
4 heads, dim_feedforward=128, ≈0.4M parameters), trained with teacher forcing
and cross-entropy loss for 1200 steps (batch 64, Adam, lr=3e-3) — no pretrained
weights, no internet access, ~20 seconds on CPU.

**Sampling & metrics.** For each question we draw K=40 samples via temperature
sampling and compute, per question: (1) **lexical entropy** — Shannon entropy
of the empirical distribution over raw output token sequences; (2) **semantic
entropy** — entropy over the oracle-mapped city id (or "UNK" if the sampled
sequence matches no known alias, tallied separately as an *unmapped/novelty
rate*); (3) mean output length; (4) correctness rate (fraction of samples whose
mapped city is in the country's true-answer set; undefined for OOD).

**Evaluation questions.** (a) *Ambiguity discrimination*: AUROC of each entropy
metric for separating ambiguous from deterministic countries (ground truth
known exactly). (b) *Length confound*: within deterministic countries only —
where true ambiguity is constant — Pearson correlation between mean answer
length and each entropy metric; any nonzero correlation here is by
construction a confound, not signal. (c) *Temperature ablation*: repeat (a) at
sampling temperatures 0.6, 1.0, 1.5. (d) *OOD behavior*: compare entropy and
unmapped rate for OOD vs. in-distribution groups.

## 4. Results

Training converged in ~20s (loss 5.01 → 0.85–0.89, plateauing — expected, since
the optimal achievable loss is bounded above 0 by the genuine label noise from
sampling among 2 aliases × up to 3 valid cities). Full numeric results are in
`results.json`; training log in `train_log.txt`; code in `experiment.py` (all
in this directory, seed fixed, exactly reproducible).

**(a) Ambiguity discrimination (T=1.0, K=40).**

| Metric | AUROC (amb vs. det) |
|---|---|
| Lexical entropy | **1.000** |
| Oracle semantic entropy | 0.965 |

Both metrics separate the two classes almost perfectly, but lexical entropy is
at ceiling while semantic entropy is not. Mean entropies: deterministic
questions have mean lexical entropy 0.70 nats (≈ ln 2, exactly what uniform
sampling over 2 aliases predicts) and mean semantic entropy 0.18 nats
(non-zero only because the model occasionally samples the wrong city — mean
correctness 89.1%); ambiguous questions have mean lexical entropy 1.54 nats and
semantic entropy 0.98 nats. The lexical-minus-semantic gap is ≈0.52–0.57 nats
in both groups — consistent with alias-choice contributing a roughly
class-independent additive offset (≈ ln 2) rather than a class-correlated
confound, which is why it does not hurt (and here slightly helps) ambiguity
separability.

**(b) Length confound (deterministic questions only).**

| Metric | Pearson r (mean length vs. entropy) |
|---|---|
| Lexical entropy | −0.009 |
| Semantic entropy | −0.058 |

Neither metric shows a meaningful length correlation in this design. We do not
read this as evidence the real-LLM length confound reported by Revisiting UQ
Evaluation (2025) is spurious in general — our alias lengths are drawn
independently of city identity and answers are single-shot per-position
samples rather than compounding, multi-step generations, so the specific
mechanism that produces length confounds in real autoregressive decoding
(errors compounding over more generation steps) is not represented here. It is
a negative result about *this* mechanism, not a general claim.

**(c) Temperature ablation.**

| Temperature | AUROC lexical | AUROC semantic |
|---|---|---|
| 0.6 | 0.966 | 0.948 |
| 1.0 | 1.000 | 0.967 |
| 1.5 | 0.992 | 0.968 |

Lexical entropy's edge over semantic entropy is consistent across all three
temperatures tested, not an artifact of one operating point.

**(d) Out-of-distribution behavior.** OOD countries (never seen in training)
show clearly elevated uncertainty signal relative to in-distribution groups:
mean semantic entropy 1.35 nats (vs. 0.18 det / 0.98 amb) and mean unmapped
("novel token sequence") rate 2.7% vs. 0.2% (det) and 0.7% (amb) — a >10×
increase. Correctness is undefined for OOD (no true answer exists), but the
entropy/novelty rise is exactly the desired behavior: the model does not
collapse to a single confidently-wrong answer for unseen entities; sampling
variance and off-manifold generations both increase, giving a usable epistemic
uncertainty signal.

## 5. Discussion

The headline finding — lexical entropy matching or beating oracle semantic
entropy — should not be read as "semantic entropy is unnecessary." Our alias
model captures only one narrow source of surface variation (a fixed 2-way
choice per entity, independent of context), whereas real paraphrase variation
is combinatorially larger, context-dependent, and — critically — the case for
semantic clustering in the literature rests heavily on it collapsing *many*
loosely related phrasings, not 2 fixed synonyms. Our setup may therefore
under-represent the very lexical explosion semantic entropy is designed to
tame. What the result does show is that the *mechanism* by which lexical
entropy is supposed to fail (surface diversity systematically inflating
uncertainty for easy questions) does not automatically occur just because
surface aliases exist — it requires the alias/paraphrase distribution to
itself correlate with class in a way that isn't present here by construction.
This is a useful sanity check on the standard narrative, obtained here only
because ground truth and clustering are exact — a comparison that is much
harder to run cleanly on real LLMs and real benchmarks.

**Limitations.** Single random seed for the main run (though the pipeline is
fully deterministic and reproducible); a 0.4M-parameter model trained for 1200
steps is not representative of frontier LLM scale or training dynamics; the
alias mechanism is a simplified stand-in for real paraphrase diversity; K=40
samples per question bounds entropy-estimate precision at the low end. These
are appropriate trade-offs for a CPU-only, minutes-scale study, but mean
results should be read as a controlled probe of a mechanism, not a claim about
production LLM behavior.

## 6. Conclusion

In a fully controlled synthetic QA world with known ground-truth ambiguity and
an oracle semantic map, lexical (surface-form) sampling entropy matched or
slightly outperformed oracle semantic-clustering entropy at detecting genuine
ambiguity, and no length-entropy confound emerged under our sampling
procedure — both contrary to the intuitive priors motivating recent semantic-UQ
work. Entropy and novel-token rate did, however, correctly spike on
out-of-distribution questions, supporting entropy-based signals for flagging
epistemic uncertainty. We see this as a small, honest data point supporting
more controlled probing of *why* semantic clustering helps (or doesn't) in
specific regimes, rather than treating "semantic > lexical" as a
mechanism-free given. Code, logs, and raw results are provided for
verification and extension (e.g., richer/contextual paraphrase models, or
compounding multi-step length confounds).
