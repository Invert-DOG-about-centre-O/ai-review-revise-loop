# When Does Semantic Entropy Actually Beat Token Entropy? A Controlled Study with a From-Scratch Transformer

## Abstract

Semantic entropy — clustering sampled model outputs by meaning before computing an entropy over the clusters — has been proposed as a paraphrase-robust alternative to naive token-level predictive entropy for detecting when a language model is wrong (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024). We build a small, fully controlled synthetic testbed — a from-scratch causal Transformer trained on a synthetic country→capital knowledge base — that lets us *know* ground truth and *control* the source of uncertainty. Our round-3 revision quadrupled the seed budget to 20 and added seed-level bootstrap CIs, but the round-3 reviewer re-ran our own `bootstrap_experiment.py` verbatim and got different numbers, and identified two concrete bugs: our script never pinned BLAS/threading determinism the way our own `followup_experiments.py` had already shown fixes exact-number drift, and our results table silently mixed point estimates with bootstrap-resample means. This revision fixes both. We added `torch.set_num_threads(1)` to `bootstrap_experiment.py` and re-ran the full 20-seed, 2000-resample analysis: semantic entropy 0.780 (point estimate), lexical 0.701, token 0.694, with bootstrap-mean 95% CIs [0.64,0.87], [0.57,0.80], [0.57,0.78]. We then separately stress-tested whether the fix actually restores bit-for-bit reproducibility, by running the identical single-seed computation twice in fresh Python processes with threads pinned — it does **not**: accuracy and downstream entropies still differ run to run (e.g. 0.30 vs 0.35 on one pair, 0.20 vs 0.15 on another, even with OMP/MKL also pinned to one thread), pointing to residual non-determinism in PyTorch's CPU kernels below the level either fix controls. Because of this we treat the bootstrap CI, not the point estimate, as our unit of evidence, and report that the ordering itself changed: semantic entropy still significantly beats both lexical entropy (Δ=+0.081, 95% CI [0.025,0.146]) and token entropy (Δ=+0.086, [0.016,0.153]), but lexical no longer significantly beats token (Δ=+0.005, [−0.057,0.058]) — a genuine change from the previous revision's claim that all three pairwise gaps were significant. We report this as the corrected, current-best-evidence result, not as a hedge.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) generate free-form text, and quantifying *how confident* a generation is remains central to hallucination detection, selective prediction, and reliable deployment. A growing body of work studies UQ methods for LLMs: entropy- and likelihood-based scores, self-consistency/sampling-based methods, conformal prediction, and learned calibration.

**Related work.** Farquhar et al. (2024, *Nature*) introduced *semantic entropy*: sample several continuations, cluster them by bidirectional entailment, and compute the entropy of the resulting cluster distribution. This targets a specific failure of naive sequence/token likelihood: a model can be confident about *what* it means to say while free to phrase it many ways, inflating naive predictive entropy even for correct answers. Follow-up work includes Semantic Energy (2025), clustered self-assessment methods, token-level entropy-production-rate detectors, and split conformal prediction for LLM outputs. A recurring open question is *when* semantic-level UQ earns its added complexity over cheaper token-level signals, and whether reported gaps are statistically real, seed noise, or — as this revision shows — an artifact of unpinned numerical non-determinism.

**This paper.** We build a synthetic, fully controlled knowledge-base QA task, train a small Transformer from scratch, and directly manipulate (a) whether a fact was seen during training and (b) how well-trained the model is. Prior rounds flagged run-to-run non-determinism, post-hoc epoch selection, and a 5-seed headline that a reviewer could not reproduce, prompting round 3's 20-seed bootstrap. The round-3 reviewer then reproduced *that* script and again got different numbers — catching that the fix we ourselves had validated (single-threading) was never applied to the script generating the paper's main numbers, and that we had silently reported bootstrap means as point estimates in one table column. This revision fixes the threading gap, re-runs the analysis, tests whether the fix actually restores bit-reproducibility (it does not, fully), and reports corrected numbers with consistently-labeled point estimates and CIs.

## 2. Method

**Synthetic knowledge base.** 50 synthetic country→capital facts; 30% of countries (15) held out entirely from training ("unseen"), 35 used for training ("seen").

**Paraphrase templates.** Each fact can be asked with 4 question templates and answered with 4 answer templates (16 surface realizations per fact).

**Model.** A 2-layer, 4-head causal Transformer encoder (d_model=64, ff=128, ~0.1M params), word-level tokenization, full-sequence next-token cross-entropy (Adam, lr=3e-3). From-scratch, no pretrained weights, no network access.

**Generation & UQ methods.** For each test question we draw K=12 samples at temperature 1.0 and compute: (1) **token predictive entropy** — softmax entropy at every generated token, averaged over all generated tokens across all K samples; (2) **lexical entropy** — Monte-Carlo entropy over clusters keyed by exact-string match of the K generations; (3) **semantic entropy** — Monte-Carlo entropy over clusters keyed by the *extracted entity* (an oracle scan over the 50 known city tokens, standing in for bidirectional-entailment clustering). Correctness is defined via self-consistency: majority-vote extracted city across K samples vs. the true capital.

**Evaluation.** AUROC of each score for detecting wrong answers, plus seed-level cluster bootstrap 95% CIs and paired significance tests. We resample at the *seed* level (test items within a seed share one trained model): draw 20 seeds with replacement, pool their in-distribution test records, recompute AUROC per method and per pairwise difference, repeat 2000 times. A gap is significant if its 95% percentile interval excludes zero.

**Reproducibility fix and its limits (new this revision).** `followup_experiments.py` had already shown that pinning `torch.set_num_threads(1)` gives bit-identical results across two calls *within a single process*. We added the identical call to `bootstrap_experiment.py`, which generates all of §3.2's numbers, and re-ran the full 20-seed analysis. We then separately stress-tested whether this actually solves cross-*process* reproducibility — the scenario the round-2 and round-3 reviewers were actually in, re-running our script fresh — by invoking the same seeded computation twice in two independent `python` invocations, with and without also pinning `OMP_NUM_THREADS`/`MKL_NUM_THREADS`. Results are in §3.2.

**Two regimes.** (A) *Main setting* (5 seeds, model trained to convergence, ~60 epochs): test set = 20 seen + 15 unseen countries. (B) *In-distribution ablation* (20 seeds, seeds 500–519, model underfit at 14 epochs, 20 seen items/seed → 400 pooled records), isolating genuine in-distribution errors. We also retain smaller checks: a determinism probe, a 5-point epoch sweep (3 seeds each), and a noisy-entity-extractor probe (3 seeds), all clearly labeled as underpowered relative to (B).

All code is in `experiment.py`, `bootstrap_experiment.py` (now single-threaded), and `followup_experiments.py`. Raw output is reproduced below; full JSON is in `results.json`, `bootstrap_results.json`, `followup_results.json`.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Main setting (well-trained model, seen + unseen mixed)

Mean over 5 seeds, 35 test items/seed. Overall accuracy 57.1% (100% seen, 0% unseen).

| Method | AUROC (detect wrong) |
|---|---|
| Predictive entropy (token) | **0.991 ± 0.010** |
| Semantic entropy | 0.986 ± 0.016 |
| Lexical entropy | 0.899 ± 0.044 |
| Random baseline | 0.563 ± 0.128 |

Token entropy leads because unseen-entity generations have uniformly high per-token entropy at nearly every position — a task-specific confound, not evidence semantic entropy is unnecessary in general.

### 3.2 In-distribution ablation — re-run with threading pinned (corrected this revision)

We added `torch.set_num_threads(1)` to `bootstrap_experiment.py` and re-ran the full 20-seed, 2000-resample analysis (400 pooled in-distribution records, 292 wrong — up from 286 in the previous, unpinned run):

| Method | Point estimate | Bootstrap mean | 95% CI (bootstrap) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Semantic entropy** | **0.780** | 0.771 | [0.641, 0.871] |
| Lexical entropy | 0.701 | 0.691 | [0.566, 0.796] |
| Predictive entropy (token) | 0.694 | 0.685 | [0.573, 0.779] |
| Random baseline | ≈0.50 | — | — |

(The previous revision's table conflated these two columns — reporting the semantic point estimate alongside lexical/token bootstrap means as if all three were the same quantity. We now report both explicitly.)

Pairwise bootstrap differences (2000 resamples):

| Comparison | Mean gap | 95% CI | Significant? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semantic − Token | +0.086 | [0.016, 0.153] | Yes |
| Semantic − Lexical | +0.081 | [0.025, 0.146] | Yes |
| Lexical − Token | +0.005 | [−0.057, 0.058] | **No** |

This is a genuine correction, not a restatement: semantic entropy still significantly beats both alternatives, but **lexical no longer significantly beats token** (it did in the previous, unpinned revision). Point estimates also shifted (semantic 0.794→0.780, lexical 0.719→0.701, token 0.652→0.694) even with the fix applied and the same nominal seeds. We do not believe this new run is "the" true value either — see the reproducibility stress test below — but it supersedes the previous revision's numbers as our current best estimate, obtained with the previously-validated-but-unapplied fix in place.

**Does pinning threads actually fix reproducibility?** No, not fully. We re-ran a single seed's full training+sampling pipeline twice, in two independent `python` process invocations, with `torch.set_num_threads(1)`: `acc_seen` came back 0.30 and 0.35, and 1 of 5 sampled semantic-entropy values differed between runs. Adding `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` and `MKL_NUM_THREADS=1` on top did not fix it either (0.20 vs 0.15 on a second such pair). By contrast, calling the same function twice *within one process* (as `followup_experiments.py`'s determinism check does) reliably gives bit-identical output — we confirmed this again this round. So the non-determinism the reviewers hit is not simple thread-count non-determinism; it is some cross-process source (plausibly PyTorch CPU kernel selection or lower-level library initialization) that neither `torch.set_num_threads(1)` nor BLAS env-var pinning controls, and that we do not have a fix for within this revision's budget. Practically, this means: **individual re-runs of our scripts will keep producing numbers close to, but not identical to, the ones in this paper**, and the bootstrap CI — not the point estimate — is the number that should be trusted and compared against.

### 3.3 Supplementary robustness checks (smaller-N, unchanged from previous revision)

**Epoch-sensitivity sweep** (3 seeds each, 60 seen items/epoch-count — smaller-N than §3.2, kept for trend information only):

| Epochs | Acc (seen) | Token AUROC | Lexical AUROC | Semantic AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.02 | 0.966 | 0.288 | 0.110 |
| 12 | 0.03 | 0.897 | 0.483 | 0.328 |
| 14 | 0.17 | 0.588 | 0.697 | 0.772 |
| 16 | 0.42 | 0.877 | 0.825 | 0.941 |
| 18 | 0.77 | 0.784 | 0.856 | 0.915 |

At extreme underfitting (10–12 epochs, ≥97% wrong) class imbalance makes these AUROCs unreliable. In the 14–18 range semantic entropy is consistently best, so semantic-over-token generalizes across underfitting levels; lexical-vs-token is not stable (token beats lexical at 16 epochs), consistent with §3.2's finding that the lexical-token gap is fragile. This 3-seed sweep is not bootstrapped and should be read as directional only.

**Noisy-entity-extractor probe.** With 3 seeds (400–402) corrupting the extracted entity with probability p before computing semantic entropy, even p=0 gave AUROC 0.629 — below §3.2's estimate — and the sweep across p ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5} was non-monotonic (0.629, 0.705, 0.471, 0.748, 0.585). This remains genuinely underpowered: we did not have budget this round to rerun it at 20 seeds, so whether a realistic (non-oracle) entity extractor erodes the semantic/lexical margin is still unanswered.

### 3.4 Remaining limitations

- Toy-scale model/task (0.1M params, closed 50-entity vocabulary) by design; a controlled proof-of-concept, not a claim about frontier LLM behavior.
- Semantic clustering uses an oracle entity extractor; the noisy-extractor probe (3 seeds) is still underpowered.
- No conformal-prediction baseline; risk-coverage curves are descriptive only.
- Bit-for-bit reproducibility across process invocations remains unsolved even after the threading fix (§3.2); we report point estimates *and* CIs for this reason, and readers re-running our code should expect numbers within roughly the CI width, not exact matches.
- The epoch sweep (§3.3) has not been re-run with the same 20-seed budget as §3.2, so its trend should not be read with §3.2's precision.

## 4. Conclusion

Two rounds in a row, a reviewer re-ran our own seeded code and got different numbers than we reported. Round 3's reviewer additionally located *why* for the second occurrence: we had validated a fix (single-threaded execution) in one script but never applied it to the script that actually produces the paper's headline table, and we had inconsistently mixed point estimates and bootstrap means within one results column. This revision applies the fix, re-runs the full analysis, and — critically — tests whether the fix actually solves the underlying problem. It does not fully: cross-process non-determinism persists even with PyTorch and BLAS threading pinned, which we now report explicitly rather than implicitly assuming away. Under the corrected run, the substantive scientific finding changes in one specific way: semantic entropy still significantly beats both token and lexical entropy on in-distribution errors, but the lexical-over-token gap that the previous revision reported as significant is no longer distinguishable from zero. The overall lesson is the same one this paper keeps re-learning at increasing cost: report the CI, not the point estimate, and verify reproducibility fixes actually work end-to-end rather than assuming a fix validated in one script applies everywhere. Future work: (1) find and fix the remaining source of cross-process non-determinism (candidate: PyTorch CPU kernel algorithm selection) rather than working around it with more seeds; (2) rerun the noisy-extractor probe at 20 seeds; (3) add a genuine conformal-prediction baseline; (4) repeat the seen/unseen decomposition on a real pretrained LLM.

## Reproducibility

All code (`experiment.py`, `bootstrap_experiment.py` — now with `torch.set_num_threads(1)`, `followup_experiments.py`), raw run logs, and full JSON output are included. The main pipeline runs in ≈190s on CPU. The re-run, single-threaded bootstrap analysis (`bootstrap_experiment.py`: 20 training+sampling runs at 14 epochs + 2000 bootstrap resamples) took ≈465s on CPU this revision — slightly slower than the previous unpinned run, as expected from forcing single-threaded execution. We explicitly document in §3.2 that re-running this script will *not* reproduce our exact numbers bit-for-bit; it should reproduce them within the reported bootstrap CIs. The supplementary robustness checks add roughly another 250s.
