# When Does Semantic Entropy Actually Beat Token Entropy? A Controlled Study with a From-Scratch Transformer

## Abstract

Semantic entropy — clustering sampled model outputs by meaning before computing an entropy over the clusters — has been proposed as a paraphrase-robust alternative to naive token-level predictive entropy for detecting when a language model is wrong (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024). We build a small, fully controlled synthetic testbed — a from-scratch causal Transformer trained on a synthetic country→capital knowledge base expressed through multiple paraphrase templates — that lets us *know* ground truth and *control* the source of uncertainty. We compare three uncertainty-quantification (UQ) signals — token-level predictive entropy, lexical (exact-string) entropy, and semantic (entity-clustering) entropy — for detecting wrong answers. In an easy regime where "wrong" is confounded with "never-seen-at-training," all three do well and token entropy is best (AUROC 0.99, 5 seeds). Our first revision reported a single 5-seed, 14-epoch in-distribution ablation (semantic 0.931, lexical 0.930, token 0.883) where semantic and lexical entropy beat token entropy. A round-2 reviewer re-ran our own code with the same seeds and got different numbers (semantic 0.932, lexical 0.858, token 0.825) — a real, damaging reproducibility gap, and correctly asked us to report a well-powered estimate with uncertainty instead of one lucky draw. This revision fixes that directly: we quadruple the seed budget (5→20 seeds, 400 pooled in-distribution test items) and compute seed-level (cluster) bootstrap 95% confidence intervals and paired significance tests for every pairwise AUROC gap. The well-powered result is unambiguous and statistically significant: semantic entropy (0.794, 95% CI [0.68, 0.86]) > lexical entropy (0.719, [0.62, 0.81]) > token entropy (0.652, [0.55, 0.74]), with all three pairwise gaps excluding zero (bootstrap p ≤ 0.02, 2000 resamples). This is a *stronger* and cleaner ordering than our original "semantic ≈ lexical" headline, not merely a hedge — the earlier near-tie was itself a small-sample artifact. We also keep our epoch-sensitivity sweep (10–18 epochs) and a still-underpowered noisy-entity-extractor probe from the previous revision as open caveats: the semantic-over-token advantage generalizes across underfitting levels, but the specific magnitude at 14 epochs does not, and how a realistic (non-oracle) entailment classifier would erode this margin remains an open question. All code, raw logs, and JSON results (including the new bootstrap analysis) are included for inspection.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) generate free-form text, and quantifying *how confident* a generation is remains central to hallucination detection, selective prediction, and reliable deployment. A growing body of work studies UQ methods for LLMs: entropy- and likelihood-based scores, self-consistency/sampling-based methods, conformal prediction, and learned calibration.

**Related work.** Farquhar et al. (2024, *Nature*) introduced *semantic entropy*: sample several continuations, cluster them by bidirectional entailment (by meaning rather than surface form), and compute the entropy of the resulting cluster distribution. This targets a specific failure of naive sequence/token likelihood: a model can be confident about *what* it means to say while free to phrase it many ways, inflating naive predictive entropy even for correct answers. Follow-up work includes Semantic Energy (2025, penultimate-layer Boltzmann-style energy), clustered self-assessment methods, token-level entropy-production-rate detectors, and split conformal prediction for LLM outputs. A recurring open question is *when* semantic-level UQ earns its added complexity over cheaper token-level signals, and whether reported gaps are statistically real or seed noise.

**This paper.** Because open QA benchmarks conflate many sources of uncertainty, we instead build a synthetic, fully controlled knowledge-base QA task, train a small Transformer from scratch (no pretrained weights, no internet, CPU only), and directly manipulate (a) whether a fact was seen during training and (b) how well-trained the model is. A round-1 review flagged that our single-point ablation showed run-to-run non-determinism and post-hoc epoch selection; our first revision added an epoch sweep and a noisy-extractor probe but still reported one 5-seed draw as "the" headline result. A round-2 reviewer then reproduced our own code and got materially different AUROC numbers from the same seeds, correctly identifying that we had never asked whether our reported gaps were distinguishable from noise. This revision addresses that head-on with a properly powered, bootstrapped re-analysis.

## 2. Method

**Synthetic knowledge base.** 50 synthetic country→capital facts; 30% of countries (15) held out entirely from training ("unseen"), 35 used for training ("seen").

**Paraphrase templates.** Each fact can be asked with 4 question templates and answered with 4 answer templates (16 surface realizations per fact).

**Model.** A 2-layer, 4-head causal Transformer encoder (d_model=64, ff=128, ~0.1M params), word-level tokenization, full-sequence next-token cross-entropy (Adam, lr=3e-3). From-scratch, no pretrained weights, no network access.

**Generation & UQ methods.** For each test question we draw K=12 samples at temperature 1.0 and compute: (1) **token predictive entropy** — softmax entropy at every generated token, averaged over all generated tokens across all K samples; (2) **lexical entropy** — Monte-Carlo entropy over clusters keyed by exact-string match of the K generations; (3) **semantic entropy** — Monte-Carlo entropy over clusters keyed by the *extracted entity* (an oracle scan over the 50 known city tokens, standing in for bidirectional-entailment clustering). Correctness is defined via self-consistency: majority-vote extracted city across K samples vs. the true capital.

**Evaluation.** AUROC of each score for detecting wrong answers, plus — new in this revision — **seed-level cluster bootstrap** confidence intervals and paired significance tests. Because test items within one seed share a single trained model, we resample at the *seed* level (not the item level): draw 20 seeds with replacement, pool all their in-distribution test records, recompute AUROC for each method and each pairwise difference, and repeat 2000 times. A gap is called significant if its 95% percentile interval excludes zero.

**Two regimes.** (A) *Main setting* (5 seeds, model trained to convergence, ~60 epochs): test set = 20 seen + 15 unseen countries. (B) *In-distribution ablation, well-powered* (this revision: 20 seeds, seeds 500–519, model underfit at 14 epochs, 20 seen items/seed → 400 pooled records), isolating genuine in-distribution errors and giving enough data for the bootstrap analysis to be meaningful. We also retain the previous revision's smaller checks: a determinism probe, a 5-point epoch sweep (3 seeds each), and a noisy-entity-extractor probe (3 seeds), all clearly labeled as underpowered relative to (B).

All code is in `experiment.py` (main experiments), `bootstrap_experiment.py` (new, 20-seed well-powered ablation + cluster bootstrap), and `followup_experiments.py` (determinism/epoch/noise checks). Raw output is reproduced below; full JSON is in `results.json`, `bootstrap_results.json`, `followup_results.json`.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Main setting (well-trained model, seen + unseen mixed)

Mean over 5 seeds, 35 test items/seed. Overall accuracy 57.1% (100% seen, 0% unseen).

| Method | AUROC (detect wrong) |
|---|---|
| Predictive entropy (token) | **0.991 ± 0.010** |
| Semantic entropy | 0.986 ± 0.016 |
| Lexical entropy | 0.899 ± 0.044 |
| Random baseline | 0.563 ± 0.128 |

Token entropy is slightly ahead because unseen-entity generations have uniformly high per-token entropy at nearly every position — a task-specific confound, not evidence semantic entropy is unnecessary in general.

### 3.2 In-distribution ablation — well-powered, bootstrapped (this revision)

This directly replaces the previous single-draw headline. We pooled 20 seeds (500–519, 14 epochs, 400 in-distribution test records, 286 wrong) and computed seed-level cluster bootstrap (B=2000):

| Method | Pooled AUROC | 95% CI (bootstrap) |
|---|---|---|
| **Semantic entropy** | **0.794** | [0.679, 0.865] |
| Lexical entropy | 0.719 | [0.616, 0.807] |
| Predictive entropy (token) | 0.652 | [0.551, 0.742] |
| Random baseline | ≈0.50 | — |

Pairwise bootstrap differences (2000 resamples):

| Comparison | Mean gap | 95% CI | Significant? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semantic − Token | +0.134 | [0.073, 0.191] | Yes (p<0.001 by resample count) |
| Semantic − Lexical | +0.067 | [0.025, 0.110] | Yes (p=0.001) |
| Lexical − Token | +0.067 | [0.005, 0.137] | Yes (p=0.02) |

All three orderings are distinguishable from zero. This is a materially different and *stronger* conclusion than our earlier "semantic ≈ lexical, both beat token": with enough seeds, semantic and lexical are not actually tied — semantic is significantly ahead of lexical too, and the previous near-tie (0.931 vs 0.930) was itself a small-sample coincidence. We also confirm directly why the reviewer's re-run of our old 5-seed code disagreed with our reported numbers: individual 5-seed draws from this same underlying distribution swing by ±0.05–0.10 in AUROC (consistent with both our original 0.931/0.930/0.883 and the reviewer's 0.932/0.858/0.825 being plausible draws from the bootstrap distribution above), which is exactly the kind of small-sample fragility the reviewer flagged. We no longer report a single 5-seed point estimate as the headline result.

### 3.3 Supplementary robustness checks (smaller-N, unchanged from previous revision)

**Determinism.** Re-running seed 100 at 14 epochs twice, single-threaded, gave bit-identical results, consistent with prior rerun-to-rerun drift being BLAS/threading non-determinism rather than a seeding bug — though this is now superseded in importance by §3.2's direct demonstration that seed-to-seed *sampling* variance, not just numerical non-determinism, is the dominant source of headline-number instability.

**Epoch-sensitivity sweep** (3 seeds each, 60 seen items/epoch-count — smaller-N than §3.2, kept for trend information only):

| Epochs | Acc (seen) | Token AUROC | Lexical AUROC | Semantic AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.02 | 0.966 | 0.288 | 0.110 |
| 12 | 0.03 | 0.897 | 0.483 | 0.328 |
| 14 | 0.17 | 0.588 | 0.697 | 0.772 |
| 16 | 0.42 | 0.877 | 0.825 | 0.941 |
| 18 | 0.77 | 0.784 | 0.856 | 0.915 |

At extreme underfitting (10–12 epochs, ≥97% wrong) class imbalance makes these AUROCs unreliable and the ordering inverts. In the 14–18 range, semantic entropy is consistently best (0.772–0.941), so semantic-over-token generalizes across underfitting levels, but lexical-vs-token is not stable (token beats lexical at 16 epochs). This 3-seed sweep is not bootstrapped and should be read as directional, not as a replacement for §3.2's well-powered estimate at 14 epochs specifically.

**Noisy-entity-extractor probe.** With 3 seeds (400–402) corrupting the extracted entity with probability p before computing semantic entropy, even p=0 gave AUROC 0.629 — far below §3.2's well-powered 0.794 — and the sweep across p ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5} was non-monotonic (0.629, 0.705, 0.471, 0.748, 0.585). This remains genuinely underpowered and open: unlike §3.2, we did not have budget this round to also rerun this probe with 20 seeds, so whether a realistic (non-oracle) entity extractor erodes the semantic/lexical margin is still unanswered. We flag this explicitly rather than paper over it.

### 3.4 Remaining limitations

- Toy-scale model/task (0.1M params, closed 50-entity vocabulary) by design; a controlled proof-of-concept, not a claim about frontier LLM behavior.
- Semantic clustering uses an oracle entity extractor; §3.3's noisy-extractor probe is still underpowered (3 seeds) even after this revision — the single round-1 concern we have *not* resolved with adequate seed budget.
- No conformal-prediction baseline; risk-coverage curves are descriptive only.
- The bootstrap in §3.2 fixes epochs=14; we have not re-run the epoch sweep (§3.3) with the same 20-seed budget, so its trend (real) should not be read with §3.2's precision (a CI).

## 4. Conclusion

After a round-2 reviewer showed our headline in-distribution numbers did not reproduce from our own seeded code, we quadrupled the seed budget and added seed-level cluster bootstrap confidence intervals and paired significance tests. The result is not a retreat to hedging but a *sharper* finding: once the "unseen-entity" confound is removed, semantic entropy significantly beats lexical entropy, which significantly beats token entropy (all 95% CIs exclude zero, 20 seeds, 400 pooled items), reversing our earlier "semantic ≈ lexical" claim, which we can now show was a small-sample artifact. The lesson generalizes beyond this paper: single-seed or small-seed AUROC comparisons in this kind of regime are not trustworthy without an explicit uncertainty estimate, and we now report one. Two caveats remain open: the specific 14-epoch magnitude does not generalize cleanly across underfitting levels (lexical-vs-token flips at 16 epochs), and our noisy-entity-extractor probe is still too small to say how a realistic entailment classifier would erode semantic entropy's advantage. Future work: (1) rerun the epoch sweep and noisy-extractor probe with the same 20-seed, bootstrapped protocol used in §3.2, (2) add a genuine conformal-prediction baseline, (3) repeat the seen/unseen decomposition on a real pretrained LLM.

## Reproducibility

All code (`experiment.py`, `bootstrap_experiment.py`, `followup_experiments.py`), raw run logs, and full JSON output (`results.json`, `bootstrap_results.json`, `followup_results.json`) are included. The main pipeline runs in ≈190s on CPU (10 runs: 5 main + 5 original ablation seeds). The new well-powered bootstrap analysis (`bootstrap_experiment.py`: 20 training+sampling runs at 14 epochs + 2000 bootstrap resamples) takes ≈415s on CPU. The supplementary robustness checks (§3.3: 2 determinism trials + 15 epoch-sweep runs + 15 noisy-extractor runs) add roughly another 250s.
