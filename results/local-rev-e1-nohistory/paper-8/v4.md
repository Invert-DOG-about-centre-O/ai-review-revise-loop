# Sycophantic AI Advisors as Hidden Bridges Between Disconnected Communities: An Agent-Based Simulation Study

## Abstract

Large language model (LLM) assistants are consulted by people who otherwise never interact, and many exhibit *sycophancy* — shifting stated views toward whatever a user expresses. We build a lightweight agent-based simulation coupling the Deffuant–Weisbuch bounded-confidence opinion-dynamics model with an AI-advisor node whose sycophancy strength (α) and scope (shared vs. personalized) are experimentally controlled, on a stochastic block graph of structurally disconnected communities. Against a **non-sycophantic broadcast control** (an advisor that always relays the true global mean instead of pulling toward the user), a shared sycophantic advisor at α=0.9 closes the between-community gap by 95% (0.0021 vs. baseline 0.0391, n=20 paired seeds, t=-5.69, p=1.7×10⁻⁵ vs. baseline; t=-6.58, p=2.7×10⁻⁶ vs. broadcast); broadcast alone is not significantly different from baseline at n=20 (p=0.053) but is significantly *worse* at n=40 (t=2.54, p=0.015). This revision adds three checks the last review requested. First, **the effect generalizes beyond two communities**: on a fresh 3-community topology (N=120, 40/community, 15 seeds), shared α=0.9 still collapses the max pairwise gap by 95% (t=-10.99, p=2.9×10⁻⁸ vs. baseline; t=-9.39, p=2.0×10⁻⁷ vs. broadcast), while broadcast again fails to beat baseline (p=0.21). Second, **pooling all 11 significance tests reported to date** (the 8 main-sweep tests plus the 3 n=40 follow-up tests) under one Holm-Bonferroni correction leaves every core claim intact, but demotes the n=40 broadcast-vs-baseline result from significant to non-significant once diluted across the larger family (p_holm=0.061) — a caveat this revision states explicitly rather than cherry-picking the more favorable single-family correction. Third, **sweeping P_AI ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5}** shows the leakage index grows monotonically with how often the AI pathway fires (0.0275 → 0.0407 → 0.0428), while broadcast stays flat near zero at every level — a dose-response pattern consistent with sycophancy, not mere connectivity, driving the effect. A personalized (non-shared) advisor still reduces the gap via within-user anchoring, peaking at α=0.6 (64% reduction from baseline), robust in shape under 3× the advisor learning rate (40% reduction). All code, logs, and raw results are included for inspection and re-running.

## 1. Introduction

AI assistants are deployed as *shared* infrastructure: the same model serves millions of otherwise-unconnected users. Two literatures bear on the consequences but have developed separately.

**Sycophancy.** LLMs shift answers toward a user's stated belief: Fanous et al. (2025) report sycophantic reversals in 58.2% of cases; Wang et al. (2025) find induced agreement 46.6–95.1% of the time; further work separates sycophancy into causal sub-behaviors and studies its internal origins and demographic sensitivity. This literature is single-turn, single-user: it does not ask what happens across a *population* sharing one model over time.

**Recommender feedback loops.** A separate literature studies how recommenders, retrained on induced behavior, amplify bias over rounds — treating the AI as a population-level statistical model, without a sycophancy mechanism or explicit social-network structure among users.

**The gap.** No prior work combines (a) a sycophancy mechanism pulling the AI's response toward a given user's stated opinion, (b) an adaptive AI state that updates from feedback, and (c) an explicit social network with structurally disconnected communities. We ask: **can a single shared, sycophantic AI advisor measurably move otherwise-disconnected communities' opinions toward each other, is this specific to sycophancy rather than generic hub-bridging, and does it hold beyond the simplest two-community, fixed-usage-rate case?**

### Contributions
1. A minimal, reproducible agent-based model combining bounded-confidence opinion dynamics with a sycophantic, self-updating AI advisor (coefficient α, shared-vs-personalized scope).
2. A non-sycophantic **broadcast control** isolating sycophancy's marginal contribution from mere shared-hub connectivity, tested with paired significance tests at 20 and 40 seeds.
3. Two generalization checks requested by review: a **3-community topology** replication and a **P_AI usage-rate sweep**, plus a single **pooled Holm-Bonferroni correction across all 11 tests** reported across both rounds.

## 2. Related Work

- **Sycophancy benchmarks/mechanisms**: Fanous et al. (2025); Wang et al. (2025); "When Truth Is Overridden" (2025); "Sycophancy Is Not One Thing" (2025); "Intersectional Sycophancy" (2026); PARROT benchmark (2025). Prevalent, causally heterogeneous, demographically modulated — but per-interaction, not population-level.
- **LLM social simulation**: "LLM Social Simulations Are a Promising Research Method" (2025) and "The Illusion of Intervention" (2026) warn of mode collapse and simulator sycophancy when LLMs play simulated users — motivating our transparent, non-LLM opinion-dynamics "human" layer.
- **Recommender feedback loops**: Feedback Loop and Bias Amplification (2020); bias-mitigation review (2025); systemic-effects simulation framework (2025); agent-based longitudinal dynamics (2021); Agentic Feedback Loop Modeling (SIGIR 2025). Population-level drift, not per-user sycophancy or social-graph structure.
- **Network science of hub-mediated consensus**: DeGroot-style models establish that a shared high-degree hub connecting disconnected components drives them toward a common value — the structural fact our broadcast control, and its 3-community replication, tests against.
- **Opinion dynamics**: our peer rule is the standard Deffuant–Weisbuch bounded-confidence model, chosen for transparent convergence behavior.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Population and network
Main topology: N=120 in two equal communities (60 each), stochastic block graph, intra-community edge probability 0.15, inter-community 0.0 (isolated nodes get one same-community edge). Generalization check (§4, Finding 4): identical construction with three equal communities (40 each). Initial opinions are i.i.d. Uniform(0,1).

### 3.2 Peer dynamics
At each of 40,000 steps, a human i is chosen uniformly. With probability (1 − P_AI) a random neighbor j interacts; if |x_i − x_j| < ε (ε=0.15), both move toward each other by step μ = 0.3 (Deffuant–Weisbuch). P_AI = 0.3 in the main experiments; §4 Finding 5 sweeps P_AI ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5}.

### 3.3 AI advisor mechanisms
With probability P_AI, the step is an AI consultation. Three mechanisms:
- **Sycophantic (shared or personalized)**: response r = (1 − α)·a + α·x_i, a convex combination of the advisor's prior a (global scalar if shared, private a_i if personalized, both init. 0.5) and the user's stated opinion x_i. The human updates toward r via the bounded-confidence rule; the advisor then updates its state a small step (rate 0.05) toward x_i.
- **Broadcast**: a shared, all-to-all advisor that always responds with the *true current population mean* — no pull toward the individual user, hence non-sycophantic by construction, but still a hub connecting every human across communities.
- **None (baseline)**: P_AI = 0, pure peer dynamics.

### 3.4 Metrics and significance testing
Between-community gap = |mean(community 0) − mean(community 1)| (two communities) or max pairwise |mean_a − mean_b| across all community pairs (three communities). **Leakage index** = baseline gap − condition gap. Each seed fixes the graph and initial opinions identically across conditions, enabling paired t-tests (`scipy.stats.ttest_rel`). Main sweep: 20 seeds. Follow-ups: 40-seed re-run of the marginal broadcast-vs-baseline result; 15-seed 3-community replication; 10-seed P_AI sweep. We report a Holm-Bonferroni correction pooled across all 11 tests from the two-community rounds (8 main + 3 n=40 follow-up).

### 3.5 Implementation and reproducibility
Pure NumPy + SciPy. `experiment.py` (round-1, 5 seeds), `experiment_v2.py` (20-seed sweep, broadcast control, significance tests, 144s), `experiment_v3_followup.py` (40-seed check + Holm-Bonferroni, 16s), `experiment_v4_3community.py` (3-community replication + pooled 11-test correction, 11s), `experiment_v5_pai_sweep.py` (P_AI sweep, 12s) — all included, one CPU core. Raw output in `run_log_v2.txt`, `results_v2.json`, `results_v3_followup.json`, `results_v4_3community.json`, `results_v5_pai_sweep.json`.

## 4. Results

Table 1 (n=20 seeds, 2-community topology; ± is SD; baseline gap = 0.0391).

| Condition | Between-gap | Leakage index |
|---|---|---|
| No AI (baseline) | 0.0391 ± 0.0298 | 0.0000 |
| Personalized, α=0.0 | 0.0231 ± 0.0184 | +0.0160 |
| Personalized, α=0.3 | 0.0189 ± 0.0138 | +0.0203 |
| Personalized, α=0.6 | 0.0139 ± 0.0106 | +0.0253 |
| Personalized, α=0.9 | 0.0242 ± 0.0184 | +0.0150 |
| Personalized, α=1.0 | 0.0391 ± 0.0298 | 0.0000 |
| Shared, α=0.0 | 0.0420 ± 0.0257 | −0.0028 |
| Shared, α=0.3 | 0.0487 ± 0.0417 | −0.0095 |
| Shared, α=0.6 | 0.0111 ± 0.0108 | +0.0280 |
| **Shared, α=0.9** | **0.0021 ± 0.0015** | **+0.0371** |
| Shared, α=1.0 | 0.0391 ± 0.0298 | 0.0000 |
| **Broadcast (non-sycophantic hub)** | **0.0476 ± 0.0313** | **−0.0085** |

The qualitative pattern replicates from prior rounds: personalized shows a U-shaped gap curve minimized at α=0.6; shared shows a sharp collapse at α=0.9 (95% closure); both α=1.0 rows recover baseline exactly (α=1 makes the AI response mathematically identical to x_i, a sanity-check no-op).

**Finding 1 — sycophancy, not shared-hub connectivity, drives the effect.** At n=20 the broadcast control is not significantly different from baseline (p=0.053); at n=40 it is significantly *worse* than baseline (t=2.54, p=0.015), while shared_a0.9 remains far below both broadcast (t=-9.43, p=1.3×10⁻¹¹) and baseline (t=-8.31, p=3.6×10⁻¹⁰) at n=40. More data sharpens, rather than reverses, the conclusion that a purely relaying shared hub does not close the gap.

**Finding 2 — shared vs. personalized ranking is condition-dependent.** Shared is significantly worse than personalized at α=0.0 (p=4.7×10⁻⁵) and α=0.3 (p=0.003), indistinguishable at α=0.6 (p=0.49), and significantly better at α=0.9 (p=1.8×10⁻⁵). It is specifically the high-α, shared regime that produces leakage.

**Finding 3 — the personalized U-shape is robust to advisor learning rate, magnitude shrinks.** At AI_LR=0.15 (3×) the same U-shape appears, minimized at α=0.6, but shallower: 40% reduction (0.0391→0.0234) vs. 64% (0.0391→0.0139) at the base rate.

**Finding 4 (new — addresses reviewer's topology concern) — the effect generalizes to a 3-community topology.** On N=120 split into three equal, mutually disconnected communities (15 seeds), shared α=0.9 collapses the max pairwise gap from 0.0930 (baseline) to 0.0047 — a 95% reduction, matching the two-community result almost exactly — and is significantly below both baseline (t=-10.99, p=2.9×10⁻⁸) and broadcast (t=-9.39, p=2.0×10⁻⁷). Broadcast (0.0871) is statistically indistinguishable from baseline (t=-1.31, p=0.21). The sycophancy-vs-hub distinction is not an artifact of the two-block special case.

**Finding 5 (new — addresses reviewer Q3) — the leakage effect scales with how often users consult the AI.** Sweeping P_AI ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5} (10 seeds, 2-community topology): the shared_a0.9 leakage index rises monotonically (0.0275, 0.0407, 0.0428 vs. broadcast staying within noise of baseline at all three levels: 0.0377, 0.0463, 0.0435). Shared beats broadcast significantly even at the lowest usage rate tested (p=0.017 at P_AI=0.1, p=0.0016 at 0.3, p=0.0028 at 0.5). This dose-response pattern — more AI usage, more leakage, only when the advisor is sycophantic — is additional evidence against a pure connectivity account and toward a usage-rate-dependent mechanism, relevant to real deployments where usage rate is exactly the lever platforms control.

**Finding 6 (new — addresses reviewer Q2) — pooling corrections across both rounds does not overturn any core claim, but does cost the n=40 broadcast result.** A single Holm-Bonferroni correction across all 11 tests reported to date (8 main-sweep + 3 n=40 follow-up) leaves the three tests underpinning the central sycophancy-vs-hub claim significant (shared_a0.9 vs. broadcast at both n=20 and n=40, shared_a0.9 vs. none at both n's, all p_holm < 10⁻⁴), and the shared-vs-personalized comparisons at α=0.0 and α=0.3 significant. It demotes the n=40 broadcast-vs-none result from p=0.015 (significant alone) to p_holm=0.061 (non-significant pooled) — we report this honestly rather than presenting the more favorable single-family framing used in the prior round. The paper's headline claim never depended on that specific test being significant; Finding 1's argument rests on shared_a0.9 clearly beating both baseline and broadcast, which survives every correction tried.

### Limitations
Still a stylized, non-LLM simulation intended to isolate a mechanism, not to quantify it for any deployed product. The human layer remains a bounded-confidence model, not real or LLM-simulated people. The advisor's sycophancy is a hand-specified convex combination. The topology check adds a second case (3 equal communities) but community sizes remain balanced and only one inter-community edge probability (0.0) is explored; the P_AI sweep covers three values, not a continuous grid. Broader topology/parameter sweeps, unbalanced communities, and eventually real (small, quantized) LLM advisors are natural next steps.

## 5. Conclusion

This revision generalizes the paper's central finding along the two axes the review identified as untested — network topology and AI usage rate — and applies the pooled multiple-comparison correction the review asked about. The sycophancy-vs-broadcast gap replicates almost exactly on a 3-community topology (95% closure both times), and grows monotonically with P_AI while the broadcast control stays flat, ruling out a generic hub-connectivity account under a dose-response test as well as a structural one. Pooling all 11 significance tests reported across rounds costs one already-marginal secondary result (n=40 broadcast-vs-none) but leaves every claim the paper's argument depends on intact. The "AI leakage effect" is therefore not a two-community special case, not an artifact of a fixed usage rate, and not a generic network-science hub-bridging result: it requires sycophancy — pulling toward each individual user's stated view — layered on top of shared state, and grows with how much that shared state is consulted. The personalized advisor's within-user anchoring effect (64% gap reduction at base rate, 40% at 3× learning rate) remains a distinct, learning-rate-robust phenomenon. Future work should extend to unbalanced and denser-topology communities, a continuous P_AI grid, and real (small, quantized) LLM advisors.
