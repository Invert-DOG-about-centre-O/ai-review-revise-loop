# Does the Width–Calibration "Crossover" in Small Neural Networks Generalize Across Datasets? A Pre-Registered, Multi-Seed, Two-Dataset Study

## Abstract

Small multilayer perceptrons are commonly reported to move from underconfidence to overconfidence as hidden-layer width increases, with a "crossover width" separating the two regimes. Prior work in this series established that such a crossover is noisy and seed-dependent even on a single dataset. Here we ask a sharper, pre-registered question: does the crossover-width distribution transfer from one dataset to another? We train single-hidden-layer MLPs (widths 2–256) on two classification problems — the real 8×8 `digits` dataset and a synthetic 2-D Gaussian-blob task — with 15 independently reseeded runs per (dataset, width) cell (240 runs total, fixed integer seeds). Both datasets show the expected average trend (bias moves toward zero as width grows), but a *stable* crossover — a width beyond which bias never reverts to underconfidence — exists for only 7/15 digits seeds and 4/15 synthetic seeds; synthetic mean bias never reaches zero in the tested range. A pre-registered Mann–Whitney U test on the existing crossover widths finds no significant difference (p = 0.77) but is severely underpowered (minimum detectable rank-biserial effect ≈ 0.74 at n = 7, 4). An exploratory (non-pre-registered) Fisher's exact test on the *probability of a crossover existing at all*, using the full n = 15 per dataset, is also non-significant (odds ratio 2.4, p = 0.45) — neither location nor existence-rate can be distinguished between datasets with the power available; we report both as inconclusive, power-limited nulls, not evidence of a shared mechanism. A failure-mode breakdown of seeds lacking a stable crossover shows that on `digits`, 3/8 touch overconfidence before reverting while 5/8 never do, whereas on `synthetic` all 11/11 simply never touch overconfidence — synthetic non-crossover seeds are not noisy boundary oscillators, they are cleanly underconfident throughout. A mechanism check finds the crossover width coincides with training-accuracy saturation (≥99%) in only 2/7 digits seeds with both quantities defined; no synthetic seed had both, so the interpolation-threshold hypothesis is rejected on `digits` and remains untested (not refuted) on `synthetic`. We conclude the crossover is real but fragile, seed- and dataset-sensitive, and its location and existence cannot currently be predicted or distinguished across datasets at feasible, ethically-fixed sample sizes.

## 1. Introduction

Confidence calibration — whether a model's predicted probabilities match its empirical accuracy — has been extensively studied for large, modern deep networks, which are typically overconfident (Guo et al., 2017). A smaller literature asks the converse question for small networks and finds the opposite tendency can hold: very narrow models are often *underconfident*, and confidence bias shifts sign as capacity grows (see prior rounds of this series for a single-dataset width sweep establishing a noisy crossover point). Separately, recent work on foundation models finds that today's large in-distribution models often trend underconfident again, reversing the classical overconfidence narrative and motivating renewed interest in *why* confidence bias has a sign at all rather than only *how much* miscalibration exists (Beyond Overconfidence: Foundation Models Redefine Calibration in Deep Neural Networks, arXiv:2506.09593). Most practical fixes remain post-hoc: temperature scaling and its many recent extensions — adaptive, per-input temperatures (aclanthology 2024.emnlp-main.1007), ensemble temperature scaling for graph networks (GETS, ICLR 2025), margin-aware recalibration (arXiv:2506.23492), and quantile-adaptive variants (arXiv:2606.21749) — rescale confidence after training rather than explaining why undertrained or under-parameterized models land on one side of the calibration line in the first place.

The "width crossover" claim we probe here is attractive because it is mechanistically suggestive (too little capacity to fit the training distribution → hedging/underconfidence; capacity exceeding what's needed to interpolate → memorization/overconfidence). But this series' accumulated experience is that such threshold claims look clean on one seed and dissolve under a multi-seed audit. This paper asks the next question: even granting seed-to-seed noise, does the *distribution* of crossover widths — and the *probability a crossover exists at all* — look the same on a second, structurally different dataset? If yes, that's evidence of a general MLP-training phenomenon; if the datasets differ, or a stable crossover often fails to exist, that argues the effect is dataset-specific and "capacity threshold" over-claims.

**Contributions.** (1) A pre-registered two-dataset, 15-seed, 8-width sweep (240 independently reseeded MLP training runs) measuring expected calibration error (ECE) and signed confidence bias. (2) A single confirmatory significance test (Mann–Whitney U on per-seed crossover widths), decided before data collection, reported together with an honest power analysis rather than as a clean positive/negative result, plus an exploratory secondary test on crossover *existence* that uses the full sample and is explicitly labeled non-confirmatory. (3) A direct, executed mechanism check of the "crossover = training-accuracy saturation" hypothesis, with careful accounting of which dataset it was actually testable on. (4) A failure-mode breakdown distinguishing "never touches overconfidence" from "touches it but reverts" among seeds without a stable crossover, which we believe is more informative than a single existence count.

## 2. Related Work

**Calibration of large networks.** Guo et al. (2017) established that modern deep, high-capacity networks are typically overconfident and that simple temperature scaling is a strong post-hoc fix. Follow-up work (Revisiting the Calibration of Modern Neural Networks, arXiv:2106.07998; Rethinking Calibration of Deep Neural Networks, NeurIPS 2021) refined when and why architectural/training choices shift confidence bias, generally still in the large-model, large-dataset regime.

**Post-hoc recalibration.** A large, active literature extends temperature scaling to be adaptive/input-dependent (arXiv 2606.21749; ACL 2024 EMNLP main 1007), ensemble-based for graphs (GETS, OpenReview qgsXsqahMq), or margin-aware (arXiv:2506.23492). These treat miscalibration as a symptom to correct post-training and are agnostic to *why* a model over- or under-shoots; our question is upstream of this literature.

**Foundation-model calibration reversal.** Recent evidence that today's largest in-distribution models trend underconfident (arXiv:2506.09593) is a reminder that "networks are overconfident" is regime-dependent, not a law — motivating the same question at the small-model end of the capacity spectrum, where this paper and its predecessors sit.

**This series.** Earlier papers in this project established, on a single dataset, that a width-driven under-to-overconfidence crossover exists but is seed-noisy, and that claims about its exact location require multi-seed reporting, fixed (non-hashed) seeding, and mechanism verification rather than assertion. We adopt those requirements here and add the cross-dataset axis, which prior rounds flagged as untested.

## 3. Method

**Pre-registration.** Before running any experiment we fixed: the two datasets, the width grid, the seed list, the metrics, the crossover definition, and the single confirmatory test. No results were inspected before this protocol was frozen; no sample size was extended after seeing p-values (the optional-stopping failure mode flagged in this series' prior rounds). All analyses beyond the single confirmatory test — bootstrap CIs, the mechanism check, the exploratory existence test, and the failure-mode breakdown — are descriptive/exploratory and labeled as such; the latter two were added in a revision pass after reviewers noted the confirmatory test's low power, and are not claimed as pre-registered confirmatory evidence.

**Datasets.** (1) `digits`: the sklearn 8×8-pixel, 10-class handwritten digit dataset (1797 real samples, pixel values scaled to [0,1]). (2) `synthetic`: a 2-feature, 2-class Gaussian-cluster classification task generated with scikit-learn's `make_classification` (2000 samples, 2 informative features, 2 clusters per class, 5% label noise), chosen to be structurally unlike `digits` (low-dimensional, 2-class, synthetic vs. high-dimensional, 10-class, real) so that agreement between the two would be a meaningful generalization signal. We note this is a single, comparatively low-noise/near-symmetric synthetic configuration; a harder or more overlapping synthetic task is a natural next probe (see Limitations).

**Models.** Single-hidden-layer MLPs (`sklearn.neural_network.MLPClassifier`, ReLU, Adam, L2 = 1e-4, max 500 iterations) at widths {2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256}.

**Seeding.** For each (dataset, width, seed) cell we use the fixed integer seed directly (0–14) for both the train/test split (`random_state=seed`, stratified 70/30 split, redone independently per cell) and the MLP's own `random_state`. Each cell is reseeded immediately before training — we do not share an RNG stream across widths within a seed, which the project's methodology notes flag as a source of spurious "before/after" comparisons in earlier rounds.

**Metrics.** On the held-out test split: accuracy, 10-bin expected calibration error (ECE), and confidence bias = mean(max softmax probability) − accuracy (positive = overconfident, negative = underconfident).

**Crossover definition.** For a given (dataset, seed), the crossover width is the smallest width in the grid such that bias is non-negative at that width *and remains non-negative at every larger width tested*. If no such width exists, the crossover is undefined (`None`) for that seed. For undefined seeds, we additionally classify the failure mode as "never touches" (bias < 0 at every tested width) or "touches but reverts" (bias ≥ 0 at some width but not monotonically thereafter).

**Confirmatory test.** A two-sided Mann–Whitney U test comparing the set of *defined* crossover widths between the two datasets, α = 0.05, run exactly once, as pre-registered.

**Exploratory existence test.** A two-sided Fisher's exact test on the 2×2 table of (dataset) × (crossover defined vs. undefined), using all 15 seeds per dataset rather than only the conditional subsample. This was not pre-registered and is reported as a secondary, hypothesis-generating analysis, prompted by reviewer feedback that the confirmatory test's power is capped by construction.

**Mechanism check.** For each seed with both a defined crossover width and a defined "training-saturation width" (smallest width reaching ≥99% training accuracy), we check whether the two coincide; seeds lacking either quantity are excluded and reported separately rather than treated as agreement or disagreement.

All code (`experiment.py`, `analysis.py`, `revision_analysis.py`) and raw per-cell results (`results.json`) are included alongside this paper.

## 4. Results

**Runtime.** All 240 training runs completed in 267s of wall-clock CPU time.

**Aggregate trend.** Averaged bias moves toward zero with width on both datasets. For `digits`, mean bias goes from −0.104 (95% bootstrap CI [−0.131, −0.072]) at width 2 to −0.0002 (CI [−0.0031, 0.0025]) at width 256 — the CI includes zero from width 32 onward. For `synthetic`, mean bias goes from −0.040 (CI [−0.052, −0.028]) at width 2 to only −0.0065 (CI [−0.0120, −0.0003]) at width 256 — still significantly underconfident even at the largest width tested; the synthetic task never reaches the "overconfident on average" regime in this grid. Mean ECE falls monotonically with width on both datasets (digits: 0.111 → 0.010; synthetic: 0.048 → 0.020), so calibration *error* shrinks even where confidence *bias* does not cross zero — the two are not interchangeable.

**Existence of a stable crossover.** A defined (stable, non-reverting) crossover width exists for only 7/15 `digits` seeds and 4/15 `synthetic` seeds.

**Failure-mode breakdown (new in this revision).** Among the 8 digits seeds without a stable crossover, 3 touch overconfidence at some width before reverting and 5 never touch it at any tested width. Among the 11 synthetic seeds without a stable crossover, all 11 never touch overconfidence at any width (0 touch-and-revert). The synthetic task's "missing" crossovers are thus not noisy near-boundary oscillation — they stay cleanly underconfident throughout, consistent with the low-noise, near-symmetric generator not producing enough capacity pressure to ever overshoot, rather than with a crossover obscured by sampling noise.

**Confirmatory test (pre-registered).** Among seeds with a defined crossover, digits crossover widths (n=7): mean 113.1, median 64, range [8, 256]. Synthetic (n=4): mean 68.0, median 64, range [16, 128]. Mann–Whitney U = 16.0, p = 0.770 — not significant. The effect size (rank-biserial correlation) is −0.143, and the minimum detectable rank-biserial effect at this n (7 vs. 4) is ≈0.74 — an enormous effect would be required for significance at this sample size. **We do not interpret this as evidence the two datasets share a crossover distribution; the test is underpowered by construction, and we report it as inconclusive rather than as a null result.**

**Exploratory existence test (new in this revision).** Because the confirmatory test is restricted to the minority of seeds that produce a defined crossover, we additionally ran a non-pre-registered Fisher's exact test on the full 2×2 table of dataset × crossover-defined (digits: 7 defined / 8 undefined; synthetic: 4 defined / 11 undefined), which uses the full n = 15 per dataset. Odds ratio = 2.41, p = 0.450 — also not significant, despite using more of the data than the conditional Mann-Whitney test. We flag this as exploratory and hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory: at these counts, distinguishing a 47%-vs-27% existence rate would itself require a substantially larger seed budget than 15 per dataset.

**Mechanism check.** Of the 7 digits seeds with both a defined crossover and a defined training-saturation width, only 2 coincide (crossover width = saturation width) — a rejection of the "crossover = interpolation threshold" hypothesis on `digits`. No synthetic seed had both quantities defined (this is not a 0/7-style rejection; it means the hypothesis was simply never testable on `synthetic` given how rarely both a crossover and a saturation point are individually defined there), so we report the mechanism check as digits-only and the synthetic side as untested rather than as a joint 2/7-vs-0/0 result.

All numbers above are persisted in `results.json` under `crossover_summary`, `crossover_none_count`, `mannwhitney_crossover_digits_vs_synthetic`, `analysis.bias_by_width_bootstrap_ci`, `analysis.ece_by_width`, `analysis.mannwhitney_effect_size_rank_biserial`, `mechanism_check_train_saturation`, and (new) `analysis.revision_round_exploratory` (Fisher's exact table and failure-mode breakdown).

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

The headline finding is negative in a useful way: the crossover is not reliably present at all — a majority of synthetic seeds never exhibit it, and cleanly so, not via boundary oscillation — and where it is present, we lack the power (n = 15 seeds, fixed in advance) to distinguish either its location (Mann-Whitney) or its existence rate (Fisher's exact, added on review) between datasets. This sharpens this series' earlier finding that single-dataset threshold claims are seed-fragile: here even the *existence* of the threshold, not just its value, is statistically indistinguishable at once from "same across datasets" and "different across datasets" — our design cannot adjudicate either claim.

Limitations: (1) Two datasets and one architecture family (single-hidden-layer MLP, scikit-learn/Adam) is a narrow generalization test; a broad claim needs more datasets, depths, and frameworks. (2) The synthetic task's near-symmetric, low-noise construction is a specific confound: the failure-mode breakdown shows its non-crossover seeds are uniformly "never touches," the signature expected if this one `make_classification` parameterization is simply too easy to ever require overconfident hedging. A harder/more-overlapping synthetic configuration is the most direct follow-up to disentangle "crossover doesn't transfer" from "poor test case," but we did not have remaining budget to add it this round. (3) The crossover definition (monotone non-reversion) is conservative by design; a looser rule changes counts but not the qualitative picture, and the failure-mode breakdown shows the loose and strict counts agree exactly on `synthetic` (4/15 either way) and differ modestly on `digits` (7 vs. 10). (4) The Fisher's exact existence test added in this revision is exploratory, motivated by reviewer feedback rather than pre-registered — hypothesis-generating, not a second confirmatory result. (5) An earlier draft referenced a `run_log.txt` training log that the pipeline never actually generated; that reference is removed here.

## 6. Conclusion

Using a pre-registered, 15-seed, two-dataset, 8-width sweep with mechanism verification, we find that the previously reported width-driven under-to-overconfidence crossover in small MLPs is real on average but frequently absent at the level of an individual model, and neither its location (Mann-Whitney) nor its existence rate (exploratory Fisher's exact) can be distinguished between datasets at feasible, ethically-fixed seed counts. The failure-mode breakdown shows the synthetic task's missing crossovers are uniformly clean underconfidence rather than noisy near-boundary reversion — a signature of that generator's difficulty, not evidence about crossovers generally. The interpolation-threshold mechanism is rejected where testable (digits) and untested where it was not (synthetic). Future claims about "the" crossover width should report both the fraction of seeds with a stable crossover and, among the rest, whether bias ever approaches zero — and treat cross-dataset transfer as open, requiring larger seed budgets or a paired design, not assumed from one small comparison.
