# Parameter-Matched Small-Network Ensembles Do Not Reliably Improve Calibration Over a Single Network of Equal Size

## Abstract

Deep ensembling is widely credited with improving calibration, but almost all evidence for this comes from ensembles that multiply the *total* parameter count relative to a single reference model. We ask a narrower, budget-honest question: at a **fixed total hidden-unit budget**, is it better to spend that budget on one network or split it across several smaller, independently-initialized networks whose softmax outputs are averaged? We compare a single 64-hidden-unit MLP ("Single-Large") against an ensemble of four 16-unit MLPs ("Ensemble-4", same total width) and a single 16-unit MLP ("Single-Small"), on a synthetic 4-class Gaussian mixture with an analytically known Bayes posterior and the real `sklearn` `digits` dataset, across 15 seeds per condition. A prior draft's headline calibration-gap claim (p=0.011) came from an unseeded, per-process-random Python `hash()` call in RNG-seed derivation — four independent reviewers rerunning our code each got a different p-value. We fixed the bug (a fixed integer seed-offset map, verified bit-identical across reruns) and reran everything. Before temperature scaling, Ensemble-4 is significantly worse-calibrated than Single-Large on both digits (p=6×10⁻⁵) and the synthetic task (p=4×10⁻⁴), driven by ensembling shrinking confidence *below* accuracy (mean confidence 0.941 vs. accuracy 0.964 on digits) — the opposite of the textbook overconfidence story. After per-model temperature scaling, the gap is not statistically significant on either dataset (digits p=0.24, synthetic p=0.98), and a paired-effect-size analysis shows the observed post-TS effect (Cohen's d=0.14 on digits) is well below what our n=15 design could reliably detect (minimum detectable d≈0.68) — so this is honestly a *null result at low power*, not a demonstrated absence of any gap. A follow-up in which we deliberately reduced weight decay four orders of magnitude (α: 1e-4→1e-8) to try to induce classical overconfidence in the base networks reproduced the same underconfidence-then-TS-absorption pattern almost exactly (post-TS gap p=0.11), suggesting this particular architecture/optimizer combination (sklearn `MLPClassifier` + Adam) simply does not become overconfident easily at this scale, regardless of regularization. We conclude that fixed-budget ensembling reliably induces mild underconfidence before calibration, and that a cheap post-hoc fix (temperature scaling) appears to absorb most of the resulting gap in our setting — though our sample size cannot rule out a small residual effect, and generalization beyond small MLPs on small datasets is untested.

## 1. Introduction

Modern neural networks are frequently miscalibrated, and post-hoc fixes such as temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017) are the standard remedy. A second story is that *deep ensembles* improve calibration by averaging out overconfident members (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017). This is almost always tested by comparing an ensemble of M full-size networks (M× the parameters of a single model) against one full-size network — an apples-to-oranges comparison from a capacity-budget perspective. For small/embedded networks, where a total parameter or FLOP budget is often the binding constraint, the operative question is different: given a fixed total budget, is it better spent on one wider network or several narrower ensembled ones?

**Related work.** Guo et al. (2017) established that a single scalar temperature fit on a validation set cheaply fixes typical overconfidence; adaptive/instance-aware variants (e.g., GETS for graph networks, ICLR 2025; sample-margin-aware recalibration, 2025) retain the single-network-vs-scaled-ensemble asymmetry. Label smoothing (Müller et al., 2019) and self-distillation (Zhang et al., 2020) are train-time alternatives. Lakshminarayanan et al. (2017) showed full-size deep ensembles are more accurate and better calibrated than a single member, without controlling for total parameter budget. Parameter-efficient ensembling methods such as BatchEnsemble (Wen et al., 2020), which share most weights across members via low-rank perturbations, target a related but distinct problem — reducing the *training/inference cost* of an M-member ensemble at roughly fixed per-member capacity — rather than our question of how to allocate a fixed *total* capacity budget between one network and several independent, non-weight-shared ones; to our knowledge the latter has not been directly isolated for calibration.

We test three width-budget allocations at matched total hidden units, on a synthetic Gaussian-mixture task with an *analytically known* Bayes-optimal posterior (a sanity check, since accuracy there is too saturated to be informative on its own) and the real `digits` dataset, which carries the main empirical weight.

## 2. Method

**Datasets.**
- *Synthetic*: K=4 classes in R¹⁰, class-conditional N(μ_k, I), equal priors (4000/1000/2000 train/val/test). Identity covariance and equal priors give an exact closed-form posterior softmax(x·μ_k − ½‖μ_k‖²), used as an auxiliary metric, not the headline result.
- *Real*: `sklearn.datasets.load_digits` (1797 8×8 images, 10 classes), standardized, stratified 60/20/20 split.

**Models.** Single-hidden-layer MLPs (`sklearn.neural_network.MLPClassifier`, ReLU, Adam, weight decay 1e-4, max 400 iterations), independently seeded per (dataset, condition, seed).
- **Single-Large**: one MLP, width 64.
- **Ensemble-4**: four independently-initialized MLPs, width 16 each (same total width as Single-Large), each on an independent bootstrap resample; probabilities averaged.
- **Single-Small**: one MLP, width 16 (unensembled reference).

**Calibration.** One scalar temperature T per condition (bounded minimization of validation NLL), applied to single models and averaged ensembles alike; metrics reported pre- and post-scaling.

**Metrics** (test set): accuracy, NLL, Brier score, 15-bin ECE, signed ECE (confidence − accuracy), mean confidence, and (synthetic only) mean L1 distance to the true Bayes posterior.

**Statistics.** 15 seeds per (dataset, condition); pre-registered primary test is Ensemble-4 vs. Single-Large post-TS ECE (one-sided paired Wilcoxon, H1: Ensemble-4 larger); pre-TS/NLL are secondary. Given all four reviewers of the previous draft independently flagged that 15 seeds cannot distinguish "no gap" from "small undetected gap," we additionally report paired Cohen's d, a bootstrap 95% CI on the mean paired difference, and the minimum detectable effect size at n=15 and n=50 (`power_analysis.py`).

**Follow-up 1: mechanism.** `mechanism_check.py` adds **Ensemble-4-NoBoot** (four members on the full, non-bootstrapped set, differing only in init) to separate "bootstrap resampling shrinks effective data" from "averaging itself shifts confidence."

**Follow-up 2: deliberately overconfident baseline.** Two reviewers asked whether the classical overconfidence-correction story would reappear if the base networks were pushed toward overconfidence. `overconfident_check.py` reruns the digits comparison with weight decay reduced 10,000× (α=1e-8, max_iter=800, otherwise identical) — the standard recipe for provoking overconfidence via reduced regularization and longer training.

**Follow-up 3: ECE bin-count sensitivity.** One reviewer asked whether 15-bin ECE on a ~360-example test set is sensitive to bin count. `bin_sensitivity.py` recomputes post-TS ECE at 10/15/20 bins for 5 seeds.

**A reproducibility bug we found and fixed.** The submitted `experiment.py` derived seeds as `seed * 31337 + hash(cname) % 1000`, where `hash()` is Python's per-process-randomized string hash (unseeded since Python 3.3). Every trained model's seed, and every downstream number, therefore varied across invocations. Four independent reviewers rerunning the unmodified script got four different primary p-values (0.30; 0.032/0.009 across two runs; 0.18/0.14; the paper's original 0.011), never converging; one reviewer traced the exact cause via repeated `python -c "hash('ensemble4')"` calls. `mechanism_check.py` never used `hash()` and reproduced correctly throughout. We replaced the offset with a fixed integer dictionary (`COND_OFFSET`) and confirmed two full reruns of the corrected script produce bit-identical `results.json`.

## 3. Results

All numbers are in `results.json`, `mechanism_results.json`, `power_analysis.json`, `overconfident_results.json`; all scripts reproduce every number below (combined runtime ≈150s on CPU, verified bit-identical across reruns).

**Synthetic (near-saturated, accuracy≈0.998).** Pre-TS ECE: 0.0025/0.0037/0.0033 (Single-Large/Ensemble-4/Single-Small); Ensemble-4 significantly worse than Single-Large (p=4.3×10⁻⁴). Post-TS ECE: 0.0019/0.0014/0.0014 — not significant (p=0.98), and Ensemble-4 is nominally *lower*. The bootstrap 95% CI on the post-TS mean difference is [-0.00079, -0.00010] — narrow but at negligible absolute magnitude (~0.0004 ECE) and near-ceiling accuracy, so we still treat this as a directional pre-TS check rather than a strong independent result.

**Digits (real data, accuracy≈0.96–0.97). Main evidence.**

| Condition | Pre-TS ECE | Post-TS ECE | Post-TS NLL | Accuracy | Mean confidence (pre-TS) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single-Large (H=64) | 0.0183 | 0.0207 | 0.1084 | 0.972 | 0.969 |
| Ensemble-4 (4×H=16) | 0.0301 | 0.0216 | 0.1194 | 0.964 | 0.941 |
| Single-Small (H=16) | 0.0232 | 0.0236 | 0.1460 | 0.955 | 0.965 |

Pre-TS, Ensemble-4 is significantly worse than Single-Large (p=6.1×10⁻⁵, mean diff +0.0118). Post-TS, the gap is not significant (p=0.244, mean diff +0.0009, n=15). Post-TS NLL is only marginally different (p=0.060). Single-Small has the worst post-TS ECE and NLL of the three — undersized single networks are not automatically a free lunch either.

**How much does the null result actually tell us?** Paired Cohen's d for the post-TS ECE difference is **0.142** on digits — small, and well under the **d≈0.675** our n=15 design would need to detect at 80% power (one-sided, α=0.05). The bootstrap 95% CI on the mean difference is **[-0.0024, 0.0040]**, straddling zero but not tightly. At n=50 (our proposed follow-up sample size), the minimum detectable effect shrinks to d≈0.357 — still larger than what we currently observe, meaning even a well-powered 50-seed rerun could plausibly still fail to resolve whether a small (d≈0.1–0.15) true effect exists. We therefore describe the post-TS finding as *underpowered-null*, not *confirmed-null*.

**Bin-count sensitivity.** Post-TS ECE (5-seed subset) at 10/15/20 bins: Single-Large 0.0159/0.0196/0.0213, Ensemble-4 0.0156/0.0164/0.0212, Single-Small 0.0193/0.0250/0.0249. Absolute ECE grows with bin count (expected — finer bins on ~360 test points are sparser and noisier), but the relative ordering and the qualitative "no reliable post-TS gap" conclusion is unchanged at every bin count tested.

**Direction of the effect (signed ECE, mechanism check, digits, pre-TS).** Single-Large −0.0013 (near-calibrated), Single-Small −0.0004, Ensemble-4-Bootstrap −0.0229 (underconfident), Ensemble-4-NoBoot −0.0142 (also underconfident, smaller). Base networks are not meaningfully overconfident to begin with, so probability-averaging pushes them past calibrated into mild underconfidence rather than correcting overconfidence.

**Is the underconfidence just an artifact of light regularization?** Reducing weight decay 10,000× (α=1e-4→1e-8, 800 iterations) to deliberately provoke overconfidence reproduced essentially the same picture: Single-Large signed pre-TS ECE −0.0028 (still slightly *under*confident, not over), Ensemble-4 −0.0229 (same underconfidence as before), post-TS gap p=0.11 (still not significant), pre-TS gap p=6.1×10⁻⁵ (unchanged). This particular architecture/optimizer (sklearn `MLPClassifier` + Adam, digits-scale data) apparently resists becoming overconfident even under much weaker regularization — so the absence of a classical overconfidence baseline here is not simply an artifact of our specific α=1e-4 choice, and the mismatch with the textbook deep-ensemble story likely reflects something about scale or optimizer rather than regularization strength alone.

**Bootstrap vs. init diversity.** Post-TS ECE for Ensemble-4-NoBoot (0.0210) is not significantly different from Ensemble-4-Bootstrap (0.0218, p=0.80) or Single-Large (0.0205, p=0.93) — inconclusive at n=15, and per the power analysis above, there may be little real gap left to attribute once TS is applied.

**Limitations of scale.** Both datasets are small (2000 and ~360 test examples) and both architectures are single-hidden-layer MLPs; we did not test CNNs, M>4, or non-classification calibration. The fixed-seed scheme in `ensemble_run` overflows numpy's 32-bit seed bound starting at raw seed=18 (`seed*7919 > 2³²−1`; not seed≈17 as we mis-stated in an earlier draft) — this does not affect any number reported here (N_SEEDS=15) but would need fixing before extending seed count.

## 4. Conclusion

At a fixed total width budget, splitting capacity across an ensemble of small networks reliably induces measurable pre-calibration underconfidence relative to one equally-large network — on digits (p=6×10⁻⁵) and a synthetic sanity check (p=4×10⁻⁴) — via probability-averaging pushing already near-calibrated small networks past the calibrated point, not correcting overconfidence as the textbook story predicts. After standard per-model temperature scaling this gap is not statistically significant on either dataset (digits p=0.24, synthetic p=0.98), but our own effect-size analysis shows this null is underpowered (observed d=0.14 vs. a detectable d≈0.68 at n=15) rather than a confirmed absence of any gap. Deliberately weakening regularization 10,000× to try to induce the classical overconfidence regime did not change this picture, suggesting the mismatch with prior deep-ensemble calibration claims is not simply a regularization artifact. A single appropriately-sized network with temperature scaling remains a simpler, no-worse (within our power to detect), and four-times-cheaper-to-train alternative to a parameter-matched ensemble in this setting. Future work: a well-powered (50+ seed, after fixing the seed-overflow bug) rerun to shrink the minimum detectable effect further; CNNs on larger image benchmarks; larger ensemble counts; and architectures/optimizers more prone to overconfidence, to test whether a pre-TS ensembling advantage re-emerges in the regime the original deep-ensemble claims were made in. More broadly: an unseeded `hash()` call silently destroyed reproducibility in our first draft, and only independent reruns by reviewers caught it — we recommend verifying bit-identical reruns before submission rather than relying on reviewers to discover such bugs.
