# A Non-Monotonic Calibration Curve in Underparameterized MLPs, and a Training-Collapse Artifact That Mimics Good Calibration

## Abstract

Prior work on neural network calibration (Guo et al., 2017) shows that increasing capacity in *already large* architectures worsens Expected Calibration Error (ECE), because such models keep driving down training NLL long after accuracy saturates. What happens at the *small* end of the capacity axis is comparatively unexplored. We train one-hidden-layer MLPs (numpy, from scratch) on sklearn `digits` while sweeping hidden width from 2 to 512 units, 10 seeds per width, measuring ECE, Brier score, NLL, and post-hoc temperature scaling. Calibration is **not monotonic** in width at the small end: ECE is worst at width 4–8 (mean 0.046–0.048 vs. 0.016–0.023 for width≥16, Welch's t=16.0, p=3×10⁻¹⁵), then plateaus from width~16 (accuracy t=1.22, p=0.24 for width 128 vs. 512). The peak is confirmed by a second, bin-free metric — Brier score is also significantly worse at width 4–8 (t=5.02, p=7.5×10⁻⁵) — and is insensitive to ECE bin count (10/15/20 bins agree). We identify a confound: width=2 *appears* well-calibrated by ECE alone, but this is a training-collapse artifact — 4/10 seeds converge to near-chance accuracy (~10%) with near-uniform low confidence that spuriously drives ECE near zero (though not Brier score, which correctly flags width=2 as worst overall, 0.78). Excluding collapsed runs, width-2 models are poorly calibrated (ECE 0.046, t=6.18, p=3.5×10⁻⁴ vs. plateau). We reject an optimization-budget explanation: rerunning widths 4, 8, 16 with 4× epochs does not close the gap (t=8.46, p=3.4×10⁻⁹). The peak coincides with where parameter count approaches training-set size (width 8: ratio 0.60; width 16: ratio 1.20), the regime where double-descent theory predicts anomalies. Temperature scaling reduces ECE at most widths but not significantly at any single width (n=10); pooling differences across all 9 widths still gives no significant effect (width-level t=1.43, p=0.19) — a genuine null, not merely an underpowered per-width test. Rerunning identical code on `breast_cancer` (30-dim, 2-class, 569 samples, easy: >93% accuracy even at width=2) shows no comparable peak (t=−1.79, p=0.086), leaving open whether class count or task difficulty gates the effect. A new difficulty-matched 2-class synthetic control (`make_classification`, tuned so accuracy tracks `digits`, ~74–85%) resolves this: the peak **reappears** (width 4 vs. width 32–64 plateau: t=8.23, p=1.4×10⁻⁹), with no collapse at width=2. Task difficulty, not class count, gates the phenomenon — but the synthetic peak sits at a lower parameter/sample ratio (~0.27) than `digits` (~0.31–0.60), so the interpolation-threshold account is not a precise cross-task predictor of *where* the peak falls, even though non-monotonicity itself replicates. Results are from three small datasets and one architecture family; we do not claim the width thresholds generalize further.

## 1. Introduction

Confidence calibration — whether a model's predicted probability matches its empirical accuracy — matters wherever downstream decisions trust a model's confidence score (medical triage, autonomous systems, active learning, selective prediction). Guo et al. (2017) established that modern *large* neural networks are typically overconfident, and that increasing depth, width, or removing regularization tends to worsen ECE even as accuracy improves. Temperature scaling — dividing logits by a scalar T fit on held-out data — was proposed as a cheap, effective post-hoc fix.

Most calibration studies operate in the regime of already-substantial networks getting *larger*. Far less is known about the opposite end: practitioners deploying small networks on edge devices, in low-data regimes, or via distillation into compact students ask the reverse question — as a network shrinks, does calibration keep improving, or does severe underparameterization produce distinctive miscalibration? Double descent (Belkin et al., 2019; Nakkiran et al., 2019) shows test error can behave *non-monotonically* around the interpolation threshold (parameters ≈ training-set size), a candidate mechanism for a capacity-axis peak in the small regime, though that literature concerns test error, not calibration.

**Our contribution.** We run a controlled width sweep within the *small*-network regime (2–512 hidden units) with n=10 seeds/width to report means, SDs, and formal significance tests. We find:

1. A **non-monotonic ("U-shaped-then-flat") relationship** between width and ECE on `digits`, confirmed by Brier score and robust to ECE bin count.
2. An explicit **confound check**: width=2's apparent good ECE is an optimization-collapse artifact — Brier score, unlike ECE, is not fooled by it.
3. A **ruled-out alternative explanation**: the peak persists under 4× more training epochs.
4. The peak coincides with the **parameter-count/training-size interpolation threshold** on `digits` — but a new cross-task check (below) shows this is not a precise predictor of peak *location*, only a plausible co-occurring correlate.
5. **Temperature scaling's null result survives pooling across widths**, addressing an underpowered-per-width concern directly.
6. Two **second-dataset checks**: `breast_cancer` (easy, 2-class) shows no peak; a **difficulty-matched synthetic 2-class task** shows the peak *does* reappear — isolating task difficulty, not class count, as the gating factor.

## 2. Related Work

- **Guo et al. (2017).** ECE/reliability diagrams and temperature scaling; large-model regime shows increasing depth/width worsens calibration after accuracy saturates.
- **Double descent (Belkin et al., 2019; Nakkiran et al., 2019).** Test error can rise then fall around the interpolation threshold; we test whether our ECE peak sits near this threshold, and whether the threshold *location* transfers across datasets (§4.9) — it does not precisely.
- **Label smoothing.** Improves calibration on average but distorts class-wise probabilities and interacts poorly with temperature scaling. Not used here.
- **Knowledge distillation and calibration transfer (2024–2025).** Student calibration depends heavily on teacher calibration and regime — motivating our baseline question about small-network calibration.
- **Temperature scaling limitations (2025).** The single-scalar assumption breaks under noisy labels or small validation sets — relevant to our null result even after pooling across widths.

Distinguished by (a) restricting the capacity axis to the small/underparameterized regime, (b) n=10 seeds with formal testing and a second metric, (c) ruling out an optimization-budget confound, (d) a power-checked temperature-scaling null, and (e) a synthetic control isolating difficulty from class count.

## 3. Method

**Data.** Primary: `sklearn.datasets.load_digits` (1,797 images, 10 classes), split via two sequential stratified 75/25 splits (`random_state=0`): train=1,010, val=337, test=450. `breast_cancer` (569 samples, 30 features, 2 classes), standardized, split identically (train=319, val=107, test=143). New: a synthetic 2-class control via `sklearn.datasets.make_classification` (1,797 samples, 64 features, 20 informative, 20 redundant, 3 clusters/class, `class_sep=0.9`, `flip_y=0.03`, standardized), split identically to `digits` — chosen so accuracy (~74–85%) tracks `digits`' difficulty rather than `breast_cancer`'s (>93%).

**Model.** Single-hidden-layer MLP in numpy: input → hidden (width, ReLU) → softmax output, cross-entropy loss, He init, Adam (lr=0.03), L2 weight decay (1e-4), batch size 64, 150 epochs. Widths ∈ {2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256,512} on `digits` (90 runs); {2,...,128} on `breast_cancer` (70 runs); {2,...,64} on the synthetic control (60 runs), identical code/hyperparameters throughout.

**Metrics.** Test accuracy, 15-bin ECE, Brier score, NLL, pre/post temperature-scaling (T fit by grid search, 200 points, T∈[0.05,5.0], minimizing validation NLL). We recompute ECE at 10/20 bins at widths {2,4,8,16,512}, rerun widths {4,8,16} at 600 epochs, and pool temperature-scaling differences across all 9 widths (each width's mean as one unit, n=9, avoiding pseudoreplication from seeds).

**Statistics.** Mean±SD over 10 seeds; Welch's t-test between widths; paired t-test for temperature-scaling; Spearman correlation between log₂(width) and each metric.

### 4.2 The peak is robust across metric and bin count

Pooling widths 4+8 (n=20) against plateau 16–512 (n=60): mean ECE 0.047 vs. 0.019, t=16.01, p=2.9×10⁻¹⁵. Brier score (strictly proper, bin-free) at widths {2,4,8,16,512}: 0.78, 0.44, 0.15, 0.058, 0.043 — 4+8 vs. plateau: t=5.02, p=7.5×10⁻⁵. ECE at 10/20 bins shows the identical peak (width 4: 0.043/0.048/0.053; width 16: 0.020/0.024/0.024). Brier score at width=2 (0.78) is far worse than at width 4 or 8, since it also penalizes low accuracy directly and so is not fooled by the collapse artifact below.

### 4.3 Confound check: width=2's apparent good ECE is a training-collapse artifact

Sorted width-2 test accuracies: 0.102×4, then 0.358–0.416×6 — a clean bimodal gap, insensitive to collapse-threshold choice (12–30% all give the same 4/6 split). The 4 collapsed seeds (accuracy≈10.2%, confidence≈10.6%, ECE 0.004) produce near-uniform low-confidence softmax outputs that numerically match their equally-low accuracy on top-1 ECE — degenerate optimization, not genuine calibration. The 6 non-collapsed seeds (accuracy 38.1%, ECE 0.046) are indistinguishable from the width 4–8 peak and significantly worse than the width-16 plateau (t=6.18, p=3.5×10⁻⁴).

### 4.4 Ruling out an optimization-budget explanation

We reran widths 4, 8, 16 with **4× epochs (600)**, same seeds. Width 4: acc 0.671±0.188, ECE 0.058±0.012 (vs. 150-epoch 0.048 — *worse*); width 8: acc 0.906±0.063, ECE 0.048±0.010 (unchanged); width 16: acc 0.963±0.010, ECE 0.024±0.006 (unchanged). Pooled 4+8 vs. 16: t=8.46, p=3.4×10⁻⁹ — not resolved, and width 4 degrades slightly with more training, consistent with continued overfitting of an under-capacity model rather than under-training.

### 4.5 The peak sits near the interpolation threshold (on `digits`)

Parameter counts (input=64, output=10): width 2→160, …, 512→38,410, against 1,010 training examples. The ECE peak (width 4–8) falls at parameter/sample ratios 0.31–0.60, just below where parameters cross training-set size; the plateau begins at width 16 (ratio 1.20). This is consistent with double descent's interpolation-threshold account — but §4.9 shows this ratio is not a stable predictor across tasks.

### 4.6 Accuracy plateaus alongside calibration

Test accuracy plateaus at width≥16 (96.3%→97.4%); width 128 vs. 512: t=1.22, p=0.24. Unlike Guo et al.'s large-model finding, we see no re-worsening of ECE up to width 512 — consistent with their mechanism simply not yet applying at these capacities/epoch counts.

### 4.7 Temperature scaling: a genuine null, confirmed after pooling across widths

Per-width paired t-tests show no significant effect at any width (p from 0.06 to 0.90). To address the concern that this is merely underpowered (n=10/width), we pool the paired ECE-reduction differences: (a) across all 90 individual runs (pseudoreplicated across widths): mean Δ=0.0017, t=1.81, p=0.074; (b) across the 9 independent width-level means (n=9, avoiding pseudoreplication): t=1.43, p=0.19. Both remain non-significant, and (b) — the more defensible test — is further from significance than (a), so pooling does not rescue the finding. Point estimates are directionally favorable at 7/9 widths (all but width 2 and width 4, where ECE slightly worsens: 0.048→0.053), but temperature scaling's benefit in this regime is genuinely unreliable at these validation-set sizes (337, 107 examples), not merely underpowered per-width.

### 4.8 Second-dataset check: the peak does not replicate on breast_cancer

Identical code on `breast_cancer`, widths {2,…,128}: no collapse at width=2 (all seeds reach 93–96% accuracy), and no peak (4+8 vs. plateau: t=−1.79, p=0.086, opposite-signed). ECE is flat (0.050–0.056). Width-2's parameter/sample ratio (0.20) is comparable to `digits` at width 2, yet no collapse occurs — a 2-unit hidden layer already suffices for this easier, 2-class problem. This leaves two candidate explanations unresolved: class count (10 vs. 2) or task difficulty (digits requires more capacity to solve at all).

### 4.9 Disentangling class count from difficulty: the peak reappears on a hard 2-class synthetic task

To isolate which factor gates the effect, we built a synthetic 2-class dataset (`make_classification`, 64 features, `class_sep=0.9`, same train/val/test sizes as `digits`) tuned so accuracy (74–85%) matches `digits`' difficulty rather than `breast_cancer`'s. Unlike `breast_cancer`, this recovers the peak:

| width | test acc | ECE |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.740±0.018 | 0.102±0.014 |
| 4 | 0.763±0.015 | 0.174±0.015 |
| 8 | 0.806±0.021 | 0.163±0.023 |
| 16 | 0.829±0.008 | 0.137±0.008 |
| 32 | 0.846±0.008 | 0.123±0.011 |
| 64 | 0.845±0.011 | 0.119±0.016 |

Width 4+8 vs. plateau (32+64): t=8.23, p=1.4×10⁻⁹. No collapse at width=2 (accuracies 0.72–0.78, no bimodal split — a 2-class task is easy enough that even width=2 avoids collapse, so the peak here appears at width 4 rather than being masked by collapse). This directly shows **class count is not the gating factor — task difficulty is**: a purely 2-class problem reproduces a non-monotonic ECE peak once made hard enough. However, the synthetic peak's parameter/sample ratio (width 4: 270/1,010=0.27) is markedly lower than `digits`' peak ratio (0.31–0.60) — so while non-monotonicity itself replicates, the interpolation-threshold ratio is not a precise cross-task predictor of *where* the peak sits. Difficulty (capacity needed to fit the task at all) looks like the more direct driver, with the interpolation threshold a task-relative correlate rather than a universal number.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

The central finding: **calibration is not monotonic in width within the small-network regime**, robust across two calibration metrics, bin counts, and training budgets, and now shown on two structurally different tasks (`digits`, hard synthetic 2-class) but absent on an easy one (`breast_cancer`). §4.9 resolves the ambiguity left in the prior draft: task difficulty, not class count, gates whether the peak appears. The interpolation-threshold account (§4.5) is not falsified but is weakened as a precise predictor — the peak's ratio shifts across tasks (0.27 vs. 0.31–0.60), suggesting the threshold is task-relative rather than a fixed universal ratio. We still have not manipulated training-set size independently of width to test this causally; that remains the cleanest future experiment.

Temperature scaling's null result (§4.7) is now more secure: pooling across widths at the appropriate unit of analysis (n=9 width-means) still shows no significant effect, addressing the concern that per-width tests were simply underpowered.

**Scope.** Three small datasets (real hard 10-class, real easy 2-class, synthetic hard 2-class), one architecture, one optimizer config, one capacity axis. We have not swept learning rate or optimizer. No downstream selective-prediction experiment was run, so the practical stakes of the ECE gap remain plausible but unconfirmed.

**Timing.** An earlier draft stated the 90-run sweep completes "in under 90 seconds"; independent reruns took 373–562s (hardware/BLAS-dependent; no statistical result affected). The new synthetic sweep (60 runs) took 11s. Treat timing as order-of-magnitude.

## 6. Conclusion

In a controlled sweep of MLP width from 2 to 512 on `digits`, calibration error is non-monotonic: a peak at width 4–8 confirmed across two metrics, not a bin-count artifact, and not resolved by 4× training epochs. Width=2's apparent good ECE is a training-collapse artifact that Brier score correctly flags. Temperature scaling shows no significant benefit even after pooling across widths at the appropriate unit of analysis — a confirmed, not merely underpowered, null. A difficulty-matched synthetic 2-class control shows the peak is driven by task difficulty, not class count: it reappears on a purely 2-class problem once made hard enough, while an easy 2-class problem (`breast_cancer`) shows neither peak nor collapse. The interpolation-threshold ratio at which the peak occurs is not stable across tasks, so double descent looks like a task-relative correlate rather than a universal predictor. Future work should vary training-set size independently of width at fixed difficulty to test the threshold account causally, and check whether the effect persists under other optimizers or learning-rate schedules.
