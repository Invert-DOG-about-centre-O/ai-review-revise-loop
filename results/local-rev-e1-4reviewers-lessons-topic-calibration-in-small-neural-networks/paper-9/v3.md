# A Non-Monotonic Calibration Curve in Underparameterized MLPs, and a Training-Collapse Artifact That Mimics Good Calibration

## Abstract

Prior work on neural network calibration (Guo et al., 2017) shows that increasing capacity in *already large* architectures worsens Expected Calibration Error (ECE), because such models keep driving down training NLL long after accuracy saturates. What happens at the *small* end of the capacity axis is comparatively unexplored. We train one-hidden-layer MLPs (numpy, from scratch) on sklearn `digits` while sweeping hidden width from 2 to 512 units, 10 seeds per width, measuring ECE, Brier score, NLL, and post-hoc temperature scaling. Calibration is **not monotonic** in width at the small end: ECE is worst at width 4–8 (mean 0.046–0.048 vs. 0.016–0.023 for width≥16, Welch's t=16.0, p=3×10⁻¹⁵), then plateaus from width~16 (accuracy t=1.22, p=0.24 for width 128 vs. 512). The peak is confirmed by a second, bin-free metric — Brier score is also significantly worse at width 4–8 (t=5.02, p=7.5×10⁻⁵) — and is insensitive to ECE bin count (10/15/20 bins agree). We identify a confound: width=2 *appears* well-calibrated by ECE alone, but this is a training-collapse artifact — 4/10 seeds converge to near-chance accuracy (~10%) with near-uniform low confidence that spuriously drives ECE near zero (though not Brier score, which correctly flags width=2 as worst overall, 0.78, since it also penalizes inaccuracy). Excluding collapsed runs, width-2 models are poorly calibrated (ECE 0.046, t=6.18, p=3.5×10⁻⁴ vs. plateau). We reject an optimization-budget explanation: rerunning widths 4, 8, 16 with 4× epochs (600 vs. 150) does not close the gap (t=8.46, p=3.4×10⁻⁹) — the peak is not simply under-training. It coincides with where parameter count approaches training-set size (width 8: 610 params vs. 1,010 examples, ratio 0.60; width 16: ratio 1.20), the regime where double-descent theory predicts anomalies, though we do not isolate this from other width-correlated factors. Temperature scaling reduces ECE at most widths but not significantly at any single width (n=10). Rerunning identical code on `breast_cancer` (30-dim, 2-class, 569 samples) shows no comparable peak or collapse (t=−1.79, p=0.086), indicating the peak is task-contingent, not universal. Results are from two small datasets and one architecture family; we do not claim the width thresholds generalize further.

## 1. Introduction

Confidence calibration — whether a model's predicted probability matches its empirical accuracy — matters wherever downstream decisions trust a model's confidence score (medical triage, autonomous systems, active learning, selective prediction). Guo et al. (2017) established that modern *large* neural networks are typically overconfident, and that increasing depth, width, or removing regularization tends to worsen ECE even as accuracy improves, because cross-entropy training keeps sharpening the softmax after classification error has plateaued. Temperature scaling — dividing logits by a scalar T fit on held-out data — was proposed as a cheap, effective post-hoc fix.

Most calibration studies, including Guo et al.'s capacity sweep, operate in the regime of already-substantial networks getting *larger*. Far less is known about the opposite end: practitioners deploying small networks on edge devices, in low-data regimes, or via distillation into compact students often ask the reverse question — as a network shrinks, does calibration keep improving, or does severe underparameterization produce distinctive (possibly worse) miscalibration? The double-descent literature (Belkin et al., 2019; Nakkiran et al., 2019) shows test error can behave *non-monotonically* around the interpolation threshold (parameter count ≈ training-set size), a natural candidate mechanism for a capacity-axis peak in the small regime, though that literature concerns test error, not calibration. Related distillation work shows student calibration is sensitive to teacher calibration and regime, and foundation-model work shows the overconfidence story reverses at very large scale — both reinforcing that capacity-calibration trends are regime-dependent, not universal.

**Our contribution.** We run a controlled width sweep within the *small*-network regime (2–512 hidden units) with n=10 seeds/width to report means, SDs, and formal significance tests. We find:

1. A **non-monotonic ("U-shaped-then-flat") relationship** between width and ECE on `digits`, confirmed by a second, bin-free metric (Brier score) and robust to ECE bin count.
2. An explicit **confound check**: width=2's apparent good ECE is an optimization-collapse artifact, not genuine calibration — and Brier score, unlike ECE, is not fooled by it.
3. A **ruled-out alternative explanation**: the peak is not an optimization-budget effect — it persists under 4× more training epochs.
4. The peak coincides with the **parameter-count/training-size interpolation threshold**, connecting to double-descent theory as a candidate (untested) mechanism.
5. **Temperature scaling's effectiveness is not statistically reliable** at any single width (n=10).
6. A **second-dataset check** (`breast_cancer`) showing the peak does *not* replicate on an easier, 2-class problem — direct evidence the phenomenon is task-dependent.

## 2. Related Work

- **Guo et al. (2017).** ECE/reliability diagrams and temperature scaling; large-model regime shows increasing depth/width worsens calibration after accuracy saturates.
- **Double descent (Belkin et al., 2019; Nakkiran et al., 2019).** Test error can rise then fall around the interpolation threshold (parameters ≈ training samples); we test whether our ECE peak sits near this threshold, though we study calibration, not raw generalization error, and do not manipulate the threshold independently of width.
- **Label smoothing.** Improves calibration on average but distorts class-wise probabilities and interacts poorly with temperature scaling. Not used here.
- **Knowledge distillation and calibration transfer (2024–2025).** Student calibration depends heavily on teacher calibration and regime — motivating our question of what a *small* network's calibration looks like as a baseline.
- **Temperature scaling limitations (2025).** The single-scalar assumption breaks under noisy labels or small validation sets — relevant to our null result at n=10 seeds/width with modest validation sets (337, 107 examples).

Distinguished by (a) restricting the capacity axis to the small/underparameterized regime, (b) n=10 seeds with formal testing and a second calibration metric, (c) a test ruling out an optimization-budget confound, and (d) directly testing whether the finding generalizes to a second, structurally different dataset.

## 3. Method

**Data.** Primary: `sklearn.datasets.load_digits` (1,797 8×8 images, 10 classes, rescaled to [0,1]), split via two sequential stratified 75/25 splits (fixed `random_state=0`): train=1,010, validation=337 (temperature fitting only), test=450. Same split shared across all runs. Second dataset (§4.6): `sklearn.datasets.load_breast_cancer` (569 samples, 30 features, 2 classes), standardized, split identically (train=319, val=107, test=143).

**Model.** Single-hidden-layer MLP in numpy: input → hidden (width, ReLU) → softmax output, cross-entropy loss, He init, Adam (lr=0.03), L2 weight decay (1e-4), batch size 64, 150 epochs. Widths ∈ {2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256,512} on `digits` (10 seeds each, 90 runs); {2,4,8,16,32,64,128} on `breast_cancer` (70 runs), identical code/hyperparameters.

**Metrics.** Test accuracy, 15-bin ECE, Brier score, and NLL, pre/post temperature-scaling. T fit by grid search (200 points, T∈[0.05,5.0]) minimizing validation NLL. We additionally recompute ECE at 10 and 20 bins at widths {2,4,8,16,512} to check bin-count sensitivity, and rerun widths {4,8,16} with 600 epochs (4×) to test an optimization-budget confound.

**Statistics.** Mean±SD over 10 seeds; Welch's t-test for between-width comparisons; paired t-test for pre/post temperature-scaling; Spearman correlation between log₂(width) and each metric across all 90 digits runs.

### 4.2 The peak is robust across metric and bin count

Pooling widths 4+8 (n=20) against plateau 16–512 (n=60): mean ECE 0.047 vs. 0.019, t=16.01, p=2.9×10⁻¹⁵. We reran widths {2,4,8,16,512} to additionally compute **Brier score** (strictly proper, bin-free) and ECE at 10/20 bins: Brier is 0.44 (width 4) and 0.15 (width 8), vs. 0.058 (width 16) and 0.043 (width 512) — 4+8 vs. plateau: t=5.02, p=7.5×10⁻⁵, so the peak is not a 15-bin artifact. ECE at 10/20 bins shows the identical peak (width 4: 0.043/0.048/0.053; width 16: 0.020/0.024/0.024). Brier score at width=2 (0.78) is far worse than at width 4 or 8 — unlike ECE, Brier also penalizes low accuracy directly, so it is not fooled by the collapse artifact below.

### 4.3 Confound check: width=2's apparent good ECE is a training-collapse artifact

Sorted width-2 test accuracies: 0.102×4, then 0.358–0.416×6 — a clean bimodal gap, insensitive to collapse-threshold choice (12–30% all give the same 4/6 split). The 4 collapsed seeds (accuracy≈10.2%, confidence≈10.6%, ECE 0.004, Brier≈0.9+) produce near-uniform low-confidence softmax outputs that numerically match their equally-low accuracy on top-1 ECE — degenerate optimization, not genuine calibration; Brier correctly flags these as the worst predictions in the sweep. The 6 non-collapsed seeds (accuracy 38.1%, ECE 0.046) are statistically indistinguishable from the width 4–8 peak and significantly worse than the width-16 plateau (t=6.18, p=3.5×10⁻⁴).

### 4.4 Ruling out an optimization-budget explanation

A natural alternative: widths 4–8 are simply under-trained relative to their ceiling at 150 epochs. We reran widths 4, 8, 16 with **4× epochs (600)**, same seeds/hyperparameters. Width 4: acc 0.671±0.188, ECE 0.058±0.012 (vs. 150-epoch 0.048 — *worse*, not better); width 8: acc 0.906±0.063, ECE 0.048±0.010 (unchanged); width 16: acc 0.963±0.010, ECE 0.024±0.006 (unchanged). Pooled 4+8 vs. 16 at 600 epochs: t=8.46, p=3.4×10⁻⁹ — the peak is not resolved, and width 4 degrades slightly with more training, consistent with continued overfitting of an under-capacity model rather than under-training. This rules out the simplest optimization-budget confound raised in review; we did not additionally sweep learning rate.

### 4.5 The peak sits near the interpolation threshold

Parameter counts (input=64, output=10): width 2→160, 4→310, 8→610, 16→1,210, …, 512→38,410, against 1,010 training examples. The ECE peak (width 4–8) falls at parameter/sample ratios 0.31–0.60, just below where parameters cross training-set size; the plateau begins at width 16 (ratio 1.20), just above it — consistent with double descent's interpolation-threshold account of non-monotonic capacity-axis behavior, and a candidate mechanism beyond our own post-hoc speculation below. But we did not manipulate parameter count independently of width (e.g. varying training-set size at fixed width), so we cannot claim to have isolated this factor from others that co-vary with width.

### 4.6 Accuracy plateaus alongside calibration

Test accuracy plateaus at width≥16 (96.3%→97.4%); width 128 vs. 512 shows no significant difference (t=1.22, p=0.24). Unlike Guo et al.'s large-model finding, we see no re-worsening of ECE up to width 512 (0.016–0.019 for width≥64) — consistent with their mechanism (overfitting to NLL after accuracy saturates) simply not yet applying at these capacities/epoch counts.

### 4.7 Temperature scaling: point-estimate gains, no significant effect at n=10

Temperature scaling lowers mean ECE at 7 of 9 widths (all except collapse-diluted width 2, and width 4, where it slightly worsens ECE: 0.048→0.053). Paired t-tests show no significant effect at any width (p from 0.06 to 0.90) — a genuine null result at this sample size.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

The central finding: **on `digits`, calibration is not monotonic in width within the small-network regime**, with a peak at width 4–8 that is robust across two calibration metrics (ECE, Brier), robust to ECE bin count, and not explained by insufficient training budget. It coincides with the parameter/sample interpolation threshold, connecting to double-descent theory as a plausible mechanism, though we have not isolated that factor from others that co-vary with width. §4.8 shows this band is not a fixed property of "small MLPs" generally — `breast_cancer` shows neither the peak nor the collapse despite a similar parameter/sample ratio at width 2, so task difficulty/class count also gates the effect.

We speculate, still without a controlled test, that the peak coincides with the region where the model has enough capacity to fit training data non-uniformly (train−test gap peaks at width 4–8: +0.047 to +0.062) but not enough to do so consistently across classes — complementary to, not exclusive with, the interpolation-threshold account in §4.5. Disentangling these (e.g., a synthetic dataset varying class count and difficulty independently at matched sample size, as reviewers suggested) is future work.

**Scope.** Two small datasets, one architecture, one optimizer config, one capacity axis (width). We ruled out a fixed-epoch confound but did not sweep learning rate or optimizer (e.g., SGD), which could affect collapse rate or peak sharpness. No downstream selective-prediction experiment was run, so the practical stakes of a ~0.027 absolute ECE gap remain plausible but unconfirmed. Temperature scaling's null result is specific to our validation set sizes (337, 107 examples).

**Timing.** An earlier draft stated the 90-run sweep completes "in under 90 seconds" on the authors' machine; independent reruns took 373–562s (hardware/BLAS-dependent, no statistical result affected). We report timing only as order-of-magnitude.

## 6. Conclusion

In a controlled sweep of MLP width from 2 to 512 on `digits`, calibration error is non-monotonic: a peak at width 4–8 confirmed across two metrics (ECE t=16.0 p=3×10⁻¹⁵; Brier t=5.0 p=7.5×10⁻⁵), not an artifact of bin count, and not resolved by 4× more training epochs (t=8.46, p=3.4×10⁻⁹ at 600 epochs). Width=2's apparent good ECE is a training-collapse artifact that Brier score, unlike ECE, correctly flags. The peak sits near the parameter/training-size interpolation threshold, a candidate link to double-descent theory we did not isolate causally. Temperature scaling shows consistent point-estimate gains but no significant effect at n=10. The peak does not replicate on `breast_cancer`, showing it is a real but dataset-contingent phenomenon. Future work should vary class count and difficulty independently (e.g., synthetic data at matched sample size) to identify the causal driver, and test whether the effect persists under different optimizers or learning-rate schedules.
