# A Non-Monotonic Calibration Curve in Underparameterized MLPs, and a Training-Collapse Artifact That Mimics Good Calibration

## Abstract

Prior work on neural network calibration (Guo et al., 2017) shows that increasing capacity in *already large* architectures (deep ResNets, wide CNNs) worsens Expected Calibration Error (ECE), because such models keep driving down training NLL long after they saturate accuracy. It is comparatively unexplored what happens at the *small* end of the capacity axis. We train one-hidden-layer MLPs (numpy, from scratch) on the sklearn `digits` dataset while sweeping hidden width from 2 to 512 units, with 10 random seeds per width, and measure ECE, NLL, and post-hoc temperature-scaling calibration. Calibration is **not monotonic** in width at the small end: ECE is worst at width 4–8 (mean ECE 0.046–0.048, vs. 0.016–0.023 for width ≥16, Welch's t=16.0, p=3×10⁻¹⁵ for pooled 4–8 vs. the ≥16 plateau), then plateaus from width ~16 onward (t=1.22, p=0.24 for accuracy at width 128 vs. 512). We identify and rule out a confound: width=2 *appears* well-calibrated on average, but this is a training-collapse artifact — 4/10 seeds converge to near-chance accuracy (~10%) with near-uniform low confidence that spuriously drives ECE near zero; the collapsed/non-collapsed split is clean (bimodal accuracy gap 10.2%→35.8%, insensitive to the exact threshold). Excluding collapsed runs, width-2 models are in fact poorly calibrated (mean ECE 0.046, worse than the width-16 plateau, t=6.18, p=3.5×10⁻⁴). Temperature scaling reduces ECE at most widths but not significantly at any single width (n=10). To test whether the width 4–8 peak generalizes, we reran the identical code on sklearn `breast_cancer` (30-dim, 2-class, 569 samples): no comparable peak or collapse appears (4+8 vs. plateau: t=−1.79, p=0.086; all width=2 seeds train successfully, 93–96% accuracy), indicating the peak is contingent on task properties such as class count, not a universal property of underparameterized MLPs. Results are from two small datasets and one architecture family; we do not claim the specific width thresholds generalize further.

## 1. Introduction

Confidence calibration — whether a model's predicted probability matches its empirical accuracy — matters wherever downstream decisions trust a model's confidence score (medical triage, autonomous systems, active learning, selective prediction). Guo et al. (2017) established that modern *large* neural networks are typically overconfident, and that increasing depth, width, or removing regularization tends to worsen ECE even as accuracy improves, because cross-entropy training keeps sharpening the softmax after classification error has plateaued. Temperature scaling — dividing logits by a scalar T fit on held-out data — was proposed as a cheap, effective post-hoc fix.

Most calibration studies, including Guo et al.'s capacity sweep, operate in the regime of already-substantial networks (ResNets, WideResNets) getting *larger*. Far less is known about the opposite end: practitioners deploying small networks on edge devices, in low-data regimes, or via distillation into compact students often ask the reverse question — as a network shrinks, does calibration keep improving, or does severe underparameterization itself produce distinctive (possibly worse) miscalibration? Related distillation work shows student calibration is sensitive to teacher calibration and training regime in non-obvious ways, suggesting capacity-calibration interactions are more complex than a single monotonic trend. Work on foundation models shows the overconfidence story reverses in some very-large-model regimes, reinforcing that "smaller/larger" calibration trends are regime-dependent rather than universal.

**Our contribution.** We run a controlled width sweep within the *small*-network regime (2–512 hidden units, single hidden layer) with n=10 seeds/width to report means, SDs, and formal significance tests, rather than informal "trend from 2 runs" claims. We find:

1. A **non-monotonic ("U-shaped-then-flat") relationship** between width and ECE on `digits`: calibration is worst in a narrow band (width 4–8), then improves and plateaus from width 16 onward.
2. An explicit **confound check**: width=2's apparent good calibration is an artifact of optimization collapse, not genuine calibration — verified by separating collapsed vs. non-collapsed seeds, with a robustness check showing the split is insensitive to the exact collapse threshold.
3. **Temperature scaling's effectiveness is not statistically reliable** at any single width (n=10), despite reducing point-estimate ECE at 7/9 widths.
4. A **second-dataset check** (breast_cancer, 2-class, 30-dim) showing the width 4–8 peak does *not* replicate there — direct evidence the phenomenon is task-dependent, bounding the claim's generality rather than leaving it as a speculative caveat.

## 2. Related Work

- **Guo et al. (2017).** Established ECE/reliability diagrams and temperature scaling; showed that in the large-model regime, increasing depth/width worsens calibration despite improving accuracy, because training over-optimizes NLL after accuracy saturates.
- **Label smoothing.** Improves calibration on average but distorts class-wise probabilities and interacts poorly with temperature scaling. Not used here; a natural extension.
- **Knowledge distillation and calibration transfer (2024–2025).** Student calibration after distillation depends heavily on teacher calibration and regime — motivating our question of what a *small* network's calibration looks like before any distillation, as a baseline.
- **Temperature scaling limitations (2025).** The single-scalar assumption breaks under noisy labels or small validation sets — relevant to our null result at n=10 seeds/width with modest validation sets (337, 107 examples).
- **Capacity vs. calibratability.** Tension between models that are *already* well-calibrated and models that are *calibratable* (responsive to post-hoc fixes) — consistent with our inconsistent post-hoc gains across widths.

Distinguished by (a) restricting the capacity axis to the small/underparameterized regime, (b) n=10 seeds with formal testing throughout, and (c) directly testing (not just discussing) whether the finding generalizes to a second, structurally different dataset.

## 3. Method

**Data.** Primary dataset: `sklearn.datasets.load_digits` (1,797 8×8 grayscale images, 10 classes, pixel range 0–16, rescaled to [0,1]). We split via two sequential stratified 75/25 splits (`train_test_split(test_size=0.25)`, fixed `random_state=0`), yielding train=1,010 (56.2%), validation=337 (18.75%, used only for temperature fitting), test=450 (25.0%) — we report the exact resulting percentages here since 60/20/20 only approximates what two sequential 75/25 splits produce. The same split is shared across all runs. Second dataset (robustness check, §4.6): `sklearn.datasets.load_breast_cancer` (569 samples, 30 features, 2 classes), standardized, split identically (train=319, val=107, test=143).

**Model.** A single-hidden-layer MLP implemented from scratch in numpy: input → hidden (width, ReLU) → output (softmax), cross-entropy loss, He init, Adam (lr=0.03), L2 weight decay (1e-4), batch size 64, 150 epochs. On `digits` we swept width ∈ {2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256,512}, 10 seeds/width (90 runs); on `breast_cancer`, identical code/hyperparameters, width ∈ {2,4,8,16,32,64,128}, 10 seeds/width (70 runs) — dropping the two largest widths since the plateau is already established by 128 and the check is whether a peak appears at all.

**Metrics.** Test accuracy, 15-bin ECE, and NLL, pre- and post-temperature-scaling (digits only; breast_cancer focuses on raw ECE since its purpose is testing for the peak, not re-verifying temperature scaling). T is fit by grid search (200 points, T∈[0.05,5.0]) minimizing validation NLL. We also record average confidence and train−test accuracy gap.

**Statistics.** Mean±SD across 10 seeds; Welch's t-test for between-width comparisons; paired t-test for pre/post temperature-scaling ECE (same seeds); Spearman correlation between log₂(width) and each metric across all 90 digits runs (log-spaced, non-linear axis).

Across all 90 runs, Spearman correlation of log₂(width) with ECE is ρ=−0.565 (p=6.6×10⁻⁹) and with NLL is ρ=−0.846 (p=9.3×10⁻²⁶): both decrease with width overall. But this monotone-looking aggregate correlation masks a non-monotonic shape, visible in the table: ECE rises from width 2→4→8 before falling and plateauing.

### 4.2 The peak-miscalibration band (width 4–8) is statistically robust

Pooling widths 4 and 8 (n=20) against the plateau region widths 16–512 (n=60): mean ECE 0.047 vs. 0.019, Welch's t=16.01, p=2.9×10⁻¹⁵. Width 4 vs. width 16 alone: t=8.76, p=1.2×10⁻⁷. In absolute terms the gap is ~0.027 ECE (0–1 scale, 0=perfect); small next to typical large-model ECEs reported by Guo et al. (often 0.05–0.15), but 2–3× the plateau's own ECE level — i.e. it more than doubles calibration error relative to what's achievable at the same accuracy with slightly more capacity, plausibly relevant for selective-prediction thresholds near the plateau's error rate, though we did not run a downstream selective-prediction experiment to confirm this.

### 4.3 Confound check: width=2's apparent good calibration is a training-collapse artifact

The raw table shows width=2 with mean ECE (0.029) *lower* than width 4 or 8 — naively suggesting calibration keeps improving as capacity shrinks. Splitting the 10 width-2 seeds by test accuracy: sorted accuracies are 0.102, 0.102, 0.102, 0.102, 0.358, 0.371, 0.378, 0.380, 0.382, 0.416 — a clean bimodal gap between 10.2% and 35.8%, so any collapse threshold between roughly 12% and 30% (we used 15%) produces the identical 4-collapsed/6-non-collapsed split; the result is insensitive to the exact cutoff. The 4 collapsed seeds (mean accuracy ≈10.2%, confidence ≈10.6%, ECE 0.004) produce near-uniform, low-confidence softmax outputs that happen to numerically match their equally-low accuracy — degenerate optimization, not genuine calibration. The 6 non-collapsed seeds (mean accuracy 38.1%, ECE 0.046) are statistically indistinguishable from width 4–8's peak and significantly *worse* than the width-16 plateau (t=6.18, p=3.5×10⁻⁴). The true small-width calibration curve, once the collapse artifact is removed, does not dip at the smallest width — it is uniformly bad from width 2 through 8, then improves and plateaus from width ≥16.

### 4.4 Accuracy plateaus alongside calibration

Test accuracy plateaus at width≥16 (96.3%→97.4%), and width 128 vs. 512 shows no significant accuracy difference (t=1.22, p=0.24). Unlike Guo et al.'s large-model finding — accuracy plateaus but ECE keeps *worsening* as capacity keeps growing — we see no re-worsening up to width 512; ECE also plateaus (0.016–0.019 for width≥64). Consistent with Guo et al.'s proposed mechanism (overfitting to NLL after accuracy saturates) simply not having taken hold yet at these capacities/epoch counts; we did not test far larger widths or longer training where it might eventually emerge.

### 4.5 Temperature scaling: point-estimate gains, no significant effect at n=10

Temperature scaling lowers mean ECE at 7 of 9 widths (all except width 2's collapse-diluted average, and width 4, where it slightly *worsens* ECE: 0.048→0.053, plausibly because T* is fit on a validation set where the width-4 model's poorly-separated logits make the NLL-minimizing T a poor match for test-set ECE). Paired t-tests (same 10 seeds, pre- vs. post-scaling) show no significant effect at any width: p-values range from 0.06 (width 2, marginal) to 0.90 (width 512). We report this as a genuine null result at this sample size rather than treating the point-estimate reduction as established.

### 4.6 Second-dataset check: the width 4–8 peak does not replicate on breast_cancer

To test whether the peak is general or `digits`-specific (64-dim input, 10 classes), we reran the identical MLP/training code, unchanged, on `breast_cancer` (30-dim, 2 classes, 569 samples), width ∈ {2,4,8,16,32,64,128}, 10 seeds each (70 runs, 4.6s total on CPU):

|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.947±0.008 | 0.050±0.006 | 0.270±0.070 |
| 4 | 0.948±0.006 | 0.050±0.004 | 0.273±0.058 |
| 8 | 0.943±0.007 | 0.054±0.007 | 0.268±0.031 |
| 16 | 0.941±0.005 | 0.056±0.002 | 0.280±0.026 |
| 32 | 0.938±0.002 | 0.055±0.002 | 0.258±0.023 |
| 64 | 0.941±0.005 | 0.053±0.004 | 0.252±0.033 |
| 128 | 0.941±0.003 | 0.054±0.002 | 0.241±0.013 |

No collapse occurs at width=2 (all 10 seeds reach 93–96% accuracy — a 2-unit hidden layer solves a 2-class problem far more easily than 10-class digits), and there is no width 4–8 peak: pooling widths 4+8 against the width≥16 plateau gives t=−1.79, p=0.086 (not significant, and in the *opposite* direction — ECE is marginally lower, not higher, at 4–8 here). ECE is essentially flat (0.050–0.056) across the entire range. This is a direct, tested answer — not a speculative caveat — to whether the digits-specific peak generalizes: it does not, at least not to a lower-class-count, easier tabular problem. This bounds our claim: the width 4–8 band and width=2 collapse are real, robust phenomena *on `digits`*, but neither is a universal property of underparameterized MLPs — both plausibly depend on class count, effective input dimensionality, and per-class sample budget, none of which we varied independently. We did not test enough datasets to identify which specific factor drives the difference.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

The central, well-supported finding is: **on `digits`, calibration is not monotonic in width within the small-network regime** — a specific underparameterized band (roughly 4–8 hidden units on this 64-dim, 10-class, ~1,800-sample problem) significantly worse calibrated than either extreme we tested, and once width exceeds a problem-dependent threshold (~16 units here), further capacity increase up to 512 units does not measurably help or hurt calibration or accuracy. §4.6 shows directly that this band is not a fixed property of "small MLPs" in general: on `breast_cancer` no comparable peak or collapse appears. We therefore narrow our claim relative to an earlier draft: the qualitative *possibility* of a non-monotonic small-width calibration curve, and of a collapse artifact mistakable for genuine calibration, are things future studies of small-network calibration should check for directly — but neither is guaranteed on any given small dataset, and predicting in advance which datasets will show the effect is open, unresolved by our two-dataset comparison.

We speculate, without directly testing it, that the width 4–8 peak on digits coincides with the region where the model has enough capacity to fit a non-trivial fraction of training data confidently but not enough to do so uniformly across classes/examples — consistent with the train−test accuracy gap also peaking there (+0.047 to +0.062, vs. +0.017 at width 2 and +0.026–0.037 for width≥16). This mechanism, if correct, predicts no peak on an easier, lower-class-count problem like breast_cancer, where even width=2 already reaches >93% accuracy with little room for a confidence/accuracy mismatch to open — consistent with what we observed, though this is a post-hoc rationalization, not a controlled test isolating class count or difficulty as the causal driver.

**Scope.** Results are from two small datasets, one architecture (1-hidden-layer MLP, ReLU), one optimizer config, and one capacity axis (width, not depth). We did not test larger real-world image datasets, convolutional architectures, or the regime where Guo et al.'s "over-capacity worsens calibration" trend might reappear at larger widths. Temperature scaling's null result is specific to our validation set sizes (337 and 107 examples); larger validation sets could plausibly detect a real but small effect.

**Reproducibility caveat on timing.** An earlier draft stated the 90-run digits sweep completes "in under 90 seconds," measured on the authors' machine. Independent reruns of the identical code on different hardware took 373–562 seconds (4–6× longer, dominated by width=256/512) — hardware/BLAS-dependent, not affecting any statistical result (all numbers reproduced bit-for-bit), but stated too precisely as if hardware-independent. We now report timing only as order-of-magnitude (see Reproducibility).

## 6. Conclusion

In a controlled sweep of MLP width from 2 to 512 hidden units on `digits`, with 10 seeds/width and formal statistical tests, calibration error is not monotonic across the small-capacity regime: a statistically robust peak of miscalibration at width 4–8 (t=16.0, p=3×10⁻¹⁵ vs. the width≥16 plateau), and the apparent exception — good calibration at width=2 — is a training-collapse artifact, verified via a threshold-insensitive collapsed/non-collapsed split. Temperature scaling shows a consistent point-estimate ECE reduction but no statistically significant effect at our sample size. Rerunning the identical procedure on a second, structurally different dataset (`breast_cancer`) shows neither the peak nor the collapse replicates there — direct evidence these are real but dataset-contingent phenomena, not universal properties of underparameterized MLPs. Future work should test more datasets varying class count and difficulty independently to identify what drives the effect's presence or absence.
