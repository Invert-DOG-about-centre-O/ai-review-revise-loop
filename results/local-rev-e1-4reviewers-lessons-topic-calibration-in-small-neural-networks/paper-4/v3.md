# Calibration Improves, Not Worsens, With Width in the Very-Small-Network Regime

## Abstract

Guo et al. (2017) established that increasing capacity in *modern* (large,
overparameterized) neural networks tends to worsen calibration even as
accuracy improves. We ask whether the same relationship holds at the
opposite end of the capacity spectrum — tiny multilayer perceptrons with
2 to 128 hidden units — where networks are plausibly underfitting rather
than overfitting. We pre-registered the hypothesis that expected
calibration error (ECE) before post-hoc temperature scaling would
increase with width, matching the large-model literature, and tested it
with a permutation null across three binary classification datasets (two
synthetic, one real: two-moons, circles, and Wisconsin breast cancer), 7
widths, and 10 fixed seeds per condition (n=70 per dataset). The
pre-registered hypothesis was **rejected in the opposite direction**: ECE
before scaling *decreases* significantly with log-width in all three
datasets (Spearman ρ = −0.52, −0.76, −0.36; permutation p ≤ 0.003 in
every case, surviving Bonferroni correction). The effect survives
controlling for test accuracy (partial ρ = −0.47, −0.74, −0.32,
p<0.008) *and*, newly, for training accuracy (partial ρ = −0.38, −0.59,
−0.36, p≤0.002), so it is not a proxy for the well-known accuracy–ECE
relationship under either measure of fit. Mean training-set ECE also
falls monotonically with width, consistent with an underfitting-relief
mechanism. Extending the width grid to 256/512/1024 — new in this
revision — finds the decreasing trend does *not* continue indefinitely:
on two-moons it significantly reverses (ρ=+0.36, p=0.022) between widths
128–1024, giving the first located evidence, within this study's own
architecture, of the turning point toward the large-model regime; on
circles and breast cancer the trend flattens (p=0.65, p=0.26) rather than
reversing, i.e. no evidence of a turning point in the same range. We
report full pre-registration, seeds, and raw/aggregated data.

## 1. Introduction

Calibration — the property that a model's predicted confidence matches
its empirical accuracy — is central to using neural network outputs as
decision-relevant probabilities. The dominant empirical narrative,
established by Guo et al. (2017), is that increasing depth, width, and
training duration in modern deep networks *worsens* calibration relative
to older, smaller architectures, even while classification accuracy
improves. This has motivated a large post-hoc calibration literature
(temperature scaling, Platt scaling, isotonic regression, focal-loss
training) and further studies of *why* capacity correlates with
overconfidence.

Almost all of this literature studies capacity increases in an already
large regime — ResNets, Vision Transformers, and similar — where models
are past the point of underfitting their training data. Much less is
known about the *very small* end of the capacity axis: MLPs with a
handful of hidden units, of the kind used in low-resource, embedded, or
tabular settings, where a network may still be underfitting. If
overconfidence in large models arises partly from a model's ability to
memorize and produce sharp, high-confidence decision boundaries, a
severely underparameterized network might show the opposite pattern:
poor calibration driven by *underfitting*, which added capacity could
relieve rather than worsen, at least until some capacity threshold is
crossed.

**Related work.** Guo et al. (2017) is the foundational reference
establishing depth/width/weight-decay/batch-normalization as
calibration-relevant factors in large networks, and temperature scaling
as a simple, strong post-hoc fix — both of which we adopt directly.
Mukhoti et al. (2020) show that focal-loss training implicitly regularizes
predictive entropy and improves calibration in large networks *without*
post-hoc correction — a training-time analogue of the mechanism we
study, but aimed at curbing overconfidence in overparameterized models,
the opposite direction from the underfitting-relief effect we find here.
Work on the "calibration generalization gap" and double-descent in
uncertainty quantification studies how ECE behaves across under- and
over-parameterized regimes for larger models, generally finding a
roughly monotonic ECE–error relationship in the overparameterized
regime. We are not aware of prior work that specifically isolates the
underfitting-driven end of the width axis for small MLPs under this kind
of pre-registered design, or that empirically locates a within-family
turning point between the two regimes, as we attempt in Section 3.

**Contribution.** We run a small, fully pre-registered, multi-dataset
study of the width–calibration relationship for MLPs in the 2–128 hidden
unit range, plus an extended 128–1024 sweep added in this revision:
fixed integer seeding, a null hypothesis and permutation test decided
before looking at the data, a leave-one-width-out robustness check, two
independent partial-correlation checks (test accuracy and training
accuracy) ruling out accuracy as a full confound under either measure, a
training-set diagnostic of the underfitting mechanism, an explicit
accounting of optimizer non-convergence, a direct search for the
turning point between the underfitting-relief and overconfidence
regimes, honest reporting of non-significant results with their minimum
detectable effect, and full release of code, raw per-run results, and
analysis outputs.

## 2. Method

**Datasets.** Three binary classification tasks: (1) `two-moons` (2D,
synthetic, noise=0.30, n=600), (2) `circles` (2D, synthetic, noise=0.15,
factor=0.5, n=600), and (3) Wisconsin breast cancer (30D, real, n=569).
Each is split 50/25/25 into train/validation/test, stratified by label;
features standardized using train-set statistics.

**Models.** Single-hidden-layer MLPs (`sklearn.neural_network.MLPClassifier`,
ReLU, Adam, L2 penalty 1e-4, max_iter=2000) with hidden widths
**W ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128}** for the main study. For each
(dataset, width) we train **10 replicates** with fixed integer seeds
`seed = dataset_idx*100000 + width_idx*1000 + rep_idx` — explicit
index-based, not hash-based, so reruns are bit-identical. This gives
3 × 7 × 10 = **210 trained models**. A separate extended sweep (Section
3) adds W ∈ {256, 512, 1024} with the same seed formula continued at
width indices 7–9, i.e. 3 × 3 × 10 = 90 more models (128 is re-included
at its original width index 6 for continuity).

**Calibration measurement.** We compute 15-bin ECE on the held-out test
set from each model's P(y=1), before (`ECE_pre`) and after (`ECE_post`)
temperature scaling. Temperature T is fit on the *validation* split
(disjoint from the test split) by grid search minimizing binary
cross-entropy over log(T) ∈ [log 0.05, log 20].

**Pre-registered hypotheses** (see `experiment.py` docstring):
- **H1**: `ECE_pre` positively correlated with log2(width) per dataset
  (Spearman ρ); permutation null shuffling width labels, 20,000
  permutations, two-sided.
- **H2**: Temperature scaling reduces ECE — paired one-sided Wilcoxon
  (`ECE_pre > ECE_post`), pooled per dataset (n=70).
- **H3** (secondary): `ECE_post` (not the pre/post delta, to avoid a
  mechanical confound) remains correlated with log2(width) after
  scaling. Same permutation-null design as H1.

The pre-registration treats H1 and H3 (3 datasets each = 6 directional
correlation tests) as one family for Bonferroni correction, since both
test the same underlying width–ECE relationship at different pipeline
stages; H2's pairwise comparisons were pre-registered as a separate
family. Pooling all nine directional tests into a single stricter
correction (α=0.05/9=0.0056) does not change any conclusion: the
weakest H1 result (breast cancer, p=0.0030) still clears this bar.

**Generalization bar** (pre-registered): a hypothesis generalizes only if
it holds in the same direction with p<0.05 in ≥2 of 3 datasets.

**Robustness and mechanism checks (one pre-registered, four added in
response to review).** (a) *Leave-one-width-out* (pre-registered). (b)
*Accuracy-confound checks*: partial Spearman correlation of `ECE_pre`
with log2(width), controlling separately for (i) test accuracy and (ii)
*training* accuracy via rank residuals — the latter added this revision
because the underfitting story is fundamentally about training-set fit,
not test-set fit, and a reviewer asked whether the test-accuracy control
alone was sufficient. (c) *Underfitting-mechanism check*: training-set
accuracy and training-set ECE by width. (d) *Convergence check*: whether
sklearn's Adam solver hit `max_iter` without declaring convergence, by
width. (e) *Extended-width sweep* (new): the same architecture and
pipeline trained at W ∈ {256, 512, 1024}, to test directly whether the
decreasing ECE_pre–width trend continues, flattens, or reverses beyond
128 — the most direct evidence this study can offer on the "unlocated
turning point" flagged by three of four round-2 reviewers. We also
verified that `followup.py`'s independently retrained models reproduce
`experiment.py`'s `ECE_pre` exactly (max abs. difference 0.0 across all
210 matched pairs), ruling out drift between the two scripts as a
confound for checks (b)–(d). All checks use the same fixed-seed formula
as the main experiment.

**Power context.** At n=70 pairs, the paired Wilcoxon test's minimum
detectable rank-biserial effect at α=0.05 (one-sided), 80% power, is
≈0.30 (normal approximation).

All code, raw per-run results, aggregated analysis, and robustness/
follow-up outputs (including the new extended-width sweep) are released
alongside this paper. Total wall-clock time: 178.9s for the 210
main-experiment trainings; the extended-width sweep (90 more models,
widths to 1024) took 185.4s.

## 3. Results

**H1 — rejected, in the opposite direction, in all three datasets.**
`ECE_pre` *decreases* significantly with log-width in every dataset:

| Dataset | Spearman ρ (log2 width vs ECE_pre) | permutation p | Bonferroni (α=0.05/6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| two-moons | −0.523 | 0.0000 | significant |
| circles | −0.762 | 0.0000 | significant |
| breast cancer | −0.357 | 0.0030 | significant |

**Robustness.** Leave-one-width-out recomputation never flips sign in any
of the 21 checks (e.g. circles ranges from −0.70 to −0.79).

**Is this just an accuracy effect?** Test accuracy rises substantially
with width on two datasets (moons 0.808→0.925, circles 0.641→0.955) and
only mildly on breast cancer (0.966→0.971, ρ=0.18, p=0.14). Controlling
for **test** accuracy, the width–ECE_pre relationship survives in all
three: partial ρ = −0.471 (moons, p<0.0001), −0.737 (circles, p<0.0001),
−0.316 (breast cancer, p=0.008). Controlling instead for **training**
accuracy — new this revision, since the underfitting-relief story is
about training-set fit specifically — the relationship survives equally
well: partial ρ = −0.383 (moons, p=0.0011), −0.588 (circles, p<0.0001),
−0.358 (breast cancer, p=0.0023). Both controls point the same direction
and remain significant everywhere, so the width effect is not an
artifact of either accuracy measure.

**Underfitting-mechanism check.** Training-set ECE falls monotonically
with width in all three datasets (circles: 0.096 at width 2 → 0.030 at
width 128; moons: 0.069 → 0.033; breast cancer: 0.035 → 0.010), in step
with training accuracy rising (circles: 0.654 → 0.966). Consistent with,
though not conclusive proof of, the underfitting-relief story.

**Convergence check.** Of 210 runs, 208 converged before `max_iter`; the
2 exceptions were both on circles, at widths 4 and 8, with no
non-convergence at width ≥16 anywhere. Rules out non-convergence as a
confound at the wide end.

**Does the trend reverse at larger widths?** Extending to W ∈
{256, 512, 1024}: on two-moons, ECE_pre *stops decreasing and
significantly reverses* — Spearman ρ = +0.360 (p=0.022) across the
128–1024 range, with mean ECE rising from 0.046 (width 128) to 0.059
(width 1024) while test accuracy is flat-to-declining (0.925→0.911). On
circles and breast cancer, the trend instead **flattens** with no
significant slope in either direction (circles ρ=0.074, p=0.65; breast
cancer ρ=0.184, p=0.26) — ECE hovers in a narrow band (circles:
0.036–0.048; breast cancer: 0.021–0.031) rather than continuing to fall
or clearly reversing. This is the first direct evidence, within this
study's own pipeline, that the underfitting-relief regime has a finite
range: on one of three datasets we locate a reversal inside a tractable
compute budget; on the other two we find a plateau, not a located
turning point, in the same range.

**H2 — temperature scaling significantly reduces ECE on 1 of 3
datasets.**

| Dataset | mean ECE_pre → post | Wilcoxon p (one-sided) | rank-biserial | vs. MDE (~0.30) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| two-moons | 0.062 → 0.060 | 0.194 | 0.057 | below MDE — underpowered null |
| circles | 0.070 → 0.055 | <0.0001 | 0.371 | above MDE — real effect |
| breast cancer | 0.0312 → 0.0311 | 0.861 | −0.114 | below MDE — underpowered null |

We do not claim temperature scaling "fails" on moons/breast cancer;
observed effects are too small relative to this study's power at n=70 to
distinguish from zero. We did not test alternative post-hoc correctors
(Platt scaling, isotonic regression) or training-time alternatives
(e.g. focal loss, per Mukhoti et al. 2020); this is left to future work.

**H3 — secondary, partial generalization.** `ECE_post` remains
significantly negatively correlated with log-width on moons (ρ=−0.485,
p=0.0000) and circles (ρ=−0.429, p=0.0000), but not breast cancer
(ρ=−0.208, p=0.082) — meeting the pre-registered 2/3 bar.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

Within the 2–128 hidden-unit range on these three simple binary tasks,
adding capacity relieves miscalibration that looks like underfitting,
rather than introducing the overconfidence seen when scaling
already-large modern networks. This is not a contradiction of Guo et al.
(2017) — it is a different capacity regime, and two independent
accuracy-controlled partial correlations (test and, new here, training
accuracy) plus the training-set ECE trend give converging support to the
underfitting-relief reading over a purely accuracy-driven artifact. The
extended-width sweep gives the first concrete (if partial) answer to
where a turning point might sit: on two-moons the reversal is located
within 128–1024 widths on this exact architecture; on circles and breast
cancer we instead find a plateau in the same range, so a "single located
turning point" is not established across datasets — the transition, if
one exists for those two, lies beyond 1024 units or takes a different
form (e.g. flattening rather than reversing).

**Limitations.** This remains a narrow, single-architecture-family
study: one hidden layer, one library/solver (sklearn MLPClassifier,
Adam), three low-dimensional-or-small-n binary classification datasets,
and one post-hoc calibration method (temperature scaling only). The
title's "very-small-network regime" should be read as this specific
corner of the design space. The accuracy-controlled partial correlations
are rank-based controls for one covariate at a time, not causal
identification of a width-specific mechanism, and the training-ECE
diagnostic remains correlational, not an intervention on underfitting
per se (e.g. we did not vary regularization strength or training
duration at fixed width to induce/relieve underfitting directly — a
natural next step). The extended-width sweep uses the same width-index
seed formula as the main study but is a single additional 10-seed batch
per width, not independently pre-registered, so it should be read as
exploratory rather than confirmatory. The two non-significant H2 results
remain underpowered nulls, not evidence that temperature scaling never
helps at these widths. Test/validation splits (n≈140–150 per dataset)
are small enough that per-run ECE estimates carry sampling noise,
mitigated by pooling across 10 seeds and using rank-based tests.

## 5. Conclusion

In tiny MLPs (2–128 hidden units) on three binary classification
datasets, expected calibration error before post-hoc correction
decreases significantly and robustly with width — opposite to the
overconfidence-with-capacity pattern in large modern networks. This
holds after controlling separately for test accuracy and training
accuracy, and is accompanied by a matching decrease in training-set ECE
consistent with an underfitting-relief mechanism; optimizer
non-convergence is rare and not concentrated at wide networks.
Extending the width grid to 1024 units locates a significant reversal on
one of three datasets and a plateau (not yet a reversal) on the other
two, giving the first concrete evidence of where the underfitting-relief
regime's own limits lie, without yet establishing a single shared
turning point. Temperature scaling reduces the gap further on one of
three datasets and leaves an underpowered-but-plausible residual effect
on the other two. We release full code, fixed seeds, raw per-run
results, and all robustness/mechanism/extended-width outputs so every
number here can be regenerated exactly.
