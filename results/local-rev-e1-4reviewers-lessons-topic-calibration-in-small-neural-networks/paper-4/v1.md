# Calibration Improves, Not Worsens, With Width in the Very-Small-Network Regime

## Abstract

Guo et al. (2017) established that increasing capacity in *modern* (large,
overparameterized) neural networks tends to worsen calibration even as
accuracy improves. We ask whether the same relationship holds at the
opposite end of the capacity spectrum — tiny multilayer perceptrons with
2 to 128 hidden units — where networks are plausibly underfitting rather
than overfitting. We pre-registered the hypothesis that expected
calibration error (ECE) before post-hoc temperature scaling would
increase with width, matching the large-model literature, and tested it
with a permutation null across three binary classification datasets (two
synthetic, one real: two-moons, circles, and Wisconsin breast cancer), 7
widths, and 10 fixed seeds per condition (n=70 per dataset). The
pre-registered hypothesis was **rejected in the opposite direction**: ECE
before scaling *decreases* significantly with log-width in all three
datasets (Spearman ρ = −0.52, −0.76, −0.36; permutation p ≤ 0.003 in
every case, surviving Bonferroni correction), and the trend is stable
under leave-one-width-out robustness checks. Temperature scaling
significantly reduced ECE on only one of three datasets (circles,
p<0.0001); on the other two, the paired Wilcoxon test was non-significant
and the estimated effect sizes (rank-biserial 0.06 and −0.11) fall below
the study's own minimum detectable effect (~0.30 at n=70, 80% power), so
these should be read as underpowered nulls, not evidence of no effect. We
report these results with their full pre-registration, seeds, and
raw/aggregated data released alongside this paper.

## 1. Introduction

Calibration — the property that a model's predicted confidence matches
its empirical accuracy — is central to using neural network outputs as
decision-relevant probabilities. The dominant empirical narrative,
established by Guo et al. (2017), is that increasing depth, width, and
training duration in modern deep networks *worsens* calibration relative
to older, smaller architectures, even while classification accuracy
improves — networks become overconfident as they grow. This has motivated
a large post-hoc calibration literature (temperature scaling, Platt
scaling, isotonic regression) and further studies of *why* capacity
correlates with overconfidence (e.g., double-descent-style analyses of
uncertainty quantification, and studies of the "calibration
generalization gap" across parameterization regimes).

Almost all of this literature studies capacity increases in an already
large regime — ResNets, Vision Transformers, and similar — where models
are past the point of underfitting their training data. Much less is
known about the *very small* end of the capacity axis: MLPs with a
handful of hidden units, of the kind used in low-resource, embedded, or
tabular settings, where a network may still be underfitting. If
overconfidence in large models arises partly from a model's ability to
memorize and produce sharp, high-confidence decision boundaries, a
severely underparameterized network might show the opposite pattern:
poor calibration driven by *underfitting* (systematically wrong,
possibly still miscalibrated, decision boundaries), which added capacity
could relieve rather than worsen, at least until some capacity threshold
is crossed.

**Related work.** Guo et al. (2017, "On Calibration of Modern Neural
Networks") is the foundational reference establishing depth/width/weight
decay/batch normalization as calibration-relevant factors in large
networks, and temperature scaling as a simple, strong post-hoc fix — both
of which we adopt directly. Work on the "calibration generalization gap"
and on double-descent in uncertainty quantification studies how ECE
behaves across under- and over-parameterized regimes for larger models
(e.g. Vision Transformers), generally finding a roughly monotonic
ECE–error relationship in the overparameterized regime; our study
targets a much smaller capacity range that this line of work does not
cover directly. Follow-on post-hoc calibration methods (parameterized
temperature scaling, auxiliary calibration networks) extend temperature
scaling's expressivity but are not needed at the scale we study here,
since a single scalar temperature is already the standard first
baseline.

**Contribution.** We run a small, fully pre-registered, multi-dataset
study of the width–calibration relationship for MLPs in the 2–128 hidden
unit range, applying the lessons of prior work in this line of research:
fixed integer seeding (never derived from a randomized hash), a null
hypothesis and permutation test decided before looking at the data, a
leave-one-width-out robustness check, honest reporting of non-significant
results together with their minimum detectable effect, and full release
of code, raw per-run results, and analysis outputs.

## 2. Method

**Datasets.** Three binary classification tasks, chosen to vary
generating process and dimensionality: (1) `two-moons` (2D, synthetic,
noise=0.30, n=600), (2) `circles` (2D, synthetic, noise=0.15, factor=0.5,
n=600), and (3) Wisconsin breast cancer (30D, real, n=569, from
scikit-learn). Each is split 50/25/25 into train/validation/test,
stratified by label; features are standardized using train-set
statistics.

**Models.** Single-hidden-layer MLPs (`sklearn.neural_network.MLPClassifier`,
ReLU activation, Adam solver, L2 penalty 1e-4, max_iter=2000) with hidden
widths **W ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128}**. For each (dataset, width) pair
we train **10 replicates** with fixed integer seeds computed as
`seed = dataset_index*100000 + width_index*1000 + rep_index` — an
explicit index-based mapping, not a hash of any string, so reruns are
bit-identical. This gives 3 × 7 × 10 = **210 trained models**.

**Calibration measurement.** We compute 15-bin expected calibration
error (ECE) on the held-out test set from each model's predicted
P(y=1), both before (`ECE_pre`) and after (`ECE_post`) post-hoc
**temperature scaling**. Temperature T is fit on the *validation* split
(disjoint from the test split used for ECE) by grid-search minimizing
binary cross-entropy over log(T) ∈ [log 0.05, log 20], refined with a
local grid. `ECE_pre` and `ECE_post` are always computed from the *same*
trained model applied to the *same* test set — no model is retrained
between the two measurements, avoiding the before/after model-mismatch
failure mode.

**Pre-registered hypotheses and tests** (decided before running the
experiment; see `experiment.py` docstring, committed with this paper):

- **H1** (primary): `ECE_pre` is *positively* correlated with
  log2(width), per dataset, via Spearman ρ. Null: permutation test
  shuffling the width label among the 70 (width, ECE_pre) pairs actually
  observed, 20,000 permutations, two-sided.
- **H2** (primary): Temperature scaling reduces ECE — paired Wilcoxon
  signed-rank test, one-sided (`ECE_pre > ECE_post`), pooled per dataset
  (n=70 pairs).
- **H3** (secondary): `ECE_post` — not the pre/post *delta*, since
  correlating a delta with `ECE_pre` is mechanically confounded when the
  delta is arithmetically derived from `ECE_pre` — remains correlated
  with log2(width) after scaling, i.e. whether scaling equalizes or just
  shifts calibration across widths. Same permutation-null design as H1.

**Generalization bar** (pre-registered): a hypothesis is deemed to
generalize only if it holds in the same direction with p<0.05 in at
least 2 of the 3 datasets.

**Robustness check.** For H1, we additionally recompute the Spearman
correlation seven times, each time leaving out one width's data
entirely, to confirm the trend is not driven by a single extreme width
group (`robustness.py`).

**Power context.** With n=70 pairs, the paired Wilcoxon test's minimum
detectable rank-biserial effect at α=0.05 (one-sided) and 80% power is
approximately 0.30 (normal approximation). We report this alongside any
non-significant H2 result rather than treating "not significant" as
"no effect."

All code, the raw per-run results (`raw_results.json`, 210 rows), the
aggregated analysis (`analysis.json`), the robustness check output
(`robustness.json`), and the run log (`run_log.txt`) are included
alongside this paper. Total wall-clock time for all 210 trainings was
178.9 seconds on CPU.

## 3. Results

**H1 — rejected, in the opposite direction, in all three datasets.**
Contrary to the pre-registered positive-correlation hypothesis
(motivated by the large-model overconfidence literature), `ECE_pre`
*decreases* significantly with log-width in every dataset:

| Dataset | Spearman ρ (log2 width vs ECE_pre) | permutation p | Bonferroni (α=0.05/6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| two-moons | −0.523 | 0.0000 | significant |
| circles | −0.762 | 0.0000 | significant |
| breast cancer | −0.357 | 0.0030 | significant |

Mean ECE_pre falls monotonically (with minor non-monotonicity at width
64 on moons) from the smallest to largest width tested, e.g. on circles:
0.110 (width 2) → 0.064 (width 16) → 0.042 (width 128). This pattern
holds in all 3/3 datasets, exceeding the pre-registered 2/3
generalization bar for a *negative* relationship — the opposite of what
was pre-registered.

**Robustness.** Leave-one-width-out recomputation of the correlation
never flips sign in any of the 21 (3 datasets × 7 dropped widths) checks;
the correlation magnitude stays within a narrow band around the
full-sample estimate in every case (e.g. circles ranges from −0.70 to
−0.79 across all seven leave-one-out refits). The trend is not an
artifact of a single width group.

**H2 — temperature scaling significantly reduces ECE on 1 of 3
datasets.**

| Dataset | mean ECE_pre → post | Wilcoxon p (one-sided) | rank-biserial | vs. MDE (~0.30) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| two-moons | 0.062 → 0.060 | 0.194 | 0.057 | below MDE — underpowered null |
| circles | 0.070 → 0.055 | <0.0001 | 0.371 | above MDE — real effect |
| breast cancer | 0.0312 → 0.0311 | 0.861 | −0.114 | below MDE — underpowered null |

Mean fitted temperature also differed by dataset: circles had mean
T=0.78 (models were on average *underconfident*, so sharpening via T<1
helped substantially), while moons (T=1.18) and breast cancer (T=1.03)
were closer to neutral, leaving less room for a single scalar correction
to help. We do not claim temperature scaling "fails" on moons/breast
cancer — the observed effects are simply too small relative to this
study's power at n=70 to be distinguished from zero.

**H3 — secondary, partial generalization.** `ECE_post` remains
significantly negatively correlated with log-width on moons (ρ=−0.485,
p=0.0000) and circles (ρ=−0.429, p=0.0000), but not breast cancer
(ρ=−0.208, p=0.082). This meets the pre-registered 2/3 bar for
generalization in the negative direction: temperature scaling shifts ECE
down but does not eliminate the width-dependent trend on two of three
datasets.

**Accuracy.** As a sanity check, mean test accuracy across widths was
0.876 (moons), 0.881 (circles), 0.968 (breast cancer) — none showed a
strong width dependence (widths 2 and 128 differ by only a few points on
each dataset), so the ECE_pre trend is not simply tracking a large
accuracy gain at higher width; even at matched accuracy, wider networks
in this range are better calibrated.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

The result is best read as: **within the 2–128 hidden-unit range on
these three simple binary tasks, adding capacity relieves miscalibration
that looks like underfitting, rather than introducing the overconfidence
seen when scaling already-large modern networks.** This is not a
contradiction of Guo et al. (2017) — it is a different capacity regime.
It suggests the width–calibration relationship is non-monotonic across
the full capacity axis: harmful in the large-model regime (established
prior work), helpful in the very-small regime (this study), with the
turning point somewhere in between and outside our tested range (we
never observed ECE_pre begin rising again by width 128 on any dataset).
We do not claim to have located that turning point, and we explicitly
did not extend widths further or perform sequential/optional-stopping
testing to chase one; this remains future work.

**Limitations.** All three tasks are simple, low-dimensional or
small-n classification problems and all networks have a single hidden
layer; we do not know whether the effect holds for deeper small
networks, regression targets, or multi-class problems, and this is a
single-architecture-family study in the sense flagged as a generality
ceiling by prior work in this series. The two non-significant H2 results
are underpowered nulls (effect sizes below this study's own MDE), not
evidence that temperature scaling never helps at these widths — a larger
n would be needed to resolve them. Test/validation splits (n≈140–150 per
dataset) are small enough that per-run ECE estimates carry meaningful
sampling noise, which is exactly why we pooled across 10 seeds per
condition and used rank-based (permutation, Wilcoxon) tests rather than
point estimates.

## 5. Conclusion

In tiny MLPs (2–128 hidden units) on three binary classification
datasets, expected calibration error before post-hoc correction
decreases significantly and robustly with width — the opposite of the
overconfidence-with-capacity pattern documented for large modern
networks. Temperature scaling reduces this gap further on one of three
datasets and leaves an underpowered-but-plausible residual effect on the
other two. We release full code, fixed seeds, raw per-run results, and
robustness checks so every number here can be regenerated exactly.
