# Calibration Improves, Not Worsens, With Width in the Very-Small-Network Regime

## Abstract

Guo et al. (2017) established that increasing capacity in *modern* (large,
overparameterized) neural networks tends to worsen calibration even as
accuracy improves. We ask whether the same relationship holds at the
opposite end of the capacity spectrum — tiny multilayer perceptrons with
2 to 128 hidden units — where networks are plausibly underfitting rather
than overfitting. We pre-registered the hypothesis that expected
calibration error (ECE) before post-hoc temperature scaling would
increase with width, matching the large-model literature, and tested it
with a permutation null across three binary classification datasets (two
synthetic, one real: two-moons, circles, and Wisconsin breast cancer), 7
widths, and 10 fixed seeds per condition (n=70 per dataset). The
pre-registered hypothesis was **rejected in the opposite direction**: ECE
before scaling *decreases* significantly with log-width in all three
datasets (Spearman ρ = −0.52, −0.76, −0.36; permutation p ≤ 0.003 in
every case, surviving Bonferroni correction). Because test accuracy also
rises substantially with width on two of three datasets (e.g. circles:
0.64 → 0.95), we checked whether the ECE-width trend is just a proxy for
this accuracy gain: a partial rank correlation of ECE_pre with log-width,
controlling for test accuracy, remains significant and in the same
direction in all three datasets (partial ρ = −0.47, −0.74, −0.32, all
p<0.008), so the width effect is not fully explained by accuracy. Mean
training-set ECE also falls monotonically with width in all three
datasets, consistent with the underfitting-relief mechanism we propose.
Convergence issues (sklearn's `max_iter` warning) affected only 2 of 210
runs, both at small-to-mid widths on circles, ruling out a
non-convergence confound at the wide end of the range. Temperature
scaling significantly reduced ECE on only one of three datasets (circles,
p<0.0001); on the other two, the effect sizes (rank-biserial 0.06 and
−0.11) fall below the study's own minimum detectable effect (~0.30 at
n=70, 80% power), so these should be read as underpowered nulls. We
report full pre-registration, seeds, and raw/aggregated data alongside
this paper.

## 1. Introduction

Calibration — the property that a model's predicted confidence matches
its empirical accuracy — is central to using neural network outputs as
decision-relevant probabilities. The dominant empirical narrative,
established by Guo et al. (2017), is that increasing depth, width, and
training duration in modern deep networks *worsens* calibration relative
to older, smaller architectures, even while classification accuracy
improves. This has motivated a large post-hoc calibration literature
(temperature scaling, Platt scaling, isotonic regression) and further
studies of *why* capacity correlates with overconfidence.

Almost all of this literature studies capacity increases in an already
large regime — ResNets, Vision Transformers, and similar — where models
are past the point of underfitting their training data. Much less is
known about the *very small* end of the capacity axis: MLPs with a
handful of hidden units, of the kind used in low-resource, embedded, or
tabular settings, where a network may still be underfitting. If
overconfidence in large models arises partly from a model's ability to
memorize and produce sharp, high-confidence decision boundaries, a
severely underparameterized network might show the opposite pattern:
poor calibration driven by *underfitting*, which added capacity could
relieve rather than worsen, at least until some capacity threshold is
crossed.

**Related work.** Guo et al. (2017) is the foundational reference
establishing depth/width/weight-decay/batch-normalization as
calibration-relevant factors in large networks, and temperature scaling
as a simple, strong post-hoc fix — both of which we adopt directly. Work
on the "calibration generalization gap" and double-descent in
uncertainty quantification studies how ECE behaves across under- and
over-parameterized regimes for larger models, generally finding a
roughly monotonic ECE–error relationship in the overparameterized
regime. We are not aware of prior work that specifically isolates the
underfitting-driven end of the width axis for small MLPs under this kind
of pre-registered design; the closest related framing (calibration under
underfitting/regularization choices, e.g. as discussed in general
calibration surveys) treats it qualitatively rather than with a
directional, falsifiable test of the kind we run here. Follow-on
post-hoc calibration methods (parameterized temperature scaling,
auxiliary calibration networks) extend temperature scaling's
expressivity but are not needed at the scale we study here.

**Contribution.** We run a small, fully pre-registered, multi-dataset
study of the width–calibration relationship for MLPs in the 2–128 hidden
unit range: fixed integer seeding, a null hypothesis and permutation test
decided before looking at the data, a leave-one-width-out robustness
check, a partial-correlation check ruling out accuracy as a full
confound, a training-set diagnostic of the underfitting mechanism, an
explicit accounting of optimizer non-convergence, honest reporting of
non-significant results with their minimum detectable effect, and full
release of code, raw per-run results, and analysis outputs.

## 2. Method

**Datasets.** Three binary classification tasks: (1) `two-moons` (2D,
synthetic, noise=0.30, n=600), (2) `circles` (2D, synthetic, noise=0.15,
factor=0.5, n=600), and (3) Wisconsin breast cancer (30D, real, n=569).
Each is split 50/25/25 into train/validation/test, stratified by label;
features standardized using train-set statistics.

**Models.** Single-hidden-layer MLPs (`sklearn.neural_network.MLPClassifier`,
ReLU, Adam, L2 penalty 1e-4, max_iter=2000) with hidden widths
**W ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128}**. For each (dataset, width) we train
**10 replicates** with fixed integer seeds `seed = dataset_idx*100000 +
width_idx*1000 + rep_idx` — an explicit index-based mapping, not a hash,
so reruns are bit-identical. This gives 3 × 7 × 10 = **210 trained
models**.

**Calibration measurement.** We compute 15-bin ECE on the held-out test
set from each model's P(y=1), before (`ECE_pre`) and after (`ECE_post`)
temperature scaling. Temperature T is fit on the *validation* split
(disjoint from the test split) by grid search minimizing binary
cross-entropy over log(T) ∈ [log 0.05, log 20]. `ECE_pre`/`ECE_post` are
always computed from the *same* trained model on the *same* test set.

**Pre-registered hypotheses** (see `experiment.py` docstring):
- **H1**: `ECE_pre` positively correlated with log2(width) per dataset
  (Spearman ρ); permutation null shuffling width labels, 20,000
  permutations, two-sided.
- **H2**: Temperature scaling reduces ECE — paired one-sided Wilcoxon
  (`ECE_pre > ECE_post`), pooled per dataset (n=70).
- **H3** (secondary): `ECE_post` (not the pre/post delta, to avoid a
  mechanical confound) remains correlated with log2(width) after
  scaling. Same permutation-null design as H1.

**Generalization bar** (pre-registered): a hypothesis generalizes only if
it holds in the same direction with p<0.05 in ≥2 of 3 datasets.

**Robustness and mechanism checks (one pre-registered, three added in
response to review).** (a) *Leave-one-width-out* (pre-registered): recompute
H1's Spearman correlation seven times, each time dropping one width
entirely. (b) *Accuracy-confound check*: since test accuracy itself
varies with width, we compute the partial Spearman correlation of
`ECE_pre` with log2(width), controlling for test accuracy via rank
residuals, to test whether the width effect survives independent of the
well-known accuracy–ECE relationship. (c) *Underfitting-mechanism
check*: we additionally record training-set accuracy and training-set
ECE (same 15-bin estimator, evaluated on the training split) by width,
as a direct test of whether wider networks fit the training distribution
better and are simultaneously better calibrated on it — the pattern
predicted by the underfitting-relief story. (d) *Convergence check*: we
record, for every one of the 210 runs, whether sklearn's Adam solver hit
`max_iter` without declaring convergence, and tabulate this by width, to
rule out differential optimizer non-convergence as a confound for the
ECE_pre-vs-width trend. All four checks use the same fixed seeds as the
main experiment (`followup.py`, released with this paper).

**Power context.** At n=70 pairs, the paired Wilcoxon test's minimum
detectable rank-biserial effect at α=0.05 (one-sided), 80% power, is
≈0.30 (normal approximation).

All code, raw per-run results, aggregated analysis, robustness/follow-up
outputs, and run logs are released alongside this paper. Total wall-clock
time for the 210 main-experiment trainings was 178.9s CPU; the follow-up
diagnostics retrain the same 210 models (identical seeds) in a separate
script to add training-set and convergence instrumentation, at
comparable cost.

## 3. Results

**H1 — rejected, in the opposite direction, in all three datasets.**
`ECE_pre` *decreases* significantly with log-width in every dataset:

| Dataset | Spearman ρ (log2 width vs ECE_pre) | permutation p | Bonferroni (α=0.05/6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| two-moons | −0.523 | 0.0000 | significant |
| circles | −0.762 | 0.0000 | significant |
| breast cancer | −0.357 | 0.0030 | significant |

**Robustness.** Leave-one-width-out recomputation never flips sign in any
of the 21 checks (e.g. circles ranges from −0.70 to −0.79 across all
seven leave-one-out refits).

**Is this just an accuracy effect?** Test accuracy rises substantially
with width on two datasets — moons 0.808→0.925 (widths 2→128), circles
0.641→0.955 — and only mildly on breast cancer (0.966→0.971, not
significantly correlated with width, ρ=0.18, p=0.14). Since ECE is
mechanically related to accuracy, this raises the concern that the
ECE-width trend is a proxy for an accuracy-width trend rather than an
independent calibration effect. Controlling for test accuracy via
partial rank correlation, the width–ECE_pre relationship survives in all
three datasets: partial ρ = −0.471 (moons, p<0.0001), −0.737 (circles,
p<0.0001), −0.316 (breast cancer, p=0.008). The effect is attenuated on
moons and breast cancer relative to the raw correlation but remains
significant everywhere, indicating capacity contributes to calibration
beyond what accuracy alone would predict.

**Underfitting-mechanism check.** Training-set ECE falls monotonically
with width in all three datasets (e.g. circles: 0.096 at width 2 → 0.030
at width 128; moons: 0.069 → 0.033; breast cancer: 0.035 → 0.010), in
step with training accuracy rising (circles: 0.654 → 0.966; breast
cancer: 0.987 → 1.000). This is consistent with, though not conclusive
proof of, the underfitting-relief story: narrow networks fit the
training distribution itself poorly and are correspondingly miscalibrated
even on data they were trained on, and both training fit and calibration
improve together as width increases.

**Convergence check.** Of 210 runs, 208 converged before `max_iter`; the
2 exceptions were both on circles, at widths 4 and 8 (not the widest
settings), with no non-convergence at all on moons, breast cancer, or
any width ≥16. Non-convergence is therefore rare, not concentrated at the
wide end of the range, and cannot explain the monotonic ECE_pre decrease
observed out to width 128.

**H2 — temperature scaling significantly reduces ECE on 1 of 3
datasets.**

| Dataset | mean ECE_pre → post | Wilcoxon p (one-sided) | rank-biserial | vs. MDE (~0.30) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| two-moons | 0.062 → 0.060 | 0.194 | 0.057 | below MDE — underpowered null |
| circles | 0.070 → 0.055 | <0.0001 | 0.371 | above MDE — real effect |
| breast cancer | 0.0312 → 0.0311 | 0.861 | −0.114 | below MDE — underpowered null |

Mean fitted temperature also differed by dataset: circles had mean
T=0.78 (models were on average underconfident), while moons (T=1.18) and
breast cancer (T=1.03) were closer to neutral. We do not claim
temperature scaling "fails" on moons/breast cancer — observed effects are
too small relative to this study's power at n=70 to distinguish from
zero. We did not test alternative post-hoc correctors (Platt scaling,
isotonic regression); a more flexible corrector could plausibly behave
differently in this underfitting regime, and this is left to future work.

**H3 — secondary, partial generalization.** `ECE_post` remains
significantly negatively correlated with log-width on moons (ρ=−0.485,
p=0.0000) and circles (ρ=−0.429, p=0.0000), but not breast cancer
(ρ=−0.208, p=0.082) — meeting the pre-registered 2/3 bar.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

Within the 2–128 hidden-unit range on these three simple binary tasks,
adding capacity relieves miscalibration that looks like underfitting,
rather than introducing the overconfidence seen when scaling
already-large modern networks. This is not a contradiction of Guo et al.
(2017) — it is a different capacity regime, and the accuracy-controlled
partial correlation and training-set ECE trend give converging (though
not definitive) support to the underfitting-relief reading over a purely
accuracy-driven artifact. It suggests the width–calibration relationship
is non-monotonic across the full capacity axis: harmful in the
large-model regime (established prior work), helpful in the very-small
regime (this study), with an unlocated turning point in between — we
have no direct evidence of where it lies and did not extend widths or
run sequential tests to chase it.

**Limitations.** This remains a narrow, single-architecture-family
study: one hidden layer, one library/solver (sklearn MLPClassifier,
Adam), three low-dimensional-or-small-n binary classification datasets,
and one post-hoc calibration method (temperature scaling only, despite
citing Platt/isotonic alternatives). The title's "very-small-network
regime" should be read as this specific corner of the design space, not
a general claim about small networks, deeper small architectures,
regression targets, or multi-class problems. The accuracy-controlled
partial correlation reduces but does not eliminate concern about
confounding — it is a rank-based control for one covariate, not a causal
identification of a width-specific mechanism, and the training-ECE
diagnostic is correlational, not an intervention on underfitting per se.
The two non-significant H2 results remain underpowered nulls (effect
sizes below this study's own MDE), not evidence that temperature scaling
never helps at these widths. Test/validation splits (n≈140–150 per
dataset) are small enough that per-run ECE estimates carry sampling
noise, mitigated by pooling across 10 seeds and using rank-based tests.

## 5. Conclusion

In tiny MLPs (2–128 hidden units) on three binary classification
datasets, expected calibration error before post-hoc correction
decreases significantly and robustly with width — opposite to the
overconfidence-with-capacity pattern in large modern networks. This
holds even after controlling for the accuracy gains that also accompany
width, and is accompanied by a matching decrease in training-set ECE
consistent with an underfitting-relief mechanism; optimizer
non-convergence is rare and not concentrated at wide networks, ruling
out that confound. Temperature scaling reduces the gap further on one of
three datasets and leaves an underpowered-but-plausible residual effect
on the other two. We release full code, fixed seeds, raw per-run
results, and all robustness/mechanism checks so every number here can be
regenerated exactly.
