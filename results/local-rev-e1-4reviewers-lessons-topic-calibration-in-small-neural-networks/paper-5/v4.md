# Calibration in Small Neural Networks: Temperature Scaling Dominates Label Smoothing (at ε=0.1), But Can Backfire Near Chance

## Abstract

The literature on neural network calibration is built almost entirely on large, overparameterized models, where the dominant finding is that bigger models are more overconfident and that temperature scaling (TS) and label smoothing (LS) both help. We ask whether this picture holds when "small" means genuinely small — single-hidden-layer MLPs with widths from 2 to 256 — rather than "small by modern deep-learning standards." Across two datasets (sklearn digits, a synthetic 4-class blobs task with label noise), 8 widths, 2 training conditions (plain cross-entropy vs. label smoothing, ε=0.1), and 15 seeds per cell (480 trained models total), we test two pre-registered hypotheses. First, that raw expected calibration error (ECE) increases with width, replicating the large-model literature: this does **not** hold cleanly at the raw correlation level (Spearman ρ from −0.14 to +0.30 across cells) because width and accuracy are confounded — controlling for accuracy, width has a robust *negative* partial correlation with raw ECE in all four cells (partial ρ −0.20 to −0.51, all p<0.03), surviving the removal of the two lowest widths (partial ρ −0.57 to −0.76, all p<1e-4): at matched accuracy, wider is *better* calibrated, opposite the naive extrapolation. Second, that after TS, label smoothing at ε=0.1 confers no additional benefit over plain training: we find no evidence against this null (paired Wilcoxon, n=240, p=0.86), but the null is **ε-specific** — at ε=0.3 on digits (same seeds/pairing), LS *does* beat baseline post-TS (n=120, mean ECE reduction 0.033, p=0.014). Third, TS itself is unreliable for very narrow, near-chance-accuracy networks: it *increases* ECE in 31% of all 480 runs (42% among accuracy<0.4 runs), and a validation-size ablation shows this is not fixed by 6x more validation data at width 2. It is also **not a generic "near chance" phenomenon**: at blobs/width=2 (accuracy 0.31) TS is stable and helpful (14/15 runs improve), while at digits/width=4, matched to the same absolute accuracy, TS is unstable (8/15 worsen) — dataset-specific, not accuracy-specific. Finally, our cross-library spot check (scikit-learn's `MLPClassifier`) was re-run twice in response to review: first with `max_iter=3000` to eliminate a convergence-warning confound flagged in an earlier round, which reversed the main study's TS-helps direction (0.030→0.034, p=0.018); we then re-ran a third time with `max_iter=120`, matching the *epoch budget* rather than the convergence criterion of the main PyTorch study, and the original direction returns (0.066→0.046, p<0.0001, all 45 runs non-converged) — pinning the reversal on training regime (fixed-budget vs. converged), not on the sklearn backend itself. We release all code and raw per-run results, including two numerical corrections made in response to reviewer-verified discrepancies from the previous draft.

## 1. Introduction

Guo et al. (2017) showed that modern deep networks are frequently overconfident and that a single learned temperature parameter, fit post-hoc on a validation set, is a remarkably effective fix. A large follow-up literature examines *why* miscalibration grows with capacity and proposes training-time alternatives such as label smoothing (Müller et al., 2019). Minderer et al. (2021) revisited these findings for newer architectures, largely confirming that capacity growth worsens raw calibration.

**Gap.** Essentially all of this literature studies networks with widths in the hundreds to thousands. Genuinely small networks — the kind used in resource-constrained or tabular-data settings, where a hidden layer of width 8–64 is a real design point — are comparatively unstudied for calibration. It is not obvious that trends from the overparameterized regime extrapolate down to networks this narrow, where the model may not reliably beat chance at the low end of the width sweep, confounding width with accuracy in a way that does not arise for already-well-trained large models.

**This paper.** We run a controlled width sweep (2–256 hidden units) crossed with training condition on two datasets, 15 seeds per cell, and ask three questions: (1) does raw ECE increase with width, or is any such trend an accuracy artifact; (2) once TS is applied, does LS still help, and is that answer sensitive to the smoothing strength; (3) is TS itself reliable across the width range, including near chance, and is "near chance" itself well-defined across datasets. H1 and H2 (at ε=0.1) are pre-registered before running the analysis (fixed in `analyze.py`'s docstring). Everything else — the accuracy-confound partial correlation and its robustness, the ε=0.3 sensitivity check, the validation-size ablation, the second near-chance dataset/width comparison, and the cross-library checks — are exploratory, directly-verified follow-ups, several motivated by this paper's own review process, and are reported as such, not as confirmatory tests.

### Related work
- **Guo et al. (2017)**: capacity correlates with overconfidence; TS is a strong post-hoc fix. Our width range (2–256) sits below essentially all settings studied there.
- **Müller et al. (2019)**: LS improves calibration in large models via tighter logit clusters. We ask whether this survives a post-hoc fix, and at what smoothing strength, at much smaller capacity.
- **Minderer et al. (2021)**: revisits calibration trends for newer architectures, again overparameterized.

## 2. Method

**Datasets.** (a) `digits` (sklearn): 1797 samples, 64 features, 10 classes. (b) `blobs`: synthetic 4-class task (`make_classification`, 1500 samples, 12 features, 5% label flip noise). 60/20/20 train/val/test, stratified, split controlled by the run's seed.

**Models.** Single-hidden-layer MLPs (Linear→ReLU→Linear), widths ∈ {2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256}, Adam (lr=0.001), 120 full-batch epochs. Two main conditions: plain cross-entropy ("baseline") and label-smoothed cross-entropy (ε=0.1, "label_smooth"); a third, ε=0.3, is used only for the targeted sensitivity check on digits.

**Seeding.** Every (dataset, condition, width, seed-index) cell gets a seed from fixed integer arithmetic (no `hash()`), so reruns are bit-identical. 15 seeds/cell × 2 datasets × 2 main conditions × 8 widths = 480 models.

**Calibration metric.** ECE with 15 equal-width bins, plus NLL and accuracy, before ("raw") and after ("ts") temperature scaling (T fit by minimizing validation NLL via LBFGS, per trained model).

**Pre-registered tests** (fixed before inspecting `raw_results.json`):
- **H1** (one-sided): raw ECE increases with width. Spearman correlation, pooled over seeds, per (dataset × condition) cell (n=120 each).
- **H2** (two-sided, ε=0.1): after TS, LS makes no difference to ECE vs. baseline. Paired Wilcoxon on (ECE_ts,baseline − ECE_ts,label_smooth), paired by (dataset, width, seed_idx). We report rank-biserial effect size, a bootstrap 95% CI, and a simulation-based power analysis.

**Follow-up checks (post-hoc, directly verified, not confirmatory).**
1. *Width–accuracy confound.* Partial Spearman correlation of width vs. raw ECE controlling for accuracy (rank-residual method), per cell — full width range and after dropping widths 2 and 4.
2. *Label-smoothing strength.* Retrain digits/label_smooth at ε=0.3 (same seeds/widths/splits), paired-compare its post-TS ECE against digits/baseline post-TS ECE.
3. *Mechanism check for TS instability near chance.* Fixed 60% training split, varying validation share of the remaining 40% (n_val ≈ 89, 179, 359, 539) at digits width 2 (near-chance) and width 16 (good accuracy), 20 seeds each, tracking SD of fitted temperature and post-TS ECE vs. validation size.
4. *Does "near chance" generalize across datasets?* Compare TS behavior at digits/width=4 and blobs/width=2, two cells with almost identical mean test accuracy (≈0.31).

**Cross-library spot checks.** scikit-learn's `MLPClassifier` (digits, widths {2,32,256}, disjoint seeds, n=15/width) checks whether TS-reduces-ECE replicates directionally on a different backend/optimizer. Three passes, each triggered by review: (i) `max_iter=400` hit 10/15 `ConvergenceWarning`s and was flagged as a confound; (ii) `max_iter=3000` removed all convergence warnings but reversed the direction of the effect, prompting the question of whether the reversal reflects backend differences or the change in training regime; (iii) `max_iter=120`, matching the main study's *epoch budget* instead of its convergence criterion, to isolate the two.

All code and raw per-run JSON are included (`run_experiment.py`, `analyze.py`, `accuracy_control_check.py`, `val_size_ablation.py`, `per_seed_inverse_u_check.py`, `spotcheck_sklearn.py`, `spotcheck_sklearn_matched.py`, `eps_sensitivity_check.py`, plus `*_results.json` outputs).

## 3. Results

**H1 — the raw width–ECE trend does not replicate cleanly, and is entangled with accuracy.** Spearman ρ (n=120/cell): blobs/baseline ρ=−0.14 (p=0.94), blobs/label_smooth ρ=+0.30 (p=0.0004), digits/baseline ρ=0.00 (p=0.49), digits/label_smooth ρ=+0.12 (p=0.09). Only 1 of 4 cells is significant. On digits/baseline, raw ECE rises from width 2 (0.081) to a peak at width 16 (0.491, accuracy 0.75), then falls through widths 32–256 (0.464→0.093, accuracy 0.88→0.95) — an inverse-U that holds in 15/15 individual seeds, not just on average.

Controlling for accuracy, the partial Spearman correlation of width vs. raw ECE is **negative and significant in all four cells** (blobs/baseline −0.51, p=4e-9; blobs/label_smooth −0.46, p=1e-7; digits/baseline −0.21, p=0.02; digits/label_smooth −0.20, p=0.03). This is not an artifact of the noisiest, most extreme low-accuracy widths: dropping widths 2 and 4 only *strengthens* the effect (blobs/baseline −0.76, blobs/label_smooth −0.75, digits/baseline −0.57, digits/label_smooth −0.57, all p<1e-4). At matched accuracy, wider networks in this range are *better*, not worse, calibrated — the raw width→ECE relationship in this regime is mostly an accuracy→ECE relationship, and the residual, accuracy-controlled width effect favors wider networks.

**H2 — at ε=0.1, label smoothing adds nothing once TS is applied, but this is specific to ε=0.1.** Paired Wilcoxon on post-TS ECE (n=240): mean diff (baseline−LS) = −0.0020, p=0.86, rank-biserial = −0.014 — a coin flip (119/240 vs. 121/240). Power analysis (matched to observed SD 0.214) shows 80% power to detect a mean difference of 0.040 (≈0.19 SD), so this null is not underpowered. Retraining digits/label_smooth at **ε=0.3** and pairing against the same baseline (n=120, same seeds/splits) finds a real difference: mean post-TS ECE 0.141 (baseline) vs. 0.109 (ε=0.3), a reduction of 0.033, Wilcoxon p=0.014. So the ε=0.1 null is a fact about that specific hyperparameter, not about label smoothing as a method.

**Descriptive: TS reduces ECE overall, but not uniformly.** Baseline: 0.187→0.174 (p=0.0017); label_smooth (ε=0.1): 0.216→0.176 (p=0.0014).

**Exploratory: TS is unreliable near chance — but "near chance" is not a universal, accuracy-only threshold.** TS increases ECE in 149/480 runs (31%), rising to 42/100 (42%) among accuracy<0.4 runs. At digits width 2, baseline condition (mean accuracy 0.19, n=15), fitted temperature has mean 0.78, SD 1.04; post-TS ECE has mean 0.390, SD 0.361; raw ECE for this same n=15 baseline-only slice is comparatively stable at mean 0.081, SD 0.048. (A prior draft reported the raw-ECE figure for this cell pooled across both baseline and label-smoothing seeds, 0.076/0.041 at n=30, which two reviewers independently caught as inconsistent with the other three baseline-only statistics in the same sentence; the figure above is now recomputed on the matching n=15 baseline-only subset.) The validation-size ablation refutes a "just enlarge validation set" fix: at width 2, temperature SD across 6x more validation data (n_val 89→539) is 0.47, 0.56, 0.57, then 1.03 — it does not shrink — while at width 16 (accuracy ≈0.78) temperature SD is low and stable at every level (0.07–0.09).

But the instability is not simply a function of low accuracy in general. At blobs/width=2 (accuracy 0.31, near the 4-class chance level of 0.25), TS is stable and helpful: post-TS ECE mean 0.047 vs. raw ECE mean 0.087, only 1/15 runs worsen. At digits/width=4, matched almost exactly to that same accuracy (0.31), TS is unstable: post-TS ECE mean 0.394, 8/15 runs worsen. Two cells with essentially identical test accuracy show opposite TS behavior, so accuracy alone does not determine when TS is trustworthy; something about digits specifically (higher dimensionality, 10-way rather than 4-way structure, or ECE-bin sparsity at low accuracy) plausibly interacts with the failure mode. We do not yet have a controlled experiment isolating class count from dimensionality (e.g. a 10-class blobs variant at matched accuracy) and leave this as an open mechanistic question rather than resolve it with a single dataset pair.

**Cross-library spot checks, resolved.** The first check (`max_iter=400`, n=15) reproduced the main study's direction (0.062→0.035, p=0.095) but 10/15 runs hit `ConvergenceWarning`, flagged independently by multiple reviewers. Raising `max_iter` to 3000 (n=45) eliminated all convergence warnings but reversed the finding: raw ECE 0.030→0.034 post-TS, p=0.018. This converged run also reaches far higher accuracy at width 2 (mean 0.639, not epoch-budget-matched to our fixed-120-epoch PyTorch models, which score 0.19 at the same width) — so it could not distinguish a real backend difference from a training-regime difference. To separate the two, we re-ran a third pass with `max_iter=120`, matching PyTorch's epoch budget rather than its convergence criterion: all 45 runs now hit convergence warnings (as expected, since 120 iterations under-trains this backend too), width-2 accuracy drops to 0.363 (much closer to the PyTorch value, though not identical), and the original TS-helps direction returns decisively: raw ECE 0.066→0.046 post-TS, Wilcoxon p<0.0001. This localizes the earlier reversal to the *training regime* (fixed, modest budget vs. run-to-convergence), not to the sklearn backend or optimizer — evidence that our main finding is a property of fixed-budget-trained small networks specifically, and that the fully-converged spot check, not the fixed-budget one, is the outlier relative to the main study's setting.

## 4. Conclusion

In the genuinely small-network regime (widths 2–256), the standard calibration narrative only partially transfers, and several findings that looked clean on a first pass turn out to have real boundary conditions once probed. The raw "capacity increases miscalibration" trend does not replicate and is confounded with accuracy; controlling for accuracy — and even after removing the lowest, noisiest widths — the residual width effect on raw ECE is negative in every cell, wider is better calibrated at matched accuracy. Label smoothing at ε=0.1 adds nothing detectable beyond TS, but a stronger value (ε=0.3) does help, so this null does not extend to label smoothing in general. TS itself measurably backfires for near-chance networks, and enlarging the validation set does not fix it — but "near chance" is not a portable, accuracy-only threshold: two cells at matched accuracy (digits/width=4, blobs/width=2) show opposite TS reliability, so accuracy is not sufficient to predict when TS is trustworthy. Finally, a three-way cross-library check clarifies rather than muddies external validity: the reversal seen under full convergence is attributable to the training regime, not the backend, once epoch budget is matched — restoring confidence that the core TS-reduces-ECE finding is a property of fixed-budget small-network training rather than an artifact of our particular PyTorch implementation.

**Limitations.** One architecture family (single-hidden-layer ReLU MLPs). Both datasets are small (≤1800 samples). The ε=0.1 vs. ε=0.3 comparison is limited to one dataset (digits) and one width range; we do not have a full ε sweep, so the crossover point between "no benefit" and "real benefit" is unknown. The dataset-specificity of the near-chance TS failure (digits but not blobs, at matched accuracy) is documented but rests on a single matched pair rather than a systematic class-count sweep, and remains mechanistically unexplained. Other calibration methods (MMCE, Dirichlet calibration, focal loss) remain outside this paper's scope.
