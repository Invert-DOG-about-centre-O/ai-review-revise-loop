# Calibration in Small Neural Networks: Temperature Scaling Dominates Label Smoothing, But Can Backfire Near Chance

## Abstract

The literature on neural network calibration is built almost entirely on large, overparameterized models, where the dominant finding is that bigger models are more overconfident and that temperature scaling (TS) and label smoothing (LS) both help. We ask whether this picture holds when "small" means genuinely small — single-hidden-layer MLPs with widths from 2 to 256 — rather than "small by modern deep-learning standards." Across two datasets (sklearn digits, a synthetic 4-class blobs task with label noise), 8 widths, 2 training conditions (plain cross-entropy vs. label smoothing, ε=0.1), and 15 seeds per cell (480 trained models total), we test two pre-registered hypotheses. First, that expected calibration error (ECE) before any post-hoc correction increases with width, replicating the large-model literature: this does **not** hold cleanly in our width range at the raw correlation level (Spearman ρ ranges from −0.14, n.s., to +0.30, p<0.001, across dataset/condition cells), because width and achieved accuracy are confounded — a width-vs-accuracy-matched check we added shows that once accuracy is partialled out, width has a robust *negative* partial correlation with raw ECE in all four cells (partial ρ from −0.20 to −0.51, all p<0.03): at matched accuracy, wider is *better* calibrated, the opposite of the naive extrapolation. Second, that after post-hoc temperature scaling, label smoothing confers no additional calibration benefit over plain training: we find no evidence against this null (paired Wilcoxon, n=240 pairs, p=0.86, rank-biserial=−0.01), and the test is well powered (80% power to detect a mean ECE difference of 0.040, i.e. ~0.19 SD). A third, unplanned but directly checked finding is that temperature scaling itself is unreliable for very narrow, near-chance-accuracy networks: it *increases* ECE in 31% of all 480 runs overall, rising to 42% among the 100 runs with test accuracy below 0.4. A targeted follow-up ablation (varying validation-set size 6x at fixed accuracy) shows this instability is **not** primarily a small-validation-set artifact, as we originally speculated — enlarging the validation set at width 2 (near-chance accuracy) does not shrink the temperature-fit SD, while at width 16 (good accuracy) the fit is stable at every validation size tested. The instability tracks accuracy, not validation-set size. We replicate the core TS-reduces-ECE effect directionally in a small cross-library spot check (scikit-learn's MLPClassifier, n=15), though that check is underpowered (p=0.09) and is disclosed as such. We release all code and raw per-run results.

## 1. Introduction

Guo et al. (2017) showed that modern deep networks are frequently overconfident and that a single learned temperature parameter, fit post-hoc on a validation set, is a remarkably effective and simple fix. A large follow-up literature has examined *why* miscalibration grows with capacity (depth, width, weight decay, batch normalization) and has proposed training-time alternatives such as label smoothing (Müller et al., 2019) as complements or substitutes for post-hoc scaling. Minderer et al. (2021) revisited these findings for more recent architectures, largely confirming that capacity growth is associated with worse raw calibration.

**Gap.** Essentially all of this literature studies networks with widths in the hundreds to thousands. Genuinely small networks — the kind used in resource-constrained or tabular-data settings where a hidden layer of width 8–64 is a real design point — are comparatively unstudied for calibration behavior. It is not obvious that trends established in the overparameterized regime extrapolate down to networks this narrow, where the model may not reliably beat chance at the low end of the width sweep, and where width and achieved accuracy are consequently confounded in a way they are not for already-well-trained large models.

**This paper.** We run a controlled width sweep (2–256 hidden units) crossed with training condition on two datasets, 15 seeds per cell, and ask three questions: (1) does raw ECE increase with width in this regime, or is any such trend actually an accuracy artifact; (2) once TS is applied, does LS still help; (3) is TS itself reliable across the width range, including near chance. We pre-registered a null hypothesis and test for each of (1) and (2) before running the analysis code (fixed in `analyze.py`'s docstring). Question (3), and the two follow-up checks it motivated (accuracy-controlled correlation, validation-size ablation), emerged from inspecting the results table and are reported as exploratory, directly-verified findings, not confirmatory tests.

### Related work
- **Guo et al. (2017)**: capacity correlates with overconfidence; TS is a strong post-hoc fix. Our width range (2–256) sits below essentially all settings studied there.
- **Müller et al. (2019)**: LS improves calibration in large models via tighter logit clusters. We ask whether this survives a post-hoc fix, at much smaller capacity.
- **Minderer et al. (2021)**: revisits calibration trends for newer architectures, again overparameterized.
- **Double-descent / uncertainty literature** motivates why we did not assume a monotonic width→ECE trend a priori.

## 2. Method

**Datasets.** (a) `digits` (sklearn): 1797 samples, 64 features, 10 classes. (b) `blobs`: synthetic 4-class task (`make_classification`, 1500 samples, 12 features, 5% label flip noise). 60/20/20 train/val/test, stratified, split controlled by the run's seed.

**Models.** Single-hidden-layer MLPs (Linear→ReLU→Linear), widths ∈ {2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256}, Adam (lr=0.001), 120 full-batch epochs. Two conditions: plain cross-entropy ("baseline") and label-smoothed cross-entropy (ε=0.1, "label_smooth").

**Seeding.** Every (dataset, condition, width, seed-index) cell gets a seed from fixed integer arithmetic (no `hash()`), so reruns are bit-identical. 15 seeds/cell × 2 datasets × 2 conditions × 8 widths = 480 models.

**Calibration metric.** ECE with 15 equal-width bins, plus NLL and accuracy, before ("raw") and after ("ts") temperature scaling (T fit by minimizing validation NLL via LBFGS, per trained model).

**Pre-registered tests** (fixed before inspecting `raw_results.json`):
- **H1** (one-sided): raw ECE increases with width. Spearman correlation, pooled over seeds, per (dataset × condition) cell (n=120 each), plus leave-one-width-out robustness.
- **H2** (two-sided): after TS, LS makes no difference to ECE vs. baseline. Paired Wilcoxon on (ECE_ts,baseline − ECE_ts,label_smooth), paired by (dataset, width, seed_idx) — valid because each pair shares an identical split. We report rank-biserial effect size, a bootstrap 95% CI, and a simulation-based power analysis.

**Follow-up checks (post-hoc, directly verified, not treated as confirmatory).** Motivated by all four independent reviews of an earlier draft raising the same two concerns:
1. *Width–accuracy confound for H1.* Width and test accuracy climb together in this sweep (e.g. digits accuracy rises from 0.19 at width 2 to 0.95 at width 256), so a raw width–ECE correlation partly reflects an accuracy effect. We compute the partial Spearman correlation of width vs. raw ECE controlling for accuracy (rank-residual method), per (dataset × condition) cell, and separately check whether the digits/baseline inverse-U (ECE rising to a peak at width 16, then falling) holds within each of the 15 individual seeds rather than only in the seed-averaged summary.
2. *Mechanism check for TS instability near chance.* We originally attributed the near-chance TS-instability finding partly to small validation sets (≤360 samples). To test this directly rather than merely assert it, we ran a targeted ablation: at digits width 2 (near-chance, ~0.19 test accuracy) and width 16 (good, ~0.78 accuracy), we hold the training split fixed at 60% and vary the validation share of the remaining 40% over four levels (n_val ≈ 89, 179, 359, 539), 20 seeds each (`val_size_ablation.py`), and track the SD of the fitted temperature and of post-TS ECE across seeds as a function of validation size.

**Cross-library spot check.** A small, disjoint set of models (digits, widths {2,32,256}, 5 seeds each, n=15) trained with scikit-learn's `MLPClassifier` (different backend/optimizer) checks whether TS-reduces-ECE replicates directionally. Reported with its own p-value, not treated as confirmatory.

All code and raw per-run JSON are included (`run_experiment.py`, `analyze.py`, `accuracy_control_check.py`, `val_size_ablation.py`, `per_seed_inverse_u_check.py`, `spotcheck_sklearn.py`, plus `*_results.json` outputs).

## 3. Results

**H1 — the raw width–ECE trend does not replicate cleanly, and is entangled with accuracy.** Spearman ρ (n=120/cell): blobs/baseline ρ=−0.14 (p=0.94, opposite direction), blobs/label_smooth ρ=+0.30 (p=0.0004), digits/baseline ρ=0.00 (p=0.49), digits/label_smooth ρ=+0.12 (p=0.09). Only 1 of 4 cells shows significance, and leave-one-width-out shows sign flips in two cells. On digits/baseline, raw ECE rises from width 2 (0.081) to a peak at width 16 (0.491, accuracy 0.75), then falls through widths 32–256 (0.464→0.093, accuracy 0.88→0.95) — an inverse-U, not a monotonic increase, and one that holds in **15/15 individual seeds** (each seed's own ECE at width 16 exceeds both its width-2 and width-256 values), so it is not an artifact of averaging out-of-phase seed trajectories.

Controlling for the width–accuracy confound directly: partial Spearman correlation of width vs. raw ECE given accuracy is **negative and significant in all four cells** (blobs/baseline −0.51, p=4e-9; blobs/label_smooth −0.46, p=1e-7; digits/baseline −0.21, p=0.02; digits/label_smooth −0.20, p=0.03), even though the *raw* (unconditioned) correlation was near zero or positive. In other words, at matched accuracy, wider networks in this range are *better*, not worse, calibrated — the confound was masking a real width effect, and that effect runs opposite to the large-model literature's extrapolation. We read this as evidence that in the small-width regime, most of what looks like a width→ECE relationship is really an accuracy→ECE relationship (near-chance predictions are close to uniform and hence artificially low-ECE, as in the digits width-2 case below), and the residual, accuracy-controlled width effect — where measurable — favors wider networks.

**H2 — label smoothing adds nothing once TS is applied, and the test is well powered.** Paired Wilcoxon on post-TS ECE (n=240): mean diff (baseline−LS) = −0.0020, median = −0.0005, bootstrap 95% CI [−0.029, 0.026], p=0.86, rank-biserial = −0.014. 119/240 pairs favor baseline, 121 favor LS — a coin flip. Power analysis (matched to observed SD 0.214) shows 80% power to detect a mean difference of 0.040 (≈0.19 SD), so this null is not an underpowered non-finding. Raw (pre-TS) ECE differs substantially by condition (0.187 vs. 0.216), collapsing to 0.174 vs. 0.176 after TS.

**Descriptive: TS reduces ECE overall, but not uniformly.** Baseline: 0.187→0.174 (p=0.0017); label_smooth: 0.216→0.176 (p=0.0014); LS sees the larger absolute reduction (0.040 vs. 0.013), consistent with TS erasing most of the raw-ECE gap.

**Exploratory: TS is unreliable near chance, and this is an accuracy effect, not a validation-size artifact.** TS increases ECE in 149/480 runs (31%), rising to 42/100 (42%) among accuracy<0.4 runs. At digits width 2 (mean accuracy 0.19, n=30), fitted temperature has mean 0.84, SD 1.14; post-TS ECE has mean 0.350, SD 0.346 (range 0.003–0.835) vs. a stable raw ECE (mean 0.076, SD 0.041). We had speculated this was partly attributable to the small validation split (≤360 samples). The targeted ablation refutes the "just enlarge validation set" fix: at width 2, temperature SD across 6x more validation data (n_val 89→539) is 0.47, 0.56, 0.57, then 1.03 — **it does not shrink, and if anything is noisiest at the largest validation size tested** — while accuracy stays flat near 0.15–0.20 throughout. At width 16 (accuracy ≈0.78–0.79 across all four validation sizes), temperature SD is low and stable at every level (0.073, 0.046, 0.085, 0.085), an order of magnitude tighter than width 2 regardless of validation size. **The instability tracks near-chance accuracy itself, not validation-set size** — more validation data does not rescue a temperature fit when the underlying logits carry little signal about correctness. This sharpens our original claim into a more specific, verified mechanism rather than a plausible-but-untested attribution.

**Cross-library spot check.** scikit-learn's `MLPClassifier` (n=15, digits only): raw ECE 0.062→0.035 after TS (p=0.095) — same direction, underpowered. 10/15 runs hit `ConvergenceWarning`; we did not increase `max_iter` to keep the check cheap, and flag this as a limitation of the spot check specifically (the main PyTorch study emitted zero runtime warnings across all 480 runs).

## 4. Conclusion

In the genuinely small-network regime (widths 2–256), the standard calibration narrative only partially transfers, and the picture is more precise than a first pass suggests. The raw "capacity increases miscalibration" trend does not replicate and is confounded with accuracy; once accuracy is controlled for, the residual width effect on raw ECE is negative in every cell tested — wider is better calibrated at matched accuracy, opposite to the naive extrapolation from large-model results. Label smoothing changes raw calibration but a well-powered paired test finds no detectable difference from plain training after TS. Most notably, TS itself measurably backfires for near-chance networks, and we now have direct evidence this is an accuracy phenomenon rather than a fixable small-validation-set artifact: enlarging the validation set 6x at width 2 does not stabilize the temperature fit, while the fit is already stable at every validation size we tested at width 16. Practically, this means practitioners cannot rescue TS near chance accuracy simply by collecting a larger calibration set — the model needs to have learned enough signal for the temperature fit to be well-posed at all.

**Limitations.** One architecture family (single-hidden-layer ReLU MLPs) and, for the main results, one implementation stack; the cross-library spot check points the same direction but remains underpowered (n=15, p=0.095) and was not expanded further within this study's scope. Both datasets are small (≤1800 samples); our validation-size ablation shows the TS-instability finding is not an artifact of *this* smallness specifically, but we have not tested whether much larger absolute datasets (at matched near-chance accuracy) behave differently. Label smoothing was tested only at ε=0.1; the H2 null (no benefit beyond TS) is established for this one value and not shown to hold across the smoothing-strength axis. Other calibration methods (MMCE, Dirichlet calibration, focal loss) are outside this paper's scope. We do not propose a fix for the TS near-chance failure mode beyond flagging that validation-set enlargement is not it; a stability-aware or shrinkage-regularized temperature fit is a natural next step.
