# Pre-Calibration ECE, Not Width, Predicts Whether Temperature Scaling Helps Small Networks

## Abstract

Guo et al. (2017) showed that large, modern deep networks tend to be overconfident and that temperature scaling reliably fixes this. Much less is known about the small end of the capacity spectrum. We pre-registered two questions in one-hidden-layer MLPs (widths 4–256) trained on sklearn digits (10 classes) and a synthetic nonlinear 3-class problem, 20 fixed-seed repeats per width per dataset (n=140/dataset): (1) does the post-hoc optimal temperature T* rise monotonically with width even in this small-capacity regime, and (2) does temperature scaling actually reduce expected calibration error (ECE)? T* rises significantly with width on both datasets, but digits' correlation is driven entirely by width 4 as an outlier (excl. width 4: ρ=−0.01, n.s.), while synthetic's is a genuine gradient (ρ≈0.94). Two exploratory datasets — breast_cancer and, new in this revision, wine (3-class, 178 samples) — both side with synthetic once width 4 is excluded (breast_cancer ρ=0.36, p=2.6×10⁻⁵; wine ρ=0.21, p=0.011), so the width–T* link now holds on 3 of 4 datasets, with digits the lone counterexample, rather than resting on a single "thin arbiter" as reviewers of v3 noted. On whether scaling helps: pre-calibration ECE correlates with the ECE reduction on all four datasets, but a permutation test against a mechanical null (reduction = pre-ECE − post-ECE necessarily shares variance with pre-ECE) shows this magnitude correlation does not exceed the null on any of the four (permutation p = 1.00, 0.85, 1.00, 1.00); we also verify, via a new simulation, that this diagnostic's own false-positive rate is close to nominal 5% when pre- and post-ECE are truly independent, so the null result is not itself an artifact. The defensible evidence for "well-calibrated models get hurt" instead comes from a binary harm-rate mechanism check, which we extend in this revision to directly decouple pre-ECE from width: holding width fixed at 16 and varying training length (not width) to induce a spread of pre-ECE, models in the low-pre-ECE half are made worse by scaling in 32.7% of bootstrap resamples versus 1.1% for the high-pre-ECE half (Mann–Whitney p=1.8×10⁻⁴; within-width Spearman(pre-ECE, harm-rate)=−0.82, p=3.0×10⁻⁸) — a result not subject to the magnitude-correlation artifact and not confounded with width, addressing the central concern raised independently by three of four reviewers. We release all code, raw results, and this revision's new diagnostics.

## 1. Introduction

Modern deep networks are frequently overconfident, and Guo et al. (2017) popularized temperature scaling — dividing logits by a single scalar T fit on a held-out set — as a cheap, effective fix, evaluated on large architectures. Small networks (tens to low-hundreds of hidden units), common in resource-constrained and tabular deployments, are comparatively understudied.

**Q1 — Does the capacity–overconfidence relationship extend into the small-width regime?** **Q2 — Does post-hoc temperature scaling reliably help small networks?**

### Related work

Guo et al. (2017) established the modern baseline. Follow-up work proposes adaptive/margin-aware recalibration variants, generally on large vision/language models; recent surveys note current large models sometimes trend toward *under*confidence. To our knowledge no prior study isolates the small-width end of the capacity axis with a pre-registered, multi-seed, multi-dataset design, nor checks whether an apparent pre-ECE-predicts-benefit relationship is itself a statistical artifact of how "benefit" is computed, nor decouples pre-ECE from width in a mechanism check.

### Contributions

1. A pre-registered study of width vs. T* (digits, synthetic), plus two exploratory datasets (breast_cancer, and wine, new in this revision) to adjudicate whether an apparent width effect is genuine — now resting on 3-of-4 datasets rather than a single tie-breaker.
2. A permutation-based confound diagnostic (prompted by two v2 reviewers) showing the pre-ECE-predicts-benefit magnitude correlation is largely mechanical on all four datasets, plus a new simulation (new in this revision) confirming the diagnostic itself has a well-controlled false-positive rate.
3. A mechanism check extended in this revision to hold width fixed and vary pre-ECE via training length instead of width — directly answering the "width-confounds-pre-ECE" objection raised independently by three of four v3 reviewers — and showing the harm-rate effect survives with width held constant.

## 2. Method

**Datasets.** (a) *digits*: sklearn's 1797-sample, 10-class digit dataset. (b) *synthetic*: 3000-sample, 3-class `make_classification` (20 features, flip_y=0.03). (c) *breast_cancer* (exploratory): sklearn's 569-sample, 2-class dataset. (d) *wine* (exploratory, new in this revision): sklearn's 178-sample, 3-class, 13-feature dataset, identical pipeline, added specifically to give the width–T* tie-break a second independent exploratory vote beyond breast_cancer.

**Model.** One-hidden-layer MLP (`sklearn.neural_network.MLPClassifier`, ReLU, Adam, weight decay 1e-4, 300 iterations unless noted), widths ∈ {4,8,16,32,64,128,256}. Features standardized on train-split statistics.

**Splits and seeding.** 60/20/20 train/calibration/test, stratified, explicit integer seeds from a fixed offset table. 20 seeds × 7 widths × 4 datasets (n=140/dataset for digits, synthetic, breast_cancer, wine).

**Calibration.** T* minimizes NLL on the calibration split (`scipy.optimize.minimize_scalar`, bounds [0.05,20]) using `log(softmax_probs)` as pseudo-logits. ECE uses 15 equal-width bins.

**Pre-registered confirmatory analyses (digits, synthetic only).** H1: one-sided Spearman(log₂ width, T*), Bonferroni α=0.025. H2: one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank, pre-ECE > post-ECE.

**Exploratory follow-ups.** (i) H1 excluding width 4, all datasets. (ii) breast_cancer and wine as tie-breakers. (iii) Spearman(pre-ECE, ECE reduction), all four datasets. (iv) Mechanism check: 5 independently trained digits models per width (widths {4,16,64}), 100 bootstrap draws/cell/model over calibration-set size.

**Mechanical-confound diagnostic.** Because reduction = pre-ECE − post-ECE, Spearman(pre-ECE, reduction) can be large from shared variance alone. We construct a null by permuting post-ECE against pre-ECE 20,000 times and ask whether the observed correlation exceeds it.

**New in this revision — (a) null-calibration simulation.** To check the diagnostic itself is not producing spuriously low p-values, we resample pre-ECE and post-ECE *independently* from their empirical marginals (500 synthetic datasets per real dataset, true correlation = 0 by construction) and measure how often the permutation procedure falsely reports p<0.05. **(b) width-decoupled mechanism check.** We hold width fixed at 16 and vary training length (max_iter ∈ {10,20,40,80,150,300}, 5 seeds each, n=30 models) to induce a spread of pre-ECE independent of width, then compare bootstrap harm-rates (fraction of calibration-set resamples where scaling increases ECE) between the low- and high-pre-ECE halves.

All code and raw outputs (`experiment.py`, `experiment_dataset3.py`, `experiment_dataset4.py`, `experiment_mechanism.py`, `experiment_mechanism_v2.py`, `experiment_mechanism_v3_decouple.py`, `check_confound.py`, `check_null_calibration.py`, and corresponding `results_*.json`) are in this directory.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Width vs. optimal temperature (H1): 3 of 4 datasets now support a genuine gradient

| Dataset | ρ (all widths) | p | ρ (excl. width 4) | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| digits | 0.273 | 5.5×10⁻⁴ | **−0.008** | 0.53 (n.s.) |
| synthetic | 0.931 | 1.9×10⁻⁶² | 0.937 | 4.8×10⁻⁵⁶ |
| breast_cancer (exploratory) | 0.451 | 1.1×10⁻⁸ | 0.360 | 2.6×10⁻⁵ |
| wine (exploratory, new) | 0.122 | 0.076 (n.s.) | **0.210** | 0.011 |

Digits' significant H1 result is **not a genuine width gradient**: per-width mean T* is non-monotonic across widths 8–256, and width 4 is the only cell with mean T*<1. Synthetic, breast_cancer, and now wine all show a positive, significant correlation once width 4 is excluded (wine's raw correlation is weaker and only becomes significant after removing width 4, but the sign and significance agree with the other two). With a second independent exploratory dataset now pointing the same direction as breast_cancer, we read the width-extends-into-small-networks claim as supported on 3 of 4 datasets, with digits a specific, reproducible counterexample — no longer resting on a single dataset the paper itself distrusted as an arbiter.

### 3.2 Does temperature scaling help? Pre-ECE correlates with benefit — but the correlation is mechanical, and we now verify the test itself

| Dataset | Mean pre-ECE | Mean post-ECE | Wilcoxon p (pre>post) |
|---|---|---|---|
| digits | 0.0276 | 0.0309 | 0.99995 (no improvement) |
| synthetic | 0.0589 | 0.0441 | 2.2×10⁻⁸ (significant) |
| breast_cancer (exploratory) | 0.0316 | 0.0312 | 0.478 (no improvement) |
| wine (exploratory, new) | 0.0803 | 0.0526 | ~0 (significant) |

Spearman(pre-ECE, pre-ECE − post-ECE) is positive and significant on all four datasets, but two v2 reviewers flagged that this is expected mechanically since the outcome contains pre-ECE as a term. Permutation results:

| Dataset | corr(pre-ECE, post-ECE) | Observed ρ(pre-ECE, reduction) | Null mean [95% CI] | Permutation p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| digits | 0.635 | 0.453 | 0.631 [0.53, 0.72] | 0.9998 |
| synthetic | −0.032 | 0.865 | 0.879 [0.85, 0.91] | 0.847 |
| breast_cancer | 0.466 | 0.473 | 0.700 [0.63, 0.77] | 1.000 |
| wine (new) | 0.770 | 0.480 | 0.742 [0.66, 0.81] | 1.000 |

The observed correlation does not exceed the mechanical null on any of the four datasets. **New this revision:** a v3 reviewer asked whether the permutation diagnostic itself is well-calibrated, since post-ECE is not an arbitrary shuffle but a deterministic function of the same model as pre-ECE. We tested this directly: resampling pre- and post-ECE independently from their empirical marginals (so true correlation is exactly 0) and rerunning the full permutation procedure on 500 synthetic replicates per dataset, the false-positive rate at α=0.05 was 4.8% (digits), 6.6% (synthetic), 5.0% (breast_cancer) — all within Monte Carlo error of the nominal 5%. The diagnostic's null result is therefore not itself an artifact of a miscalibrated test. We retract the causal framing of the magnitude correlation across all four datasets and treat corr(pre-ECE, post-ECE) as descriptive: near-zero on synthetic (scaling does dataset-dependent work rather than tracking the starting point), moderate-positive elsewhere (post-ECE partially tracks pre-ECE).

### 3.3 Mechanism check: decoupling pre-ECE from width, as requested by three reviewers

The original mechanism check (5 independently trained digits models per width, bootstrap-varying calibration-set size) found the fraction of resamples where scaling made ECE strictly worse stayed high (76–89%) for two already-well-calibrated widths (16, 64) and dropped substantially (52%→30%) for a poorly-calibrated width (4). Three of four v3 reviewers independently noted this still compares different *widths'* fixed models, so it cannot separate "already well-calibrated" from "is width 16/64" as the actual driver.

**New in this revision**, we hold width fixed at 16 and instead vary training length (max_iter ∈ {10,…,300}) to induce a pre-ECE spread with width constant throughout (n=30 models). Splitting at the median pre-ECE (0.091):

| Group | n | mean pre-ECE | mean harm rate | SD across models |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low pre-ECE | 15 | 0.047 | 32.7% | 0.35 |
| High pre-ECE | 15 | 0.208 | 1.1% | 0.02 |

Mann–Whitney U (low > high) p=1.8×10⁻⁴; within-width Spearman(pre-ECE, harm-rate)=−0.82, p=3.0×10⁻⁸. This is not subject to the Section 3.2 artifact — harm-rate is a binary bootstrap outcome, not a difference containing pre-ECE as a term — and it is not confounded with width, since width is fixed at 16 throughout. It directly confirms, under a design that decouples the two explanations, that pre-ECE level rather than width per se drives the harm rate. (The absolute harm rates here are lower than Table 3.3's original 76–89%, because even this design's "low" group has higher mean pre-ECE (0.047) than the original width-16 models (0.018); the qualitative direction — lower pre-ECE, higher harm rate — is what generalizes.) We regard this as the paper's strongest remaining evidence, since it is the one result robust to both identified confounds (the magnitude artifact and the width confound).

### 3.4 Test accuracy (context)

Test accuracy rises with width before plateauing on all four datasets (digits: 0.844→0.980; synthetic: 0.748→0.839; wine: 0.811→0.983; breast_cancer similar), confirming these are genuinely underparameterized-to-adequate settings, not already saturated.

## 4. Conclusion

Within one-hidden-layer MLPs on four small classification tasks: the capacity–overconfidence extension holds on 3 of 4 datasets (synthetic, breast_cancer, wine) once the outlier-driven digits result is corrected — a meaningfully firmer basis than v3's single-dataset tie-break. On whether calibration helps, the magnitude-correlation claim remains retracted on all four datasets, and we now additionally confirm the retraction is not itself a false negative from a miscalibrated test. The paper's positive, artifact-resistant claim is the binary harm-rate mechanism check, now shown in a width-fixed design to be driven by pre-ECE level rather than width: already-well-calibrated small networks are disproportionately hurt by scaling even when width is held constant.

**Practical takeaway:** for small networks, do not apply temperature scaling by default. If pre-calibration ECE is already low, treat scaling as likely to *increase* ECE rather than decrease it, and validate via cross-validation on the calibration split before deploying it — this recommendation is now supported by a check that holds width constant, not just by cross-width comparisons. Width alone is not a reliable calibration-benefit signal; a specific numeric pre-ECE threshold remains unvalidated and descriptive of this dataset pool only.

**Limitations.** Single architecture family, scalar temperature scaling only, four datasets (three real, one synthetic), all still small (178–3000 samples). The width–T* claim is now better supported (3 of 4) but still not confirmatory-replicated. The decoupled mechanism check uses one dataset (digits) and one lever (training length) to vary pre-ECE within a fixed width; other levers (weight decay, dropout) or other datasets/widths might behave differently, and a fresh pre-registered replication — ideally on additional real tabular datasets — remains the clearest next step. Exploratory tests are not corrected for their full multiplicity beyond the two pre-registered H1/H2 tests. Generalization beyond MLPs and beyond 2–10-class problems is untested.
