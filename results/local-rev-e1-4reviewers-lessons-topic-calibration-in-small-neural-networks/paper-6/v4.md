# Width and Calibration in Small MLPs: The Sign Correlates with Task Difficulty, Not Width Alone

## Abstract

We study how expected calibration error (ECE) and post-hoc temperature-scaling (TS) change with hidden-layer width in small MLPs, holding architecture family and training budget fixed. Using real 10-class `digits` and synthetic 4-class `make_classification` data, we sweep width 2–256, run 20 seeded splits per width, and pre-register three hypotheses. Raw ECE (which does not depend on temperature search) shows opposite-signed width trends: `digits` gets *better* calibrated with width (Spearman $\rho=-0.87$, $p<10^{-4}$); `synthetic` gets *worse* ($\rho=+0.59$, $p<10^{-4}$). Both survive dropping either extreme width and non-convergence exclusion. A class-separability follow-up shows the positive width–ECE correlation on `synthetic` weakens monotonically with separability/accuracy ($\rho=0.78\to0.24$), moving toward `digits`' direction, though separability, accuracy level, and within-level width–accuracy gains (up to 22pp) are confounded and not disentangled by this design. New this round: three independent reviewers asked us to propagate a temperature-search grid-ceiling fix (previously a 100-run side diagnostic) through the full primary pipeline. We did — re-fitting $T^*$ for all 320 runs with the grid extended from $[0.1,20]$ to $[0.1,500]$ — and found the bias runs in **both directions**, not just the one we'd previously reported: at `synthetic` width 16 the original grid understated TS's benefit (ece_post corrected from 0.190 to 0.072, as before), but at `digits` width 4 — a cell we had previously argued was safe because its capped $T^*$ (18.8) sat under the ceiling — 16/20 runs were in fact pinned, and the corrected $T^*$ (41.7, not 18.8) makes TS look *worse*, not better (ece_post corrected from 0.143 to 0.188, now exceeding pre-scaling ECE). The corrected pipeline is now the paper's primary result; H1 (unaffected by $T^*$) and the sign of both headline effects are unchanged, but H2, the TS-made-worse counts, and one cell's practical conclusion about temperature scaling all change materially.

## 1. Introduction

Practitioners deploying *small* networks (embedded devices, tabular/low-data settings) need to know whether capacity itself drives calibration error, and whether temperature scaling is correctly *computed* as capacity changes. A related theoretical line (*Don't Just Blame Over-parametrization for Over-confidence*, arXiv:2102.07856) argues in binary classification that over-parameterization's effect on confidence depends on regularization, architecture, and separability rather than being universal. Our contribution is empirical and complementary: a controlled width sweep on two multiclass datasets, a test of separability as a moderator, and — prompted by round-2 and round-3 reviewer scrutiny — a full-pipeline audit of our own temperature-search implementation.

### Contribution

(1) A controlled width sweep (2–256 hidden units, fixed solver/iteration budget, 20 seeds/cell) on two datasets with pre-registered, Bonferroni-corrected hypotheses, showing opposite-signed, individually robust raw-ECE trends. (2) A class-separability follow-up showing the correlation's magnitude moves systematically with task difficulty, with the within-level confound explicitly reported rather than resolved. (3) A permutation-null-validated demonstration that an apparently significant "TS helps more when the model started worse" correlation is a statistical artifact of the pre/post arithmetic identity, confirmed to still hold after the grid-ceiling correction below. (4) **New:** the temperature-search grid-ceiling diagnostic from round 2 — previously applied only to a 100-run `synthetic` subset — is now propagated through the entire 320-run primary pipeline, revealing that the original $[0.1,20]$ grid was silently binding not just at `synthetic` widths 16/32 but also at `digits` width 4 (16/20 runs pinned), and that the resulting bias in reported TS benefit is not one-directional: it inflated TS's apparent benefit at one cell and deflated it at another.

## 2. Method

**Datasets.** (a) `digits` (sklearn): 1797 8×8 images, 10 classes, standardized. (b) `synthetic`: `make_classification`, 1800 samples, 20 features (10 informative, 5 redundant), 4 classes, 2 clusters/class, 3% label noise, standardized, seed 42, `class_sep=1.0` in the primary sweep.

**Architecture and training.** Single-hidden-layer ReLU MLP (`sklearn.neural_network.MLPClassifier`), solver `lbfgs`, `max_iter=1500` fixed across widths. Width grid {2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256}. 20 seeds/cell, fixed integer seed table, reseeded before each split/training.

**Splits and evaluation.** Stratified 50/25/25 train/val/test. Logits recovered by manually replaying the forward pass. We report accuracy, 15-bin ECE, Brier, NLL, pre- and post-scaling. $T^*$ minimizes validation NLL via grid search plus local refinement.

**Temperature grid (revised).** Round-2 diagnosed that a coarse $[0.1,20]$ grid was binding at some `synthetic` cells; that fix was previously applied only to a 100-run side script. This round, at three independent reviewers' request, we re-ran the **entire 320-run primary pipeline** fitting $T^*$ with the grid extended to $[0.1,500]$ (`experiment_v2_extgrid.py`), using the identical seed table, splits, and models as the original run, and additionally recorded per-run whether the *original* $[0.1,20]$ grid would have pinned at its ceiling. This is now the primary pipeline; all results below use it unless noted.

**Pre-registered hypotheses** (Bonferroni $\alpha=0.05/6=0.0083$): **H1** raw ECE vs. $\log_2$(width) — unaffected by the grid revision; **H2** $T^*$ vs. $\log_2$(width); **H3** whether TS's raw benefit is associated with `ece_pre` beyond the pre/post arithmetic identity, via a permutation null (own FPR validated at 0.047 on 600 true-null simulations, nominal 0.05).

**Class-separability follow-up (secondary, uncorrected, original grid).** `class_sep`$\in\{0.5,1.0,1.5,2.0,3.0\}$, same width grid, 10 seeds/cell.

Bold marks cells whose $T^*$/ece_post materially changed from the round-2 draft. At `digits` w4, correcting the ceiling *raises* reported ece_post from 0.143 to 0.188 — now above ece_pre (0.158), i.e. TS is newly classified as harmful there on average, reversing the earlier (capped-grid) conclusion that it modestly helped. At `synthetic` w16, correcting the ceiling *lowers* ece_post from 0.190 to 0.072, as previously reported. The bias direction depends on which side of the true optimum the loss surface happens to be flatter on at the cap, not on a fixed rule — we do not have a predictive account of which cells will be affected in which direction beyond "check the pin rate."

**H1/H2 (corrected).** `digits`: H1 $\rho=-0.874$ (unchanged — does not depend on $T^*$), H2 $\rho=-0.568$ (was $-0.570$). `synthetic`: H1 $\rho=+0.595$ (unchanged), H2 $\rho=+0.546$ (was $+0.577$). Both H2 values move slightly but the signs and Bonferroni significance are unchanged ($p<10^{-4}$).

**Does TS ever hurt (corrected)?** `digits`: 48/160 (30.0%, up from the previously reported 37/160), concentrated at w2 (13/20) and now also w4 (16/20 — previously miscounted as helping there due to the capped $T^*$). `synthetic`: 18/160 (11.3%, same total as before but redistributed slightly), concentrated at w2 (14/20).

**H3 (corrected).** Naive Pearson `corr(ece_pre, reduction)`: `digits` $r=-0.180$ ($p=0.023$; sign flips from the round-2 value of $+0.144$ because ece_post changed at w4/w8, but the magnitude is still small); `synthetic` $r=0.982$ ($p<10^{-115}$, up from 0.840). Under the validated permutation null, **neither survives** (`digits` $p=1.0$; `synthetic` $p=0.570$, still far above the Bonferroni threshold). The conclusion is unchanged: the large naive correlations are artifacts of the pre/post arithmetic identity, not genuine effects, before or after the grid fix.

**Class-separability sweep (unchanged from round 2, original grid; not affected by the primary-pipeline fix since it uses a separate synthetic-dataset run).** At `class_sep=1.0`, mean accuracy tops out at 0.75. Sweeping separability: $\rho=0.780$ ($p=1.6\times10^{-17}$) at `class_sep=0.5` down to $\rho=0.240$ ($p=0.032$) at `class_sep=3.0`, with within-level width–accuracy range shrinking monotonically from 22.3pp to 2.7pp. $\rho$ never crosses zero in this range — a monotonic trend toward `digits`' direction, not a demonstrated sign reversal, and separability/accuracy/within-level width gains remain confounded here, as in round 2.

## 4. Discussion and limitations

**Why the grid-ceiling correction cuts both ways.** A capped search doesn't just underestimate $T^*$ uniformly — it reports whatever the boundary value implies for ece_post, which can be better or worse than the true optimum's ece_post depending on the shape of the NLL curve beyond the cap at that specific cell. We have no principled account of which cells are at risk beyond directly checking the pin rate, which is why we now recommend, and did here, checking pinning across the *entire* grid rather than reasoning informally from mean $T^*$ values staying under the cap (as we incorrectly did for `digits` w4 in round 2).

**Separability confound.** As flagged previously, "task difficulty" bundles separability, accuracy level, and within-level width-driven accuracy gains that this design cannot isolate. A design holding mean accuracy fixed while varying `class_sep` (e.g., compensating via informative-feature count) would be needed to separate these; we did not attempt it this round because propagating the grid-ceiling fix — independently requested by three of four reviewers — was the higher-priority correctness issue, and a compensating-accuracy design needs its own calibration validation (checking the compensation doesn't itself change the accuracy–width relationship) that didn't fit this round's scope.

**Why width 16 moved more than width 32 under the extended grid.** Speculative: at width 16 the `synthetic` model's pre-softmax logit margins are apparently small enough that the validation NLL curve stays nearly flat well past $T=20$, so the coarse original grid's local-refinement step had very little signal to work with; at width 32 the model is already better separated, so the true optimum is closer to (though still past) the cap. We have not verified this mechanistically and did not investigate deeper.

**Scope.** Single architecture family, single solver (`lbfgs`; `adam` left 240/320 runs non-converged at equal budget and was discarded). Separability sweep uses only the synthetic generator and the original grid. `digits`' widths 16–256 showed zero pinning in this corrected pass, so we did not extend the grid check for it further.

**Small held-out splits.** Validation/test splits (~450 points) plus $T^*$ fit on validation alone plausibly drives both ECE confidence intervals and the 11–30% rate of TS making things worse.

**Multiplicity.** 6 pre-registered primary tests, Bonferroni-corrected; all four reported-significant directions clear $p<10^{-4}$. Grid-ceiling propagation, leave-one-out, convergence-exclusion, and the separability sweep are secondary/exploratory.

## 5. Conclusion

Raw ECE's width relationship is significant, robust, and opposite-signed between our two datasets; a separability manipulation shows the correlation's magnitude tracks task difficulty in the reconciling direction, though the mechanism remains confounded. A naive TS-benefit correlation is confirmed, before and after this round's correction, to be an arithmetic artifact via a validated permutation null. New this round: propagating the temperature-search grid-ceiling fix through the full 320-run pipeline — done in response to three independent reviewers asking why it hadn't been — shows the original $[0.1,20]$ cap biased reported TS performance in **both directions**: it understated TS's benefit at `synthetic` width 16 (as previously reported) but also overstated it at `digits` width 4, a cell we had previously and incorrectly cleared based on its capped mean $T^*$ looking safely under the ceiling. Practically: a temperature search's range should be checked for pinning across the *entire* grid, not spot-checked by whether the mean looks close to the cap, since the direction of the resulting bias is not predictable in advance; and capacity's effect on raw calibration should be expected to depend on task difficulty rather than treated as a fixed-sign property of "small networks."
