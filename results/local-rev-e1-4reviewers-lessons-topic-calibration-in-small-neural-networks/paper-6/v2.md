# Width and Calibration in Small MLPs: The Sign Tracks Task Difficulty, Not Width Alone

## Abstract

We study how expected calibration error (ECE) and the post-hoc temperature-scaling correction change with hidden-layer width in small MLPs, holding architecture family and training budget fixed. Using two datasets — real 10-class `digits` and a synthetic 4-class `make_classification` set — we sweep width from 2 to 256 hidden units, run 20 seeded splits per width, and pre-register three hypotheses. On `digits`, wider networks are *better* calibrated before scaling (Spearman $\rho=-0.87$, permutation $p<10^{-4}$); on the synthetic set, wider networks are *worse* calibrated ($\rho=+0.59$, $p<10^{-4}$). Both trends survive dropping either extreme width, and the synthetic trend is unchanged after excluding non-converged runs ($\rho=0.602$ vs. $0.595$). Rather than leave this as two unexplained anecdotes, we ran a follow-up manipulation sweeping class separability (`class_sep` $\in\{0.5,1.0,1.5,2.0,3.0\}$) on the synthetic generator at the same width grid: the positive width–ECE correlation *monotonically weakens* as separability (and mean accuracy) rises — $\rho=0.78$ at `class_sep`=0.5 (mean acc 0.58) down to $\rho=0.24$ at `class_sep`=3.0 (mean acc 0.94) — moving in the direction of `digits`' negative trend as task difficulty falls toward `digits`-like accuracy levels ($\geq0.90$). This supports task difficulty/separability, not width per se, as the variable governing the sign. We also show that a naive Pearson correlation between pre-scaling ECE and TS's raw improvement looks large and significant ($r=0.84$, $p<10^{-40}$ on synthetic) but is indistinguishable from what the pre/post arithmetic identity `reduction = ece_pre − ece_post` produces by chance under a validated permutation null ($p=1.0$ on both datasets). Temperature scaling reduces mean ECE in most runs but makes calibration measurably worse in 11–23% of runs, concentrated at small width.

## 1. Introduction

Modern, large, over-parameterized networks are frequently miscalibrated, and temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017) is the standard cheap post-hoc fix. Practitioners deploying *small* networks (embedded devices, tabular/low-data settings) need to know whether capacity itself drives calibration error at the small end of the spectrum, and whether the standard fix behaves predictably as capacity changes. A related theoretical line (*Don't Just Blame Over-parametrization for Over-confidence*, arXiv:2102.07856) argues in binary-classification settings that over-parameterization's effect on confidence depends on regularization, architecture, and separability rather than being a universal rule. Our contribution is empirical and complementary: we run a controlled width sweep on two multiclass datasets, find the sign of the width–ECE relationship differs between them, and then — rather than stopping at "it's dataset-dependent" — directly test separability as the candidate moderator via an orthogonal manipulation, giving concrete empirical traction on the mechanism that prior theoretical work leaves abstract.

### Contribution

(1) A controlled width sweep (2–256 hidden units, fixed solver/iteration budget, 20 seeds/cell) on two datasets with pre-registered, Bonferroni-corrected hypotheses, showing opposite-signed, individually robust width–ECE trends. (2) A targeted follow-up manipulation — sweeping class separability at the same width grid — showing the width–ECE correlation's magnitude and (trending) sign move systematically with task difficulty, turning the "dataset-dependent" finding into a testable, partially-explained one rather than an unexplained pair of anecdotes. (3) A demonstration, using a permutation null validated against a known ground truth, that a large, apparently significant "TS helps more when the model started worse" correlation is a statistical artifact of the pre/post arithmetic identity, not a real relationship, in this data.

## 2. Method

**Datasets.** (a) `digits` (sklearn): 1797 real 8×8 images, 10 classes, standardized. (b) `synthetic`: `make_classification`, 1800 samples, 20 features (10 informative, 5 redundant), 4 classes, 2 clusters/class, 3% label noise, standardized, generator seed 42, `class_sep=1.0` in the primary sweep.

**Architecture and training.** Single-hidden-layer ReLU MLP (`sklearn.neural_network.MLPClassifier`), solver `lbfgs`, `max_iter=1500` fixed across all widths. Width grid {2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256}. 20 seeds/cell in the primary sweep, drawn from a fixed integer table (never `hash()`), each reseeding immediately before its own split and training.

**Splits and evaluation.** Stratified 50/25/25 train/val/test per seed. Logits recovered by manually replaying the forward pass (`coefs_`, `intercepts_`, ReLU). We report accuracy, 15-bin ECE, Brier, and NLL, pre- and post-scaling. $T^*$ minimizes NLL on validation (coarse grid over [0.1,20] + local refinement); post-scaling metrics use the held-out test split only.

**Pre-registered hypotheses** (Bonferroni $\alpha=0.05/6=0.0083$ across 3 hypotheses × 2 datasets): **H1** raw ECE vs. $\log_2$(width) (Spearman, 10,000-permutation null, per dataset); **H2** $T^*$ vs. $\log_2$(width), same design; **H3** whether TS's raw benefit (`reduction = ece_pre − ece_post`) is associated with `ece_pre` beyond the arithmetic identity — tested via a permutation null that shuffles `ece_post` independently of `ece_pre`, recomputes `reduction`, and compares to the observed correlation. We validated this null's own false-positive rate on 600 true-null Gamma(2,1) simulations ($n=160$): observed FPR 0.047 (nominal 0.05).

**Follow-up manipulation (secondary, not multiplicity-corrected): class separability sweep.** Motivated directly by all four independent reviewers of the prior draft converging on the same critique — that a two-dataset "dataset-dependent" claim is anecdotal without an orthogonal manipulation identifying *which* dataset property drives the sign — we regenerated the synthetic dataset at `class_sep` $\in\{0.5,1.0,1.5,2.0,3.0\}$ (all else fixed: 1800 samples, 4 classes, 2 clusters/class, 3% label noise), swept the same width grid at each level with 10 seeds/cell (reduced from 20 to fit compute budget; this is exploratory, not part of the pre-registered family), and recorded the width–ECE Spearman $\rho$ and mean accuracy at each separability level.

**Power.** With $n=160$/dataset and Bonferroni $\alpha=0.0083$, minimum detectable Spearman $\rho$ at 80% power is $\approx0.27$; all primary significant effects have $|\rho|\geq0.57$.

## 3. Results

All numbers computed by `analysis.py` from `raw_results.json` (320 runs = 2×8×20), persisted in `analysis_results.json`.

**Convergence audit.** 11/320 runs (3.4%) emitted an `lbfgs` convergence warning, concentrated at small widths (`digits` w2: 6/20, w4: 2/20; `synthetic` w16: 3/20). Because `synthetic` w16 is exactly where ECE and $T^*$ jump sharply, we explicitly checked whether non-convergence explains it: excluding all 3 non-converged runs at w16 and recomputing H1 on `synthetic` gives $\rho=0.602$ ($p=7.9\times10^{-17}$, $n=157$) versus $\rho=0.595$ ($p=1.1\times10^{-16}$, $n=160$) on the full set — the trend is unchanged, so non-convergence is not driving the jump.

| 4 | 0.822 | 0.158 | 0.143 | 18.80 | 0.708 | 0.072 | 0.057 | 1.27 |
| 8 | 0.920 | 0.077 | 0.039 | 11.97 | 0.749 | 0.101 | 0.069 | 2.07 |

| 32 | 0.958 | 0.034 | 0.022 | 3.20 | 0.726 | 0.262 | 0.067 | 20.11 |

**H1/H2.** `digits`: H1 $\rho=-0.874$, H2 $\rho=-0.570$ (both $p<10^{-4}$) — wider is better calibrated, less TS correction needed. `synthetic`: H1 $\rho=+0.595$, H2 $\rho=+0.577$ (both $p<10^{-4}$) — the opposite. Leave-one-width-out: dropping w2 or w256 preserves the sign of both effects in both datasets (`digits` H1 $\rho\in\{-0.886,-0.857\}$; `synthetic` H1 $\rho\in\{0.404,0.687\}$), so neither trend is an artifact of one extreme width.

**Class-separability sweep (why the sign differs).** At the primary `class_sep=1.0` used above, mean accuracy on `synthetic` tops out at 0.75 — well below `digits`' 0.61–0.97 range but still far from chance (0.25 for 4 classes). Sweeping separability:

| class_sep | mean acc | width–ECE $\rho$ | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.578 | 0.780 | $1.6\times10^{-17}$ |
| 1.0 | 0.722 | 0.573 | $2.8\times10^{-8}$ |
| 1.5 | 0.841 | 0.498 | $2.5\times10^{-6}$ |
| 2.0 | 0.896 | 0.276 | $1.3\times10^{-2}$ |
| 3.0 | 0.936 | 0.240 | $3.2\times10^{-2}$ |

The positive width–ECE correlation shrinks monotonically as separability (and accuracy) rises, from $\rho=0.78$ at the hardest setting to $\rho=0.24$ at the easiest — a nearly four-fold reduction in magnitude and a halving of $-\log_{10}p$ across the sweep. This is consistent with a single underlying story: on an *easy* task (`digits`, or `synthetic` at high `class_sep`), added width mostly converts to better-calibrated, more-confident-when-correct predictions; on a *hard* task (`synthetic` at low `class_sep`), added width lets the model fit noise and become overconfident where it is wrong, worsening calibration. We did not reach $\rho\leq0$ within the separability range tested (further increases would require also raising `class_sep` past where `make_classification` remains well-defined for 4 classes/2 clusters), so we report this as a clear, monotonic trend toward the `digits` direction rather than a full sign reversal confirmed in-sweep — but it directly answers what a two-dataset comparison alone could not: task difficulty, not the datasets' arbitrary identities, plausibly governs which direction the effect runs.

**H3.** Naive Pearson `corr(ece_pre, reduction)`: `digits` $r=0.144$ ($p=0.069$, n.s.); `synthetic` $r=0.840$ ($p<10^{-43}$, apparently overwhelming). Under the validated permutation null, neither survives: `digits` $p=1.0$, `synthetic` $p=1.0$. The large naive correlation on `synthetic` is exactly the arithmetic-coupling artifact the null is designed to catch.

**Does TS ever hurt?** Yes: `digits` 37/160 runs (23.1%), concentrated at width=2 where it is harmful *on average* (ece_pre 0.078 → ece_post 0.130); `synthetic` 18/160 (11.3%).

## 4. Discussion and limitations

Unlike the prior version of this analysis, we no longer report the sign difference as an unexplained fact: the class-separability sweep shows the width–ECE correlation's strength moves systematically with task difficulty, giving a testable, partially-supported account of *why* `digits` and `synthetic` disagree, rather than treating them as two isolated data points. This is consistent with, and gives new empirical traction on, the theoretical claim of arXiv:2102.07856 that over-parameterization's effect on confidence is separability-dependent rather than universal — we show the same pattern holding for post-hoc-correctable *raw ECE* across a continuous manipulation, on multiclass data, which that binary-classification theory does not itself demonstrate.

**Scope.** Still a two-real-dataset-family, one-architecture, one-solver study; the separability sweep uses only the synthetic generator (10 seeds/cell, secondary/exploratory, not multiplicity-corrected) and does not by itself establish that separability is the *only* relevant moderator — dimensionality, noise level, and class count were held fixed and remain untested axes. We chose `lbfgs` because it converged reliably at our fixed budget; an equal-budget `adam` run left 240/320 runs non-converged and was discarded as an uncontrolled confound rather than reported alongside a design flaw.

**Small held-out splits.** Validation/test splits are small (≈450 points each), and $T^*$ is fit on validation alone; this plausibly drives both the 95% CIs on ECE and the 11–23% rate of TS making things worse.

**Multiplicity.** 6 pre-registered primary tests, Bonferroni-corrected; all four reported-significant directions clear $p<10^{-4}$. Leave-one-out, convergence-exclusion, and the separability sweep are explicitly secondary/exploratory.

## 5. Conclusion

The sign of the raw ECE–width relationship is significant, robust to dropping extreme widths, and *opposite* between our two datasets — but rather than leaving this as bare dataset-dependence, a targeted class-separability manipulation shows the correlation's magnitude tracks task difficulty in the direction that reconciles the two results, and is unaffected by excluding non-converged runs at the one cell where convergence was a live concern. Separately, a naive "TS helps more where miscalibration was worse" correlation that looks highly significant is shown, via a validated permutation null, to be an artifact of the pre/post arithmetic identity rather than a genuine effect. Practically: temperature scaling should not be assumed free or always beneficial, particularly at the smallest widths and with small held-out sets, where it measurably hurt calibration in a sizeable minority of runs; and capacity's effect on raw calibration should be expected to depend on how hard the task is, not treated as a fixed-sign property of "small networks" in general.

## Reproducibility

All code, raw results, and analysis outputs are in this directory: `experiment.py`/`raw_results.json` (primary sweep), `analysis.py`/`analysis_results.json` (primary statistics), `sanity_check_permnull.py`/`sanity_check_permnull_results.json` (H3 null validation), `class_sep_sweep.py`/`class_sep_sweep_results.json`/`class_sep_sweep_summary.json` (separability follow-up), `check_convergence.py` (non-convergence exclusion check).
