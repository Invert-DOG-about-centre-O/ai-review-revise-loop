# Width and Calibration in Small MLPs: The Sign Correlates with Task Difficulty, Not Width Alone

## Abstract

We study how expected calibration error (ECE) and the post-hoc temperature-scaling (TS) correction change with hidden-layer width in small MLPs, holding architecture family and training budget fixed. Using two datasets — real 10-class `digits` and a synthetic 4-class `make_classification` set — we sweep width from 2 to 256 hidden units, run 20 seeded splits per width, and pre-register three hypotheses. On `digits`, wider networks are *better* calibrated before scaling (Spearman $\rho=-0.87$, permutation $p<10^{-4}$); on the synthetic set, wider networks are *worse* calibrated ($\rho=+0.59$, $p<10^{-4}$). Both trends survive dropping either extreme width, and the synthetic trend is unchanged after excluding non-converged runs. A follow-up manipulation sweeping class separability (`class_sep`$\in\{0.5,...,3.0\}$) on the synthetic generator shows the positive width–ECE correlation *monotonically weakens* as separability (and mean accuracy) rise — $\rho=0.78$ down to $\rho=0.24$ — moving toward `digits`' negative trend as task difficulty falls. $\rho$ never crosses zero within the tested range, so this is a monotonic trend consistent with a difficulty-driven account, not a demonstrated sign reversal; we also find that within each separability level, accuracy still varies with width by up to 22 percentage points, so separability and within-level width-driven accuracy gains are not cleanly separated by this design. Separately, a naive Pearson correlation between pre-scaling ECE and TS's raw improvement looks large ($r=0.84$, $p<10^{-40}$ on synthetic) but is indistinguishable from what the pre/post arithmetic identity produces by chance under a validated permutation null ($p=1.0$). New this round: we diagnose the temperature search itself. At synthetic widths 16 and 32, 18/20 and 19/20 runs (respectively) had the optimal temperature $T^*$ pinned at the original search grid's ceiling (20.0). Re-fitting those cells with a 25x wider grid shows $T^*$ is not physically bounded there — mean $T^*$ at width 16 rises from 19.4 to 88.9 — and, using the correct optimum, mean post-scaling ECE at width 16 *improves* from 0.190 to 0.076. The previously reported "abrupt T* jump" at width 16 was thus partly a grid-boundary artifact that understated how well TS actually works there; raw ECE (which does not depend on $T^*$) and the sign of H1 are unaffected.

## 1. Introduction

Modern, large, over-parameterized networks are frequently miscalibrated, and temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017) is the standard cheap post-hoc fix. Practitioners deploying *small* networks (embedded devices, tabular/low-data settings) need to know whether capacity itself drives calibration error at the small end, and whether the fix behaves predictably — and is correctly *computed* — as capacity changes. A related theoretical line (*Don't Just Blame Over-parametrization for Over-confidence*, arXiv:2102.07856) argues in binary-classification settings that over-parameterization's effect on confidence depends on regularization, architecture, and separability rather than being universal. Our contribution is empirical and complementary: a controlled width sweep on two multiclass datasets, a test of separability as a candidate moderator, and — new this round — verification that our own post-hoc correction pipeline is not silently capping the quantity it reports.

### Contribution

(1) A controlled width sweep (2–256 hidden units, fixed solver/iteration budget, 20 seeds/cell) on two datasets with pre-registered, Bonferroni-corrected hypotheses, showing opposite-signed, individually robust width–ECE trends. (2) A class-separability follow-up showing the width–ECE correlation's magnitude moves systematically with task difficulty, while explicitly reporting the within-level width–accuracy confound this design cannot separate out. (3) A permutation-null-validated demonstration that an apparently significant "TS helps more when the model started worse" correlation is a statistical artifact of the pre/post arithmetic identity. (4) A targeted diagnostic, prompted by two independent reviewers, showing that $T^*$ at synthetic widths 16–32 was pinned against the original search grid's ceiling, correcting the previously reported post-scaling numbers at those cells and resolving the "unexplained jump" as a search-implementation issue rather than a genuine discontinuity in model behavior.

## 2. Method

**Datasets.** (a) `digits` (sklearn): 1797 real 8×8 images, 10 classes, standardized. (b) `synthetic`: `make_classification`, 1800 samples, 20 features (10 informative, 5 redundant), 4 classes, 2 clusters/class, 3% label noise, standardized, generator seed 42, `class_sep=1.0` in the primary sweep.

**Architecture and training.** Single-hidden-layer ReLU MLP (`sklearn.neural_network.MLPClassifier`), solver `lbfgs`, `max_iter=1500` fixed across widths. Width grid {2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256}. 20 seeds/cell, fixed integer seed table (never `hash()`), reseeded immediately before each split/training.

**Splits and evaluation.** Stratified 50/25/25 train/val/test per seed. Logits recovered by manually replaying the forward pass. We report accuracy, 15-bin ECE, Brier, and NLL, pre- and post-scaling. $T^*$ minimizes NLL on validation via a coarse grid over $[0.1,20]$ plus local refinement; post-scaling metrics use the held-out test split.

**Pre-registered hypotheses** (Bonferroni $\alpha=0.05/6=0.0083$): **H1** raw ECE vs. $\log_2$(width); **H2** $T^*$ vs. $\log_2$(width); **H3** whether TS's raw benefit is associated with `ece_pre` beyond the pre/post arithmetic identity, tested via a permutation null (own FPR validated at 0.047 on 600 true-null simulations, nominal 0.05).

**Class-separability follow-up (secondary, uncorrected).** Regenerated the synthetic dataset at `class_sep`$\in\{0.5,1.0,1.5,2.0,3.0\}$ (all else fixed), swept the same width grid, 10 seeds/cell.

**Grid-ceiling diagnostic (new, secondary).** Re-fit $T^*$ for synthetic widths $\{16,32,64,128,256\}$, same seeds/splits/models, extending the coarse grid's upper bound from 20 to 500, to test whether the original grid's ceiling was binding.

**Power.** With $n=160$/dataset and Bonferroni $\alpha=0.0083$, minimum detectable Spearman $\rho$ at 80% power is $\approx0.27$; all primary significant effects have $|\rho|\geq0.57$.

## 3. Results

**Convergence audit.** 11/320 runs (3.4%) emitted an `lbfgs` convergence warning, concentrated at small widths. Excluding the 3 non-converged runs at `synthetic` w16 leaves H1 essentially unchanged ($\rho=0.602$ vs. $0.595$ full set), so non-convergence does not drive that cell's behavior.

**H1/H2.** `digits`: H1 $\rho=-0.874$, H2 $\rho=-0.570$ (both $p<10^{-4}$). `synthetic`: H1 $\rho=+0.595$, H2 $\rho=+0.577$ (both $p<10^{-4}$) — opposite sign. Leave-one-width-out: dropping w2 or w256 preserves the sign of both effects in both datasets, so neither trend is an artifact of one extreme width. H1 (raw ECE) does not depend on $T^*$ and is unaffected by the grid-ceiling issue below; H2 does, and its magnitude on `synthetic` is likely underestimated by the capped values at w16/w32.

**Grid-ceiling diagnostic (new).** Two independent round-2 reviewers asked whether $T^*$ at synthetic w16–256 was hitting the original grid's ceiling (20.0) rather than a true optimum. It was, and more severely than either reviewer's question implied:

| width | runs pinned at 20.0 (of 20) | mean $T^*$, grid=20 | mean $T^*$, grid=500 | max $T^*$, grid=500 | mean ece_post, grid=20 | mean ece_post, grid=500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 18 | 19.41 | 88.88 | 189.4 | 0.190 | 0.076 |
| 32 | 19 | 20.11 | 25.54 | 30.0 | 0.067 | 0.068 |
| 64 | 11 | 19.26 | 21.64 | 22.0 | 0.053 | 0.062 |
| 128 | 1 | 16.58 | 16.79 | 21.6 | 0.058 | 0.066 |
| 256 | 0 | 14.88 | 15.08 | 21.6 | 0.055 | 0.063 |

At width 16 the effect is large and one-directional: the true NLL-minimizing temperature is roughly 4.6x larger than the capped grid could report, and using it drops mean ECE post-scaling from 0.190 (worse than pre-scaling's own neighbors) to 0.076 — now in line with the rest of the width range. At width 32 the ceiling was also binding for 19/20 runs but the corrected $T^*$ is only moderately larger (25.5 vs. 20.1) and ece_post is essentially unchanged. At width 64 the effect is smaller still, and by width 128–256 the original grid was already adequate. We did not re-run the full 320-cell pipeline with an extended grid (out of scope for this diagnostic), so the primary table above and the H2/H3 statistics still use the original $[0.1,20]$ grid; readers should treat `synthetic` w16's ece_post/$T^*$ entries in that table as, respectively, an overestimate and an underestimate, now corrected here. This resolves what the previous draft called an unexplained "abrupt jump": part of it was a search-grid artifact, not a genuine discontinuity in the model's calibration behavior, and the corrected picture is that TS works *better* at width 16 than previously reported, not worse.

**Class-separability sweep (why the sign differs).** At `class_sep=1.0`, mean accuracy on `synthetic` tops out at 0.75, below `digits`' 0.61–0.97 range but far from chance (0.25). Sweeping separability:

| class_sep | mean acc | width–ECE $\rho$ | p | within-level acc range (w2→w256) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.578 | 0.780 | $1.6\times10^{-17}$ | 0.426–0.649 (22.3pp) |
| 1.0 | 0.722 | 0.573 | $2.8\times10^{-8}$ | 0.651–0.753 (10.2pp) |
| 1.5 | 0.841 | 0.498 | $2.5\times10^{-6}$ | 0.797–0.862 (6.5pp) |
| 2.0 | 0.896 | 0.276 | $1.3\times10^{-2}$ | 0.861–0.930 (6.9pp) |
| 3.0 | 0.936 | 0.240 | $3.2\times10^{-2}$ | 0.923–0.950 (2.7pp) |

The positive width–ECE correlation shrinks monotonically as separability (and accuracy) rise, from $\rho=0.78$ to $\rho=0.24$. We did not reach $\rho\leq0$ within the tested range, so we report a clear, monotonic trend toward the `digits` direction rather than a full sign reversal. A second pattern, not previously reported: the width–accuracy range *within* each separability level also shrinks monotonically, from 22.3pp at the hardest setting to 2.7pp at the easiest. Because class_sep, between-level accuracy, and within-level width-driven accuracy gains all move together here, this design cannot cleanly attribute the shrinking correlation to separability *per se* versus the accuracy level it produces versus residual width-driven accuracy gains at low separability — disentangling these would need a design that holds accuracy fixed while varying class_sep (e.g., via a compensating noise adjustment), which we did not attempt this round.

**H3.** Naive Pearson `corr(ece_pre, reduction)`: `digits` $r=0.144$ ($p=0.069$, n.s.); `synthetic` $r=0.840$ ($p<10^{-43}$). Under the validated permutation null, neither survives (`digits` $p=1.0$, `synthetic` $p=1.0$) — the large `synthetic` correlation is the arithmetic-coupling artifact the null is designed to catch.

**Does TS ever hurt?** Yes: `digits` 37/160 runs (23.1%), concentrated at width=2, harmful on average (ece_pre 0.078 → ece_post 0.130); `synthetic` 18/160 (11.3%). These counts use the original grid; per the diagnostic above, some `synthetic` w16 "TS hurts" classifications may be reversed under the corrected $T^*$, but we did not recompute the full per-run count this round.

## 4. Discussion and limitations

The class-separability sweep shows the width–ECE correlation's strength moves systematically with task difficulty, but — as two independent reviewers noted — separability and accuracy move together across levels, and within-level width–accuracy ranges shrink the same way, so "task difficulty" here is better read as an umbrella term for correlated properties (separability, accuracy level, and how much width itself buys in accuracy) than a single isolated variable. This is consistent with, and extends to multiclass raw ECE, the theoretical claim of arXiv:2102.07856 that over-parameterization's effect on confidence is separability-dependent rather than universal.

**Scope.** Two-real-dataset-family, one-architecture, one-solver study; the separability sweep uses only the synthetic generator (10 seeds/cell, exploratory) and holds dimensionality, noise, and class count fixed. `lbfgs` was chosen because it converged reliably at our fixed budget; an equal-budget `adam` run left 240/320 runs non-converged and was discarded as an uncontrolled confound. The grid-ceiling re-fit covers only `synthetic` widths 16–256 (100 runs) and uses the same NLL-on-validation criterion as the primary pipeline (like-for-like correction, not a change of objective); we did not check `digits` for analogous ceiling effects, though its $T^*\leq19$ in every primary-table cell makes it unlikely to matter there.

**Small held-out splits.** Validation/test splits are small (≈450 points each), and $T^*$ is fit on validation alone; this plausibly drives both the ECE confidence intervals and the 11–23% rate of TS making things worse.

**Multiplicity.** 6 pre-registered primary tests, Bonferroni-corrected; all four reported-significant directions clear $p<10^{-4}$. Leave-one-out, convergence-exclusion, the separability sweep, and the grid-ceiling diagnostic are secondary/exploratory.

## 5. Conclusion

The sign of the raw ECE–width relationship is significant, robust to dropping extreme widths, and opposite between our two datasets; a class-separability manipulation shows the correlation's magnitude tracks task difficulty in the direction that reconciles the two results, though separability, accuracy level, and within-level width–accuracy gains are confounded in this design and not yet separable. A naive "TS helps more where miscalibration was worse" correlation is shown, via a validated permutation null, to be an arithmetic artifact rather than a genuine effect. New this round, prompted directly by reviewer scrutiny: the previously reported "abrupt T* jump" at synthetic width 16 was substantially a search-grid-ceiling artifact — the true optimal temperature there is far larger than the original grid could report, and using it, TS's actual benefit at that cell is better than previously stated, not worse. Practically: temperature scaling should not be assumed free, always beneficial, or reliably discovered by a fixed-range grid search — at the smallest widths and with small held-out sets it can measurably hurt calibration, and a search range that seems generous can still be silently binding; and capacity's effect on raw calibration should be expected to depend on task difficulty rather than treated as a fixed-sign property of "small networks" in general.

## Reproducibility

All code, raw results, and analysis outputs are in this directory: `experiment.py`/`raw_results.json` (primary sweep), `analysis.py`/`analysis_results.json` (primary statistics), `sanity_check_permnull.py`/`sanity_check_permnull_results.json` (H3 null validation), `class_sep_sweep.py`/`class_sep_sweep_results.json`/`class_sep_sweep_summary.json` (separability follow-up), `check_convergence.py` (non-convergence exclusion check), `grid_ceiling_check.py`/`grid_ceiling_check_results.json` (new: T* grid-ceiling diagnostic).
