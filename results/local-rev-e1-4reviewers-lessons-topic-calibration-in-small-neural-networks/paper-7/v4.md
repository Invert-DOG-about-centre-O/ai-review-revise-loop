# At Matched Parameter Budget, a Single Small Network Calibrates Better Than an Ensemble of Even Smaller Ones

## Abstract

Deep ensembles are widely reported to improve calibration, but that literature almost always compares an ensemble against a single network of the *same* width per member — the ensemble gets more total parameters. We ask the narrower question: at a **fixed total parameter budget**, is it better to spend it on one wider network or on an ensemble of several narrower ones, for post-hoc-calibrated Expected Calibration Error (ECE)? Using single-hidden-layer MLPs on sklearn `digits` and a synthetic 4-class Gaussian-blob task, with a pre-registered, Bonferroni-corrected primary test (n=20 paired seeds/dataset, Wilcoxon on calibrated ECE), we find the *opposite* of the ensemble-calibration intuition on `digits`: a single wide (width-32) network beats a matched-budget K=4 (width-8) ensemble (mean diff −0.030, p<1e-5, 95% bootstrap CI [−0.037, −0.022]). On `blobs` the same comparison is smaller and does not survive our pre-registered correction (p=0.070) — inconclusive on the primary test. A granularity sweep (K=2,4,8) on both datasets shows the effect scales with how underfit members are: on digits it vanishes at K=2 and is 6x larger at K=8; on blobs it is present even at K=2 and grows sharply at K=8. An isolated single width-8 network is itself worse-calibrated than width-32 on both datasets, and a **direct** paired test (isolated width-8 vs. the K=4 ensemble itself) shows the ensemble is significantly *worse* than the single underfit network it is built from — but **only on digits** (mean diff −0.013, p=0.017); the identical direct test on `blobs`, newly run for this revision, finds **no** residual cost (mean diff −0.0001, p=0.65) — member underfitting alone fully accounts for blobs' gap, while on digits it explains only part of it. We probe why: a mechanism check ruling out a shared-temperature bottleneck (per-member temperatures make the ensemble *worse* still) is now supplemented with a diversity analysis — the size of digits' residual gap correlates with how much ensemble members disagree with each other (Spearman r=−0.66, p=0.0015 for pairwise probability distance; r=−0.59, p=0.006 for prediction variance), consistent with, though not proof of, a Jensen's-inequality-style cost of averaging diverse, underfit, nonlinear probability outputs. A multi-draw robustness check (averaging 3 independent width-8 initializations instead of 1) confirms the underfitting-attribution estimate was not a fluke: it moves from 57% to 66% of the full gap, if anything strengthening rather than undermining the original single-draw estimate. We do not claim this generalizes beyond MLPs on these two datasets; we report it as a concrete counterexample to the intuition that ensembling always helps calibration, and as evidence that any residual ensembling-specific cost — where it exists at all — is tied to member diversity, worth checking before adopting ensembling as a calibration fix under a tight parameter budget.

## 1. Introduction

Modern neural networks are frequently overconfident (Guo et al., 2017). Temperature scaling — dividing logits by one scalar fit on held-out data — is a strong, cheap post-hoc fix, though it struggles with small validation sets and highly accurate models (Wang, 2023). Minderer et al. (2021) find some modern architectures are well-calibrated without post-hoc correction, underscoring that calibration is architecture- and scale-dependent — a caution we take seriously in scoping our claims to one architecture family. Increasing depth/width tends to worsen calibration even as it improves accuracy, linked to overfitting NLL rather than 0/1 loss (Guo et al., 2017; Mukhoti et al., 2020). Deep ensembles are a well-established remedy, often framed as approximate Bayesian model averaging (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017).

**The gap.** Almost all ensembling-for-calibration evidence compares K full-size networks against one network of the same size — the ensemble gets K times the parameters. A practitioner with a fixed budget instead faces: one wider network, or K narrower ones? Small networks are exactly the regime where this trade-off is forced (edge deployment, tiny classifiers, per-user models). We are not aware of prior work isolating calibration under equal-parameter-budget ensembling vs. single-network scaling; this paper runs that comparison and checks sensitivity to budget split (K=2,4,8) and dataset.

**Contribution.** (1) A controlled, equal-budget comparison of a single MLP vs. K=4 ensemble, matched parameters, two datasets, pre-registered with Bonferroni correction. (2) A K=2/4/8 granularity sweep on both datasets plus an isolated-narrow-network control, separating "ensembling" from "member underfitting." (3) A direct paired test of isolated width-8 vs. the ensemble itself — now run on **both** datasets — showing the residual ensembling-specific cost is real on digits but fully explained by underfitting alone on blobs. (4) Two mechanistic probes: a shared-vs-per-member-temperature intervention (ruled out as the cause) and a new diversity/disagreement correlation analysis that gives the first positive (correlational) account of what predicts the residual's size. (5) A multi-draw robustness check on the isolated-width-8 control confirming its attribution estimate is not an artifact of using one initialization.

## 2. Method

**Datasets.** (a) `digits` (sklearn): 1,797 8×8 images, 10 classes, 64 features. (b) `blobs`: synthetic 4-class Gaussian mixture (`make_blobs`, 1,800 points, 20 features, cluster std 6.0, fixed generator seed). Both small and CPU-fast by design.

**Architectures.** Single-hidden-layer MLPs (Linear→ReLU→Linear), Adam, 150 epochs, full-batch, CPU. Primary single model: hidden width 32. Primary ensemble: K=4 independently-trained width-8 members, matched to within 1–2% of single-model parameters (digits: 2,410 vs. 2,440; blobs: 804 vs. 816). Ensemble prediction = mean of member softmax probabilities. Sweep reruns at K=2 (width 16) and K=8 (width 4) on both datasets, plus an isolated single width-8 network (no ensembling). At K=8, width (4) is small relative to input dimensionality (64 digits, 20 blobs); part of the amplification could reflect a degenerate regime rather than a smooth continuation — flagged as open.

**Splits/seeding.** 20 seeds/dataset, 50/25/25 train/val/test (stratified). All seeds explicit fixed integers; sub-seeds via `seed*1000+offset`, never a string hash.

**Calibration.** Temperature fit by grid search over [0.05,10.0] (400 points) minimizing validation NLL; boundary-hit logged if within one grid step of an edge. Ensemble's primary analysis uses one shared T applied to every member's logits before averaging.

**Metrics.** 15-bin ECE, NLL, Brier on held-out test, before/after temperature scaling.

**Pre-registered primary test.** H1: calibrated ECE is lower for the K=4 ensemble than width-32, at matched budget. Two-sided Wilcoxon on d = ECE_single,cal − ECE_ensemble,cal, primary family {H1a: digits, H1b: blobs}, Bonferroni α=0.025, plus a 20,000-permutation sign-flip robustness check. Everything else — sweep, isolated-width-8 controls, both direct isolated-vs-ensemble tests, the diversity probe, the multi-draw check, boundary diagnostics, and the temperature mechanism check — is secondary/exploratory and uncorrected.

**Power.** Simulated Wilcoxon power at n=20, α=0.025: 0.3→15.6%, 0.5→42.5%, 0.8→84.0%, 1.0→96.2% (3,000 sims/effect size). Blobs' effect (≈0.49) is not reliably detectable at this n, flagged in advance.

## 3. Results

**Primary test.** On `digits`: mean diff = −0.0296, Wilcoxon p=3.8×10⁻⁶ (survives Bonferroni), permutation p=1.5×10⁻⁴, rank-biserial r=0.99, bootstrap CI [−0.0373,−0.0220], LOO-robust (p=7.6×10⁻⁶). On `blobs`: mean diff −0.0016, p=0.070, does **not** survive correction, fragile to LOO (p=0.123). Reported as **inconclusive**, consistent with ~43% power at this effect size.

**Granularity sweep (both datasets).** On digits: vanishes at K=2 (−0.0008, p=0.60), 6x larger at K=8 (−0.190, p=1.9×10⁻⁶). On blobs: already significant at K=2 (−0.0013, p=0.014), and −0.0384 (p=1.9×10⁻⁶) at K=8. The qualitative pattern (sharp growth with narrower members) replicates; the K=2 detail differs by dataset, a genuine, honestly-reported difference.

**Isolating underfitting.** An isolated width-8 network (single draw) is itself worse-calibrated than width-32 on both datasets (digits: −0.0168, p=3.8×10⁻⁶, ≈57% of the K=4 gap; blobs: −0.0015, p=0.027, ≈94% of blobs' K=4 gap).

**Direct isolated-vs-ensemble test — both datasets.** On digits (as in v3): the K=4 ensemble is significantly *worse* than its own isolated underfit member (mean diff −0.0128, p=0.017) — a real residual cost beyond underfitting. **New this revision:** the identical direct test on `blobs` finds essentially nothing (mean diff −0.0001, p=0.65). Combined with the attribution fractions above, blobs' entire (already-inconclusive) primary gap is explained by member underfitting alone, with no detectable extra ensembling-specific cost; digits' gap is not. This is the paper's clearest cross-dataset contrast: the *underfitting* mechanism generalizes across both datasets, but the *residual ensembling cost* mechanism does not — it is digits-specific in this data, not a universal property of matched-budget ensembling.

**Diversity/disagreement mechanism probe (new).** Since a shared-temperature bottleneck was already ruled out (below), we tested a second candidate: does the residual gap track how much ensemble members disagree? For each digits seed we computed mean pairwise L1 distance between calibrated member probability vectors and mean per-example variance of member probabilities, and correlated both (Spearman) against that seed's residual gap. Both correlate significantly: r=−0.66 (p=0.0015) for pairwise distance, r=−0.59 (p=0.0061) for variance — seeds with more disagreeing members show a *larger* ensemble-worse-than-member cost. This is consistent with a Jensen's-inequality-style account (averaging diverse, underfit, nonlinear softmax outputs can miscalibrate confidence in a way individual members' errors don't predict), but it is a correlational, within-digits-only finding, not a causal manipulation, and we did not run the equivalent probe on blobs since blobs shows no residual to explain.

**Multi-draw robustness of the isolated-width-8 control (new).** The single-draw isolated-width-8 estimate reused ensemble member 0's seed; we reran it averaging 3 independent width-8 initializations per pipeline seed on digits. The attribution estimate moves from −0.0168 (57% of the full gap, single draw) to −0.0194 (66%, 3-draw average; p=1.9×10⁻⁶ either way) — if anything larger, not smaller, with the extra draws. The single-draw estimate was not an outlier fluke inflating the residual; averaging reduces noise without reversing the conclusion that underfitting explains a majority but not all of the digits gap.

**Uncalibrated (raw) ECE.** Before scaling, the single model is far better calibrated on both datasets (digits: −0.146, p=1.9×10⁻⁶; blobs: −0.062, p=1.9×10⁻⁶); temperature scaling closes most, not all, of this on digits.

**Boundary-hit diagnostic.** Single model's fitted T never hit the grid boundary (0/20, either dataset). K=4 ensemble's shared T hit the lower boundary on 2/20 blobs seeds, 0/20 digits seeds — a plausible contributor to blobs' noisier primary signal.

**Shared-vs-per-member temperature.** Mean training NLL: 0.334 (K=4 members) vs. 0.060 (single) on digits (5.6x); 0.103 vs. 0.028 on blobs (3.7x). Letting each member fit its own temperature makes the ensemble's ECE **worse** (digits: shared beats per-member by 0.034, p<1e-5; blobs: by 0.003, p=0.011), likely because fitting 4 temperatures on a small validation split overfits each scalar to noise. Shared-temperature bottleneck is refuted as an explanation for the residual; the diversity probe above is a more promising, though still incomplete, positive account.

**Multiplicity.** Primary family remains exactly {H1a, H1b}, Bonferroni α=0.025 each. All other analyses, including every one added in this revision, are secondary/exploratory and uncorrected.

## 4. Limitations

One architecture family (single-hidden-layer MLPs), one optimizer, two small datasets. Only the digits K=4 primary test clears our confirmatory bar; blobs' primary comparison remains inconclusive and we have not re-run it at higher n. At K=8, width-4 is small relative to input dimensionality, so part of the amplification may reflect a degenerate regime rather than a smooth trend continuation; untested at intermediate K or width. The diversity-mechanism correlation is exploratory, based on n=20 digits seeds, uncorrected for the two diversity measures tested (which are themselves correlated with each other), and correlational — it identifies what predicts the residual's *size*, not a manipulated cause; we did not, for instance, experimentally vary member diversity while holding underfitting fixed. The multi-draw check used only 3 draws and only on digits; we did not repeat it on blobs (where there is no residual to attribute). We tested exactly one post-hoc calibration method (temperature scaling) and one aggregation rule (mean softmax); Platt scaling, isotonic regression, per-class temperature, alternative ECE binning, and focal-loss training (Mukhoti et al., 2020) remain unchecked, so we cannot rule out some effect being specific to this calibration/aggregation recipe. Validation splits for temperature fitting are small (a few hundred points), a plausible contributor to blobs' boundary-hit cases. Single library/framework (PyTorch, CPU) throughout.

## 5. Conclusion

At a fixed parameter budget, one moderately wide network beat a K=4 ensemble of narrower ones on calibrated ECE on `digits`, with a large, seed-robust effect; on `blobs` the same primary test is inconclusive. This is not a fixed "ensembling is bad" law: the effect vanishes at K=2 and grows sharply at K=8 on both datasets. An isolated narrow-network control shows most of the digits gap, and *all* of the blobs gap, is attributable to member underfitting rather than ensembling per se — a direct paired test (isolated width-8 vs. the ensemble itself, now run on both datasets) confirms a real residual ensembling-specific cost on digits but finds none on blobs, making clear that "ensembling adds a cost beyond underfitting" is a digits-specific finding in our data, not a universal one. A shared-temperature bottleneck was ruled out as the cause of that digits residual; a new diversity/disagreement analysis instead finds the residual's size correlates with how much ensemble members disagree, a first (correlational) positive account consistent with a Jensen's-inequality-style averaging cost, and a multi-draw robustness check confirms the underlying underfitting-attribution estimate was not an artifact of a single lucky or unlucky initialization. Practical takeaway, scoped honestly: under a tight budget, ensembling small networks for calibration is not a free win; its cost depends on how narrow the members are and, where a residual cost exists beyond underfitting, on how much those members disagree — check both, at your intended K, against a single equally-sized network rather than assuming ensembling is superior by default.
