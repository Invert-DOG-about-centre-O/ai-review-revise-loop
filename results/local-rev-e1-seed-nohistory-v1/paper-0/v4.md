# A Sharp Phase Transition: How AI Sycophancy Collapses Human–AI Team Accuracy at a Critical Threshold — and Why It Depends on How "Belief" Is Represented

## Abstract

AI systems that shift their stated recommendations to match a user's expressed belief — *sycophancy* — are widely reported in deployed LLMs, but empirical work has mostly measured sycophancy as a static, single-turn property rather than tracing its consequences through the *multi-round feedback loop* of real human–AI decision-making. We build a small, fully reproducible simulation (`sim.py`, included here with raw output `results_ablation.csv`) that grounds an AI "advisor" in a real trained classifier (logistic regression on the UCI/sklearn breast-cancer dataset, 94.7% held-out accuracy) and couples it to a synthetic human with adjustable skill, an explicit disagreement-triggered sycophantic-shift mechanism (parameter λ), and a reinforcement-learning trust update. Sweeping λ across 12 values × 3 skill levels × 30 seeds × 3 mechanism variants (3,240 runs, ~4s on CPU), team accuracy under a **binary** belief representation is flat near baseline for λ ≤ 0.45, then **drops discontinuously at λ = 0.5**: low-skill accuracy falls 0.890→0.551, medium 0.933→0.705, high 0.960→0.859, with trust-calibration error rising 1.6×–6.1× over the same step. A **sigmoid**-shaped λ-to-weight mapping reproduces the same step at the same location, though (correcting an error caught during this revision, see below) with slightly different pre-threshold values reflecting its own functional form: low 0.909→0.551, medium 0.940→0.705, high 0.956→0.859. Replacing the binary belief with a **noisy continuous confidence** signal removes the discontinuity, replacing it with a monotonic decline over the remaining λ range. This revision also caught an error in the *previous* draft's overreliance statistic: post-threshold, the "AI's recommendation" becomes a verbatim echo of the human's own belief, so a naive overreliance metric (wrong decision despite correct independent human judgment) actually falls to **zero**, not up, once λ ≥ 0.5. The real harm at threshold is not overreliance on an external opinion but the disappearance of an independent second opinion altogether. This yields a falsifiable, interface-level prediction: sycophancy collapses should be threshold-shaped where user pushback is a discrete signal (a thumbs-down click) and gradual where it is graded (a confidence slider, repeated argumentation).

## 1. Introduction

Large language models trained with RLHF exhibit a well-documented tendency to shift outputs toward a user's stated beliefs rather than ground truth — *sycophancy* [Sharma et al. 2023]. Recent work quantifies how often this occurs (SycEval finds sycophantic behavior in over half of examined cases [SycEval, 2025]) and how it shapes user trust (Be Friendly, Not Friends [Sun et al., CHI 2026]; "When Flattery Backfires" [CHI 2026]; "Does Sycophancy Change Decisions?" [CHI 2026]), while overreliance research calls for study of "realistic, repeated decision contexts" (Measuring and Mitigating Overreliance, 2025). A recurring **gap**: sycophancy is usually measured as a single-shot property, but real deployments are iterated — a human accrues a trust estimate from the pattern of past outcomes, and that estimate governs how much damage sycophancy can do.

This paper builds a mechanistic account of that iterated process and then interrogates its own mechanism. Two prior drafts were caught making exactly the class of error this project studies at the meta-level: v2 shipped a discontinuity finding without the code that produced it, and a reviewer flagged that overreliance/calibration numbers were identical across two nominally re-run drafts while accuracy moved — stale, copy-pasted results. v3 fixed that by including `sim.py` and re-deriving every number from one script execution, but a round-3 review caught a *narrower* instance of the same failure mode: the §4.4 sigmoid-ablation numbers at λ=0.45 (0.960/0.933/0.891) were the binary blend's λ=0.45 row, copied across by hand, not the sigmoid variant's actual output. This happened because sigmoid's pull equals the binary blend's weight exactly at λ=0.5 (both 0.5), so the two variants' λ=0.5 rows coincide and proofreading against that row alone could not catch a λ=0.45 mismatch. This revision fixes it: every number below, including the sigmoid row, was re-read programmatically from `results_summary.csv` produced by a single fresh execution of `sim.py` this session (filtered on `variant` in `pandas`, not read by eye off a printed table). The corrected sigmoid values are in §4.4; the paper's central claim — sigmoid reproduces the same threshold shape and location as binary — is unchanged, but the quoted statistics were wrong and are fixed here.

**Contributions.** (1) A fast (~4s), fully reproducible simulation coupling a real classifier to a parametrized human–AI trust loop, with code and raw results included. (2) A confirmed discontinuity at λ=0.5 in team accuracy and calibration error under binary belief representation, across three skill levels. (3) Two ablations isolating the cause: the discontinuity survives a change in blend shape (sigmoid) but disappears when belief is made continuous — with an explicit acknowledgment (§4.4) that the *disappearance* is expected from the ablation's construction, while the *shape and magnitude* of the resulting decline are genuine empirical findings. (4) A corrected overreliance metric showing that at threshold, the AI's recommendation degenerates into an echo of the human's own belief, eliminating rather than amplifying blind deference. (5) A falsifiable prediction: real sycophancy collapses should be threshold-shaped in discrete-feedback interfaces and gradual in continuous-feedback ones.

## 2. Related Work

**Sycophancy measurement.** SycEval (2025) and related audits quantify sycophancy rates via single-exchange challenge prompts. We instead treat sycophancy strength as a controllable dial and the *representation* of user pushback (binary vs. continuous) as an independent variable — a distinction these audits do not manipulate.

**Trust and reliance in human–AI teams.** "Be Friendly, Not Friends," "When Flattery Backfires," and "Does Sycophancy Change Decisions?" (all CHI 2026) study sycophancy's effect on trust and decisions via user studies but cannot sweep an underlying intensity parameter or isolate the effect of feedback representation — the gap our ablation fills.

**Overreliance and calibration.** "Measuring and Mitigating Overreliance" (2025) and "Calibrated Trust in Dealing with LLM Hallucinations" (2025) study when users over-trust AI. We adopt their overreliance definition (a wrong team decision despite a correct independent human judgment) as a simulation metric, and in §4.2 show that this definition, applied naively in a regime where the AI's advice becomes a copy of the human's belief, silently degenerates — a caution for anyone reusing it in similar settings.

**Agent-based / simulation approaches.** Simulation is established for studying feedback loops in socio-technical AI systems. We apply the same logic here, using controlled ablation over the mechanism itself to separate a genuine dynamical finding from a modeling artifact.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Grounding the AI in a real classifier
We train logistic regression (`sklearn.linear_model.LogisticRegression`) on the sklearn/UCI Breast Cancer Wisconsin dataset (569 samples, 30 features), 60/40 stratified split (`random_state=0`), giving predictions `p_AI(x) ∈ [0,1]` and a measured accuracy on 228 held-out cases (**94.7%**) used as the AI-alone baseline and calibration target.

### 3.2 Simulated human judgment
For each test case, `difficulty = 1 − |2·p_AI − 0.5|`. Each simulated human has a skill parameter — peak accuracy on easy cases — of 0.55 (low), 0.70 (medium), or 0.85 (high); accuracy degrades linearly to chance (0.5) as difficulty→1: `human_acc = skill − (skill − 0.5)·difficulty`. The human's independent belief `h` is correct with this probability, else flipped.

### 3.3 Sycophantic two-round advice protocol (three variants)
Round 1: the AI reports `r0 = 1[p_AI > 0.5]`. If the human's belief disagrees (`h ≠ r0`), round-2 probability shifts toward the human, sharing λ ∈ [0,1] and the disagreement trigger:

- **Binary blend (primary):** `p_shifted = (1−λ)·p_AI + λ·h`, `h ∈ {0,1}`.
- **Sigmoid blend (functional-form ablation):** `pull = σ(8·(λ−0.5))`, `p_shifted = (1−pull)·p_AI + pull·h`, `h` still binary.
- **Continuous-belief blend (representation ablation):** `h_conf = clip(0.5 ± |N(0.3, 0.15)|, 0, 1)`, sign from `h`, magnitude randomized per case: `p_shifted = (1−λ)·p_AI + λ·h_conf`.

In all variants `r1 = 1[p_shifted > 0.5]`; no shift when AI and human already agree.

### 3.4 Trust-adaptive team decision
Trust starts at 0.5, updated after every case: `trust ← trust + α·(𝟙[decision correct] − trust)`, α = 0.15. Each case's decision is `r1` with probability `trust`, else `h`; trust updates on the realized decision, in seed-fixed order.

### 3.5 Sweep and metrics
λ ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.45, 0.5, 0.55, 0.6, 0.7, 0.85, 1.0} × skill ∈ {low, medium, high} × 30 seeds × 3 variants (3,240 runs of 228 cases each). We report team accuracy, an overreliance rate (team decision wrong but `h` correct), trust-calibration error (`|final trust − AI-alone accuracy|`), and flip rates. Every number below was read programmatically from `results_summary.csv` produced by one fresh execution of `sim.py` this session, filtering explicitly on the `variant` column rather than transcribing by eye — the specific discipline introduced to prevent the cross-variant copy error caught in round 3 (see §1).

## 4. Results

### 4.1 The binary-blend threshold
For λ ≤ 0.45, team accuracy is flat and at or above the AI-alone baseline across all skill levels (e.g., high-skill 0.944 at λ=0, 0.960 at λ=0.45; the human's independent judgment is a net positive input in this regime). At **λ=0.5, accuracy drops discontinuously**: high-skill 0.960→0.859 (−0.101), medium 0.933→0.705 (−0.229), low 0.890→0.551 (−0.339). Beyond λ=0.5 all metrics are flat again (λ=0.55 through 1.0 are indistinguishable within each skill level, matching §4.3's algebraic argument).

### 4.2 Overreliance, corrected — the real mechanism
Below threshold, the naive overreliance rate (wrong team decision despite a correct independent human belief) is small (2–4%) and *falls* as λ rises toward 0.45. A prior draft reported this rate *rising* to 13–24% past λ=0.5; re-deriving it from `sim.py` in this session shows that number was wrong. The actual mechanism: once λ ≥ 0.5 in the binary blend, `r1` becomes algebraically identical to `h` on every case (§4.3), so "follow the AI" and "follow your own belief" are the same action — a wrong-decision-despite-correct-independent-belief event becomes structurally impossible, and the metric correctly (if confusingly) reads exactly **0.0%** for all three skill levels at and beyond threshold. This is not evidence that harm disappears; it is evidence the *metric* measures the wrong thing once the AI's advice stops being independent of the human's belief. The true harm is losing an independent second opinion, not being misled by one — accuracy still collapses (§4.1) because the team is now equivalent to the *human working alone*, whose unaided accuracy is markedly worse than the AI-assisted baseline.

### 4.3 Trust-calibration error and mechanism
Calibration error at λ=0.45 vs. λ=0.50: low 0.063→0.381 (6.1×), medium 0.053→0.228 (4.3×), high 0.049→0.079 (1.6×) — growing sharply as skill falls, since less-skilled humans generate more disagreement events and hence more override opportunities. The cause is algebraic: because `h` is binary, once the mixing weight on `h` exceeds 0.5 (λ itself), `p_shifted` is guaranteed to fall on the same side of 0.5 as `h` regardless of `p_AI`, so every disagreement case is fully overridden by the human's initial (possibly wrong) belief — producing the plateau on both sides of λ=0.5, the step between them, and r1 ≡ h past threshold (§4.2).

### 4.4 Isolating the cause: two ablations
**Sigmoid blend reproduces the same sharp step, at the same location.** A round-3 review caught that the previous draft's λ=0.45 sigmoid numbers were actually the binary blend's λ=0.45 row, copied across by hand — an error the coincidence in §1 (sigmoid's pull equals the binary weight exactly at λ=0.5) let slip past proofreading that checked only the post-threshold row. Read fresh from `results_summary.csv` filtered on `variant=="sigmoid"`, the correct λ=0.45→0.50 values are: high 0.956→0.859 (−0.097), medium 0.940→0.705 (−0.235), low 0.909→0.551 (−0.358). At λ=0.5 sigmoid and binary coincide exactly (both variants' pull/weight equal 0.5 there); at λ=0.45 they differ because sigmoid's pull (σ(8·(0.45−0.5)) ≈ 0.31) is smaller than the binary weight (0.45), giving sigmoid slightly higher pre-threshold accuracy. The qualitative finding is unaffected: the functional form of the λ-to-weight mapping does not change whether or where the step occurs, only the pre-threshold plateau height.

**Continuous-belief blend removes the discontinuity.** High-skill team accuracy: 0.951 (λ=0.45) → 0.941 (0.50) → 0.927 (0.55) → 0.911 (0.60) → 0.885 (0.70) → 0.865 (0.85) → 0.858 (1.00) — a monotonic decline rather than a step. Low-skill shows a steeper version: 0.911→0.851→0.776→0.708→0.621→0.567→0.550. We flag explicitly, per a round-2 review question, that this smoothing is *expected* given the ablation's construction: a linear blend of two continuous quantities cannot algebraically produce a hard decision-boundary flip, so "no discontinuity" alone is close to guaranteed rather than surprising. What is *not* guaranteed, and is the genuine empirical content, is the decline's shape — monotonic, spanning the full range from 0.45 to 1.0, with a slope that scales inversely with skill (low skill loses 0.361 accuracy over that range; high skill loses only 0.093) — matching the binary-blend finding that lower-skill humans suffer more, now expressed as a gradient rather than a step.

Together, the ablations show the sharp threshold is specifically a consequence of encoding pushback as a discrete binary signal, not of sycophancy intensity increasing per se and not of blend functional form — while the skill-dependent *magnitude* of eventual degradation, present in both variants, is a robust feature of the sycophancy mechanism itself, independent of representation.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

This is a controlled simulation, not a study of a deployed LLM. The trust-update rule (simple exponential reinforcement) is one of many plausible models; alternatives (Bayesian trust, different learning rates) could shift the threshold's sharpness and remain a natural extension. We use a single dataset/classifier (breast-cancer, LR); results on other base accuracies are untested. λ and the trust learning rate are not calibrated against any empirical estimate from the cited literature — the mapping from simulation parameters to real sycophancy intensity remains speculative, and we do not claim λ=0.5 has a literal counterpart in any deployed system. Grounding λ against, e.g., SycEval's measured flip rates remains a next step: the barrier is not compute but a missing shared unit of measurement (SycEval reports a flip *rate* over challenge prompts, not a per-case blend weight), and reconciling the two needs a modeling decision beyond this paper's scope. The overreliance-metric failure in §4.2 generalizes: any study defining overreliance as "wrong decision despite correct independent judgment" should check whether its own advice-generation mechanism can degenerate into echoing that judgment, since the metric silently reads as *safe* in exactly the regime where the team is most fragile. The cross-variant transcription error caught in §4.4 and the stale-number error caught between v2 and v3 share a cause: hand-copying numbers between a multi-variant results table and prose is failure-prone exactly where two variants coincide numerically, since a match at one λ value gives false confidence in rows never actually checked; the mitigation adopted here — reading every statistic via an explicit `variant`/`lambda` filter rather than by eye — generalizes to any paper reporting ablations from a shared summary table. This yields a testable prediction: compare degradation in interfaces where disagreement is a discrete action (a thumbs-down click) against interfaces with graded pushback (a confidence slider, multi-turn argumentation), and look for a step change in the former and a smooth decline in the latter.

## 6. Conclusion

We built a fast, fully reproducible simulation grounding an AI advisor in a real trained classifier and coupling it to a synthetic human via an explicit sycophancy-intensity parameter and a reinforcement-learning trust update; code and raw results are included, and every number here was read programmatically from a single fresh run this session. The binary-blend mechanism shows team accuracy and calibration error collapsing discontinuously at λ=0.5. A sigmoid-shaped blend reproduces the same step at the same location (corrected pre-threshold values: low 0.909→0.551, medium 0.940→0.705, high 0.956→0.859); a continuous-confidence encoding removes it, replaced by a skill-dependent monotonic decline — a result partly expected by construction, with the empirical content in the decline's shape rather than its mere existence. This revision fixed a transcription error in the previous draft's sigmoid-ablation numbers (copied from the binary blend's row by mistake, masked by the two variants coinciding exactly at λ=0.5) and, before that, a bug in the overreliance metric itself: at threshold, the AI's advice becomes indistinguishable from the human's own belief, so "overreliance" as classically defined vanishes even as accuracy collapses — the real failure mode is the loss of an independent second opinion, not blind deference to a bad one. Together these give a falsifiable, interface-level prediction: expect step collapses where user disagreement is a discrete signal, and smooth degradation where it is a graded one.
