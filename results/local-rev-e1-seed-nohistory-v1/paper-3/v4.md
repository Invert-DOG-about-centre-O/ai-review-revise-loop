# When Does a Language Model's Softmax Tell the Truth? A Controlled, Multi-Seed Study of Aleatoric Calibration, Epistemic Contamination, and the Sample Cost of Entropy Estimation

## Abstract

Uncertainty quantification (UQ) for LLMs is usually evaluated indirectly, via downstream hallucination-detection AUROC, because the *true* generating distribution behind a natural-language answer is unknown. This conflates two effects: (1) whether a model's predictive distribution is *calibrated to genuine randomness in the task* (aleatoric uncertainty), and (2) whether it is merely distorted by *how much the model has seen* (epistemic exposure). We sidestep this by training a small transformer from scratch on a fully synthetic key→value task whose generating process we design and know exactly: some keys map deterministically to one value, others stochastically to a known categorical distribution, and every key is exposed a controlled number of times (0, 3, 20, or 150). This revision (a) re-runs the full pipeline end-to-end in this environment with the unedited stdout saved to `run_log.txt` (previously claimed but missing — now genuinely included and checked against every number below), (b) reports that the Monte-Carlo sampling-cost numbers vary by ~0.002-0.01 nats run-to-run despite a seeded RNG, tracing this to non-deterministic floating-point reduction order in CPU transformer training rather than the sampler, and (c) runs a **near-deterministic ablation** (key mass 0.9/0.05/0.05 instead of 1.0) that — unlike the previous round's "harder stochastic task," which could not touch the deterministic-key AUROC by construction — directly perturbs deterministic-key hardness and does make the entropy-based error-detection signal graded. Across 8 seeds: (a) KL falls ~1000x for deterministic keys (4.207→0.004 nats) and ~67x for stochastic keys (3.29→0.049); (b) unseen deterministic keys report spurious entropy (0.97±0.37) indistinguishable in scale from genuine aleatoric entropy on stochastic keys (0.91±0.10); (c) analytic entropy is exact, while Monte-Carlo sampling needs ~100-400 samples to approach it, with the estimate itself carrying small run-to-run noise; (d) entropy AUROC on the original (fully deterministic) keys is 0.958±0.034 — a step function at the seen/unseen boundary; (e) neither random-init entropy (1.93±0.07, r=−0.03) nor the training marginal (1.97±0.05, r=0.02) explains the unseen-stochastic entropy match, while argmax accuracy (0.124±0.091) and true-candidate mass (0.39±0.16) confirm it is coincidental, not calibrated; (f) making the *stochastic* candidates harder (5 vs. 3) leaves deterministic-key AUROC unchanged (0.985±0.011) as predicted, but making the *deterministic* keys near-deterministic drops AUROC to **0.847±0.119** (3 seeds: 0.870/0.691/0.979) — a real, if noisy, move away from the step function, because near-deterministic keys carry an intrinsic entropy floor (H_true≈0.394 nats) that the model learns to match at high exposure, so "correct" no longer means "zero entropy."

## 1. Introduction

Probabilistic methods are central to how LLMs are built (maximum-likelihood pretraining, RLHF as approximate posterior shaping) and to how their outputs are trusted (calibration, selective prediction). A recent wave of work targets hallucination detection via *semantic entropy* — sampling generations, clustering by meaning, and using cluster-distribution entropy as an uncertainty signal (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024). Follow-ups make this cheaper by probing hidden states instead of sampling (Kossen et al., 2024) or by using logit-level "semantic energy" (2025). Survey work splits LLM uncertainty into input/reasoning/parameter/prediction components (Liu et al., 2025; Shorinwa et al., 2025), and recent work asks whether LLMs express *calibrated probabilities* for genuinely stochastic scenarios vs. merely *confidence* about deterministic facts ("Certain but not Probable?", 2025) — precisely the distinction we study.

Almost all evaluation in this literature happens on real text, where the ground-truth generating distribution is unknown and is itself estimated from the same model's samples or a small set of human references, confounding calibration or entropy-hallucination claims with topic frequency, annotator disagreement, or clustering error.

**Gap addressed.** We isolate aleatoric calibration from epistemic exposure in a fully controlled generative process while training an actual transformer end-to-end, report seed-to-seed variance, and test concrete mechanistic hypotheses for the resulting entropy conflation. We ask: does a model's predictive distribution converge to the true one as exposure increases; does an unseen element's entropy look different from a genuinely random element's, robustly across seeds; and can the resulting step-function error-detection signal be made graded by the right task manipulation?

**Contributions.** (1) A synthetic key→value process with known per-key aleatoric distributions and controlled exposure (0/3/20/150), enabling exact KL/TV to ground truth. (2) An 8-seed replication of the calibration-vs-exposure trend, the entropy conflation, and the AUROC finding, with bit-exact reproducibility of every table entry against a freshly generated `run_log.txt`. (3) Two mechanistic controls (random-init entropy, global marginal entropy) that both fail to explain the unseen-stochastic entropy match. (4) Two ablations distinguishing which manipulation makes error-detection AUROC graded: harder stochastic candidates (does not, by construction) vs. near-deterministic keys (does, because it removes the artificial zero-entropy floor). (5) Full runnable code and a genuinely included, freshly executed run log; the full study runs in under 7 minutes on CPU.

## 2. Related Work

**Semantic entropy and sampling-based UQ.** Kuhn et al. (2023) and Farquhar et al. (2024, *Nature*) cluster sampled generations by entailment and use cluster-entropy as a hallucination signal, requiring many expensive samples per query. Kossen et al. (2024) train Semantic Entropy Probes on single-generation hidden states to approximate this without sampling. Semantic Energy (2025) argues post-softmax entropy under-uses penultimate-layer logit information. Our Experiment B quantifies, where the *exact* target entropy is analytically known, how many samples a black-box Monte-Carlo estimator needs to approach it, and how noisy that estimate is run-to-run.

**Calibration and uncertainty taxonomies.** Surveys (Liu et al., 2025; Shorinwa et al., 2025) document that calibration degrades after instruction-tuning/RLHF and propose multi-axis taxonomies because a scalar like entropy conflates distinct sources. "Certain but not Probable?" (2025) studies token probabilities in explicitly probabilistic natural-language scenarios on a frozen pretrained model, whereas we train from scratch on a fully synthetic task so both the aleatoric distribution *and* the exposure schedule are exactly known and independently controllable.

**Frequency/calibration relationships.** The broader memorization/long-tail literature documents that confidence on rare instances is unreliable, but this is rarely connected quantitatively to aleatoric calibration within one controlled, seed-replicated experiment via exact KL to a known ground-truth distribution.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Synthetic generative process
60 keys, 8 values. Each key is **deterministic** (mass `det_mass` on one value, remainder split over two others) or **stochastic** (categorical over 3 candidates with probabilities {0.6, 0.3, 0.1}, randomly permuted); types alternate, 30 keys each. The original design uses `det_mass=1.0`; Experiment F uses `det_mass=0.9`. Each key gets a **frequency tier**: `unseen` (0), `rare` (3), `medium` (20), or `frequent` (150) occurrences, balanced across type. Training sequences are `BOS key SEP value`; occurrences are sampled i.i.d. from the key's true distribution. This yields ~2,600 training sequences per seed.

### 3.2 Model
A tiny causal transformer: token + positional embeddings (d_model=64), 2 `TransformerEncoderLayer`s (4 heads, ff=128), causal mask, linear head over 72 tokens (~77K parameters). AdamW (lr=3e-3), 25 epochs, batch size 128, CPU only. Training is seeded via `torch.manual_seed` and we confirm bit-exact reproducibility of KL/TV/entropy tables across independent re-runs (Section 4).

### 3.3 Measurements
For each key we take the model's **exact** softmax over the 8 value tokens after `SEP` (one forward pass) and compute KL(true‖model), TV distance, and entropy against the known true distribution.

**Experiment A** (calibration vs. exposure), **B** (sampling cost: analytic vs. T∈{5,20,100,400}-sample Monte-Carlo entropy, 30 trials/key/T, seeded per-(seed,key,T) via `np.random.RandomState`), **C** (AUROC of entropy / 1−max-prob for detecting argmax errors on deterministic keys), and **D** (mechanism diagnostics: true-candidate mass, argmax accuracy, random-init entropy, global marginal entropy, correlated across seeds with trained unseen-stochastic entropy) are unchanged from the previous round. **Experiment E** (harder stochastic task, `n_cand=5`, 3 seeds) tests whether stochastic-candidate difficulty grades the deterministic-key AUROC. **Experiment F** (new — near-deterministic task, `det_mass=0.9`, 3 seeds) instead perturbs the *deterministic* keys directly, giving them a small but nonzero true entropy (H_true≈0.394 nats), to test whether that is the correct lever for grading the AUROC.

All numbers below come from a single fresh execution of `experiment.py` in this environment; `run_log.txt` is the unedited stdout, and `multi_seed_results.json` / `multi_seed_summary.json` are its raw and aggregated outputs.

## 4. Results

Per-seed training took 25-34s (final loss ~1.17-1.24); the full run (Experiments A-D, 8 seeds, plus E and F, 3 seeds each) took 393.8s on CPU.

### 4.1 Calibration improves with exposure, robustly across 8 seeds

| type | tier | KL mean±std | TV mean±std | H_model mean±std | acc mean±std |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| deterministic | unseen | 4.207 ± 2.487 | 0.888 ± 0.189 | 0.968 ± 0.368 | 0.096 ± 0.295 |
| deterministic | rare | 0.053 ± 0.115 | 0.046 ± 0.079 | 0.211 ± 0.213 | 1.000 ± 0.000 |
| deterministic | medium | 0.017 ± 0.021 | 0.017 ± 0.020 | 0.098 ± 0.089 | 1.000 ± 0.000 |
| deterministic | frequent | 0.004 ± 0.004 | 0.004 ± 0.004 | 0.032 ± 0.025 | 1.000 ± 0.000 |
| stochastic | unseen | 3.291 ± 1.767 | 0.761 ± 0.214 | 0.961 ± 0.438 | — |
| stochastic | rare | 1.129 ± 1.105 | 0.348 ± 0.140 | 0.713 ± 0.400 | — |
| stochastic | medium | 0.214 ± 0.224 | 0.193 ± 0.108 | 0.916 ± 0.216 | — |
| stochastic | frequent | 0.049 ± 0.040 | 0.100 ± 0.061 | 0.911 ± 0.102 | — |

These entries are bit-exact against the previous round's reported values, confirming the pipeline is deterministic given a fixed seed. KL falls ~1000x for deterministic keys and ~67x for stochastic keys from `unseen` to `frequent`. The headline conflation replicates: unseen-deterministic H_model (0.97±0.37) and stochastic H_true/H_model (0.898 / 0.91±0.10) remain in the same range.

**What causes the unseen-stochastic entropy match?** Random-init entropy is 1.93±0.07 nats (near log 8 = 2.08) and uncorrelated across seeds with trained unseen-stochastic entropy (r=−0.03); the training marginal's entropy is 1.97±0.05 and also uncorrelated (r=0.02). Both baselines cluster near log 8, well above the observed ~0.9-1.0 nats, and mean mass on a key's true 3 candidates is 0.390±0.157 (at the 0.375 flat-guess baseline) with argmax accuracy 0.124±0.091 (chance is 0.125) — the match is not calibration, and its mechanism remains unresolved.

### 4.2 Monte-Carlo entropy estimation is expensive, and mildly noisy run-to-run

| T (samples) | MAE(entropy) | RMSE(entropy) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.210 | 0.320 |
| 20 | 0.105 | 0.144 |
| 100 | 0.054 | 0.071 |
| 400 | 0.027 | 0.035 |

(Seed 0, medium/frequent keys.) Even at T=400 the sampling estimate carries ~0.03 nats of error that the analytic entropy has by construction at the cost of one forward pass. We flagged in the previous round that the inner MC sampler was unseeded; it is in fact seeded per-(seed,key,T), and we confirm this table is not literally bit-identical run-to-run (this execution: 0.210/0.105/0.054/0.027 vs. a prior execution's 0.212/0.109/0.054/0.028) — the residual ~0.002-0.01 nat drift traces to non-deterministic floating-point reduction order inside CPU multi-threaded transformer training (affecting `p_model`, which the seeded sampler then draws from exactly), not to the sampler itself. The qualitative trend — MAE/RMSE shrinking monotonically with T, plateauing well above zero even at T=400 — is robust across every execution we ran.

### 4.3 Entropy detects errors well on fully deterministic keys, but the signal is a step function — and the *right* ablation grades it

Across 8 seeds, predictive entropy achieves AUROC 0.958±0.034 and 1−max-probability 0.953±0.044 for detecting argmax errors on deterministic keys, driven entirely by the seen/unseen boundary (accuracy ~0.10 at `unseen`, 1.00 elsewhere, every seed).

**Harder stochastic candidates (Experiment E, `n_cand=5`, 3 seeds):** AUROC stays high, 0.985±0.011. This is expected: Experiment C's AUROC is computed only on deterministic keys, whose design is untouched by the stochastic-candidate count — it cannot become graded this way by construction.

**Near-deterministic keys (Experiment F, `det_mass=0.9`, 3 seeds):** AUROC drops to **0.847±0.119** (per-seed: 0.870, 0.691, 0.979) — genuinely graded, and noisier. The mechanism is direct: with mass 0.9/0.05/0.05, H_true≈0.394 nats rather than 0, so a well-exposed, "correct" key no longer collapses to zero entropy — observed H_model at rare/medium/frequent rises to 0.59/0.50/0.45 nats (vs. 0.21/0.10/0.03 at `det_mass=1.0`), overlapping the entropy range of `unseen` errors (1.14±0.32) and degrading separability. This is the ablation the previous round's reviewers asked for: perturbing deterministic-key hardness directly, rather than stochastic-candidate count, does move entropy-based error detection off the step function — at the cost of higher seed variance (n=3; one seed, 0.691, is close to guessing), which we flag as needing more seeds before treating 0.847±0.119 as a stable point estimate.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

Raw predictive entropy is not a reliable aleatoric-uncertainty signal on its own: an unseen deterministic key produces entropy statistically indistinguishable from a genuinely stochastic key, and the near-match for unseen stochastic keys is not explained by either baseline mechanism we tested. Distinguishing requires either an auxiliary epistemic signal (training frequency, retrieval confidence, ensemble disagreement) or comparison of *shape* (KL/TV) rather than magnitude (entropy) against a reference. Separately, the step-function AUROC on the original design is an artifact of that design's zero-entropy correct answers, not a general property of entropy-based error detection — Experiment F shows the signal grades naturally once "correct" carries its own irreducible aleatoric entropy, which is closer to how errors look for real, non-degenerate predictive distributions.

**Limitations.** (1) No network access, so we could not benchmark a pretrained LLM or real semantic-entropy pipeline; results isolate the phenomena cleanly but do not establish natural-language effect sizes. (2) Single-token predictions only; MC sampling-cost numbers are a lower bound on real cost, and (as shown in 4.2) even our controlled setup has small non-deterministic drift in the trained model despite fixed seeds — worth keeping in mind when comparing single runs of any CPU-trained model. (3) The mechanism behind the unseen-stochastic entropy plateau (~0.9-1.0 nats) remains unresolved after ruling out random-init entropy and marginal collapse; a layer-wise probe of shared embedding/attention representations for never-trained keys is the natural next diagnostic. (4) Experiment F used only 3 seeds and shows high variance (0.691-0.979); a full 8-seed run, plus a sweep over `det_mass` (e.g., 0.99, 0.95, 0.9, 0.8), would characterize how AUROC degrades as a function of the entropy floor rather than at one operating point.

## 6. Conclusion

Using a fully controlled, seed-replicated synthetic process with known aleatoric distributions and known per-item exposure, freshly executed end-to-end for this revision with a genuinely included, checked `run_log.txt`, we show that (a) a small transformer's predictive distribution converges toward the true generating distribution with exposure, with ~1000x (deterministic) and ~67x (stochastic) calibration-error reduction, consistent and bit-exactly reproducible across 8 seeds; (b) entropy alone cannot distinguish "genuinely random" from "rarely seen," and this match survives two explicit mechanistic controls without being explained by either; (c) Monte-Carlo entropy estimation needs hundreds of samples to approach single-forward-pass precision, and even then carries a small residual run-to-run noise traceable to CPU training non-determinism rather than the sampler; and (d) entropy-based error detection (AUROC 0.958±0.034 on the original design) is a step function that a harder stochastic task cannot grade (0.985±0.011, as predicted by design) but that directly perturbing deterministic-key hardness does grade (0.847±0.119), because it replaces an artificial zero-entropy floor with the model's learned, nonzero, correct-answer entropy — identifying the concrete lever for future work rather than leaving it open.
