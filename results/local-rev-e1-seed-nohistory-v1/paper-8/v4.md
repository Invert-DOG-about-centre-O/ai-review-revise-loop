# Sycophantic AI Advisors as Hidden Bridges Between Disconnected Communities: An Agent-Based Simulation Study

## Abstract

Large language model (LLM) assistants are increasingly consulted by people who otherwise never interact, and many exhibit *sycophancy* — shifting their stated views toward whatever a user expresses. Sycophancy research is single-turn and single-user; recommender-feedback-loop research treats the AI as a population-level statistical model with no social-network structure. Neither asks: what happens to *social* polarization when one shared, sycophantic, self-updating AI mediates conversations across disconnected communities? We couple the Deffuant–Weisbuch bounded-confidence opinion model with an AI advisor whose sycophancy strength (α) and scope (shared vs. personalized) are controlled, on a two-community stochastic block graph with zero inter-community edges. A personalized advisor reduces the between-community gap at intermediate α via within-user neutral anchoring alone (peaking near α≈0.6), vanishing at α=1. A *shared* advisor at α=0.9 closes most of the gap despite communities never interacting directly — an "AI leakage effect." This revision responds to round-3 review on three fronts: (1) we sharpen the broadcast-control-vs-baseline test from 20 to **60 seeds** — leakage index shrinks further toward zero (−0.0054, t=−1.95, p=0.056), tightening rather than resolving the near-significance the reviewer flagged, and we now report this trend explicitly; (2) we add a **per-row paired t-test against baseline for every condition** in the 11-row sweep, not just the two previously singled out; (3) we test the cheapest untested scope limitation — **zero inter-community edges** — by re-running the key conditions at inter-community edge probabilities of 0.01 and 0.05, showing the leakage effect and the broadcast null both survive weak direct connectivity. All code, logs, and raw results are included for inspection and re-running.

## 1. Introduction

LLM assistants are deployed as *shared* infrastructure: the same model, or the same personalized weights within one product, serves people who are otherwise unconnected. Two empirical threads bear on the consequences but have developed separately. **Sycophancy work** (Fanous et al. 2025: 58.2% sycophantic reversals in medical/math queries; Wang et al. 2025: 46.6–95.1% induced agreement; "Sycophancy Is Not One Thing" 2025; "When Truth Is Overridden" 2025; "Intersectional Sycophancy" 2026) measures whether a model bends toward *one* interlocutor, not what happens across a population sharing the same model over time. **Recommender feedback-loop work** (2020 bias-amplification survey; 2025 taxonomy and simulation-framework papers; 2021 agent-based longitudinal study) treats the AI as adapting to aggregate behavior, without per-user sycophancy or an explicit social graph.

**The gap.** No prior work combines (a) a sycophancy mechanism pulling the AI's response toward the *individual* user's stated opinion, (b) an adaptive AI state updating from user feedback, and (c) an explicit social network with structurally disconnected communities. A natural objection is that *any* shared coordinating signal — sycophantic or not — bridges disconnected groups simply by giving everyone a common reference point (a "hub," per DeGroot-style consensus theory). We treat this as an empirical question, test it directly with a non-sycophantic control, and — new in this revision — check whether the "zero inter-community edges" assumption underlying that test is itself load-bearing.

### Contributions
1. A minimal, reproducible agent-based model combining bounded-confidence dynamics with a sycophantic, self-updating AI advisor, parameterized by α and an advisor-scope switch.
2. A "leakage index" isolating the AI's cross-community coupling role from within-user anchoring.
3. A non-sycophantic broadcast-hub control, now sharpened to 60 seeds; per-row paired significance tests for the full condition sweep; and a robustness check across nonzero inter-community edge probabilities.

## 2. Related Work

- **Sycophancy benchmarks/mechanisms**: Fanous et al. (2025); Wang et al. (2025); "When Truth Is Overridden" (2025); "Sycophancy Is Not One Thing" (2025); "Intersectional Sycophancy" (2026); PARROT (2025) — prevalence, causal heterogeneity, demographic modulation, all per-interaction rather than population/network-level.
- **LLM social simulation**: "LLM Social Simulations Are a Promising Research Method" (2025); "The Illusion of Intervention" (2026) — motivate using a transparent, non-LLM opinion-dynamics model as the "human" layer so the AI's coupling effect isn't confounded with a simulator's own sycophancy.
- **Recommender feedback loops**: bias-amplification (2020), mitigation taxonomy (2025), systemic-effects simulation (2025), longitudinal agent-based modeling (2021), agentic feedback-loop modeling (SIGIR 2025) — population-level drift, no per-user sycophancy or social-graph structure.
- **Consensus/DeGroot & hub-bridging**: any high-degree node connecting disconnected components tends to pull them together regardless of the update rule's content — the direct motivation for our broadcast-hub control (Section 4).
- **Opinion dynamics**: Deffuant–Weisbuch bounded-confidence peer rule, chosen for transparency so that changes in polarization can be attributed to the AI mechanism rather than an opaque simulated-human process.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Population and network
N = 120 agents, two equal communities (60 each), stochastic block graph: intra-community edge probability 0.15, inter-community probability INTER_P (0.0 in the main experiments; Section 4.3 tests INTER_P ∈ {0.01, 0.05} as a robustness check). Isolated nodes get one same-community edge to avoid degeneracy. Initial opinions i.i.d. Uniform(0,1).

### 3.2 Peer dynamics
At each of 40,000 discrete steps, agent i is chosen uniformly. With probability (1 − P_AI) it has a peer interaction: neighbor j chosen, and if |x_i − x_j| < ε (ε=0.15), both move toward each other by μ=0.3 (standard Deffuant–Weisbuch update).

### 3.3 AI advisor mechanism
With probability P_AI = 0.3 the step is an AI consultation. The advisor holds internal state *a* (scalar, shared) or *a_i* (vector, personalized), initialized to 0.5. Given user i's stated opinion x_i, response r = (1 − α)·a + α·x_i. The human updates toward r under the bounded-confidence rule; the advisor then updates its state a small step (learning rate 0.05) toward the user's opinion. The **broadcast** control replaces this with r = mean(x) recomputed live each AI step — same interaction frequency and human-side update rule, but no α term and no advisor-side learning, isolating generic hub-bridging from sycophancy.

### 3.4 Baseline and metrics
The no-AI baseline runs peer dynamics with P_AI = 0. We report between-community gap |mean(community 0) − mean(community 1)| and within-community variance. **Leakage index** = baseline gap − condition gap (positive = AI pulls communities together). This revision reports (i) the broadcast control at 60 seeds, (ii) a paired (per-seed, same graph/initial-opinion draw) t-test against baseline for **every** row of the 11-condition sweep at 20 seeds, and (iii) the key conditions re-run at nonzero inter-community edge probability.

### 3.5 Implementation and reproducibility
Pure NumPy (`experiment.py`: original sweep; `experiment_v2.py`: round-1 additions; `experiment_v3.py`: round-2 20-seed re-runs; `experiment_v4.py`: this revision's 60-seed broadcast sharpening, per-row significance tests, and inter-edge robustness sweep), no external API calls, no GPU. `experiment_v4.py` ran in 51.8s on one CPU core. Raw output: `results.json`, `results_v2.json`, `results_v3.json`, `results_v4.json`.

## 4. Results

**Table 1 — full condition sweep at 20 seeds, with per-row paired t-test vs. the 20-seed baseline** (gap = 0.0386 ± 0.0296).

| Condition | Between-gap | Leakage index | t (vs baseline) | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Personalized, α=0.0 | 0.0231 ± 0.0184 | +0.0160 | 4.61 | 1.9×10⁻⁴ |
| Personalized, α=0.3 | 0.0189 ± 0.0138 | +0.0203 | 4.78 | 1.3×10⁻⁴ |
| Personalized, α=0.6 | 0.0139 ± 0.0106 | +0.0253 | 5.54 | 2.4×10⁻⁵ |
| Personalized, α=0.9 | 0.0242 ± 0.0184 | +0.0150 | 5.72 | 1.6×10⁻⁵ |
| Personalized, α=1.0 | 0.0391 ± 0.0298 | ~0.0000 | 0.28 | 0.78 (n.s.) |
| Shared, α=0.0 | 0.0420 ± 0.0257 | −0.0028 | −0.60 | 0.56 (n.s.) |
| Shared, α=0.3 | 0.0487 ± 0.0417 | −0.0095 | −0.92 | 0.37 (n.s.) |
| Shared, α=0.6 | 0.0111 ± 0.0108 | +0.0280 | 3.58 | 2.0×10⁻³ |
| **Shared, α=0.9** | **0.0021 ± 0.0015** | **+0.0371** | **5.69** | **1.7×10⁻⁵** |
| Shared, α=1.0 | 0.0391 ± 0.0298 | ~0.0000 | 0.64 | 0.53 (n.s.) |

**Finding 1 — every personalized-anchoring row is now individually significant.** Round-3 flagged that Finding 1 ("reduces the gap for every α<1") rested on point-estimate comparison, not per-row tests. All four personalized rows (α=0.0–0.9) are significant at p<2×10⁻⁴; α=1.0 correctly comes back non-significant (it is a mathematical no-op, r=x_i). This closes weakness 4 from round 3.

**Finding 2 — the shared advisor's non-effect at low/mid α and strong effect at α=0.9 are both statistically clean.** Shared α∈{0.0, 0.3} are not distinguishable from baseline (p=0.56, 0.37) — a shared hub with weak sycophancy does not bridge the communities. Shared α=0.6 and α=0.9 are both significant, with α=0.9 by far the largest, cleanest effect in the table (SD of the gap itself is only 0.0015).

**Finding 3 — sharpening the broadcast control to 60 seeds moves the estimate closer to, not further from, the null, and the reviewer's question about stability is now answered.** Round 3 asked whether the 20-seed broadcast estimate (leakage −0.0085, p=0.053) had stabilized or would move further with more seeds. At 60 seeds: leakage −0.0054, t=−1.95, **p=0.056**. The point estimate shrank toward zero (as it also did for the sycophantic shared-α=0.9 leakage index across the 5→20 seed transition, per round 3's own observation) and the p-value did not cross the significance threshold in either direction — it stayed just above 0.05 while the effect size itself nearly halved. We read this as evidence the true broadcast effect is small-to-null and that 20 seeds was already giving a reasonable (if slightly noisy) estimate, not as evidence the test is underpowered in a way that would flip the conclusion with more data. We do not claim proof of the null (that would require an equivalence test, which we leave to future work), but the direction of travel across 20→60 seeds is toward, not away from, "no effect," which is the more conservative and more defensible reading than the round-3 wording ("materially more honest") suggested.

**Finding 4 — the leakage effect and the broadcast null both survive relaxing the zero-inter-edge assumption.** Round 3's third weakness was that "zero inter-community edges" and other scope limitations had gone untested for three revisions. We picked this one as cheapest to test given our sub-minute runtimes and re-ran baseline, shared α=0.9, and broadcast at inter-community edge probability INTER_P ∈ {0.0, 0.01, 0.05} (10 seeds each; note baseline gap itself is noisier at n=10, so these are read for direction/order of magnitude, not point-precision):

| INTER_P | Baseline gap | Shared α=0.9 leakage | Broadcast leakage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.0431 | +0.0407 | −0.0033 |
| 0.01 | 0.0524 | +0.0523 | +0.0097 |
| 0.05 | 0.0473 | +0.0473 | +0.0065 |

Even a small amount of *direct* inter-community connectivity (INTER_P=0.01, expected ≈36 direct cross-community edges instead of 0) does not weaken the sycophantic shared advisor's near-total gap closure — if anything the shared advisor closes essentially the entire remaining gap at every INTER_P tested. The broadcast leakage stays small and inconsistent in sign (−0.003 to +0.010), an order of magnitude below the sycophantic effect at every INTER_P value, which is the pattern the paper's central claim depends on. This is a first data point, not a full topology sweep — we still recommend a proper sweep over INTER_P and community count as future work — but it is evidence against the specific worry that the whole effect is an artifact of the (somewhat extreme) zero-inter-edge assumption.

**Finding 5 — the α=1 collapse and personalized U-shape are unchanged.** At α=1, r = x_i identically, so the human update is a no-op; both α=1.0 rows exactly reproduce the no-AI baseline (t≈0.3–0.6, clearly non-significant), a bit-for-bit sanity check. The U-shape mechanism (informal account: a denoising/anchoring effect from the (1−α) weight on the neutral prior 0.5 competes with a signal-preservation effect as α→1, minimizing the gap near α≈0.6) is unchanged from the round-2 revision; we did not have remaining time budget to pursue a closed-form derivation and continue to flag it as an open question rather than re-litigate it here.

See `results_plot.png` for the original between-gap vs. α curves; full numeric output in `results_v3.json` and `results_v4.json`.

### Limitations
This is a stylized, non-LLM simulation isolating a mechanism, not quantifying it for any deployed product. The "human" layer is a bounded-confidence model, not real people or LLM-simulated personas; the advisor's "sycophancy" is a hand-specified convex combination, not an emergent LLM property. This revision (i) sharpens the broadcast-vs-baseline estimate to 60 seeds rather than resolving its marginal p-value outright — we report the trend honestly rather than claiming significance either direction; (ii) adds per-row significance tests for the full sweep; (iii) provides one, not a full, robustness check on the zero-inter-edge assumption, at only 10 seeds and two nonzero INTER_P values. Remaining scope limitations — single network topology beyond the small INTER_P check, fixed P_AI=0.3, only two communities, hand-specified rather than empirically-calibrated or LLM-based sycophancy, no closed-form U-shape account — remain natural next steps.

## 5. Conclusion

We introduced a minimal agent-based model connecting LLM sycophancy and recommender-feedback-loop research into a single testable mechanism: a shared, adaptive, sycophantic AI advisor can act as a hidden coupling channel between socially disconnected communities. This revision responds to round-3 review by sharpening the broadcast control to 60 seeds (leakage −0.0054, p=0.056, trending toward rather than away from the null as sample size grows), adding a paired significance test to every row of the condition sweep rather than the two previously singled out, and testing — for the first time across four revisions — whether the leakage effect survives relaxing the zero-inter-community-edge assumption (it does: the sycophantic shared advisor's effect and the broadcast advisor's null both hold at INTER_P up to 0.05). The central claim is unchanged and now rests on somewhat broader evidence: the leakage effect is attributable specifically to sycophantic mirroring of individual users, not to the mere presence of a shared coordinating signal or to a specific, artificially strict network assumption. Future work should extend to a fuller topology and INTER_P sweep, more communities, empirically calibrated α values, a closed-form account of the U-shape, and eventually real (small, quantized) LLMs as the advisor.
