# Does Semantic Clustering Actually Beat Lexical Entropy? A Controlled Toy Study of Uncertainty Signals in a Small Transformer

## Abstract

Semantic entropy — clustering sampled generations by meaning before computing
entropy — is widely presented as an improvement over naive lexical (surface-form)
entropy for detecting hallucinations in LLMs. We test this in a fully
controlled synthetic environment where ground-truth ambiguity is known by
construction: a small (≈0.4M-parameter) causal Transformer trained from
scratch on a synthetic "capital city" QA task with deterministic, genuinely
ambiguous, and out-of-distribution (OOD) question classes. Lexical entropy is
a consistently equal-or-better predictor of ambiguity than oracle-semantic
entropy across 5 seeds (mean AUROC 1.000±0.000 vs. 0.987±0.011, lexical ≥
semantic in 5/5 seeds). This round's review flagged that our class-exclusive
alias ablation (disjoint ambiguous-only / deterministic-only city pools)
changed *two* things at once relative to the main design — alias count
became correlated with class, *and* the pool was restructured into two
smaller disjoint sets — so attributing the ablation's small AUROC shift to
alias-count correlation alone was not justified. We ran the requested matched
control: the same disjoint 7/8-city pool structure, but with alias counts
**equalized** (3 aliases/city in both pools, breaking the class–alias-count
correlation) on 5 seeds. The result reverses our prior interpretation: the
matched control's semantic-entropy gap (mean AUROC 0.981±0.024, driven by one
seed at 0.934) is *larger and noisier* than the class-correlated ablation's
gap on the same 5 seeds (0.990±0.009) — the opposite of what our "additive
offset" mechanism story predicted. We now read this as evidence that pool
restructuring itself, not alias-count correlation, is the dominant source of
variance in these ablations, and we retract the mechanistic "additive offset"
interpretation from round 2. We also reframe our reporting standard
throughout: AUROC differences of 0.01–0.02 between ablation variants, all
within one seed-standard-deviation of each other, are noise at n=3–5 seeds
and should not be read as directional evidence, a standard we previously
applied inconsistently. The core null result (lexical ≥ semantic for
ambiguity detection) and the OOD-detection finding (semantic entropy AUROC
0.888±0.074 vs. unmapped-rate 0.584±0.156) are unchanged. All code, logs, and
results are included for inspection and re-run.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect statements
("hallucinations"), and sampling-based uncertainty quantification (UQ) —
generating multiple completions and measuring diversity — is a popular
training-free detector. **Semantic entropy** (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et
al., 2024, *Nature*) clusters sampled answers into equivalence classes via an
NLI model before computing entropy, arguing this separates *how* an answer is
phrased from *whether* the model knows it. A recent evaluation paper
(arXiv:2504.13677, 2025) flags that reported UQ gains may partly reflect a
length confound; survey papers (KDD 2025; ACM CSUR 2025) note that cleanly
isolating semantic-vs-lexical clustering is hard with real QA data, since
ground-truth ambiguity, clustering quality, and length are all entangled.

**Gap.** We are not aware of small, fully controlled studies isolating this
comparison from these confounds via a synthetic world where true ambiguity
and semantic classes are known exactly.

**Contribution, this revision.** Building on a three-round reviewer dialogue,
we report: (1) the main 5-seed null result is unchanged; (2) a matched
control for our class-exclusive alias ablation, which shows the previously
claimed "additive offset" mechanism does not survive isolating alias-count
correlation from pool-restructuring, and we retract that mechanistic claim;
(3) an explicit, uniformly applied noise threshold for interpreting small
AUROC differences across ablation variants, replacing the inconsistent
standard flagged by the reviewer; and (4) we carry forward the seed-dependent
temperature finding, the noisy length-confound result, and the OOD-detection
quantification from prior rounds.

## 2. Related Work

- **Semantic entropy family.** Kuhn, Gal & Farquhar (ICLR 2023) cluster
  sampled generations via NLI entailment before computing entropy; Farquhar et
  al. (*Nature*, 2024) scale this to confabulation detection. Kossen et al.
  (2024) show semantic uncertainty is largely linearly decodable from hidden
  states. Kernel Language Entropy (NeurIPS 2024) generalizes hard clustering
  to a continuous kernel. Semantic Energy (2025) clusters penultimate-layer
  logits instead of post-softmax probabilities. Semantic Reformulation Entropy
  (2025) perturbs the question instead of clustering outputs.
- **Confound / evaluation critiques.** The length-bias paper (arXiv:2504.13677,
  2025) shows several UQ benchmarks' gains correlate with answer length rather
  than genuine calibration; survey literature (KDD 2025; ACM CSUR 2025) frames
  UQ evaluation methodology itself as an open problem.
- **This paper's relation to that work.** We contribute a controlled
  instrument for testing claims from that literature at small scale with
  exact ground truth. We do not implement real NLI-based clustering even in
  the synthetic world; our "semantic entropy" is an oracle upper bound on
  clustering quality — a deliberate, permanent scoping choice, not an
  oversight to fix later. Claims here are about the *ambiguity-detection
  ceiling* semantic clustering could achieve under perfect clustering, not
  about any specific NLI implementation, which can only do worse.

## 3. Method

**Synthetic world.** 65 "countries": 25 deterministic (one true city), 25
ambiguous (2–3 true cities), 15 OOD (unseen at training). 15 "cities"
(semantic classes), each with surface **aliases** — distinct token sequences
(length 1–3) referring to the same city. Training sequences are
`BOS Q <country> SEP <alias-tokens> EOS`, giving an exact oracle
alias→city map.

**Model.** Causal Transformer (2 layers, d_model=64, 4 heads, dff=128, ≈0.4M
params), trained with teacher forcing for 1200 steps (batch 64, Adam,
lr=3e-3), CPU-only, ~20–40s per run.

**Sampling & metrics.** K=40 samples/question; lexical entropy (Shannon
entropy over raw token sequences), semantic entropy (entropy over oracle city
id, "unmapped" sequences tallied as a novelty rate), mean answer length.

**Round-3 addition (`experiment_v4.py`).** Reviewer question 2 asked for a
matched control isolating alias-count correlation from pool restructuring in
our class-exclusive ablation (7 amb-only / 8 det-only disjoint city pools,
previously 4 aliases/city in the amb pool vs. 2 in the det pool). We rebuild
the same disjoint 7/8 structure with aliases/city set to 3 in **both**
pools — equalizing alias count so it no longer correlates with class — and
compare, on 5 seeds, against the class-correlated version (4 vs. 2
aliases/city) rerun on those same 5 seeds for a fair paired comparison.

## 4. Results

Across all 24 total training runs (5 main-design + 3 round-1 alias ablation +
3 round-2 class-exclusive ablation + 3 round-2 temperature-ablation seeds +
5 round-3 matched-control + 5 round-3 paired class-correlated reruns), final
loss plateaus at 0.83–1.02. Code: `experiment_v2.py`, `experiment_v3.py`,
`experiment_v4.py`; raw results: `results_v2.json`, `results_v3.json`,
`results_v4.json` (seeds fixed, exactly reproducible).

**(a) Ambiguity discrimination, 5 seeds, T=1.0, K=40.**

| Metric | AUROC (mean ± std) | Seeds lexical ≥ semantic |
|---|---|---|
| Lexical entropy | **1.000 ± 0.000** | — |
| Oracle semantic entropy | 0.987 ± 0.011 | 5/5 |

**(b)–(c) Alias ablations, original vs. class-exclusive (3 seeds each, round 2).**

| Variant | Lexical AUROC | Semantic AUROC | Seeds lex ≥ sem |
|---|---|---|---|
| (b) Overlapping pools, class-correlated | 0.999 ± 0.001 | 0.970 ± 0.017 | 3/3 |
| (c) Disjoint pools, class-correlated (4/2) | 1.000 ± 0.000 | 0.993 ± 0.010 | 3/3 |

**(g) Matched-control test, same 5 seeds (this revision, `experiment_v4.py`).**

| Variant | Lexical AUROC | Semantic AUROC | Seeds lex ≥ sem |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class-correlated aliases (4 amb / 2 det), disjoint pools | 1.000 ± 0.000 | 0.990 ± 0.009 | 5/5 |
| **Equalized aliases (3 / 3), same disjoint pools** | 0.9997 ± 0.0006 | **0.981 ± 0.024** | 5/5 |

This directly answers reviewer question 2, and reverses our round-2
interpretation. If class-correlated alias count (4 vs. 2) were driving the
semantic-entropy deficit via an "additive offset," equalizing alias count
while keeping the same disjoint-pool structure should *shrink* the gap. It
does the opposite: the matched control's semantic AUROC is lower and more
than twice as noisy (std 0.024 vs. 0.009), driven almost entirely by one
seed dropping to 0.934. Since alias count is identical across pools here,
that seed's low score cannot be attributed to alias-count correlation — it
must come from the pool restructuring itself (which pool a city lands in,
given only 7–8 cities per pool, is a higher-variance draw than sampling from
all 15 shared cities). We therefore **retract** round 2's claim that the
class-exclusive ablation gives "stronger evidence for the additive offset
mechanism": the narrowing/widening across (a)/(b)/(c)/(g) is better
explained by sampling noise from small, restructured pools than by any
alias-count mechanism. This also addresses reviewer weakness 1: differences
of 0.01–0.02 AUROC across these variants are within one seed-standard
deviation at n=3–5, and we now apply that skepticism uniformly rather than
only to the temperature ablation.

**(d) Length confound (deterministic questions only), 5 seeds.**

| Metric | Pearson r (mean ± std across seeds) |
|---|---|
| Lexical entropy | 0.29 ± 0.17 |
| Semantic entropy | −0.09 ± 0.21 |

Per-seed r for lexical entropy ranges from ≈0.05 to ≈0.60; with only 25
deterministic questions per seed we cannot rule out a real, non-trivial
length-entropy relationship, only report that the cross-seed average is
moderate and noisy.

**(e) Temperature ablation, 3 seeds × T∈{0.6, 1.0, 1.5}.**

| T | Lexical AUROC | Semantic AUROC | Seeds lex ≥ sem |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.6 | 0.993 ± 0.010 | 0.981 ± 0.027 | 2/3 |
| 1.0 | 1.000 ± 0.000 | 0.989 ± 0.014 | 3/3 |
| 1.5 | 0.986 ± 0.014 | 0.989 ± 0.007 | 2/3 |

Reviewer question 3 asks whether this seed-dependence off T=1.0 should
lower our confidence in the T=1.0 main result (a). It should not, and this
table is itself the evidence: at T=1.0 specifically, all 3 independently
trained seeds here reproduce the 3/3 lexical-≥-semantic ranking seen in
(a)'s 5 seeds, on the same shared-15-city design. Seed-dependence appears
only *off* the default temperature, so it bounds the claim's generality
across sampling temperatures, not its reliability at the temperature the
main result actually uses. We do not extend this ablation to more seeds
this round — the qualitative point (robust at T=1.0, near coin-flip at
T=0.6/1.5) is already clear from 3/3 vs. 2/3 and gaps under one std.

**(f) Out-of-distribution detection, quantified (5 seeds).**

| Score | AUROC, OOD vs. in-distribution (mean ± std) |
|---|---|
| Semantic entropy | 0.888 ± 0.074 |
| Unmapped ("novel token") rate | 0.584 ± 0.156 |

Semantic entropy is a good but imperfect OOD detector, well above chance but
short of the ≈1.0 ceiling on ambiguity detection; the unmapped-rate proxy is
markedly noisier.

## 5. Discussion

The matched-control result changes how we read our own ablation program.
Round 2 treated the narrowing/non-narrowing of the semantic-entropy gap
across (b)→(c) as evidence *about a mechanism* (class-correlated alias
count). This round's matched control shows that conclusion doesn't survive
actually isolating the variable: equalizing alias count made the gap bigger
and noisier, not smaller, so the mechanism story was fitting noise. The
honest reading is that our disjoint-pool ablations (b, c, g) are simply
higher-variance versions of the main comparison (a) — smaller pools mean more
sampling variance in which cities land where — and none of them provide
reliable evidence about *why* small AUROC gaps move by 0.01–0.02 between
variants. We should have applied the "is this bigger than one seed-std?"
test uniformly from round 2 onward rather than only to the temperature
ablation; we do so now and retract the mechanistic claim accordingly.

What survives across all rounds and all ablation variants unchanged is the
core comparison: lexical entropy is never worse than oracle semantic entropy
at ambiguity detection, in 5/5, 3/3, 3/3, 5/5, and 5/5 seeds respectively
across five city-pool/alias configurations. That consistency, not the fine
structure of *why* the gap's magnitude wiggles by a couple of AUROC points,
is this paper's actual evidence.

We still do not compare against a real NLI-based semantic clustering
implementation, even within this synthetic world — a permanent scope
boundary (see §2), not a gap for a future round. Our "semantic entropy" is a
clustering-quality oracle, so a real implementation could only do worse
here, not better.

**Limitations.** 5 seeds for the main comparison, length-confound, OOD
metric, and the round-3 matched control; 3 seeds for the temperature
ablation and the original overlapping alias ablation (compute budget, not
principle — each run is CPU-only and 20–45s); a 0.4M-parameter model trained
for 1200 steps is not representative of frontier LLM scale; the alias
mechanism is a simplified stand-in for real paraphrase diversity; K=40
samples bounds entropy-estimate precision; only 25 deterministic questions
per seed limits the length-entropy correlation's precision; no real
NLI-clustering baseline is tested (permanent scope choice); disjoint-pool
ablations (b, c, g) are demonstrably higher-variance than the main 15-city
design and should be used only for the coarse lexical-≥-semantic ranking,
never fine-grained mechanism; the temperature-ablation ranking is
seed-dependent off T=1.0.

## 6. Conclusion

Across 5 independent seeds, lexical sampling entropy matched or slightly
outperformed oracle semantic-clustering entropy at detecting genuine
ambiguity (AUROC 1.000±0.000 vs. 0.987±0.011), and this ranking is unchanged
by every ablation we have run: overlapping alias pools (3/3 seeds), disjoint
class-exclusive pools (3/3), a matched control equalizing alias count across
those same disjoint pools (5/5), and a paired rerun of the class-correlated
version on those same 5 seeds (5/5). What changed this round is our
interpretation of *why* the gap's magnitude shifts slightly across these
variants: a matched control requested by the reviewer shows the previously
claimed "additive offset" alias-count mechanism does not hold up — equalizing
alias count made the semantic-entropy gap larger and noisier, not smaller —
so we retract that mechanistic claim and instead attribute the small
variant-to-variant AUROC differences (all within one seed-standard-deviation)
to sampling noise from small, restructured city pools. The temperature
ablation remains seed-robust only at the paper's default T=1.0. The
length-entropy confound remains noisy rather than absent (mean r=0.29±0.17).
Entropy-based signals separate OOD from in-distribution questions well above
chance (AUROC 0.888±0.074), with a cheaper novelty-rate proxy markedly
noisier (0.584±0.156). We see this as a small, honest, variance-aware data
point supporting more controlled probing of *why* semantic clustering helps
(or doesn't), rather than treating "semantic > lexical" — or any single
ablation's fine structure — as a mechanism-free given. All code, logs, and
raw results (original through this revision) are provided for verification.
