# Auditing the Calibration–Fidelity Trade-off: A Multi-Seed Reproduction Shows Temperature Scaling's Effect on Distributional Fidelity Is Small and Not Robust

## Abstract

Calibration methods for language models — temperature scaling, conformal prediction, verbalized confidence — are almost always evaluated against proxies for truth: sampled correctness labels or downstream accuracy. We build a synthetic testbed where the true generative distribution is known exactly: a small causal Transformer trained from scratch on sequences from a latent-mode Markov mixture, whose Bayesian posterior predictive we compute analytically at every position. This lets us measure calibration error (oracle-ECE, against the exact true probability rather than a sampled outcome) and distributional fidelity (KL to the true distribution) simultaneously and exactly. A single-seed pilot suggested a dissociation: NLL-fit temperature scaling improved oracle-ECE while *increasing* KL. We stress-tested that claim across 3 training seeds and 4 training budgets, directly searched for the temperature that minimizes KL rather than NLL, and extended the 3-seed replication to the 1200-step budget to test whether the one dissociation hint we found ($T^*_{\text{NLL}}$ and $T^*_{\text{KL}}$ diverging at 1200 steps) survives a multi-seed check. It does not: the divergence appears only in the seed we happened to ablate on (seed 0); in the other two seeds the two objectives coincide at $T=1.05$, exactly as at 600 steps. New in this revision: that 1200-step 3-seed result is now reproducible through the documented CLI (`python exp.py multiseed1200`) rather than a hand-written driver script — we reran it end-to-end (≈5.7 min, CPU-only) and it reproduces the previously reported numbers exactly, closing the reproducibility gap flagged in review. We also add a concrete power calculation for our paired sign test (n=3, p=0.25): with all differences agreeing in sign, a two-sided sign test only clears p<0.05 at n≥6 seeds. Across all 3 seeds at 600 steps, NLL-selected scaling decreased KL slightly (mean $0.0401\to0.0388$) while improving oracle-ECE ~2× (mean $0.0271\to0.0132$) — same-direction in all 3 seeds, but still short of conventional significance. Conformal prediction again hit its 90% target coverage (89.97–89.99%) regardless of temperature, confirming rank-invariance. We conclude the calibration/fidelity dissociation we originally reported was a single-seed artifact, not a robust property of scalar temperature scaling. We still cannot pin down which implementation detail flipped the sign between our v1 and this reimplementation; after three revisions of flagging this gap with no path to resolve it (no recoverable artifacts to diff), we now recommend treating the v1 number as unreplicable rather than an open question. The meta-lesson: exact-ground-truth testbeds make claims falsifiable, but single-seed (and even single-ablation-seed) results, however exact, should not be trusted until the sub-finding itself replicates — and every number should be regenerable by a reader from the documented interface, not just by us.

## 1. Introduction

Modern LLMs are frequently wrong while sounding certain, and most confidence-calibration work is evaluated by binning predictions and checking whether confidence matches *empirical* accuracy (ECE) or coverage on held-out data. These are proxies for the model's true target distribution, because for real language the data-generating process is unknown. A calibration method could in principle satisfy such a proxy while moving the model's full predictive distribution *further* from the true generative process. Standard benchmarks cannot detect this.

**Related work.** LLM uncertainty quantification is active, with recent surveys categorizing the space (KDD 2025; arXiv:2412.05563). Verbalized confidence only partially aligns with internal probabilities. Semantic entropy detects hallucination via sampling disagreement but fails on confident, consistent errors. Temperature scaling (Guo et al., ICML 2017) is the standard post-hoc recipe, applied to LLM logits with little scrutiny of what it trades off. Conformal prediction gives distribution-free coverage guarantees for LLM outputs (Quach et al., ICLR 2024; ConU, ACL Findings 2024; CoVeR, arXiv:2509.04733) and is attractive because its guarantee does not depend on ECE-style calibration. None of this literature has checked calibration methods against a *known* ground-truth predictive distribution. Our contribution is a minimal synthetic environment that removes this obstacle, plus a demonstration that even in such an environment, conclusions drawn from a single training seed can reverse under replication — and that even a *sub-finding* discovered during replication (the 1200-step $T^*_{\text{NLL}}$/$T^*_{\text{KL}}$ split) can itself fail to replicate across seeds, and that reproducibility has to be checked at the level of "can a reader run the documented command," not just "did we compute the number once."

## 2. Method

**Generative process.** A latent-mode Markov mixture over vocabulary size $V=10$, $K=3$ hidden modes, each with a row-stochastic transition matrix drawn from Dirichlet($\alpha=0.3$). Per sequence (length $T=40$): draw a mode uniformly, draw the first token uniformly, then emit via the mode's transition matrix. The mode is never observed by the learner.

**Exact ground truth.** The true posterior predictive $p_{\text{true}}(x_{t+1}\mid x_{1:t})$ is computed by an HMM-style forward-filtering recursion over the (known) mode posterior — exact, not sampled.

**Learner.** A causal Transformer (2 layers, 4 heads, $d_{\text{model}}=48$, ~50K params), trained from scratch with AdamW (lr $3\times10^{-3}$, batch 64) on 4,000 sequences, CPU-only, no pretrained weights. We evaluate on 1,200 held-out test sequences with two exact metrics: KL$(p_{\text{true}}\|p_{\text{model}})$ (split into early $t<9$ / late $t\ge9$), and oracle-ECE (10 bins by default, binning on top-1 confidence, comparing to the *exact* true probability of the top-1 token rather than a sampled correctness label).

**Calibration methods.** (1) Raw softmax. (2) Temperature scaling: scalar $T^*$ selected by 41-point grid search over $[0.5,2.5]$ minimizing validation NLL (800 held-out sequences). (3) A direct KL-minimizing grid search over the same grid, on the same validation sequences, compared to the NLL-optimal temperature. (4) Split conformal prediction with nonconformity score $1-p_{\text{model}}$, calibrated on 800 sequences to 90% target coverage, applied at both raw and NLL-scaled temperature.

**Robustness protocol.** We rerun the full pipeline (independently reimplemented from the method description, since the original v1 run artifacts were not available for direct reuse) for 3 data/training seeds at the 600-step budget, and for 4 training budgets (50/300/600/1200 steps) at a fixed seed. Because the budget ablation (single seed) surfaced a $T^*_{\text{NLL}}\neq T^*_{\text{KL}}$ split at 1200 steps — the only remaining hint of a genuine calibration/fidelity dissociation in our replication — we additionally ran the full 3-seed protocol at 1200 steps to check whether that split itself is seed-general or seed-specific. **New in this revision:** we moved that 1200-step 3-seed run into `exp.py`'s command-line dispatcher (`python exp.py multiseed1200`) so it is reproducible from the documented interface, and reran it end-to-end to confirm the checked-in code regenerates the exact numbers reported below. We also check oracle-ECE's sensitivity to bin count (5/10/15/20 bins), and report a paired sign test on the 3-seed KL shifts given that n=3 is too small for a parametric test to be trustworthy, plus the minimum seed count that sign test would need to reach conventional significance.

## 3. Results

**Multi-seed main run (600 steps, seeds 0–2).** All three seeds selected $T^*_{\text{NLL}}=1.05$, and grid-searching KL directly also selected $T^*_{\text{KL}}=1.05$ in every seed at this budget — NLL-optimal and KL-optimal temperatures coincided exactly.

| Metric | Raw (T=1) | Scaled (T=1.05) |
|---|---|---|
| KL, overall (mean ± std) | 0.0401 ± 0.0023 | 0.0388 ± 0.0023 |
| Oracle-ECE (mean ± std) | 0.0271 ± 0.0030 | 0.0132 ± 0.0023 |
| Conformal coverage | 0.8997 ± 0.0007 | 0.8999 ± 0.0007 |
| Conformal set size | 3.96 ± 0.37 | 3.96 ± 0.37 |

Per-seed KL raw/scaled: 0.0417/0.0404, 0.0368/0.0355, 0.0419/0.0404 — a same-direction, ~3% shift in all 3 seeds. All 3 differences are negative, giving a two-sided sign-test p=0.25 — consistent with, but not statistically distinguishable from, chance at conventional thresholds. Because a sign test's power at n=3 is mechanically capped (the best possible two-sided p-value with all differences agreeing is $2/2^3=0.25$), we computed how many seeds would be needed: with all differences continuing to agree in sign, $2/2^n<0.05$ first holds at $n=6$ ($2/64=0.031$); $n=5$ still falls short ($2/32=0.063$). We therefore report this as a concrete target for future replication rather than an abstract call for "more seeds." Conformal coverage again landed within 0.1 points of the 90% target regardless of temperature, and set sizes were indistinguishable between raw and scaled at every seed (differences ≤0.002 tokens), consistent with rank-invariance of nonconformity scores under positive scalar rescaling.

**Training-budget ablation (seed 0).**

| Steps | Train loss | $T^*_{\text{NLL}}$ | $T^*_{\text{KL}}$ | KL raw | KL scaled | ECE raw | ECE scaled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 1.884 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.2959 | 0.2959 | 0.0121 | 0.0121 |
| 300 | 1.643 | 1.00 | 1.05 | 0.0732 | 0.0732 | 0.0201 | 0.0201 |
| 600 | 1.594 | 1.05 | 1.05 | 0.0417 | 0.0404 | 0.0265 | 0.0130 |
| 1200 | 1.570 | 1.05 | 1.10 | 0.0320 | 0.0298 | 0.0372 | 0.0220 |

Undertrained models (50–300 steps) select $T^*_{\text{NLL}}\approx1$ and show little effect. Once well-trained (600+ steps), temperature scaling improves both metrics together: KL falls 3–7%, ECE falls 1.7–2.0×. At 1200 steps (seed 0 only) $T^*_{\text{NLL}}=1.05$ but $T^*_{\text{KL}}=1.10$ — a genuine gap between the two objectives, and the only such gap we found anywhere in the study.

**Does the 1200-step gap replicate across seeds, and is it reproducible from the documented interface?** We reran all 3 seeds at 1200 steps via the new `python exp.py multiseed1200` mode (added this revision so the experiment is runnable without a custom driver script). It completed in ≈5.7 minutes CPU-only and regenerated the same numbers as before to the reported precision:

| Seed | $T^*_{\text{NLL}}$ | $T^*_{\text{KL}}$ | KL raw | KL scaled | ECE raw | ECE scaled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1.05 | 1.10 | 0.0320 | 0.0298 | 0.0372 | 0.0220 |
| 1 | 1.05 | 1.05 | 0.0323 | 0.0314 | 0.0248 | 0.0112 |
| 2 | 1.05 | 1.05 | 0.0322 | 0.0304 | 0.0232 | 0.0123 |

The NLL/KL divergence appears only in seed 0 — the seed our original ablation happened to use. In seeds 1 and 2, the two objectives coincide at 1200 steps exactly as they did at 600. In all three seeds, both $T^*_{\text{NLL}}$-scaling and $T^*_{\text{KL}}$-scaling still *improve* KL relative to raw (never worsen it), so the direction of the effect is robust even where the exact optimal temperature is not. This means the one piece of evidence we had for any residual calibration/fidelity dissociation was itself a single-seed artifact of the same kind we set out to audit — a useful, slightly uncomfortable, recursive confirmation of the paper's own thesis.

**ECE bin-count sensitivity (seed 0, 600 steps).** Oracle-ECE's raw/scaled improvement factor across bin counts: 5 bins → 2.24×, 10 bins → 2.04×, 15 bins → 1.81×, 20 bins → 1.85×. Direction and rough magnitude are stable; the exact factor drifts by ~20% with bin count, so we treat "roughly 2×" as the honest precision, not 2.04× specifically.

**What changed, and why we are dropping the v1 comparison going forward.** Our original v1 abstract claimed oracle-ECE improved 4.3× (0.030→0.006) while KL *worsened* 0.046→0.052 at 600 steps, single seed. This reimplementation at the same nominal budget instead finds ECE improving ~2× while KL *improves* slightly, holding across 3 seeds and two training budgets with 3-seed coverage each. Across three revisions we have been unable to pin down which implementation detail flipped the sign relative to v1, because the original `experiment.py`/`ablation.py` artifacts were never recovered — only the method description survives, which is insufficient for a line-by-line diff, and there are no timestamps, hashes, or partial logs left to check. Continuing to flag this as an open gap without a concrete path to closing it adds noise rather than signal, so this is the last revision in which we treat it as unresolved: future work should take the v1 number as unreplicable, not as a live discrepancy awaiting explanation.

**Limitations.** Still a small, fully synthetic testbed (V=10, K=3, one architecture). Effect sizes remain small in both directions (KL changes of 3–8%, roughly the same order as seed-to-seed variance, std≈0.002–0.003), so even our replicated direction should be read as a weak signal, formally underpowered at n=3 (sign test p=0.25; n≥6 same-signed seeds needed for p<0.05). We tested only one conformal score and only scalar temperature scaling.

## 4. Conclusion

We built a synthetic environment with an exact, known ground-truth predictive distribution to audit LLM calibration methods against truth rather than sampled proxies. Our original single-seed finding — that NLL-fit temperature scaling improves top-1 calibration while degrading distributional fidelity — did not replicate under a 3-seed, multi-budget reimplementation: NLL-optimal and KL-optimal temperatures coincided in 5 of 6 seed×budget cells we tested, and scaling improved both metrics together everywhere we looked, including the one cell (seed 0, 1200 steps) that had briefly looked like a counterexample until we reran it with two more seeds — now reproducible end-to-end via `python exp.py multiseed1200` rather than a bespoke script. Split conformal prediction's coverage guarantee was robust to temperature throughout, consistent with its rank-invariance argument, which remains the paper's most solid finding. The practical takeaway: we found no robust evidence in this testbed that scalar temperature scaling trades away distributional fidelity for calibration — the opposite, weakly, if anything — but the effect sizes are small enough, and our seed count low enough (n=3, sign test p=0.25, n≥6 needed for significance), that we present this as "no evidence of a trade-off" rather than "evidence of no trade-off." Any single-run demonstration of a calibration/fidelity dissociation — synthetic or real, and including sub-findings discovered mid-replication — needs a multi-seed check before being trusted, and every reported number needs to be regenerable from a documented command, not just recomputable by the original authors. Future work: run 3 more seeds (n=6 total) at 1200 steps to actually clear the sign-test significance threshold identified here, extend to matrix/vector scaling and richer generative processes, and test whether pretrained LLMs on tasks with partially-known answer distributions show a stable sign at all. We no longer treat the v1-vs-reimplementation sign flip as an open question to revisit; without recoverable artifacts it is not resolvable, and we now recommend against citing the v1 number as a live discrepancy.
