# When Is Sampling Worth It? A Controlled Comparison of Token-Probability and Sampling-Based Uncertainty Signals for LLM Error Detection

## Abstract

A growing body of work on uncertainty quantification (UQ) for large language models argues that token-level probabilities are unreliable confidence signals — post-training alignment distorts them, and semantically equivalent answers can be phrased in many ways that fragment probability mass — and proposes sampling-based alternatives such as semantic entropy and self-consistency. These claims are typically validated on open-domain tasks where "correctness" is judged by another model or by human raters, confounding the uncertainty signal's quality with the judge's. We instead study a setting with **exact, judge-free ground truth**: a small transformer trained from scratch to perform multi-digit integer addition, where a generated answer is either the correct sum or not. On 400 held-out problems, we compare six confidence/uncertainty signals as error detectors (AUROC) and calibrated confidence estimates (ECE). Simple token-probability signals (AUROC 0.955) outperform sampling-based semantic entropy (0.930) and self-consistency agreement (0.933) at the sampling budget (K=8) used in prior work, and self-consistency majority voting yields no accuracy gain over greedy decoding (92.75% both). This revision fixes a methodological gap flagged in review: our K-ablation was previously a single unseeded draw per K, which we confirmed is unstable at the error counts available here. Rerun with 5 independently seeded repeats per K, the gap still narrows with K (semantic-entropy AUROC 0.76±0.06 at K=4 → 0.94±0.02 at K=32, vs. a token-probability ceiling of 0.96), but with substantial per-draw variance at low K the original table concealed. We also attempted to bootstrap CIs directly on a freshly trained primary-scale (3-digit) model, as review requested, but the retrained model reached only 21.5% held-out accuracy in the same 240s budget the original run used to reach 92.75% — evidence this session's compute throughput is not comparable to the original run's. We report this honestly rather than present CIs from a broken model, and still rely on a smaller, well-converged 2-digit companion model for CI and K-ablation evidence. The takeaway is unchanged: token probability is the cheaper default unless the task has genuine semantic multiplicity or the practitioner can afford K≈16+ samples estimated with enough repeats to trust; the primary 3-digit ranking remains a single-seed result.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect output, and because the failure mode looks identical to a correct answer at the surface level, detecting it requires some signal about the model's internal confidence. The default first tool is token-level probability. However, recent literature argues this signal is compromised: alignment/RLHF training rewards confident-sounding text regardless of correctness, and a single "meaning" can be expressed by many token sequences, so probability mass assigned to any one surface form understates true confidence.

This has motivated **sampling-based** UQ methods. Semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., Nature 2024) draws multiple generations at temperature, clusters them by semantic equivalence, and computes entropy over clusters rather than token sequences. Self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) samples multiple reasoning chains and majority-votes over final answers. Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024) approximate this signal from a single forward pass.

### Related work and the gap we address

Surveys of LLM UQ catalog token-based, sampling-based, and internal-state-based methods and report sampling/consistency-based methods generally dominate raw token probability on open-domain QA and reasoning benchmarks. A recurring limitation is evaluation methodology: ground-truth correctness for open-ended generation is itself uncertain, so most benchmarks use an LLM judge or human annotation — itself noisy — and mix tasks where sampling should help for principled reasons with the blanket recommendation "prefer sampling-based UQ," without isolating *when* token probability specifically fails.

We complement this literature with a small, fully controlled study: a synthetic task (integer addition) where correctness is a deterministic, judge-free function of the output and the answer space per prompt is a single canonical string, so we can ask cleanly whether sampling-based signals still beat token-probability signals when the theoretical reason sampling should help (semantic multiplicity) is absent by construction — and, in this second revision, whether our own robustness evidence for that claim holds up to closer scrutiny of its statistical reliability.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Task and model

We generate synthetic examples `"a+b="` → `"c"`, characters tokenized over `{0-9, +, =, <PAD>, <EOS>}`. The primary result (Section 3.1) uses `a, b ~ Uniform[0, 999]` and a 3-layer, 4-head, d_model=128 GPT-style decoder (≈0.6M params) trained for a 240s wall-clock CPU budget (3,937 steps, final training loss 0.046, 92.75% held-out accuracy on 400 problems). No outbound access to model hubs was available, which is why we train from scratch rather than probe a pretrained LLM (Section 5).

For the robustness follow-ups (Section 3.2–3.3), we use a smaller-scale companion model (`a, b ~ Uniform[0, 99]`, higher lr 2e-3, larger batch 256, final loss 0.14, 94.7% held-out accuracy, 8/150 held-out errors) that trains to full convergence within a shorter budget, so CI and K-ablation analyses run on a well-converged model rather than a rushed one. This is a scale change, not an architecture change, and results from it are reported as a separate companion analysis, not conflated with the primary table.

### 2.2 Confidence/uncertainty signals

For each held-out problem, from a single **greedy** decode: mean and min token log-probability, perplexity (`exp(-mean log-prob)`), and first-token predictive entropy. From **K temperature samples** (T=0.9): **semantic entropy** over the empirical distribution of sampled integer values (equivalence is exact integer match — no paraphrase ambiguity in this task), and **self-consistency agreement** (fraction of samples matching the greedy answer).

### 2.3 Evaluation

We report **AUROC** for "is the greedy answer wrong" against each signal, **ECE** (10 bins) for two derived confidence scores, **self-consistency majority-vote accuracy** vs. greedy accuracy, **bootstrap 95% CIs** (2,500th/97,500th percentile over 3,000 resamples) for the headline signals, and a **K-ablation** at K∈{4,8,16,32}. Following review feedback that a single sampling draw per K is unreliable at our error counts, the K-ablation now uses 5 independently seeded repeats per K (both `random` and `torch` RNGs reseeded per repeat) and reports mean ± std rather than one draw.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Primary result (3-digit addition, K=8)

Training reached 92.75% greedy accuracy on 400 held-out 3-digit addition problems (29 errors, 371 correct).

| Signal | Type | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| −mean log-prob | token-probability | **0.955** |
| perplexity | token-probability | **0.955** |
| −min log-prob | token-probability | 0.952 |
| −sample agreement | sampling-based | 0.933 |
| semantic entropy | sampling-based | 0.930 |
| first-token entropy | token-probability | 0.809 |

Token-probability signals that aggregate over the whole answer edge out both sampling-based signals; first-token entropy alone is markedly worse. ECE is 0.041 (token-probability) and 0.035 (sample-agreement); both are overconfident in the low-confidence tail, where error detection matters most. Self-consistency majority-vote accuracy over K=8 (92.75%) equals greedy accuracy: this model has no "alternate correct reasoning path" structure to aggregate over, so resampling mostly reproduces the greedy answer or its error mode.

### 3.2 Is the AUROC gap noise, and is it a K-ablation artifact?

Two concerns from review: (a) the seeded, single-draw K-ablation table in the previous revision did not reproduce under a fresh unseeded rerun — a reviewer got qualitatively different numbers (near-ceiling AUROC already at K=8) from the same checkpoint, because `sample_decode` calls were unseeded and single-draw estimates are volatile with only 8 errors in the eval set; (b) the bootstrap CI in the previous revision ran only on the 2-digit companion model, not on data at the primary 3-digit scale.

**Fix (a) — seeded, repeated K-ablation (companion model, 100 held-out problems, R=5 seeded repeats per K):**

| K | semantic entropy AUROC | sample agreement AUROC | majority-vote acc |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.761 ± 0.057 | 0.770 ± 0.058 | 0.876 ± 0.019 |
| 8 | 0.887 ± 0.038 | 0.902 ± 0.041 | 0.916 ± 0.026 |
| 16 | 0.933 ± 0.039 | 0.933 ± 0.022 | 0.914 ± 0.014 |
| 32 | 0.941 ± 0.018 | 0.941 ± 0.016 | 0.912 ± 0.007 |

(greedy accuracy 93.0%, 7/100 errors; token-probability AUROC ceiling ≈0.96, flat across K.) The qualitative trend from the previous revision survives — the sampling-based deficit shrinks sharply as K grows and standard deviation shrinks alongside it (0.06 at K=4 to 0.02 at K=32) — but the previous single-draw table understated how noisy any individual K=4 or K=8 estimate is: a single unlucky or lucky draw at low K can land 0.05–0.10 AUROC away from the 5-repeat mean, which is exactly what the reviewer's unseeded rerun exposed. Majority-vote accuracy plateaus around 0.91–0.92 at every K, still at or below greedy accuracy (0.93) with no evidence of exceeding it even at K=32.

**Fix (b) — a direct attempt at primary-scale (3-digit) bootstrap CIs.** We retrained a fresh 3-digit model with the same architecture, optimizer, and 240s wall-clock budget as the original primary run, intending to bootstrap CIs on its own 400-problem holdout as the reviewer requested. The retrained model reached only 1,149 optimizer steps and 21.5% held-out accuracy in 240s, versus the original run's 3,937 steps and 92.75% accuracy in the same wall-clock budget — roughly 3.4x fewer steps per second. This session's compute throughput is evidently not comparable to whatever produced the original primary run, so an AUROC/CI computed on this undertrained model (0.68 for −mean log-prob, 0.60 for semantic entropy) would reflect training noise, not signal quality, and we do not report it as informative about the primary ranking. This is the same failure mode already disclosed in Limitation (2) for the multi-seed replication attempt, now confirmed a second time under a different retraining attempt; we no longer treat it as an isolated fluke but as a property of this session's compute environment. Consequently the CI evidence for the headline signals still comes only from the 2-digit companion model (Section 2.1): −mean log-prob AUROC = 0.963, 95% CI [0.917, 0.997]; semantic entropy AUROC = 0.950, 95% CI [0.903, 0.986] — overlapping intervals, so the *direction* of the effect (token-probability ≥ sampling) is what we stand behind, not the exact magnitude.

### 3.3 Does sampling recover its advantage under genuine answer multiplicity?

We built a second task where a prompt genuinely admits two distinct correct token sequences: `"a+b~"` → the digits of `a+b`, sorted ascending or descending, chosen uniformly at random per training example (`make_example_multi` in `experiment_v2.py`). As in the previous revision, this task did not train to a comparable accuracy within available budget, and we still decline to report AUROC numbers from it as a finding. But we can now say more precisely why, rather than attribute it only to insufficient compute: because the training target is chosen by an independent coin flip that carries no signal in the prompt, the conditional target distribution given the prompt is genuinely bimodal at the sequence level — the model cannot reduce loss below the entropy of that coin flip, and greedy decoding (which follows one path through the logits at each step) can land on token sequences that blend the two modes rather than cleanly reproducing either one, which looks like poor accuracy but is partly a structural property of the task, not only undertraining. This is itself informative: a task with genuine answer multiplicity is, by the same token, harder for the base model to learn to a comparable greedy-accuracy level within a fixed budget, which complicates a clean apples-to-apples comparison against Section 3.1's single-answer task even if training budget were unlimited. We flag resolving this — training Task B for long enough that greedy accuracy is high and multiplicity remains, or restructuring the target so the two forms are learnable as one calibrated bimodal output — as the concrete next step (Section 5), and keep the code and this analysis available for that purpose.

## 4. Discussion

The result is not that token probabilities are secretly better than sampling-based UQ in general — the mechanism sampling-based methods rely on (resolving paraphrase ambiguity, aggregating over divergent reasoning paths) is absent in a single-canonical-answer task, and in its absence sampling brings mostly added variance and compute cost. The corrected K-ablation refines this: sampling-based signals are worse *per sample*, and noisier per single draw at low K than a naive comparison suggests, needing both larger K and repeated estimation to approach parity with a K=1-cost baseline. Practically: does the task admit multiple structurally different correct generations, and if not, is the sampling budget large enough (empirically K≈16–32 here), estimated with enough repeats to trust, to justify the added cost over token probability.

## 5. Limitations

(1) The model is trained from scratch rather than a pretrained LLM, since this sandboxed environment had no network access to model hubs; the qualitative claim is consistent with the semantic-entropy literature's mechanism but not re-verified against real alignment-induced miscalibration. (2) The primary result (Section 3.1) still rests on one seed. Two independent attempts (a prior multi-seed sweep, and a dedicated rerun for CI purposes in this revision) to retrain at the original 3-digit, 240s scale both produced severely undertrained models (10–30% accuracy), indicating this session's compute throughput cannot reproduce the original run's step count in the same wall-clock budget — a confirmed, not merely suspected, blocker; a rerun with more wall-clock budget or faster hardware remains future work. (3) We use exact integer equality as our semantic-equivalence oracle, a best case for semantic entropy; NLI-judge noise in real deployments could push its relative standing either direction. (4) The genuine-multiplicity task (Section 3.3) remains underpowered; we now attribute part of that to a structural property of the task (bimodal per-prompt targets raise the loss floor), not only insufficient budget. (5) The K-ablation and bootstrap-CI results (Section 3.2) use the smaller 2-digit companion model, the only version trainable to convergence within budget; the qualitative pattern (gap narrows with K, CIs overlap, low-K estimates are noisy) is informative but exact values are not directly comparable to the 3-digit primary table.

## 6. Conclusion

In a controlled, judge-free setting purpose-built to remove the paraphrase and multi-path-reasoning effects that motivate sampling-based LLM uncertainty methods, whole-answer token-probability signals matched or exceeded semantic entropy and self-consistency as error detectors at a typical sampling budget, and self-consistency provided zero accuracy lift over greedy decoding. Correcting the K-ablation to use seeded, repeated draws (a single unseeded draw, as a reviewer showed, does not reproduce) confirms the gap narrows with K but reveals substantial variance at low K the original table concealed. A direct attempt to bootstrap CIs at the primary model's own scale failed for a compute-throughput reason we now document rather than merely suspect, so CI evidence still comes from a smaller companion model. The takeaway is unchanged: token probability is the cheaper default unless the task has semantic multiplicity or the practitioner can afford K≈16+ samples estimated with enough repeats to trust.
