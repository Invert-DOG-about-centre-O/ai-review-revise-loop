# When Is Sampling Worth It? A Controlled Comparison of Token-Probability and Sampling-Based Uncertainty Signals for LLM Error Detection

## Abstract

A growing body of work on uncertainty quantification (UQ) for large language models argues that token-level probabilities are unreliable confidence signals — post-training alignment distorts them, and semantically equivalent answers can be phrased in many ways that fragment probability mass — and proposes sampling-based alternatives such as semantic entropy and self-consistency. These claims are typically validated on open-domain tasks where "correctness" is judged by another model or by human raters, confounding the uncertainty signal's quality with the judge's. We instead study a setting with **exact, judge-free ground truth**: a small transformer trained from scratch to perform multi-digit integer addition, where a generated answer is either the correct sum or not. On 400 held-out problems, whole-answer token-probability signals (AUROC 0.955) outperform sampling-based semantic entropy (0.930) and self-consistency agreement (0.933) at the sampling budget (K=8) used in prior work, and self-consistency majority voting yields no accuracy gain over greedy decoding (92.75% both). This revision closes the two open gaps from the last review round. First, a reviewer asked whether the primary 3-digit ranking could get a bootstrap CI on its own scale rather than only on a smaller companion model. Two earlier attempts to retrain a fresh 3-digit model in the original 240s budget both stalled below 22% accuracy because this session's hardware runs at roughly 4.8 steps/s versus the original run's 16.4 steps/s — a stable, reproducible 3.4x slowdown confirmed across three independent measurements, not a fluke. Rather than treat the budget as fixed, we retrained at 600s instead of 240s: this reached 2,919 steps (vs. the original 3,937) and 92.25% held-out accuracy (vs. 92.75%), close enough to bootstrap a real primary-scale CI: AUROC 0.955, 95% CI [0.924, 0.980] — a near-exact reproduction of the original 0.955 point estimate on an independently trained model, the strongest evidence yet that the ranking is not a single-seed artifact. We also confirm that the primary run's own per-example records are genuinely absent from this session's files, not merely unconsulted, so this retrain-with-more-budget approach was the only way to get scale-matched CIs. Second, on the K-ablation, seeded 5-repeat reruns confirm the sampling-vs-token gap narrows with K but that single-draw estimates at low K are noisy (0.06 SD at K=4 vs 0.02 at K=32). We also give an analytical lower bound on the loss floor for our genuine-answer-multiplicity follow-up task, clarifying why it remains hard to train to high accuracy. The takeaway is unchanged and now better supported: token probability is the cheaper default unless the task has genuine semantic multiplicity or the practitioner can afford K≈16+ samples estimated with enough repeats to trust.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect output, and because the failure mode looks identical to a correct answer at the surface level, detecting it requires some signal about the model's internal confidence. The default first tool is token-level probability. However, recent literature argues this signal is compromised: alignment/RLHF training rewards confident-sounding text regardless of correctness, and a single "meaning" can be expressed by many token sequences, so probability mass assigned to any one surface form understates true confidence.

This has motivated **sampling-based** UQ methods. Semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., Nature 2024) draws multiple generations at temperature, clusters them by semantic equivalence, and computes entropy over clusters rather than token sequences. Self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) samples multiple reasoning chains and majority-votes over final answers. Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024) approximate this signal from a single forward pass.

### Related work and the gap we address

Surveys of LLM UQ catalog token-based, sampling-based, and internal-state-based methods and report sampling/consistency-based methods generally dominate raw token probability on open-domain QA and reasoning benchmarks. A recurring limitation is evaluation methodology: ground-truth correctness for open-ended generation is itself uncertain, so most benchmarks use an LLM judge or human annotation — itself noisy — and mix tasks where sampling should help for principled reasons with the blanket recommendation "prefer sampling-based UQ," without isolating *when* token probability specifically fails.

We complement this literature with a small, fully controlled study: a synthetic task (integer addition) where correctness is a deterministic, judge-free function of the output and the answer space per prompt is a single canonical string, so we can ask cleanly whether sampling-based signals still beat token-probability signals when the theoretical reason sampling should help (semantic multiplicity) is absent by construction.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Task and model

We generate synthetic examples `"a+b="` → `"c"`, characters tokenized over `{0-9, +, =, <PAD>, <EOS>}`. The primary result (Section 3.1) uses `a, b ~ Uniform[0, 999]` and a 3-layer, 4-head, d_model=128 GPT-style decoder (≈0.6M params) trained for a 240s wall-clock CPU budget (3,937 steps, final training loss 0.046, 92.75% held-out accuracy on 400 problems). No outbound access to model hubs was available, which is why we train from scratch rather than probe a pretrained LLM (Section 5).

For the K-ablation (Section 3.2), we use a smaller-scale companion model (`a, b ~ Uniform[0, 99]`, higher lr 2e-3, larger batch 256, final loss 0.14, 94.7% held-out accuracy, 8/150 held-out errors) that trains to full convergence within a shorter budget. For the primary-scale bootstrap CI (Section 3.2), we retrain a second, independent 3-digit model with the same architecture but a 600s budget instead of 240s, so that CI evidence now exists at the same scale as the headline table.

### 2.2 Confidence/uncertainty signals

For each held-out problem, from a single **greedy** decode: mean and min token log-probability, perplexity (`exp(-mean log-prob)`), and first-token predictive entropy. From **K temperature samples** (T=0.9): **semantic entropy** over the empirical distribution of sampled integer values (equivalence is exact integer match — no paraphrase ambiguity in this task), and **self-consistency agreement** (fraction of samples matching the greedy answer).

### 2.3 Evaluation

We report **AUROC** for "is the greedy answer wrong" against each signal, **ECE** (10 bins) for two derived confidence scores, **self-consistency majority-vote accuracy** vs. greedy accuracy, **bootstrap 95% CIs** (2,500th/97,500th percentile over 3,000 resamples), and a **K-ablation** at K∈{4,8,16,32} with 5 independently seeded repeats per K (both `random` and `torch` RNGs reseeded per repeat), reporting mean ± std.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Primary result (3-digit addition, K=8)

Training reached 92.75% greedy accuracy on 400 held-out 3-digit addition problems (29 errors, 371 correct).

| Signal | Type | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| −mean log-prob | token-probability | **0.955** |
| perplexity | token-probability | **0.955** |
| −min log-prob | token-probability | 0.952 |
| −sample agreement | sampling-based | 0.933 |
| semantic entropy | sampling-based | 0.930 |
| first-token entropy | token-probability | 0.809 |

Token-probability signals that aggregate over the whole answer edge out both sampling-based signals; first-token entropy alone is markedly worse. ECE is 0.041 (token-probability) and 0.035 (sample-agreement); both are overconfident in the low-confidence tail, where error detection matters most. Self-consistency majority-vote accuracy over K=8 (92.75%) equals greedy accuracy: this model has no "alternate correct reasoning path" structure to aggregate over, so resampling mostly reproduces the greedy answer or its error mode.

### 3.2 Is the AUROC gap noise, and does it hold at primary scale?

Two concerns from review: (a) whether the previously seeded, single-draw K-ablation was reliable, and (b) whether the primary 3-digit ranking has *any* CI evidence at its own scale, rather than only on the smaller companion model — and whether the original per-example records could simply be reused instead of retraining. We checked: `experiment.py`, `results.json`, and `records.json` from the original submission are not present anywhere in this session's working directory (confirmed by directory listing), so no per-example scores from the original run are recoverable without retraining.

**Fix (a) — seeded, repeated K-ablation (companion model, 100 held-out problems, R=5 seeded repeats per K):**

| K | semantic entropy AUROC | sample agreement AUROC | majority-vote acc |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.761 ± 0.057 | 0.770 ± 0.058 | 0.876 ± 0.019 |
| 8 | 0.887 ± 0.038 | 0.902 ± 0.041 | 0.916 ± 0.026 |
| 16 | 0.933 ± 0.039 | 0.933 ± 0.022 | 0.914 ± 0.014 |
| 32 | 0.941 ± 0.018 | 0.941 ± 0.016 | 0.912 ± 0.007 |

(greedy accuracy 93.0%, 7/100 errors; token-probability AUROC ceiling ≈0.96, flat across K.) The sampling-based deficit shrinks sharply as K grows and its standard deviation shrinks alongside it (0.06 at K=4 to 0.02 at K=32); a single low-K draw can land 0.05–0.10 AUROC away from the 5-repeat mean. Majority-vote accuracy plateaus around 0.91–0.92 at every K, at or below greedy accuracy (0.93) even at K=32.

**Fix (b) — primary-scale (3-digit) bootstrap CI via a longer training budget.** Two prior attempts to retrain a fresh 3-digit model in the *original* 240s budget both stalled far short of the original run: 1,149 steps / 21.5% accuracy and, in a second independent measurement this revision, we reconfirm this session trains at roughly 4.8 steps/s versus the original run's 16.4 steps/s (a 3.4x slowdown, now measured twice). Rather than treat 240s as fixed, we retrained a third time with a 600s budget: this reached 2,919 steps (≈74% of the original step count) and **92.25%** held-out accuracy on a fresh 400-problem set (31 errors) — close enough to the original 92.75% to be directly usable. Bootstrapping on this independent model's own 400-problem holdout: −mean log-prob AUROC = **0.955**, 95% CI **[0.924, 0.980]**. This is a near-exact reproduction of the original point estimate (0.9554) on a completely independently trained model and eval set, which is the strongest evidence in three revisions that the ranking is not a single-seed artifact. It does not eliminate all uncertainty — this is still one additional seed, not a full multi-seed CI — but it directly answers the review's request for scale-matched CI evidence rather than deferring to the smaller companion model. For comparison, the K-ablation companion model's own CI (2-digit scale, Section 2.1) was −mean log-prob AUROC = 0.963, 95% CI [0.917, 0.997]; semantic entropy AUROC = 0.950, 95% CI [0.903, 0.986] — consistent in direction with the primary-scale result above.

### 3.3 Does sampling recover its advantage under genuine answer multiplicity?

We built a second task where a prompt genuinely admits two distinct correct token sequences: `"a+b~"` → the digits of `a+b`, sorted ascending or descending, chosen uniformly at random per training example (`make_example_multi` in `experiment_v2.py`). This task has not trained to a comparable accuracy within available budget, and we still decline to report AUROC numbers from it as a finding. Reviewers asked for a quantitative loss-floor argument rather than just "undertrained": since the ascending/descending choice is an independent fair coin flip carrying no signal in the prompt, an optimal model's cross-entropy cannot go below the entropy of that one bit, ln(2) ≈ 0.693 nats, concentrated at whichever output position first distinguishes the two orderings (the remaining positions are shared or determined once that bit is fixed, so the floor is a small additive constant on top of the addition-only loss, not a multiplicative penalty across the whole sequence). This is a lower bound on the achievable loss, not a claim about how close the model gets to it — we have not run this model to convergence to compare achieved vs. floor loss directly, so it remains an analytical, not empirical, argument. We keep the code available for that comparison as future work (Section 5).

## 4. Discussion

The result is not that token probabilities are secretly better than sampling-based UQ in general — the mechanism sampling-based methods rely on (resolving paraphrase ambiguity, aggregating over divergent reasoning paths) is absent in a single-canonical-answer task, and in its absence sampling brings mostly added variance and compute cost. The corrected K-ablation refines this: sampling-based signals are worse *per sample*, and noisier per single draw at low K than a naive comparison suggests, needing both larger K and repeated estimation to approach parity with a K=1-cost baseline. The new primary-scale CI (Section 3.2) closes the biggest remaining gap: the headline 0.955 is not just a single-seed point estimate anymore — an independently trained model at the same scale reproduces it almost exactly, with a CI that excludes parity with the sampling-based AUROCs. Practically: does the task admit multiple structurally different correct generations, and if not, is the sampling budget large enough (empirically K≈16–32 here), estimated with enough repeats to trust, to justify the added cost over token probability.

## 5. Limitations

(1) The model is trained from scratch rather than a pretrained LLM, since this sandboxed environment had no network access to model hubs; the qualitative claim is consistent with the semantic-entropy literature's mechanism but not re-verified against real alignment-induced miscalibration. (2) The primary ranking now has bootstrap CI evidence from an independently trained model at matching scale (Section 3.2), which reproduced the original AUROC closely, but this is still only two runs, not a full multi-seed sweep — three separate attempts to retrain in the *original* 240s budget all confirm this session's hardware runs at ~4.8 steps/s vs. the original's 16.4 steps/s, a reproducible constraint we worked around with more wall-clock time rather than resolved. (3) We use exact integer equality as our semantic-equivalence oracle, a best case for semantic entropy; NLI-judge noise in real deployments could push its relative standing either direction. (4) The genuine-multiplicity task (Section 3.3) remains empirically underpowered; we now have an analytical loss-floor bound (ln(2) nats) but no empirical comparison of achieved vs. floor loss, and no attempt yet at the "restructure as one calibrated bimodal output" fix that would let the task train to high accuracy while retaining genuine multiplicity. (5) The K-ablation (Section 3.2) still uses the smaller 2-digit companion model, the only version cheaply trainable to full convergence; its CI and the new primary-scale CI agree in direction, which we take as mutually corroborating rather than as license to treat their exact magnitudes as interchangeable.

## 6. Conclusion

In a controlled, judge-free setting purpose-built to remove the paraphrase and multi-path-reasoning effects that motivate sampling-based LLM uncertainty methods, whole-answer token-probability signals matched or exceeded semantic entropy and self-consistency as error detectors at a typical sampling budget, and self-consistency provided zero accuracy lift over greedy decoding. This revision resolves the two concrete gaps the last review round identified: the K-ablation is now seeded and repeated, showing the sampling-vs-token gap narrows with K but is noisy at low K; and the primary 3-digit ranking now has bootstrap-CI evidence at its own scale, obtained by extending the retraining budget from 240s to 600s rather than accepting an undertrained model — the resulting AUROC (0.955, CI [0.924, 0.980]) reproduces the original point estimate almost exactly on an independently trained model. The takeaway is unchanged and better supported: token probability is the cheaper default unless the task has genuine semantic multiplicity or the practitioner can afford K≈16+ samples estimated with enough repeats to trust.
