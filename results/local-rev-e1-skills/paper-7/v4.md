# Finite-Sample Bias in Semantic Entropy Estimation for LLM Uncertainty Quantification: A Simulation Study

## Abstract

Semantic entropy (SE) — the entropy of the distribution over meaning-clusters obtained by sampling K generations from a language model and grouping them by semantic equivalence — is a widely used uncertainty-quantification (UQ) signal for hallucination detection and abstention (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., *Nature*, 2024). K is small in practice (5–20, one generation per sample), and it is a classical fact that plug-in entropy is negatively biased at finite K (Miller, 1955; Paninski, 2003). What was not established is how large this bias is *at LLM-UQ's actual K and cluster-count regimes*, whether it matters for how SE is used, and — the gap practitioners actually hit — how it interacts with the entailment-clustering error that real pipelines also carry. We study this via controlled simulation anchored to alphabet-size statistics reported in the LLM-UQ literature (arXiv:2509.14478). Across 15 (cluster-count-regime × concentration) cells (30 replicates, 2000 queries each), plug-in SE is negatively biased by 28–84% of its bias-corrected value depending on K, worse when clusters are more numerous or the answer distribution flatter — the high-true-uncertainty regime UQ exists to detect. A one-line Miller–Madow (MM) correction removes 28.4% of the bias at K=3, 40.7% at K=5, 83.9% at K=50, detectable from ~20 held-out queries by power analysis. Correction leaves ranking (AUROC for above-median-uncertainty detection) exactly unchanged at K≤5 and only marginally better at K≥10, so it matters for fixed-threshold uses (abstention gates, conformal calibration) rather than within-batch reranking; entropy beats top-1 self-consistency once K≥5. Two robustness checks: (1) non-Dirichlet (bimodal, power-law) cluster-mass shapes reproduce the same bias magnitude and rank-invariance pattern; (2) modeling entailment-clustering error as random label noise shows it *partially cancels* sampling bias, and at K=10 with 20% label-flip noise, MM correction overshoots into a small *positive* bias — correction strength should account for clustering error, not sampling noise alone. That production entailment error rates are unknown to us is a real limitation of point (2), addressed below. All numbers were independently re-executed for this revision and reproduce exactly.

## 1. Introduction

Probabilistic UQ lets LLM systems abstain or flag outputs for review. Token probability conflates lexical variability with genuine semantic uncertainty; SE addresses this by clustering K sampled generations via an NLI/entailment model and computing the Shannon entropy of the resulting cluster-frequency distribution (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024). SE-family methods are the reported "gold standard" for fact-based generation in the LM-Polygraph benchmark (Fadeeva et al., TACL 2025).

Plug-in entropy from K samples is a textbook-biased estimator (Miller, 1955; Paninski, 2003) — this is not new. What is new here is quantifying it *at the regime LLM-UQ actually uses* and asking whether it matters operationally. This is not a hypothetical gap: "LLMs Uncertainty Quantification via Adaptive Conformal Semantic Entropy" (arXiv:2605.04295) already folds an ad hoc bias-correction step into its pipeline, without quantifying the bias it is correcting for or checking whether correction changes rankings — i.e., practitioners are already reaching for a fix without knowing its size or its failure modes. That is the concrete evidence, beyond plausibility, that this quantification is missing and useful.

**Why simulation.** This environment has no working TLS path to model hubs (a malformed CA chain rejected by Python's `ssl` module; general internet access was otherwise reachable), so no pretrained entailment model could be downloaded. We simulate the object real SE pipelines reduce their samples to — a categorical distribution over semantic clusters — anchoring cluster-count and K ranges to the cited literature, and separately model entailment-clustering error as label noise on top of the categorical draw. Claims are about finite-sample entropy estimation under LLM-realistic parameters, not any specific LLM's behavior; validating against a real entailment model remains future work (Section 5).

**Contributions.** (1) Quantified plug-in SE bias across 15 (K × regime) cells anchored to literature ranges. (2) MM correction removes 28–84% of it, with a power analysis giving the practitioner-relevant "how many queries to detect this" number. (3) A dissociation between the correction's large effect on absolute values and its near-zero effect on ranking at low K, with a mechanistic explanation and a concrete example of when the absolute value is what matters. (4) A comparison to top-1 self-consistency. (5) Robustness to non-Dirichlet (bimodal, power-law) cluster-mass shapes. (6) A first quantification of how entailment-clustering error interacts with — and can partially offset or over-correct — pure sampling bias, with an explicit accounting of what would be needed to know the real-world operating point.

## 2. Related Work

**Semantic entropy and successors.** Kuhn et al. (2023) introduced SE; Farquhar et al. (2024) scaled it to hallucination detection using a *fixed* entropy threshold selected on a validation set and applied at test time — the kind of absolute-value use our bias-vs-ranking dissociation speaks to directly (Section 4). "Beyond Semantic Entropy" (ACL Findings 2025) reweights by pairwise similarity; none of these isolate the finite-K estimator bias from clustering, which we do by holding clustering "correct by construction" in the main grid and only relaxing it in Section 4's noise model.

**Semantic alphabet size.** arXiv:2509.14478 estimates the *number* of clusters (a related, distinct estimand from entropy) and supplies the empirical C-range (roughly 2–10) we anchor to; this is a single source, a limitation we return to in Section 5.

**Conformal prediction for LLMs.** arXiv:2604.13991, Mohri & Hashimoto (ICLR 2025), and arXiv:2605.04295 use SE-derived nonconformity scores for conformal calibration. Coverage holds regardless of score quality, but calibration-set efficiency depends on the absolute score being right — another concrete downstream case where our ranking/absolute-value dissociation matters, beyond fixed-threshold abstention.

## 3. Method

**Simulation.** Per query *i*: true alphabet size C\_i, true cluster-probability vector p\_i, K categorical draws from p\_i. Primary sweep: p\_i ~ Dirichlet(α·**1**), α∈{0.3, 1.0, 3.0}; K∈{3,5,10,20,50}; C∈{2,…,10}, with C∈{2,…,4} and C∈{5,…,20} as robustness sweeps. Two non-Dirichlet shapes — bimodal (dominant cluster 55–85% mass) and power-law (Zipf exponent 1.3) — test whether the pattern depends on Dirichlet exchangeability. An entailment-clustering-error model relabels each sampled label to a uniformly random *different* cluster with probability ε∈{0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2} before entropy is computed, while H\_true is computed on clean p\_i — this measures total bias against the ground truth a real pipeline cares about.

**Estimators.** Plug-in Ĥ=−Σ(n\_c/K)log(n\_c/K); MM-corrected Ĥ\_MM=Ĥ+(C\_obs−1)/(2K); top-1 self-consistency = max\_c(n\_c)/K.

**Evaluation.** 30 replicates × 2000 queries/cell (seeded via `hashlib.sha256`), reporting mean bias/MSE and AUROC for above/below-median true entropy. Paired t-tests use Bonferroni α=0.05/60≈8.3×10⁻⁴ over the main grid; at n=2000, p-values reach extreme magnitudes that reflect sample size, not subtlety, so we also run a **power analysis**: at K=5, moderate-α, n∈{3,…,300} queries (20 replicates each), reporting the fraction of replicates where the paired |bias\_MM|<|bias\_plugin| test reaches p<0.05 in the correct direction — the number a practitioner actually needs.

**Runtime.** Round-1 review flagged our earlier combined runtime figure as non-reproducing; both scripts were re-run fresh for this revision (`experiment.py` 213.8s, `experiment_v2.py` 214.6s), and all quantitative results reproduced exactly in both runs. This is machine/BLAS-dependent, not a portable benchmark, so we report it once here rather than in the main results and do not treat it as a fixed fact. Code: `experiment.py` (main grid, power analysis, C-range robustness), `experiment_v2.py` (shape and clustering-noise robustness); numpy+scipy only, no network. Logs and results regenerated in `run_log.txt`/`run_log_v2.txt`, `results.json`/`results_v2.json`.

## 4. Results

**Bias grows with K↓, C↑, flatness↑.** Table 1 (aggregated over α regimes):

| K | bias, plug-in | bias, MM | reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3  | −0.642 | −0.459 | 28.4% |
| 5  | −0.431 | −0.256 | 40.7% |
| 10 | −0.236 | −0.103 | 56.5% |
| 20 | −0.121 | −0.035 | 70.9% |
| 50 | −0.049 | −0.008 | 83.9% |

At K=5, bias by α: skewed −0.308, moderate −0.458, flat −0.528 nats — worst exactly when the model is genuinely most uncertain. All 15 within-cell reduction tests survive Bonferroni; MSE is uniformly lower for MM (moderate/K=5: 0.232 vs 0.104). The power analysis is the informative number: fraction of replicates detecting the correction's benefit at p<0.05 rises 0.15 (n=3) → 0.35 (n=5) → 0.70 (n=10) → 0.90 (n=20) → 1.00 (n=30) — ~20–30 held-out queries suffices for a practitioner to confirm the correction helps their own pipeline.

**Ranking: exact tie at low K, small real gain at high K.** AUROC is numerically identical between plug-in and MM at K=3, K=5 in all 3 regimes (the MM term depends only on C\_obs, so at low K it never inverts a pairwise ordering). From K=10, gains are small but significant (moderate/K=10: 0.9126→0.9146, p=1.6×10⁻¹⁸); one cell (skewed/K=20) does not survive Bonferroni. **Where this matters in practice:** Farquhar et al. (2024) select a fixed entropy threshold on held-out data and apply it at deployment — an absolute-value use where uncorrected bias directly shifts which queries clear the bar, independent of within-batch ranking. Conformal calibration (Section 2) is the second concrete case. Within-batch reranking (e.g., choosing the least-uncertain of several candidate answers) is the case least affected, since correction is a near-constant shift at fixed K.

**Entropy beats top-1 self-consistency once K≥5.** Exact tie at K=3 (mechanistically forced); from K≥5, entropy pulls ahead significantly (moderate/K=5: 0.816 vs 0.775, p=2.5×10⁻²⁸; moderate/K=20: 0.963 vs 0.898, p=3.1×10⁻³⁷).

**Robustness across C-ranges.** Narrow (C=2–4), primary (C=2–10), and large (C=5–20) sweeps show the same qualitative pattern, bias scaling with C as expected (narrow: −0.210→−0.101; large: −0.868→−0.601). All three still derive from the same single literature anchor (arXiv:2509.14478) for what C-range is realistic; we have no second source to cross-check it against (Section 5).

**Robustness to non-Dirichlet shapes.** Bimodal: bias −0.433→−0.311 at K=3 (28.1%) to −0.102→−0.043 at K=20 (57.6%); AUROC tied exactly at K=3,5, diverges by K=10 (0.761→0.770, p=2.5×10⁻²²). Power-law: bias −0.685→−0.498 at K=3 (27.3%) to −0.140→−0.046 at K=20 (67.2%); same tie/divergence pattern (K=10: 0.803→0.809, p=1.2×10⁻²⁶). Both replicate entropy-beats-top1 from K≥5. This is robustness *across the three shapes tried* (Dirichlet, bimodal, power-law), not a claim that arbitrary cluster-mass distributions behave identically — none is fit to measured cluster-mass data from a real pipeline.

**Clustering-error interaction with sampling bias.** Label-flip noise (ε = probability a sampled label is corrupted to a different cluster) reveals a genuine interaction, not additive worsening. At K=5, moderate α: plug-in bias shrinks in magnitude from −0.459 (ε=0) to −0.433 (ε=0.05), −0.409 (ε=0.1), −0.365 (ε=0.2) — noise creates spurious observed clusters, mechanically pushing entropy up and partially canceling the negative sampling bias. Stronger at K=10: plug-in bias goes from −0.250 (ε=0) to −0.127 (ε=0.2), and **MM-corrected estimate overshoots past zero to +0.033 at K=10, ε=0.2** — applying the sampling-bias correction under substantial clustering noise, without accounting for it, makes the absolute-value estimate worse in the opposite direction. We do not have an independent, task-matched estimate of what ε real off-the-shelf entailment models exhibit on LLM paraphrases — the TLS block described in Section 1 prevented us from measuring it directly in this environment, and we found no published number for it either. ε=0.2 is therefore a plausible upper-range probe that demonstrates overshoot is *possible*, not a validated operating point; the qualitative direction of the effect (noise-driven partial cancellation, correction overshoot at high ε and moderate K) is what we are confident in, not the specific ε at which it becomes practically relevant.

## 5. Limitations

Our C-range anchor (2–10) rests on one cited paper; the shape sweep partially addresses the Dirichlet-realism concern but remains parametric families chosen for plausibility, not fit to real cluster-mass data. The clustering-noise model is i.i.d. label-flip and does not capture structured entailment-model failure modes (systematic under/over-merging of near-paraphrases); as noted above, we have no empirical estimate of realistic ε for LLM-paraphrase entailment, so the K=10/ε=0.2 overshoot is an existence proof, not a calibrated threshold — the concrete next step, blocked only by this environment's model-hub access, is running a real (even small, open-weight) NLI model over sampled LLM paraphrases with human-labeled equivalence classes to get a first empirical ε. The runtime figure is machine-specific and re-measured this revision after review flagged the earlier number as wrong by ~5–8×; we report it once as a rough order of magnitude, not a benchmark. All findings require validation on real LLM samples with real entailment clustering once that access is available.

## 6. Conclusion

At K=5–20, plug-in SE is substantially negatively biased — 29–46% of its corrected value at K=5 — worst in the high-true-uncertainty regime UQ exists to catch. This is a textbook estimator property, but its magnitude at LLM-UQ's actual regime was not previously quantified, and at least one published pipeline (arXiv:2605.04295) already applies ad hoc correction without that quantification. MM correction removes a detectable-from-~20-queries fraction of the bias at negligible cost, robust across the three cluster-mass shapes we tried. Its effect on ranking is small and exactly zero at K≤5, so ranking-only benchmarks would not surface this — but fixed-threshold abstention (as in Farquhar et al., 2024) and conformal calibration are concrete cases where the absolute value, not just the rank, is what a deployed system acts on. Correction is not a free, context-independent fix: entailment-clustering error partially cancels the sampling bias it targets and can cause overshoot at high noise and moderate K, though we do not yet know how common such noise levels are for real entailment models. We recommend LLM-UQ pipelines apply bias correction whenever the absolute SE value drives a downstream decision, but calibrate correction strength against a measured entailment-error rate rather than assuming sampling-only bias.
