# Finite-Sample Bias in Semantic Entropy Estimation for LLM Uncertainty Quantification: A Simulation Study

## Abstract

Semantic entropy (SE) — the entropy of the distribution over meaning-clusters obtained by sampling K generations from a language model and grouping them by semantic equivalence — is a widely used uncertainty-quantification (UQ) signal for detecting hallucinations and calibrating abstention (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., *Nature*, 2024). In practice K is small (5–20) because each sample costs a full generation, and it is a classical, mechanically-expected fact that the plug-in entropy estimator is negatively biased at finite K (Miller, 1955; Paninski, 2003). What is not established is how large this bias is *at the sample sizes and semantic-alphabet-size regimes actually used in LLM-UQ pipelines*, and whether it matters for how SE is actually used. We study this via a controlled simulation calibrated to semantic-alphabet-size statistics reported in the LLM-UQ literature. Across 15 (cluster-count-regime × concentration) cells (30 replicates, 2000 queries each), plug-in SE is negatively biased by 28–84% of its bias-corrected value depending on K, growing worse when the number of plausible clusters is larger or the answer distribution is flatter — the "high true uncertainty" regime where accurate UQ matters most. A one-line Miller–Madow (MM) correction removes 28.4% of the bias at K=3, 40.7% at K=5, and 83.9% at K=50; a power analysis (the practically relevant number, since raw significance at n=2000 is uninformatively strong) shows the correction's benefit is reliably detectable from as few as ~20 held-out queries. Despite this large effect on the entropy *value*, correction leaves ranking performance (AUROC for above-median-uncertainty detection) exactly unchanged at K≤5 and only marginally better at K≥10; entropy-based scores beat the cheaper top-1 "self-consistency" baseline once K≥5. Two extensions probe robustness raised by initial review: (1) replacing the Dirichlet cluster-mass model with bimodal and power-law mass distributions reproduces the same bias magnitude and rank-invariance pattern, so the finding is not an artifact of the Dirichlet assumption; (2) modeling entailment-clustering error as random label noise shows it *partially cancels* the negative sampling bias (spurious extra clusters push entropy up) and, at K=10 with 20% label-flip noise, the MM correction overshoots into a small *positive* bias — meaning bias correction should not be applied blindly without knowledge of the clustering error rate. We conclude plug-in SE is well-suited to relative ranking even at low K, but its absolute value requires bias correction — applied cautiously, since correction strength should account for clustering noise, not sampling noise alone.

## 1. Introduction

Probabilistic UQ is central to making LLM outputs trustworthy: knowing *when* a model doesn't know lets systems abstain or flag outputs for review. Token-level probability is a poor proxy because it conflates lexical variability with genuine semantic uncertainty. SE addresses this by clustering K sampled generations into semantic-equivalence classes (via an NLI/entailment model) and computing the Shannon entropy of the cluster-frequency distribution (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024). SE-family methods are named the "gold standard" for fact-based generation tasks in the LM-Polygraph benchmark (Fadeeva et al., TACL 2025), which compares a dozen+ UQ methods.

A separate, less-discussed problem is *estimation error*: SE as computed in practice is a plug-in entropy estimate from only K samples, and K is small because every sample is an expensive full generation (K=5–20 in the cited work). Plug-in entropy is known to be negatively biased at finite samples, with bias on the order of (observed categories − 1)/(2K) (Miller, 1955; Paninski, 2003) — not a new discovery, but a fact whose practical size *at LLM-UQ's actual K and cluster-count regimes* has not been directly quantified in the literature we surveyed.

**Why this matters.** "Estimating Semantic Alphabet Size for LLM Uncertainty Quantification" (arXiv:2509.14478) studies the finite-K estimation problem for the *number* of clusters and reports empirical alphabet sizes on real QA benchmarks are typically small (roughly 2–10, mostly 1–3 for confidently-answered queries) — we anchor our C sweep to this. "LLMs Uncertainty Quantification via Adaptive Conformal Semantic Entropy" folds a bias-correction step into its SE pipeline before conformal calibration but does not quantify the bias magnitude or its ranking-vs-calibration consequences as a function of K — the gap we target. "Beyond Semantic Entropy" (ACL Findings 2025) shows SE's cluster-independence assumption breaks down for non-uniformly-similar clusters, motivating our sweep of cluster-mass *shape*, not just concentration.

**Why simulation.** This environment has no working TLS path to model hubs (a malformed CA certificate is correctly rejected by Python's `ssl` module), so no pretrained weights could be downloaded; general internet access was otherwise reachable, isolating the failure to the model-hub TLS chain. We therefore simulate the object real SE pipelines reduce their samples to — a categorical distribution over semantic clusters — abstracting away the upstream entailment-clustering step to study the downstream sampling/estimation statistics in isolation, anchoring cluster-count and budget ranges to the literature above. Claims are about *finite-sample entropy estimation under LLM-realistic parameters*, not any specific LLM's behavior.

**Contributions.** (1) A controlled simulation quantifying plug-in SE bias across 15 (K × regime) cells, anchored to literature-reported ranges. (2) A demonstration that MM correction removes 28–84% of this bias (K-dependent), with a power analysis giving the practically relevant "how many queries to detect this" answer rather than leaning on inflated n=2000 significance. (3) A dissociation between the correction's large effect on absolute values and its negligible-at-low-K effect on ranking, with a mechanistic explanation. (4) A comparison to top-1 self-consistency. (5) **New in this revision:** robustness of the bias/ranking pattern to non-Dirichlet (bimodal, power-law) cluster-mass shapes, and a first quantification of how entailment-clustering error interacts with — and can partially offset or over-correct — the pure sampling bias.

## 2. Related Work

**Semantic entropy and successors.** Kuhn et al. (2023) introduced SE; Farquhar et al. (*Nature*, 2024) scaled it to hallucination detection, both using K=5–20. Follow-ups target the *clustering/scoring* step: "Beyond Semantic Entropy" adds pairwise similarity weighting; vision-language and hedging-signal work extends token-level ideas elsewhere. None targets the finite-K estimator bias in isolation from clustering — our simulation holds clustering "correct by construction" to isolate the pure sampling-statistics effect (Section 5 now also relaxes this).

**Semantic alphabet size.** arXiv:2509.14478 is the closest work: it estimates the *number* of clusters (a related, distinct estimand from entropy) and supplies the empirical C-range we anchor to.

**Conformal prediction for LLMs.** Conformal factuality filtering (arXiv:2604.13991), coherent-factuality conformal reasoning (Mohri & Hashimoto, ICLR 2025), and Adaptive Conformal SE pipelines use SE-derived nonconformity scores. Coverage holds regardless of score quality, but efficiency (set size) depends on calibration — why the bias-vs-ranking dissociation matters: a biased score can rank well while giving a wrong absolute threshold, inflating conformal sets unnecessarily.

**Calibration more broadly.** LM-Polygraph benchmarks via ranking metrics (AUROC/PRR), not absolute calibration — consistent with our finding that ranking is comparatively robust to the bias we quantify, meaning this class of benchmark would not surface the issue.

## 3. Method

**Simulation model.** For each query *i* we draw a true semantic-alphabet size C\_i, a true cluster-probability vector p\_i, and K categorical samples from p\_i (K stochastic generations, pre-clustered). Primary sweep: p\_i ~ Dirichlet(α·**1**) with α∈{0.3 (skewed), 1.0 (moderate), 3.0 (flat)}; K∈{3,5,10,20,50}; C∈{2,…,10} (anchored to arXiv:2509.14478), with C∈{2,…,4} and C∈{5,…,20} as robustness sweeps. **New:** two non-Dirichlet cluster-mass *shapes* — bimodal (one dominant cluster at 55–85% mass, remainder split unevenly) and power-law (Zipf exponent 1.3) — probing whether the bias/ranking pattern depends on the Dirichlet-exchangeability assumption, per reviewer question. **New:** an entailment-clustering-error model where each of the K sampled labels is independently relabeled to a uniformly random *different* cluster with probability ε∈{0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2} before entropy is computed, while H\_true is still computed on the clean p\_i — this measures total bias (sampling + clustering noise) against the ground truth a real pipeline wants, per reviewer question.

**Estimators.** Plug-in entropy Ĥ=−Σ(n\_c/K)log(n\_c/K); MM-corrected Ĥ\_MM=Ĥ+(C\_obs−1)/(2K); top-1 self-consistency = max\_c(n\_c)/K.

**Evaluation.** For each cell: 30 replicates of 2000 queries (seeded via `hashlib.sha256` for reproducibility), reporting mean bias/MSE, Spearman correlation with true entropy, and AUROC for above/below-median true entropy. Paired t-tests across replicates use a Bonferroni threshold α=0.05/60≈8.3×10⁻⁴ over the ~60-comparison main grid. **We flag explicitly:** with 2000 queries/replicate, p-values on a deterministic, mechanically-expected effect reach extreme magnitudes (p<10⁻⁵³) that indicate sample size, not subtlety — the power analysis below is the metric that actually answers "how much data does a practitioner need."

**Power analysis.** At the realistic K=5, moderate-α cell, we sweep n∈{3,…,300} queries (20 replicates each), reporting the fraction of replicates where the paired |bias\_MM|<|bias\_plugin| test reaches p<0.05 in the correct direction.

Code: `experiment.py` (main grid, power analysis, C-range robustness) and `experiment_v2.py` (shape robustness, clustering-noise interaction), numpy+scipy only, no network. Raw output in `run_log.txt`/`run_log_v2.txt`; full results in `results.json`/`results_v2.json`. Combined runtime: 28.4s + 27.0s on CPU.

## 4. Results

**Bias grows with K↓, C↑, and flatness↑.** Table 1 (aggregated over α regimes):

| K | bias, plug-in | bias, MM | reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3  | −0.642 | −0.459 | 28.4% |
| 5  | −0.431 | −0.256 | 40.7% |
| 10 | −0.236 | −0.103 | 56.5% |
| 20 | −0.121 | −0.035 | 70.9% |
| 50 | −0.049 | −0.008 | 83.9% |

At K=5, bias by α regime: skewed −0.308, moderate −0.458, flat −0.528 nats — worse exactly when the model is genuinely most uncertain. All 15 within-cell reduction tests survive Bonferroni; MSE is uniformly lower for MM (e.g. moderate/K=5: 0.232 vs 0.104). As flagged in Method, these p-values are strong mainly because n=2000; **the power analysis is the informative number**: fraction of replicates detecting the correction's benefit at p<0.05 rises 0.15 (n=3) → 0.35 (n=5) → 0.70 (n=10) → 0.90 (n=20) → 1.00 (n=30) — i.e. ~20–30 held-out queries is enough for a practitioner to confirm the correction helps their own pipeline.

**Ranking: exact tie at low K, small real gain at high K.** AUROC is numerically identical between plug-in and MM at K=3 and K=5 in all 3 regimes (paired p=nan — zero variance in the difference). Mechanistically, the MM term depends only on C\_obs, so at low K it never inverts a pairwise ordering. From K=10, gains are small but significant (moderate/K=10: 0.9126→0.9146, p=1.6×10⁻¹⁸); one cell (skewed/K=20, p=0.043) does not survive Bonferroni. **Correction matters for absolute-value uses (thresholding, conformal calibration) but not for within-batch ranking.**

**Entropy beats top-1 self-consistency once K≥5.** Exact tie at K=3 (mechanistically forced: with 3 samples the duplicate-group partition determines both statistics identically). From K≥5, entropy scores pull ahead significantly (moderate/K=5: 0.816 vs 0.775, p=2.5×10⁻²⁸; moderate/K=20: 0.963 vs 0.898, p=3.1×10⁻³⁷).

**Robustness across C-ranges.** Narrow (C=2–4), primary (C=2–10), and large (C=5–20) sweeps all show the same qualitative pattern (all p<10⁻⁵³), with bias magnitude scaling with C as expected (narrow: −0.210→−0.101; large: −0.868→−0.601).

**New: robustness to non-Dirichlet cluster shapes.** Replacing Dirichlet with bimodal (dominant-cluster) and power-law mass distributions reproduces the same qualitative story. Bimodal: bias −0.433→−0.311 at K=3 (28.1% reduction) to −0.102→−0.043 at K=20 (57.6%); AUROC tied exactly at K=3,5 (paired p=nan) and diverges significantly by K=10 (0.761→0.770, p=2.5×10⁻²²). Power-law: bias −0.685→−0.498 at K=3 (27.3%) to −0.140→−0.046 at K=20 (67.2%); same K=3,5 exact AUROC tie, significant divergence from K=10 (0.803→0.809, p=1.2×10⁻²⁶). Both shapes also replicate the entropy-beats-top1 pattern from K≥5 (e.g. power-law K=5: 0.701 vs 0.666). This directly answers reviewer question 2: the bias magnitude and the rank-invariance-at-low-K result are not artifacts of the Dirichlet exchangeability assumption.

**New: clustering-error interaction with sampling bias.** Modeling entailment-clustering error as random label-flip noise (ε = probability each sampled label is corrupted to a different cluster) reveals a genuine interaction, not a simple additive worsening. At K=5, moderate α: plug-in bias is −0.459 at ε=0, and *shrinks in magnitude* to −0.433 (ε=0.05), −0.409 (ε=0.1), −0.365 (ε=0.2) — label noise creates spurious extra observed clusters, mechanically pushing entropy up and partially canceling the negative sampling bias. This effect is stronger at K=10: plug-in bias goes from −0.250 (ε=0) to −0.127 (ε=0.2), and the **MM-corrected estimate overshoots past zero into a small positive bias (+0.033) at K=10, ε=0.2** — i.e., applying the sampling-bias correction under substantial clustering noise, without accounting for that noise, actively makes the absolute-value estimate worse in the opposite direction. This answers reviewer question 3: clustering error does not simply add to sampling bias — it can partially offset it, and naive MM correction can overshoot once clustering noise is non-trivial, so correction strength should ideally be informed by an estimate of the entailment model's error rate rather than applied as a fixed sampling-only formula.

## 5. Limitations

Our C-range anchor (2–10) comes from a single cited paper's reported empirical range (reviewer question 1); we lack per-query empirical C/observed-cluster-count distributions from that or another source to replace the uniform-C-with-Dirichlet-p model directly, so this remains a single-source, distributional (not per-query-empirical) anchor. The new shape sweep (bimodal, power-law) partially addresses concern about the Dirichlet assumption's realism but is still a parametric family chosen for plausibility, not fit to real per-query cluster-mass data. The clustering-noise model is a simple i.i.d. label-flip and does not capture structured entailment-model failure modes (e.g. systematic under- or over-merging of near-paraphrases); it is a first-order probe of the interaction, not a validated model of real entailment error rates. All findings, as before, ultimately require validation on real LLM samples with real entailment-based clustering once model-hub access is available.

## 6. Conclusion

At K=5–20, plug-in SE is substantially negatively biased — 29–46% of its corrected value at K=5 — worst exactly in the high-true-uncertainty regime. A one-line MM correction removes a detectable-from-~20-queries fraction of this bias at negligible implementation cost, confirmed robust to non-Dirichlet cluster-mass shapes. Its effect on ranking (the metric most UQ benchmarks report) is comparatively small and exactly zero at K≤5, so ranking-only benchmarks would not surface this issue even though it matters for fixed-threshold abstention and conformal calibration. Crucially, this revision shows the correction is not a free, context-independent fix: entailment-clustering error partially cancels the sampling bias it targets and can cause the correction to overshoot at realistic noise levels and moderate K. We recommend LLM-UQ pipelines using SE at low K apply bias correction whenever the absolute value is used downstream, but calibrate correction strength (or validate net bias empirically) against their own entailment model's error rate rather than assuming a sampling-only bias model. Future work: validate on real LLM generations with real entailment clustering, and fit the clustering-noise model to measured entailment-model error rates rather than a swept ε.
