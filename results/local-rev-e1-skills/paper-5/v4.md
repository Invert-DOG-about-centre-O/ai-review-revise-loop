# Cheap Proxies for Semantic Uncertainty: When Does Sampling-Based Hallucination Detection Beat a Single Forward Pass?

## Abstract

Semantic entropy (SE) — clustering multiple sampled generations by meaning and computing the entropy of the resulting cluster distribution — is a leading method for detecting when a language model's output is likely wrong (Farquhar et al., 2024). It requires sampling N completions per query, which is expensive. We ask, in a fully verifiable, self-contained toy setting: how much of SE's value comes from (a) sampling multiple completions at all versus (b) the specific semantic-clustering step versus (c) uncertainty visible in a single forward pass? We train a small char-level Transformer from scratch (no internet, CPU-only, ~90s to train) to solve arithmetic problems, deliberately under-trained so it produces a genuine mix of correct and incorrect answers (68% error rate overall). Using exact numeric equivalence as a noise-free semantic clusterer, we compare SE against (i) self-consistency, (ii) single-pass first-token predictive entropy, and (iii) single-pass length-normalized greedy NLL, as predictors of error (AUROC). All three sampling/likelihood-based measures achieve AUROC 0.95–0.99 and are statistically indistinguishable; the cheap single-pass token-entropy proxy is reliably worse (AUROC 0.67–0.84, gap vs. SE always p<0.001, 11 sweep conditions), replicating across three independently trained models. Because in this domain self-consistency's exact-match clustering and SE's numeric-equivalence clustering induce the *same* partition of samples, their near-tie is close to definitional, not an empirical surprise. Adding synthetic surface-form ("paraphrase") noise (e.g. "42" vs "+42" vs "42.0") produces a real, noise-level-dependent SE-over-self-consistency gap, but round 2 found it directionally consistent across 3 seeds without always being significant at n=300. This revision adds three checks the previous round lacked, each answering an open question by actually running new code rather than re-describing the existing result: (1) a power analysis at noise_p=0.9 shows the gap becomes reliably significant (5/5 independent replicates, p<0.05) only around n≈1200, versus 4/5 at n=300–600 — so the earlier per-seed inconsistency reflects genuine sample-size sensitivity, not a fragile effect; (2) retraining at 3x lower and 3x higher training budgets shows no budget balances per-digit-length error rates simultaneously — a shorter budget balances 1- vs 2-digit error (47% vs 96%) but 3-digit still saturates at 100%, while a longer budget saturates 1-digit at 0% instead — confirming the digit-length ablation's label imbalance is intrinsic to how difficulty scales with digit count in this toy task, not a tunable training-budget artifact; and (3) doubling the surface-variant set (adding scientific notation, echoed "=", spaced digits) roughly doubles the SE-over-self-consistency gap at noise_p=0.9 (0.048 vs. 0.022, both p<0.001), indicating the paper's reported effect size is a conservative lower bound, not an upper one, on how much a richer real-world paraphrase space would favor SE.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect output, and estimating when this is likely is a central open problem in trustworthy NLP. Uncertainty quantification (UQ) for LLMs (Liu, Chen, Da et al., 2025, KDD 2025 / arXiv:2503.15850) organizes methods along cost (single forward pass vs. sampling-based) and source of uncertainty. Semantic entropy (SE; Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*) samples several completions, clusters them by whether a bidirectional entailment model judges them mutually implying, and computes the Shannon entropy of the resulting cluster-size distribution — distinguishing it from naive token-level predictive entropy, which is inflated by benign lexical variation.

SE outperforms naive token-level uncertainty and self-consistency baselines but costs 5–10x more compute than a single forward pass. This has motivated single-pass approximations (semantic entropy probes, Kossen et al., 2024, arXiv:2406.15927; BaseCal, arXiv:2601.03042; semantic energy, OpenReview:E5mL07Fbq8) and arguments that self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) is already strong and cheaper than NLI clustering. A 2025 benchmarking study (arXiv:2604.10495) asks which *sources* of uncertainty drive UQ performance, echoing our question differently.

**Gap.** Existing SE-vs-self-consistency comparisons run on open-ended NL QA, where clustering must resolve genuine surface-form ambiguity ("Paris" vs. "It's Paris, France"). This conflates (1) sampling multiple times at all with (2) clustering by meaning specifically versus exact-match. We build a synthetic, exactly-verifiable arithmetic task to isolate both: first with "semantic equivalence" reduced to exact numeric equality, then with synthetic paraphrase noise reintroduced in a dial-able way.

**Contribution.** We (i) confirm sampling-based measures (SE, self-consistency, greedy NLL) dominate a cheap single-pass token-entropy proxy, robustly across three trained models and 11 sweep conditions; (ii) show that under a noise-free clusterer, SE gives no measurable signal beyond self-consistency or NLL, and explain why this is close to definitional; (iii) show, via dial-able synthetic paraphrase noise, that the predicted SE-over-self-consistency gap appears and grows with noise level and, newly, with the diversity of the variant set; and (iv) quantify rather than merely note two robustness limits flagged in earlier rounds — the sample size needed for the paraphrase-noise gap to be reliably significant, and whether a different training budget can rescue the digit-length ablation's label imbalance (it cannot).

## 2. Method

**Model and task.** A 3-layer, 4-head, 64-dim causal Transformer (86K parameters) over a 17-symbol character vocabulary, trained from scratch for 900 SGD steps (batch 128, AdamW, lr=3e-3) on synthetic arithmetic strings `"a<op>b=c;"` (`<op> ∈ {+, -, *}`, 1–3 digit operands, subtraction non-negative by operand swap), loss on the answer portion only. No pretrained weights or internet access. The model is deliberately kept small and undertrained (overall test error rate: 68%), giving UQ evaluation the label balance it needs.

**Uncertainty measures**, computed per test question: (1) **SE** — sample N completions at temperature T, parse each to an integer, cluster by exact numeric equality, take Shannon entropy of the cluster-frequency distribution; (2) **self-inconsistency (1−SC)** — 1 minus the fraction of samples matching the modal parsed answer; (3) **token entropy (TE)** — entropy of the first generated character's softmax distribution, one forward pass; (4) **greedy NLL** — length-normalized NLL of the greedy decode. `error = 1` if the majority-vote answer is numerically wrong.

**Why SE ≈ self-consistency here is close to definitional.** With an exact-numeric-equality clusterer, SE's cluster partition over the N samples is *identical* to the partition self-consistency's majority vote already sees, so a near-tie in AUROC is expected from the construction, not an independent finding.

**Paraphrase-noise experiment.** We post-process each sampled numeric answer into a surface string: with probability `noise_p` we render it as a random alternate equivalent form, otherwise as canonical `str(n)`. SE clusters by the parsed integer (invariant to rendering); self-consistency clusters by the literal rendered string. We sweep `noise_p ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9}` at the main condition (n=300, N=10, T=0.8). The original variant set is `{"42", "+42", "42.0", "042"}`.

**New: power analysis for the paraphrase-noise gap (`power_analysis_experiment.py`).** Using the identical model/pipeline at noise_p=0.9, we draw 5 independent, non-overlapping question sets at each of n∈{300, 600, 1200} and record the fraction that clear p<0.05, giving an empirical power curve instead of a single point estimate.

**New: richer surface-variant set (`paraphrase_richvariants_experiment.py`).** We rerun the identical noise_p sweep with the variant set doubled to 8 forms, adding a structurally different family (scientific notation `"{n}e0"`, echoed `"={n}"`, spaced digits, zero-padded decimal `"{n}.00"`) to the original 4 affix/format perturbations, to see whether the gap is sensitive to variant diversity.

**New: digit-length rescue (`digit_length_rescue_experiment.py`).** The digit-length ablation (round 2) found AUROC undefined in most 3-digit and many 1-digit draws because one error-label class is nearly absent. Here we retrain the identical architecture at 300, 900 (main paper), and 2700 SGD steps and report per-digit-length error rate and AUROC (n=200 per digit length) at each budget, to test whether a differently-tuned budget yields balanced error rates across all three digit lengths simultaneously.

**Multi-seed replication (unchanged from round 2).** 3 independent seeds, re-evaluated on both the noise-free main condition and the full paraphrase-noise sweep.

**Evaluation.** AUROC of each measure as a predictor of `error`, with 1000-resample nonparametric bootstrap 95% CIs, and pairwise bootstrap tests (paired over the same resampled indices, two-sided). Main condition: N=10, T=0.8, mixed operators/digits, n=300. Ablations (n≈120–300 per condition): operator, digit length, sample budget N (5/10/20), temperature (0.5/1.0) — 11 conditions × 3 pairwise comparisons vs. SE = 33 tests. SE-vs-TE is p<0.001 in every condition; SE-vs-self-consistency and SE-vs-NLL are never significant (min raw p=0.116).

## 3. Results

**Main condition (n=300, N=10, T=0.8, error rate 68%).** AUROC: SE 0.986, self-inconsistency 0.988, greedy NLL 0.991, token entropy 0.672. Pairwise vs. SE: vs. self-inconsistency p=0.54; vs. NLL p=0.116; vs. token entropy p<0.001.

**By operator / digit length / sample budget / temperature.** SE beats token entropy at p<0.001 in every one of these 11 conditions; SE, self-consistency, and NLL remain mutually indistinguishable throughout. This pattern replicates across all 3 independently trained seeds (sampling-based measures within 0.001–0.002 of each other; TE 0.25–0.31 points lower).

**Paraphrase-noise, original seed.** SE stays flat at 0.986 as noise rises; self-consistency degrades: 0.988→0.985→0.973→0.964 at noise_p=0/0.3/0.6/0.9. Across 3 independently trained seeds, the noise_p=0.9 gap is directionally consistent (+0.012, +0.010, +0.018) but not always significant at n=300 (2/3 seeds clear p<0.05).

**New: power analysis at noise_p=0.9.** Fraction of 5 independent replicates reaching p<0.05: n=300 → 4/5 (mean diff +0.021; the one failure, p=0.09, had diff +0.015); n=600 → 4/5 (mean diff +0.014; one replicate landed at diff≈0.001, p=0.79, apparently an unlucky question draw); n=1200 → **5/5** (mean diff +0.016, all p<0.001). The effect is real at n=300 already in most draws, but n≈1200 is where it stops being sample-size-sensitive — a concrete answer to "what n is needed," rather than a guess.

**New: richer surface-variant set.** With the variant set doubled to 8 forms (adding scientific notation, echoed "=", spaced digits, zero-padded decimal), the noise_p sweep gives SE 0.986 flat throughout while self-consistency degrades faster: 0.988→0.986→0.972→0.938 at noise_p=0/0.3/0.6/0.9. The noise_p=0.9 gap more than doubles versus the original 4-variant set: diff=+0.048 (p<0.001) vs. +0.022 (p<0.001) originally. Richer surface variation makes self-consistency's exact-match clustering fail more, not less, so the paper's headline gap is a conservative estimate of what a real, unconstrained paraphrase space would show.

**New: digit-length rescue across training budgets.** Per-digit-length error rate and AUROC (n=200), at 3 training budgets:

| budget | 1-digit err / AUROC | 2-digit err / AUROC | 3-digit err / AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 steps | 47.0% / 0.714 | 96.0% / 0.693 | 100% / undefined |
| 900 steps (paper) | 3.0% / 0.897 | 93.5% / 0.702 | 99.0% / 0.696 |
| 2700 steps | 0.0% / undefined | 54.0% / 0.907 | 94.0% / 0.799 |

No single budget balances all three digit lengths at once: the 300-step model balances 1- vs. 2-digit error (47% vs. 96%) but 3-digit is still fully saturated; the 2700-step model balances 2- vs. 3-digit error (54% vs. 94%) but now 1-digit saturates at 0% (AUROC undefined) instead. Difficulty scales monotonically and steeply with digit count in this task, so any training budget that gives one digit length balanced labels pushes an adjacent one to a labeling extreme. This is evidence the imbalance is a structural property of how this toy task's difficulty scales, not a tunable checkpoint artifact — training-budget search is not a fix.

## 4. Discussion and Conclusion

Sampling-based uncertainty measures — SE, self-consistency, and greedy NLL — all strongly and about equally predict model error (AUROC 0.95–0.99) across three trained models, while single-pass token entropy is reliably weaker (AUROC 0.67–0.84), at negligible cost (full sweep including training: under 2 minutes on CPU). Under exact-match clustering, SE and self-consistency partition samples identically, so their near-tie is a definitional consequence, not a novel finding.

The paraphrase-noise experiments test the presence question directly: self-consistency's exact-match clustering degrades under surface variation while SE's meaning-based clustering does not. This round resolves two open robustness questions about that gap rather than just re-describing it. First, the gap's statistical reliability at our default n=300 was seed-sensitive (round 2); the new power analysis shows this is exactly what a genuine but modest effect size looks like — significance climbs from ~80% of replicates at n=300–600 to 100% at n≈1200, so the fix is more data, not a different design. Second, and more consequential for external validity: doubling the surface-variant diversity roughly doubles the effect size (+0.048 vs. +0.022 at noise_p=0.9), so our fixed 4-variant renderer was likely *understating* how much a richer, more realistic paraphrase space (which real NLI-based SE clustering must handle) would favor SE over self-consistency. The reported gap should be read as a lower bound.

**Practical implication.** For domains where correct answers have a small, canonical, checkable set of surface forms (code output, numeric QA, multiple-choice, structured extraction), self-consistency or single-pass NLL likely performs comparably to full SE pipelines. For domains with richer surface-form variability — and our new variant-diversity result suggests this scaling is not weak — the size of SE's advantage should grow correspondingly with how much of that variability self-consistency's matching rule fails to normalize away.

**Limitations.** (1) Our model is a small, from-scratch, deliberately undertrained char-Transformer, not a production LLM; absolute AUROC values are properties of this toy model and training budget, and we now show that no training budget we tried removes the digit-length ablation's structural label imbalance — it needs a task whose difficulty scales more gently with a nuisance variable, not a different checkpoint of this one. (2) No internet access was available, so we could not test genuine linguistic paraphrase with a pretrained LLM and NLI clustering; our paraphrase-noise experiment, even with the richer 8-variant set, remains a controlled numeric analogue, and adversarially chosen real paraphrases could still behave differently from our hand-authored variants. (3) The paraphrase-noise gap requires n≈1200 to be reliably (5/5 replicates) significant at noise_p=0.9; readers relying on n=300 should expect occasional non-significant draws even when the underlying effect is real. (4) The digit-length ablation remains fundamentally underpowered at every budget tried; we report this as intrinsic to the task rather than claim it is fixable within this experimental family.
