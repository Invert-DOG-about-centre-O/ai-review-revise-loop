# Cheap Proxies for Semantic Uncertainty: When Does Sampling-Based Hallucination Detection Beat a Single Forward Pass?

## Abstract

Semantic entropy (SE) — clustering multiple sampled generations by meaning and computing the entropy of the resulting cluster distribution — is a leading method for detecting when a language model's output is likely wrong (Farquhar et al., 2024). It requires sampling N completions per query, which is expensive. We ask, in a fully verifiable, self-contained toy setting: how much of SE's value comes from (a) sampling multiple completions at all versus (b) the specific semantic-clustering step versus (c) uncertainty visible in a single forward pass? We train a small char-level Transformer from scratch (no internet, CPU-only, ~90s to train) to solve arithmetic problems, deliberately under-trained so it produces a genuine mix of correct and incorrect answers (68% error rate overall). Using exact numeric equivalence as a noise-free semantic clusterer, we compare SE against (i) self-consistency confidence, (ii) single-pass first-token predictive entropy, and (iii) single-pass length-normalized greedy negative log-likelihood (NLL), as predictors of error (AUROC). All three sampling/likelihood-based measures achieve AUROC 0.95–0.99 and are statistically indistinguishable from each other; the cheap single-pass token-entropy proxy is reliably and substantially worse (AUROC 0.67–0.84, gap vs. SE always p<0.001 across 11 sweep conditions). This pattern replicates across three independently trained models (different init/data-stream seeds). Because in this domain self-consistency's exact-match clustering and SE's numeric-equivalence clustering induce the *same* partition of samples, their near-tie is close to definitional, not an empirical surprise — we make this precise below. To test the paper's central claim directly rather than only by absence, we add a follow-up experiment that reintroduces synthetic surface-form ("paraphrase") noise: the same numeric answer is sometimes rendered as a different string (e.g. "42" vs "+42" vs "42.0"). Self-consistency, which matches on the literal rendered string, degrades monotonically as noise increases (AUROC 0.988→0.985→0.973→0.964), while SE — invariant to surface form by construction — stays flat at 0.986, producing a significant SE-over-self-consistency gap once noise is high enough (p=0.03 at 60% noise, p<0.001 at 90% noise). This is, to our knowledge, the first demonstration of SE's predicted advantage over self-consistency inside a fully controlled, verifiable environment, complementing the noise-free result that isolates when SE offers nothing extra.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect output, and estimating when this is likely is a central open problem in trustworthy NLP. Uncertainty quantification (UQ) for LLMs (Liu, Chen, Da et al., 2025, KDD 2025 / arXiv:2503.15850) organizes methods along cost (single forward pass vs. sampling-based) and source of uncertainty. Within the sampling-based family, semantic entropy (SE; Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*) samples several completions, clusters them by whether a bidirectional entailment model judges them mutually implying ("the same answer said differently"), and computes the Shannon entropy of the resulting cluster-size distribution — distinguishing it from naive token-level predictive entropy, which is inflated by benign lexical variation.

SE outperforms naive token-level uncertainty and self-consistency baselines but costs 5–10x more compute than a single forward pass (Farquhar et al., 2024). This has motivated single-pass approximations (semantic entropy probes, Kossen et al., 2024, arXiv:2406.15927; BaseCal, arXiv:2601.03042; semantic energy, OpenReview:E5mL07Fbq8) and arguments that self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) — the fraction of samples agreeing with the majority answer — is already strong and cheaper than NLI clustering. A 2025 benchmarking study (arXiv:2604.10495) asks which *sources* of uncertainty, not which estimator, drive UQ performance, echoing our question differently.

**Gap.** Existing SE-vs-self-consistency comparisons run on open-ended NL QA, where clustering must resolve genuine surface-form ambiguity ("Paris" vs. "It's Paris, France"). This conflates two sources of SE's advantage: (1) sampling multiple times at all, and (2) clustering by meaning specifically versus exact-match. No prior study isolates these, and none we found directly demonstrates the SE-over-self-consistency gap in a fully controlled, dial-able setting. We build a synthetic, exactly-verifiable arithmetic task to do both: first with "semantic equivalence" reduced to exact numeric equality (isolating whether clustering-by-meaning adds anything once there is no paraphrase noise), then with synthetic paraphrase noise reintroduced in a dial-able way (testing whether the predicted advantage appears once that noise exists).

**Contribution.** Using a from-scratch, CPU-trainable char-level Transformer and a fully reproducible pipeline, we (i) confirm sampling-based measures (SE, self-consistency, greedy NLL) dominate a cheap single-pass token-entropy proxy, robustly across three trained models and 11 sweep conditions; (ii) show that under a noise-free clusterer, SE gives no measurable signal beyond self-consistency or NLL, and explain why this is close to definitional rather than surprising; and (iii) show, via dial-able synthetic paraphrase noise, that the predicted SE-over-self-consistency gap does appear and grows with noise level once the ambiguity the noise-free design removes is put back.

## 2. Method

**Model and task.** A 3-layer, 4-head, 64-dim causal Transformer (86K parameters) over a 17-symbol character vocabulary, trained from scratch for 900 SGD steps (batch 128, AdamW, lr=3e-3) on synthetic arithmetic strings `"a<op>b=c;"` (`<op> ∈ {+, -, *}`, 1–3 digit operands, subtraction non-negative by operand swap), loss on the answer portion only. No pretrained weights or internet access (CPU sandbox has no outbound network; confirmed by a failed download attempt). The model is deliberately kept small and undertrained so it does not saturate (overall test error rate: 68%), giving UQ evaluation the label balance it needs.

**Uncertainty measures**, computed per test question: (1) **semantic entropy (SE)** — sample N completions at temperature T, parse each to an integer, cluster by exact numeric equality, take Shannon entropy (nats) of the cluster-frequency distribution; (2) **self-inconsistency (1−SC)** — 1 minus the fraction of samples matching the modal parsed answer; (3) **token entropy (TE)** — entropy of the first generated character's softmax distribution, one forward pass, no sampling; (4) **greedy NLL** — length-normalized negative log-likelihood of the greedy decode, one forward pass per token. `error = 1` if the majority-vote answer is numerically wrong.

**Why SE ≈ self-consistency here is close to definitional.** With an exact-numeric-equality clusterer, SE's cluster partition over the N samples is *identical* to the partition self-consistency's majority vote already sees — both are functions of the same multiset of parsed integers. They are not literally the same statistic (SE is the entropy of the entire cluster-size distribution; 1−SC looks only at the modal cluster's share, so they can rank cases differently when there are several comparably-sized minority clusters), but both are monotonically related to "how concentrated is the sample distribution on one answer," so they induce highly correlated rankings whenever the modal cluster dominates or is absent — the typical case at our error rates. A near-tie in AUROC is thus the expected outcome of the construction, not an independent finding. This motivates the paraphrase-noise experiment: the interesting question is not whether SE beats self-consistency when they share a clusterer, but whether it does when they don't.

**Paraphrase-noise experiment.** Using the identical trained model and pipeline, we post-process each sampled numeric answer into a surface string: with probability `noise_p` we render it as a random alternate equivalent form (`"+42"`, `"42.0"`, `"042"`), otherwise as canonical `str(n)`. SE still clusters by the *parsed integer* (invariant to rendering — operationally "clustering by meaning"). Self-consistency now clusters by the *literal rendered string*, mirroring how it operates on raw text where paraphrase is possible. We sweep `noise_p ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9}` at the main condition (n=300, N=10, T=0.8).

**Multi-seed replication.** We retrain the identical architecture from 3 independent seeds (different weight init and training-data stream) and re-evaluate the main condition for each, to check whether the SE≈SC≈NLL≫TE pattern is a property of one training run or replicates.

**Evaluation.** AUROC of each measure as a predictor of `error`, with 1000-resample nonparametric bootstrap 95% CIs, and pairwise bootstrap tests (paired over the same resampled indices, two-sided). Main condition: N=10, T=0.8, mixed operators/digits, n=300. Ablations (n≈120–300 per condition): operator, operand digit length, sample budget N (5/10/20), temperature (0.5/1.0) — 11 conditions × 3 pairwise comparisons vs. SE = 33 tests total. We report raw p in every panel: the SE-vs-TE effect is p<0.001 in every single condition (survives Bonferroni-33 trivially), while SE-vs-self-consistency and SE-vs-NLL are never significant (min raw p=0.116 across all 33) — correction changes no conclusion.

## 3. Results

**Main condition (n=300, N=10, T=0.8, error rate 68%).** AUROC (95% CI): SE 0.986 (0.975–0.995), self-inconsistency 0.988 (0.975–0.997), greedy NLL 0.991 (0.983–0.997), token entropy 0.672 (0.607–0.733). Pairwise vs. SE: vs. self-inconsistency diff=−0.0015, p=0.54; vs. NLL diff=−0.0047, p=0.116; vs. token entropy diff=+0.314, p<0.001. The three sampling/likelihood measures cluster near-ceiling and are mutually indistinguishable; token entropy is ~0.3 AUROC points worse, the only gap that survives correction.

**By operator (n=120 each).** SE AUROC: + 0.961, − 0.978, × 1.000. Token entropy: + 0.575, − 0.836, × 0.657 — uniformly worse than SE (all p<0.001) but the absolute gap is task-structure-dependent.

**By digit length (n=120 each).** 1-digit: error rate 4.2% (few error labels) — SE 0.909, SC 0.937, NLL 0.941, TE 0.927, all close, underpowered. 2-digit: error rate 94.2% — SE 0.798, SC 0.796, NLL 0.850, TE 0.631 (SE-vs-TE raw p=0.118, low power from only 7 correct-label questions). 3-digit: error rate 100%, AUROC undefined — the model never solves 3-digit problems in this training budget, a capacity limit, not a UQ finding. This remains the weakest ablation: only the 2-digit condition has workable label balance, and each condition uses one random draw rather than resampled problems.

**By sample budget N (n=150 each).** SE AUROC: N=5 → 0.996, N=10 → 0.981, N=20 → 0.952 (tracks differing random draws per N — self-consistency and NLL show the same non-monotonic pattern). Token entropy stays ~0.2 below SE at every N (p<0.001); no sampling-based measure improves from N=5 to N=20.

**By temperature (n=150 each).** T=0.5: SE 0.994, SC 0.991, NLL 0.990, TE 0.694. T=1.0: SE 0.999, SC 0.999, NLL 0.997, TE 0.795. Token entropy improves somewhat at higher temperature but stays well below the sampling-based measures at both settings (p<0.001).

**Multi-seed replication.** Three independently trained models (different init/data-stream seeds), same main condition:

| seed | err rate | SE | SC | NLL | TE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.667 | 0.983 | 0.984 | 0.984 | 0.734 |
| 1 | 0.690 | 0.990 | 0.990 | 0.988 | 0.681 |
| 2 | 0.660 | 0.982 | 0.982 | 0.982 | 0.738 |

The SE≈SC≈NLL≫TE pattern replicates in every seed (sampling-based measures within 0.001–0.002 of each other at each seed; TE 0.25–0.31 points lower). The original run (main condition above) sits inside this same range, so the near-tie among sampling-based measures and the token-entropy gap are not artifacts of one lucky training run.

**Paraphrase-noise experiment (n=300, N=10, T=0.8).** As surface-form noise increases, SE — which clusters by parsed numeric value and is invariant to rendering — stays essentially flat, while self-consistency computed on the literal rendered string degrades:

| noise_p | AUROC SE | AUROC SC (surface-string) | SE − SC | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.986 | 0.988 | −0.001 | 0.54 |
| 0.3 | 0.986 | 0.985 | +0.001 | 0.63 |
| 0.6 | 0.986 | 0.973 | +0.013 | 0.03 |
| 0.9 | 0.986 | 0.964 | +0.022 | <0.001 |

At `noise_p=0`, this reduces to the main-condition near-tie. As noise rises, self-consistency's exact-string matching increasingly fails to recognize that `"42"` and `"+42"` mean the same thing, undercounting agreement and pulling its AUROC down; SE, unaffected by rendering, opens a small but statistically significant and monotonically growing gap. This is the mechanism SE's real-world NLI clustering is designed to exploit, reproduced end-to-end in a controlled sandbox rather than only inferred from its absence.

## 4. Discussion and Conclusion

Sampling-based uncertainty measures — SE, self-consistency, and greedy NLL — all strongly and about equally predict model error (AUROC 0.95–0.99) across three trained models, while single-pass token entropy is reliably weaker (AUROC 0.67–0.84), at negligible cost (full sweep including training: under 2 minutes on a CPU).

Two results together clarify what SE's semantic-clustering step buys. First, when the clusterer is exact numeric equality, SE and self-consistency partition the same samples the same way, so their near-tie is close to a definitional consequence of the construction (§2) rather than a novel finding — the noise-free design can only show an *absence* of extra value, not test for a *presence* of it. Second, the paraphrase-noise follow-up directly tests the presence question: once samples can express the same answer as different strings, self-consistency's exact-match clustering degrades while SE's meaning-based clustering does not, opening a real, significant, noise-level-dependent gap. Together these pin down the mechanism: SE's advantage over self-consistency is attributable specifically to resolving surface-form diversity, absent when there is none, and reappears predictably as that diversity is dialed back in — a controlled demonstration, not only an inference, though still within a synthetic domain with numeric rather than linguistic paraphrase.

**Practical implication.** For domains where correct answers have a small, canonical, checkable set of surface forms (code output, numeric QA, multiple-choice, structured extraction), self-consistency or single-pass NLL likely performs comparably to full SE pipelines and NLI-clustering compute can likely be skipped. For domains with richer surface-form variability, the size of SE's advantage should scale with how much of that variability self-consistency's matching rule fails to normalize away — a testable, quantitative prediction rather than a qualitative one.

**Limitations.** (1) Our model is a small, from-scratch, deliberately undertrained char-Transformer, not a production LLM; absolute AUROC values and the 100%-error 3-digit regime are properties of this toy model and training budget. (2) No internet access was available, so we could not test genuine linguistic paraphrase with a pretrained LLM and NLI clustering; our synthetic paraphrase-noise experiment is a controlled numeric analogue, and testing whether the same noise-level-dependent gap holds under real linguistic paraphrase is the natural next step. (3) The digit-length ablation remains underpowered outside the 2-digit condition and uses one random draw per condition. (4) The paraphrase renderer uses a small, fixed set of 4 surface variants chosen to be self-consistency-breaking but SE-invariant by construction; a richer or adversarial variant set could change the effect size, though the qualitative direction (SE flat, SC degrading with noise) follows from the mechanism regardless of the specific templates.
