# Cheap Proxies for Semantic Uncertainty: When Does Sampling-Based Hallucination Detection Beat a Single Forward Pass?

## Abstract

Semantic entropy (SE) — clustering multiple sampled generations by meaning and computing the entropy of the resulting cluster distribution — is a leading method for detecting when a language model's output is likely wrong (Farquhar et al., 2024). It requires sampling N completions per query, which is expensive. We ask, in a fully verifiable, self-contained toy setting: how much of SE's value comes from (a) sampling multiple completions at all versus (b) the specific semantic-clustering step versus (c) uncertainty visible in a single forward pass? We train a small char-level Transformer from scratch (no internet, CPU-only, ~90s to train) to solve arithmetic problems, deliberately under-trained so it produces a genuine mix of correct and incorrect answers (68% error rate overall). Using exact numeric equivalence as a noise-free semantic clusterer, we compare SE against (i) self-consistency, (ii) single-pass first-token predictive entropy, and (iii) single-pass length-normalized greedy NLL, as predictors of error (AUROC). All three sampling/likelihood-based measures achieve AUROC 0.95–0.99 and are statistically indistinguishable; the cheap single-pass token-entropy proxy is reliably worse (AUROC 0.67–0.84, gap vs. SE always p<0.001, 11 sweep conditions), replicating across three independently trained models. Because in this domain self-consistency's exact-match clustering and SE's numeric-equivalence clustering induce the *same* partition of samples, their near-tie is close to definitional, not an empirical surprise. To test the paper's central claim directly, we add synthetic surface-form ("paraphrase") noise (e.g. "42" vs "+42" vs "42.0"): self-consistency, which matches on the literal string, degrades as noise increases, while SE stays flat, producing a significant SE-over-self-consistency gap at high noise. This revision adds two checks the previous round lacked: (1) we re-run the full paraphrase-noise sweep across the same 3 independently trained models used for the noise-free replication, finding the *direction* of the gap is consistent at the highest noise level in all three seeds (+0.012, +0.010, +0.018 AUROC) but statistical significance is not — 2 of 3 seeds clear p<0.05 at noise_p=0.9, one does not (p=0.06) — so the effect is real but small enough to be seed-sensitive at n=300; and (2) we re-run the digit-length ablation with 8 resampled problem draws per digit length instead of one, which quantifies rather than merely notes the label-imbalance problem: even with resampling, AUROC is undefined (single-class labels) in over half of 3-digit draws and in 3/8 one-digit draws, confirming this ablation needs a much larger n, not just point-estimate re-reporting, before its numbers should be trusted.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect output, and estimating when this is likely is a central open problem in trustworthy NLP. Uncertainty quantification (UQ) for LLMs (Liu, Chen, Da et al., 2025, KDD 2025 / arXiv:2503.15850) organizes methods along cost (single forward pass vs. sampling-based) and source of uncertainty. Semantic entropy (SE; Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*) samples several completions, clusters them by whether a bidirectional entailment model judges them mutually implying, and computes the Shannon entropy of the resulting cluster-size distribution — distinguishing it from naive token-level predictive entropy, which is inflated by benign lexical variation.

SE outperforms naive token-level uncertainty and self-consistency baselines but costs 5–10x more compute than a single forward pass. This has motivated single-pass approximations (semantic entropy probes, Kossen et al., 2024, arXiv:2406.15927; BaseCal, arXiv:2601.03042; semantic energy, OpenReview:E5mL07Fbq8) and arguments that self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) is already strong and cheaper than NLI clustering. A 2025 benchmarking study (arXiv:2604.10495) asks which *sources* of uncertainty drive UQ performance, echoing our question differently.

**Gap.** Existing SE-vs-self-consistency comparisons run on open-ended NL QA, where clustering must resolve genuine surface-form ambiguity ("Paris" vs. "It's Paris, France"). This conflates (1) sampling multiple times at all with (2) clustering by meaning specifically versus exact-match. We build a synthetic, exactly-verifiable arithmetic task to isolate both: first with "semantic equivalence" reduced to exact numeric equality, then with synthetic paraphrase noise reintroduced in a dial-able way.

**Contribution.** We (i) confirm sampling-based measures (SE, self-consistency, greedy NLL) dominate a cheap single-pass token-entropy proxy, robustly across three trained models and 11 sweep conditions; (ii) show that under a noise-free clusterer, SE gives no measurable signal beyond self-consistency or NLL, and explain why this is close to definitional; (iii) show, via dial-able synthetic paraphrase noise, that the predicted SE-over-self-consistency gap appears and grows with noise level, and (iv) newly this round, check that this gap's *direction* — but not always its statistical significance at our sample size — replicates across independently trained models, and quantify (rather than only flag) the label-imbalance limitation of the digit-length ablation via resampling.

## 2. Method

**Model and task.** A 3-layer, 4-head, 64-dim causal Transformer (86K parameters) over a 17-symbol character vocabulary, trained from scratch for 900 SGD steps (batch 128, AdamW, lr=3e-3) on synthetic arithmetic strings `"a<op>b=c;"` (`<op> ∈ {+, -, *}`, 1–3 digit operands, subtraction non-negative by operand swap), loss on the answer portion only. No pretrained weights or internet access. The model is deliberately kept small and undertrained (overall test error rate: 68%), giving UQ evaluation the label balance it needs.

**Uncertainty measures**, computed per test question: (1) **SE** — sample N completions at temperature T, parse each to an integer, cluster by exact numeric equality, take Shannon entropy of the cluster-frequency distribution; (2) **self-inconsistency (1−SC)** — 1 minus the fraction of samples matching the modal parsed answer; (3) **token entropy (TE)** — entropy of the first generated character's softmax distribution, one forward pass; (4) **greedy NLL** — length-normalized NLL of the greedy decode. `error = 1` if the majority-vote answer is numerically wrong.

**Why SE ≈ self-consistency here is close to definitional.** With an exact-numeric-equality clusterer, SE's cluster partition over the N samples is *identical* to the partition self-consistency's majority vote already sees. Both are monotonically related to "how concentrated is the sample distribution on one answer," so they induce highly correlated rankings whenever the modal cluster dominates or is absent (the typical case at our error rates). A near-tie in AUROC is thus expected from the construction, not an independent finding.

**Paraphrase-noise experiment.** Using the identical trained model and pipeline, we post-process each sampled numeric answer into a surface string: with probability `noise_p` we render it as a random alternate equivalent form (`"+42"`, `"42.0"`, `"042"`), otherwise as canonical `str(n)`. SE still clusters by the parsed integer (invariant to rendering). Self-consistency clusters by the literal rendered string. We sweep `noise_p ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9}` at the main condition (n=300, N=10, T=0.8).

**Multi-seed replication (noise-free and paraphrase).** We retrain the identical architecture from 3 independent seeds (different weight init and training-data stream). We re-evaluate the noise-free main condition for each (as before), and this round additionally re-run the *full paraphrase noise_p sweep* for each of the 3 seeds (`paraphrase_seed_experiment.py`), to check whether the noise-level-dependent gap — not just the noise-free near-tie — is a property of one training run.

**Digit-length ablation with resampling.** The original ablation drew one fixed problem set of n=120 per digit length. This round we instead draw 8 independent problem sets of n=60 per digit length (`digit_length_experiment.py`, same trained model, same generation settings) and report, per digit length, the mean error rate, how many of the 8 draws have both classes present (AUROC defined), and the mean/min/max AUROC over the draws where it is defined — quantifying the label-imbalance problem directly instead of noting it qualitatively.

**Evaluation.** AUROC of each measure as a predictor of `error`, with 1000-resample nonparametric bootstrap 95% CIs, and pairwise bootstrap tests (paired over the same resampled indices, two-sided). Main condition: N=10, T=0.8, mixed operators/digits, n=300. Ablations (n≈120–300 per condition): operator, digit length, sample budget N (5/10/20), temperature (0.5/1.0) — 11 conditions × 3 pairwise comparisons vs. SE = 33 tests. SE-vs-TE is p<0.001 in every condition (survives Bonferroni-33 trivially); SE-vs-self-consistency and SE-vs-NLL are never significant (min raw p=0.116).

## 3. Results

**Main condition (n=300, N=10, T=0.8, error rate 68%).** AUROC: SE 0.986 (0.975–0.995), self-inconsistency 0.988 (0.975–0.997), greedy NLL 0.991 (0.983–0.997), token entropy 0.672 (0.607–0.733). Pairwise vs. SE: vs. self-inconsistency diff=−0.0015, p=0.54; vs. NLL diff=−0.0047, p=0.116; vs. token entropy diff=+0.314, p<0.001.

**By operator / digit length / sample budget / temperature.** SE beats token entropy at p<0.001 in every one of these conditions (e.g. by operator: SE 0.961/0.978/1.000 for +/−/×, TE 0.575/0.836/0.657); SE, self-consistency, and NLL remain mutually indistinguishable throughout. Full per-condition numbers are unchanged from the prior round and archived in `results.json`.

**Multi-seed replication, noise-free (3 seeds).** The SE≈SC≈NLL≫TE pattern replicates in every seed (sampling-based measures within 0.001–0.002 of each other per seed; TE 0.25–0.31 points lower), confirming this is not an artifact of one training run (`seed_results.json`).

**Paraphrase-noise experiment, original seed (n=300, N=10, T=0.8).** SE stays flat at 0.986 as noise rises; self-consistency on the literal rendered string degrades: 0.988→0.985→0.973→0.964 at noise_p=0/0.3/0.6/0.9, gap p=0.54/0.63/0.03/<0.001.

**Paraphrase-noise, multi-seed check (new this round).** We re-ran the full noise_p sweep on the same 3 independently trained models used above (`paraphrase_seed_experiment.py`):

| seed | noise_p=0 diff (p) | 0.3 diff (p) | 0.6 diff (p) | 0.9 diff (p) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | −0.001 (0.74) | +0.001 (0.84) | −0.001 (0.84) | **+0.012 (0.024)** |
| 1 | −0.000 (0.97) | +0.001 (0.84) | +0.012 (0.072) | +0.010 (0.060) |
| 2 | +0.000 (1.00) | −0.002 (0.64) | +0.002 (0.67) | **+0.018 (0.004)** |

(diff = AUROC_SE − AUROC_SC-surface; positive favors SE.) The direction is consistent across all three seeds: the gap is essentially zero at noise_p≤0.3 and positive at noise_p=0.9 in every seed. But statistical significance at n=300 is not fully consistent — seeds 0 and 2 clear p<0.05 at noise_p=0.9, seed 1 does not (p=0.06, and its noise_p=0.6 point is also only marginal at p=0.072). The effect is real and reproducible in direction and rough magnitude (+0.010 to +0.018 at noise_p=0.9 across seeds, versus +0.022 on the original seed), but it is small enough relative to n=300 that individual-seed significance tests are somewhat noise-sensitive — a materially more honest picture than reporting only the original seed's clean p<0.001.

**Digit-length ablation, resampled (new this round).** 8 draws of n=60 per digit length, same trained model:

| digits | mean err rate | draws with defined AUROC | AUROC mean (defined draws) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.7% | 5/8 | 0.766 |
| 2 | 93.3% | 7/8 | 0.746 |
| 3 | 99.6% | 2/8 | 0.657 |

Even with 8x the resampling of the original single-draw design, more than half the 3-digit draws and 3/8 of the 1-digit draws have only one error-label class present (AUROC undefined) at n=60 per draw — the ablation is fundamentally starved of the minority label at these digit lengths given this model's error-rate profile, not merely under-sampled in a way one extra draw fixes. The 2-digit condition is closest to usable (7/8 draws defined) but even there AUROC varies enough across draws (mean 0.746) that a much larger per-draw n, not more draws, is the needed fix; we report this honestly as unresolved rather than claim it is fixed.

## 4. Discussion and Conclusion

Sampling-based uncertainty measures — SE, self-consistency, and greedy NLL — all strongly and about equally predict model error (AUROC 0.95–0.99) across three trained models, while single-pass token entropy is reliably weaker (AUROC 0.67–0.84), at negligible cost (full sweep including training: under 2 minutes on CPU).

Two results clarify what SE's semantic-clustering step buys. When the clusterer is exact numeric equality, SE and self-consistency partition the same samples the same way, so their near-tie is close to a definitional consequence of the construction rather than a novel finding. The paraphrase-noise follow-up tests the presence question directly: once samples can express the same answer as different strings, self-consistency's exact-match clustering degrades while SE's meaning-based clustering does not, opening a real, noise-level-dependent gap. This round's multi-seed check refines that conclusion: the gap's *direction* is robust across independently trained models, but its *statistical significance at n=300* is not always robust — one of three seeds misses p<0.05 even at the highest noise level tested. We read this as evidence the mechanism is genuine but the effect size in this synthetic domain is modest, consistent with the round-1 review's observation that even the clearest single-seed gap (+0.022 at noise_p=0.9) is numerically small; readers should treat the noise-dependent SE advantage as a real, replicable-in-direction but not always significant-per-seed effect at this sample size, not as a large, easily-detected one.

**Practical implication.** For domains where correct answers have a small, canonical, checkable set of surface forms (code output, numeric QA, multiple-choice, structured extraction), self-consistency or single-pass NLL likely performs comparably to full SE pipelines. For domains with richer surface-form variability, the size of SE's advantage should scale with how much of that variability self-consistency's matching rule fails to normalize away.

**Limitations.** (1) Our model is a small, from-scratch, deliberately undertrained char-Transformer, not a production LLM; absolute AUROC values and the near-100%-error 3-digit regime are properties of this toy model and training budget. (2) No internet access was available, so we could not test genuine linguistic paraphrase with a pretrained LLM and NLI clustering; our synthetic paraphrase-noise experiment is a controlled numeric analogue. (3) The digit-length ablation, even after this round's 8-draw resampling, remains underpowered: most 3-digit draws and a substantial fraction of 1-digit draws have no defined AUROC because one error-label class is nearly absent at this model's current training budget; fixing it needs either a differently-tuned model with more balanced per-digit-length error rates or substantially larger per-draw n, not just more draws. (4) The paraphrase-noise SE-over-self-consistency gap is directionally consistent across 3 seeds but not always individually significant at n=300 per seed — readers should not treat the single clean p<0.001 result from the original seed as representative of the typical per-seed significance level. (5) The paraphrase renderer uses a small, fixed set of 4 surface variants chosen to be self-consistency-breaking but SE-invariant by construction; a richer or adversarial variant set could change the effect size.
