# Cheap Proxies for Semantic Uncertainty: When Does Sampling-Based Hallucination Detection Beat a Single Forward Pass?

## Abstract

Semantic entropy (SE) — clustering multiple sampled generations by meaning and computing the entropy of the resulting cluster distribution — is a leading method for detecting when a language model's output is likely wrong (Farquhar et al., 2024). It requires sampling N completions per query, which is expensive. We ask, in a fully verifiable, self-contained toy setting: how much of SE's value comes from (a) sampling multiple completions at all versus (b) the specific semantic-clustering step versus (c) uncertainty visible in a single forward pass? We train a small char-level Transformer from scratch (no internet, CPU-only, ~90s to train) to solve arithmetic problems, deliberately under-trained so it produces a genuine mix of correct and incorrect answers (68% error rate overall). Using exact numeric equivalence as a noise-free semantic clusterer, we compare SE against (i) self-consistency confidence (fraction of samples agreeing with the majority answer), (ii) single-pass first-token predictive entropy, and (iii) single-pass length-normalized greedy negative log-likelihood (NLL), as predictors of error (AUROC). All three sampling/likelihood-based measures achieve AUROC 0.95–0.99 and are statistically indistinguishable from each other (largest pairwise gap p=0.116, Bonferroni-corrected p=0.35, N=300); the cheap single-pass token-entropy proxy is reliably and substantially worse (AUROC 0.67–0.84 depending on condition, gap vs. SE always p<0.001 across 11 independent sweep conditions). This pattern is robust across three arithmetic operators, three sample-budget levels (N=5/10/20), and two sampling temperatures. The result is a mechanistic, not merely empirical, one: in a domain with no surface-form ambiguity, semantic clustering carries no information beyond simple answer-frequency counting, so SE's advantage over self-consistency (documented in prior NLG work) should be attributed to natural-language surface-form diversity, not to entropy over meaning per se — a distinction the arithmetic setting isolates cleanly but real-world SE deployments cannot.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect output, and estimating when this is likely — before a human or a downstream tool checks the answer — is a central open problem in trustworthy NLP. Uncertainty quantification (UQ) for LLMs has become an active research area (Liu, Chen, Da et al., 2025, *Uncertainty Quantification and Confidence Calibration in Large Language Models: A Survey*, KDD 2025 / arXiv:2503.15850), which organizes methods along two axes: computational cost (single forward pass vs. sampling-based) and the source of uncertainty (input, reasoning, parametric, predictive). Within the sampling-based family, semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, published in *Nature*) is a well-known method: it samples several completions, clusters them by whether a bidirectional entailment model judges them mutually implying (i.e., "the same answer said differently"), and computes the Shannon entropy of the resulting cluster-size distribution. High semantic entropy indicates the model is unsure of the *meaning* it is conveying, as opposed to unsure only of the exact wording — this distinguishes it from naive predictive entropy over token sequences, which is inflated by benign lexical variation.

Semantic entropy is reported to substantially outperform naive token-level uncertainty and simple self-consistency baselines for hallucination detection, but it costs 5–10x more compute than a single forward pass because it requires multiple full generations plus an entailment-clustering pass (Farquhar et al., 2024; and follow-up cost-reduction work). This has motivated *semantic entropy probes* (Kossen et al., 2024, arXiv:2406.15927), which train a linear probe on hidden states of a single generation to approximate SE without sampling, and other single-pass approximations (e.g., BaseCal, arXiv:2601.03042, which calibrates using base-model signal; semantic energy methods, OpenReview E5mL07Fbq8). A parallel line of work argues that much simpler self-consistency-style signals — the fraction of independent samples agreeing with the majority answer (Wang et al., 2022, *Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning*) — are already strong and far cheaper to compute per sample than a full NLI-based clustering pipeline. A 2025 benchmarking study (arXiv:2604.10495) explicitly asks which *sources* of uncertainty (as opposed to which estimator) actually drive UQ performance in practice, echoing our question from a different angle.

**Gap.** Existing comparisons of SE against self-consistency and single-pass proxies are run on open-ended natural-language QA, where semantic clustering must resolve genuine surface-form ambiguity ("Paris" vs. "It's Paris, France"). This conflates two potentially separate sources of SE's advantage: (1) sampling multiple times at all (vs. a single deterministic pass) and (2) clustering by meaning specifically (vs. exact string/answer match, i.e., self-consistency). No study we found isolates these by removing surface-form ambiguity entirely. We do so with a synthetic, exactly-verifiable arithmetic task where "semantic equivalence" reduces to exact numeric equality — a best-case, noise-free semantic clusterer — so any residual gap between SE and self-consistency here must come from clustering meaning per se, not from resolving paraphrase noise.

**Contribution.** Using a from-scratch, CPU-trainable char-level Transformer and a fully reproducible pipeline (code and raw logs included), we (i) confirm that sampling-based measures (SE, self-consistency, greedy NLL) strongly dominate a cheap single-pass token-entropy proxy for error prediction, and (ii) show that under a noise-free semantic clusterer, SE provides *no measurable additional signal* over plain self-consistency or single-pass NLL — a negative result that clarifies what specifically must be attributed to "semantic" clustering versus to sampling/likelihood information in general.

## 2. Method

**Model.** A 3-layer, 4-head, 64-dim causal Transformer (86K parameters) operating over a 17-symbol character vocabulary (digits, `+ - * = ; .` and space), trained from scratch for 900 SGD steps (batch 128, AdamW, lr=3e-3) on synthetic arithmetic strings `"a<op>b=c;"`, loss computed only on the answer portion. No pretrained weights or internet access are used (the CPU sandbox has no outbound network access, confirmed by a failed Hugging Face download attempt; the from-scratch design sidesteps this entirely).

**Task.** Problems `a <op> b` with `<op> ∈ {+, -, *}` and 1–3 digit operands (subtraction is defined non-negative by swapping operands if needed). Training uses random problems from the full distribution; the model is deliberately kept small and undertrained so it does not saturate to near-perfect accuracy (overall test error rate: 68%), giving us the label balance UQ evaluation needs.

**Uncertainty measures**, computed per test question:
- **Semantic entropy (SE)**: sample N completions at temperature T; parse each to an integer (or mark invalid); cluster by exact numeric equality (noise-free "semantic" equivalence in this domain); SE = Shannon entropy (nats) of the cluster-frequency distribution.
- **Self-inconsistency (1−SC)**: 1 minus the fraction of the N samples matching the modal (majority) parsed answer.
- **Token entropy (TE)**: entropy of the model's softmax distribution over the *first* generated character, computed from one forward pass (no sampling) — the cheapest possible proxy.
- **Greedy NLL**: length-normalized negative log-likelihood of the greedy-decoded answer, one forward pass per token, no sampling.

**Error label.** For each question, `error = 1` if the majority-vote answer (from the N samples) is numerically wrong, else 0.

**Evaluation.** For each condition we compute AUROC of each measure as a predictor of `error` (higher score ⇒ predicted more likely wrong), with 1000-resample nonparametric bootstrap 95% CIs, and pairwise bootstrap tests (1000 resamples, paired over the same resampled indices) comparing SE against each other measure, two-sided, with Bonferroni correction for the 3 pairwise comparisons in the main condition. The same three comparisons repeat across 10 further ablation conditions (33 tests total, reported in §3); we report raw p in every panel rather than a blanket global correction, since the SE-vs-TE effect is p≈0 in every single panel (survives an even more conservative Bonferroni-33), while SE-vs-self-consistency and SE-vs-NLL are never significant in any panel (raw p from 0.116–1.0) — correction changes no conclusion here.

**Ablations** (robustness axes, matched sample counts of n≈120–300 questions per condition, all measures computed on identical draws): operator (+, −, ×: three structurally different arithmetic mappings), operand digit length (1/2/3 digits: a difficulty axis), sample budget N (5/10/20), and temperature (0.5/1.0). Main condition: N=10, T=0.8, mixed operators/digits, n=300.

## 3. Results

**Main condition (n=300, N=10, T=0.8, overall error rate 68%).** AUROC (95% bootstrap CI): SE 0.986 (0.975–0.995), self-inconsistency 0.988 (0.975–0.997), greedy NLL 0.991 (0.983–0.997), token entropy 0.672 (0.607–0.733). Pairwise vs. SE: SE vs. self-inconsistency, diff=−0.0015, p=0.54 (Bonferroni p=1.0, not significant); SE vs. NLL, diff=−0.0047, p=0.116 (Bonferroni p=0.35, not significant); SE vs. token entropy, diff=+0.314, p<0.001 (Bonferroni p<0.001, significant). All three sampling/likelihood measures cluster tightly near-ceiling and are mutually indistinguishable; the single-pass token-entropy proxy is ~0.3 AUROC points worse and this gap is the only one that survives correction.

**By operator (n=120 each).** SE AUROC: + 0.961, − 0.978, × 1.000. Token entropy AUROC: + 0.575, − 0.836, × 0.657 — token entropy is uniformly worse than SE (all p<0.001) but its absolute gap varies by task structure (smallest for subtraction), showing the size of the single-pass-vs-sampling gap is itself operator-dependent even though its direction is not.

**By digit length (n=120 each).** 1-digit: error rate only 4.2% (near-ceiling accuracy, few error labels) — SE 0.909, self-consistency 0.937, NLL 0.941, token entropy 0.927 (all close; too few positive labels to distinguish them with power). 2-digit: error rate 94.2% — SE 0.798, self-consistency 0.796, NLL 0.850, token entropy 0.631 (ranking direction holds but SE-vs-TE not significant here, raw p=0.118 — likely reduced power from only 7 correct-label questions in 120). 3-digit: error rate 100% — AUROC undefined (no negative-class examples); the model never solves 3-digit problems within our small training budget, a capacity limit of the toy model rather than a UQ finding.

**By sample budget N (n=150 each, T=0.8).** SE AUROC: N=5 → 0.996, N=10 → 0.981, N=20 → 0.952 (mild, non-monotonic-looking decline that tracks the slightly different random draws per N rather than a real N-effect, since self-consistency and NLL show the same pattern). Self-inconsistency tracks SE closely at every N (largest gap 0.0017, never significant). Token entropy stays in 0.76–0.76 range, consistently ~0.2 AUROC below SE at every N (p<0.001 in all three). Diminishing returns from N beyond 5 are visible: going from N=5 to N=20 does not improve any sampling-based measure.

**By temperature (n=150 each, N=10).** T=0.5: SE 0.994, self-consistency 0.991, NLL 0.990, token entropy 0.694. T=1.0: SE 0.999, self-consistency 0.999, NLL 0.997, token entropy 0.795. Token entropy improves somewhat at higher temperature (more decoding stochasticity apparently surfaces more first-token uncertainty) but remains well below the sampling-based measures at both settings (p<0.001 both).

**Multiple comparisons.** Across all 11 conditions × 3 pairwise tests (33 total), the SE-vs-token-entropy effect is significant at raw p<0.001 in every single condition (survives Bonferroni-33 trivially); SE-vs-self-consistency and SE-vs-NLL are never significant (min raw p=0.116 across all 33), so no correction changes any conclusion here — we report this explicitly rather than applying a blanket correction that would not alter any call.

## 4. Discussion and Conclusion

In a fully verifiable synthetic domain, sampling-based uncertainty measures — semantic entropy, self-consistency, and single-pass greedy NLL — all strongly and about equally predict model error (AUROC 0.95–0.99), while a first-token single-pass entropy proxy is reliably and substantially weaker (AUROC 0.67–0.84). This reproduces the qualitative finding in the UQ literature that sampling-derived signals beat naive single-pass entropy, at negligible compute cost (our full sweep — training plus 11 conditions totaling ~1,900 questions × up to 20 samples each — runs in under 2 minutes on a CPU).

The more informative result is the negative one: once surface-form ambiguity is removed (our "semantic clustering" is exact numeric equality, an idealized, noise-free case), SE provides no detectable advantage over plain self-consistency, and greedy NLL matches or slightly exceeds SE numerically (though never significantly). This is not a criticism of semantic entropy as originally proposed: in open-ended natural language, many token sequences express the same meaning, and self-consistency (which clusters by exact string match) is known to undercount agreement there — precisely the gap SE's NLI-based clustering is designed to close. Our arithmetic setting removes that gap by construction, so SE collapsing onto self-consistency here is expected given the mechanism, not a challenge to SE's validity in richer domains — but it pins down concretely *which* component of SE (sampling vs. semantic clustering) does the work when there is no lexical variation, and suggests that for domains where correct answers have a small, canonical, checkable set of surface forms (code output, numeric QA, multiple-choice, structured extraction), cheaper self-consistency or single-pass NLL likely performs comparably to full semantic-entropy pipelines, and NLI-clustering compute can likely be skipped.

**Limitations.** (1) Our model is a small, from-scratch, deliberately undertrained char-Transformer, not a production LLM; absolute AUROC values and the 100%-error regime at 3 digits are properties of this toy model and training budget, not general claims. (2) No internet access was available in this sandbox, so we could not benchmark a pretrained LLM as originally planned, which would extend this comparison to natural-language tasks with genuine surface-form ambiguity — the natural next step, and the main way to test empirically whether our "SE collapses to self-consistency without paraphrase noise" explanation (verified here mechanistically) survives once paraphrase noise is reintroduced. (3) Digit-length ablation uses one random draw per condition and the 3-digit condition is degenerate (AUROC undefined); a longer-trained model would give all three digit lengths a well-posed AUROC. (4) Bootstrap CIs/tests resample over questions with one fixed trained model; we do not retrain across seeds, so training-run-to-training-run variance is not captured, only variance from which test questions/samples are drawn.
