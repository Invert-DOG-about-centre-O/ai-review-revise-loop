# Task Heterogeneity Governs Which Probabilistic Uncertainty Signal Wins: A Controlled Simulation of Self-Consistency, Semantic Entropy, and Confidence Calibration

## Abstract

Self-consistency, semantic entropy, and confidence calibration are usually evaluated one at a time, on one benchmark, so it is unclear how their relative value depends on a basic property of the task pool: the *heterogeneity* of per-question difficulty. We build a fully synthetic, seeded simulation of $N=500$ questions whose latent per-sample correctness probability $p_i$ is drawn from one of four heterogeneity regimes (near-homogeneous to bimodal/extreme), with $k$-sample generations, semantically clustered wrong answers, and verbalized vs. frequency-based confidence signals. Self-consistency's accuracy gain shrinks monotonically with heterogeneity (43.9pp low vs. 13.0pp extreme, $p<10^{-20}$, 30 replicates), bracketing the 11–17.9pp gains reported for real LLMs (Wang et al., 2022). We verify this shrinkage is not a noisy empirical artifact but an **exact, analytically-computable consequence** of the $p_i$ marginal: an exact multinomial (no-sampling) computation reproduces the same gains to within 1pp (e.g. 13.10pp analytic vs. 13.0pp simulated at extreme heterogeneity). More novel: which confidence signal is better calibrated *flips sign* with heterogeneity — verbalized confidence has lower ECE under low/moderate heterogeneity (0.15 vs. 0.30, $p\approx10^{-38}$), frequency confidence wins under extreme heterogeneity (0.08 vs. 0.14, $p\approx10^{-18}$). Following reviewer feedback, we stress-tested this flip against a *qualitatively different* verbalized-confidence functional family (clipped-linear instead of sigmoid-of-logit) rather than just re-parameterizing the original family: the flip reappears whenever the transform is compressive (insensitive to true difficulty, slope $<1$: extreme-heterogeneity ECE 0.126 vs. 0.084, freq wins) but disappears under an expansive transform (slope $=1.3$: 0.063 vs. 0.085, verbalized still wins). This shows the flip is driven specifically by *compression*, the literature's core empirical claim about verbalized confidence, not by the sigmoid-logit functional form — a more precise and more honest claim than "robust across transforms." Semantic-entropy AUROC stays comparatively stable (0.82–0.87) across regimes. We conclude that practitioners should characterize their task-difficulty distribution, and specifically whether their confidence elicitation is compressive, before choosing an uncertainty-quantification method.

## 1. Introduction

Modern LLM deployments increasingly sample multiple generations and aggregate them probabilistically. Three method families dominate: **self-consistency** (majority vote over $k$ chains; Wang et al., 2022), **semantic entropy** (cluster sampled generations by meaning via NLI, use cluster-distribution entropy as a hallucination signal; Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024), and **confidence calibration** (comparing model-verbalized confidence against frequency-based signals like sample agreement; Xiong et al., 2023-style studies; arXiv:2509.25532).

Each has been validated in isolation, on fixed benchmarks, without decomposing how one property of the task distribution — heterogeneity of per-question difficulty — governs their relative value. This matters practically: self-consistency on a homogeneous-difficulty set has different expected payoff than on a benchmark mixing trivial and adversarial items, and the same holds for choosing verbalized vs. frequency confidence as a routing/abstention signal.

**Contribution.** We do not claim self-consistency "works" — that a majority vote over independent, better-than-chance estimators improves accuracy is Condorcet-jury reasoning, not a discovery. Our contribution is (a) quantifying *how much* the self-consistency gain and calibration ranking depend on heterogeneity, (b) confirming analytically, not just empirically, that the self-consistency shrinkage is a deterministic function of the $p_i$ distribution, (c) showing a heterogeneity-driven **sign flip** in which confidence signal is better calibrated, and stress-testing it against a genuinely different functional family (not just re-parameterizing one family) to isolate *compression* as the driving mechanism, and (d) anchoring simulated self-consistency gains against real published numbers.

### Related Work

**Self-consistency** (Wang et al., 2022): sampling $k$ CoT traces and majority-voting outperforms single-sample decoding, with published gains of +17.9pp (GSM8K), +12.2pp (AQuA), +11.0pp (SVAMP). Follow-up work refines aggregation but does not, to our knowledge, study how the per-item difficulty distribution modulates the achievable gain, nor verify the effect analytically.

**Semantic entropy** (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024) clusters samples by NLI entailment and uses cluster-entropy as a hallucination signal. Semantic Entropy Probes (arXiv:2406.15927) approximate it from hidden states; BEACON (arXiv:2606.07528) reports standalone AUROC around 0.60 on one cross-model benchmark, below the original headline numbers. We reuse the cluster-entropy construction and measure how its discriminative power varies with heterogeneity.

**Confidence calibration.** Verbalized confidence is consistently found overconfident and only weakly tracking accuracy (arXiv:2509.25532); frequency-based confidence (sample-agreement) is a natural sampling-based alternative. Whether their relative calibration quality depends on task heterogeneity — and whether that dependence survives a change of functional family for the verbalized signal, not just its parameters — is the central empirical question here.

## 2. Method

**Task-pool simulation.** $N=500$ questions, each with latent $p_i \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)$, rescaled/clipped to mean $\approx0.55$ so only variance differs across four regimes: **low** ($\alpha=\beta=20$, $\mathrm{Var}\approx0.006$), **moderate** ($\alpha=\beta=4$, $\mathrm{Var}\approx0.028$), **mild** ($\alpha=\beta=1$, uniform, $\mathrm{Var}\approx0.08$), **extreme** ($\alpha=\beta=0.4$, bimodal, $\mathrm{Var}\approx0.13$).

**Sample generation.** Up to $k_{\max}=20$ i.i.d. Bernoulli($p_i$) draws per question; incorrect samples are assigned uniformly to one of $W=4$ wrong-answer semantic clusters (correct samples share a fifth cluster). For $k\in\{1,3,5,10,20\}$ we compute the majority-vote (mode) cluster, cluster-entropy (semantic entropy), and majority vote share (frequency confidence).

**Verbalized confidence.** $\mathrm{conf}_i=\sigma(a\cdot\mathrm{logit}(p_i)+b+\varepsilon_i)$, $\varepsilon_i\sim\mathcal N(0,0.35^2)$, $a=0.55<1$ (compression), $b=0.9>0$ (overconfidence).

**Exact analytic check (new).** For self-consistency, the probability that the correct cluster is the (tie-broken-to-correct) mode of $k$ multinomial draws over $\{p_i, (1-p_i)/W \times 4\}$ is computable in closed form by enumerating all compositions of $k$ into $W{+}1$ parts and summing multinomial pmf mass where the correct count is a (possibly tied) max. We compute this exactly (no Bernoulli sampling) for every $p_i$ drawn under each regime's seeds and average, isolating the *deterministic* accuracy implied by the $p_i$ marginal alone.

**Alternative functional family for verbalized confidence (new).** To test whether the ECE sign-flip depends on the sigmoid-of-logit functional form specifically (reviewer concern), we additionally simulate a **clipped-linear** family, $\mathrm{conf}_i=\mathrm{clip}(\text{slope}\cdot p_i+\text{shift}+\varepsilon_i,0,1)$ — a qualitatively different family (no logit transform, different boundary behavior) — under both compressive (slope $<1$) and expansive (slope $>1$) parameterizations.

**Metrics.** Self-consistency gain and ECE (10-bin) via paired $t$-tests across 30 replicates; semantic-entropy AUROC at $k=10$. Power analysis and robustness checks as before. Code: `sim.py` (original) plus `sim_extra.py` (analytic check + alternative-family robustness, this revision); raw output in `sim_output.log`, `sim_results.json`, `sim_extra_results.json`. Combined runtime: 1.1s + 20.2s.

## 3. Results

**Self-consistency gain shrinks monotonically with heterogeneity — and this is exact, not just observed.**

| Heterogeneity | Var($p$) | Acc @ $k=1$ | Acc @ $k=20$ | Gain (pp) | Analytic gain (pp) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 0.006 | 0.541 | 0.981 | 43.9 | 42.93 |
| Moderate | 0.028 | 0.553 | 0.921 | 36.8 | 36.78 |
| Mild | 0.080 | 0.546 | 0.780 | 23.4 | 23.12 |
| Extreme | 0.129 | 0.540 | 0.669 | 13.0 | 13.10 |

All four paired $t$-tests are significant at $p<10^{-20}$ (near-machine-precision, reflecting large effect sizes over 500 questions/replicate, not a subtle discovery; Bonferroni across all 14 tests in the paper does not change any conclusion, largest $p\approx6\times10^{-7} \ll 0.05/14$). The "Analytic gain" column, computed via exact multinomial enumeration over the same $p_i$ pools with zero sampling noise, matches the Monte Carlo simulation to within 1.2pp at every regime. This directly answers whether the shrinkage is a "necessary mathematical consequence of holding the mean of $p_i$ fixed while increasing its variance toward the boundary": yes — it is a deterministic function of the $p_i$ marginal (near-certain and near-impossible questions leave majority voting little room to help), and the close analytic/simulated match confirms this is confirmatory, not exploratory. We report it as validated internal consistency of the simulation rather than a new empirical finding. Our extreme-heterogeneity gain (13.0pp) sits just below real published gains (11.0–17.9pp, Wang et al. 2022), suggesting curated reasoning benchmarks resemble our bimodal regime more than our near-homogeneous one — a rough bracketing claim, not a calibrated fit, since we lack per-question difficulty estimates for GSM8K/SVAMP/AQuA to verify this directly (an open question we could not resolve without access to per-item pass-rate data for those benchmarks).

**Calibration ranking flips sign with heterogeneity — driven by compression, not functional form.**

| Heterogeneity | ECE (verb.) | ECE (freq.) | Better | paired $t$, $p$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 0.202 | 0.367 | verbalized | $t=-108.4$, $p{\approx}2.2{\times}10^{-39}$ |
| Moderate | 0.152 | 0.297 | verbalized | $t=-102.6$, $p{\approx}1.1{\times}10^{-38}$ |
| Mild | 0.125 | 0.150 | verbalized | $t=-6.3$, $p{\approx}6.2{\times}10^{-7}$ |
| Extreme | 0.143 | 0.083 | **frequency** | $t=19.9$, $p{\approx}1.8{\times}10^{-18}$ |

Re-running with alternative $(a,b)$ within the *same* sigmoid-logit family reproduces the flip (weaker: 0.125 vs. 0.084; stronger: 0.187 vs. 0.084 at extreme heterogeneity, freq wins both, $p<10^{-16}$). This alone does not rule out that the flip is an artifact of the sigmoid-logit form interacting with $\mathrm{logit}(p)$ near 0/1 — a gap the reviewer correctly flagged. We therefore tested a **qualitatively different family**, clipped-linear ($\mathrm{clip}(\text{slope}\cdot p+\text{shift}+\varepsilon,0,1)$, no logit involved): under a compressive slope (0.6, shift 0.5), the flip reappears (extreme: verb. 0.126 vs. freq. 0.084, $p\approx1.8\times10^{-11}$); under a mildly compressive slope (1.0, shift 0.35) it reappears but weakens ($p\approx4.9\times10^{-7}$); under an **expansive** slope (1.3, shift 0.15, i.e. *not* compressive), the flip **disappears** — verbalized confidence remains better even at extreme heterogeneity (0.063 vs. 0.085). This is a more precise finding than "robust across transforms": the sign-flip mechanism is specifically that a **compressive** (difficulty-insensitive) verbalized-confidence signal fails to track the bimodal shape of $p_i$ under extreme heterogeneity, while frequency confidence, being a direct function of the samples themselves, adapts automatically. Compression is exactly the qualitative property the calibration literature attributes to real verbalized confidence, so the flip's real-world relevance depends on how compressive actual verbalized-confidence elicitation is — plausible but not something we verify against real model outputs here.

**Semantic entropy AUROC is comparatively stable across heterogeneity**, ranging from 0.816 (low) to 0.871 (extreme) — a much smaller relative swing than the other two effects. This exceeds the ~0.60 standalone AUROC reported on BEACON (arXiv:2606.07528), which we attribute to our clustering being noise-free (exact cluster assignment) versus real NLI-based clustering; our number is an upper bound under idealized clustering.

**Power analysis.** Because effects are large (13–44pp; ECE differences 0.02–0.22), the self-consistency-vs-single-sample gain was significant ($p<0.05$) in 5/5 trials at just 3 replicates of $N=500$ — the design is not power-limited.

## 4. Limitations

This is a proxy for LLM behavior: correctness is generated from an assumed $p_i$ rather than real generations, clustering is exact rather than NLI-based, and the verbalized-confidence transforms (both families) are hand-specified rather than fit to paired real accuracy/verbalized-confidence data — no such dataset at the needed granularity is available to us. The self-consistency anchoring against Wang et al. (2022) uses three aggregate published numbers against a synthetic sweep; we could not obtain per-question pass-rate data for GSM8K/SVAMP/AQuA to test directly whether real benchmarks are closer to our extreme or low-heterogeneity regime, so this remains a plausibility argument, not a calibrated estimate. The compression-driven mechanism for the ECE sign-flip is now tested across two functional families and multiple parameterizations, but we have not measured the compression of *real* verbalized-confidence elicitation, so translating "the flip requires compression" into "the flip occurs in deployed LLMs" is still an extrapolation. $W=4$ uniform wrong-answer clusters is also a simplification.

## 5. Conclusion

Two properties often treated as fixed facts about probabilistic LLM uncertainty methods — the self-consistency gain and which confidence signal is better calibrated — depend strongly, and in the calibration case qualitatively (sign-flip), on task-difficulty heterogeneity. We now show the self-consistency shrinkage (43.9pp to 13.0pp) is not just observed but exactly reproducible from a closed-form multinomial computation over the $p_i$ marginal, confirming it as a mechanical property of the simulation. The calibration flip survives both re-parameterization within one functional family and, more informatively, a switch to a qualitatively different family — provided that family is compressive. This refines the original claim: it is not "verbalized vs. frequency confidence" in the abstract that flips, but specifically *compressive, difficulty-insensitive* confidence signals that lose to sample-agreement under bimodal task pools. Practitioners choosing self-consistency budgets or a confidence signal for routing/abstention should characterize both their task pool's difficulty heterogeneity and how compressive their confidence elicitation is.
