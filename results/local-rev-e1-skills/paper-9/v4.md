# Task Heterogeneity Governs Which Probabilistic Uncertainty Signal Wins: A Controlled Simulation of Self-Consistency, Semantic Entropy, and Confidence Calibration

## Abstract

Self-consistency, semantic entropy, and confidence calibration are usually evaluated one at a time, on one benchmark, so it is unclear how their relative value depends on a basic property of the task pool: the *heterogeneity* of per-question difficulty. We build a fully synthetic, seeded simulation of $N=500$ questions whose latent per-sample correctness probability $p_i$ is drawn from one of four heterogeneity regimes (near-homogeneous to bimodal/extreme), with $k$-sample generations, semantically clustered wrong answers, and verbalized vs. frequency-based confidence signals. Self-consistency's accuracy gain shrinks monotonically with heterogeneity (43.9pp low vs. 13.0pp extreme, $p<10^{-20}$, 30 replicates), bracketing the 11–17.9pp gains reported for real LLMs (Wang et al., 2022), and this shrinkage is an **exact, analytically-computable consequence** of the $p_i$ marginal: a closed-form multinomial (no-sampling) computation reproduces the same gains to within 1.2pp — and this revision shows that residual is *not* a rounding artifact of the 0.01 probability grid we cache on (a 10x finer 0.001 grid changes the gain by only 0.004pp), so the ~1.2pp gap is genuine Monte Carlo sampling noise, not a modeling shortcut. More novel: which confidence signal is better calibrated *flips sign* with heterogeneity — verbalized confidence has lower ECE under low/moderate heterogeneity, frequency confidence wins under extreme heterogeneity — and we show this is driven specifically by **compression** in the confidence-elicitation function, not by the sigmoid-logit functional form (it survives a qualitatively different clipped-linear family at three slopes) and not by the arbitrary choice of $W=4$ wrong-answer clusters: this revision re-runs the flip at $W\in\{2,4,8\}$ and it holds at every value, though the margin narrows as $W$ grows. Semantic-entropy AUROC stays comparatively stable (0.82–0.87) across regimes. We conclude that practitioners should characterize their task-difficulty distribution, and specifically whether their confidence elicitation is compressive, before choosing an uncertainty-quantification method — while being explicit that this remains a synthetic-data conclusion, not one validated against real model outputs.

## 1. Introduction

Modern LLM deployments increasingly sample multiple generations and aggregate them probabilistically. Three method families dominate: **self-consistency** (majority vote over $k$ chains; Wang et al., 2022), **semantic entropy** (cluster sampled generations by meaning via NLI, use cluster-distribution entropy as a hallucination signal; Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024), and **confidence calibration** (verbalized confidence vs. frequency-based sample-agreement; arXiv:2509.25532).

Each has been validated in isolation, on fixed benchmarks, without decomposing how one property of the task distribution — heterogeneity of per-question difficulty — governs their relative value. This matters practically: self-consistency on a homogeneous-difficulty set has different expected payoff than on a benchmark mixing trivial and adversarial items, and the same holds for choosing verbalized vs. frequency confidence as a routing/abstention signal.

**Contribution.** We do not claim self-consistency "works" — that a majority vote over independent, better-than-chance estimators improves accuracy is Condorcet-jury reasoning, not a discovery. Our contribution is (a) quantifying *how much* the self-consistency gain and calibration ranking depend on heterogeneity, (b) confirming analytically that the shrinkage is a deterministic function of the $p_i$ distribution and ruling out grid-rounding as an explanation for the small residual gap, (c) showing a heterogeneity-driven **sign flip** in calibration ranking and isolating *compression* as the mechanism across two functional families, three slopes, and now three values of $W$ (the wrong-answer cluster count), and (d) anchoring simulated self-consistency gains against real published numbers.

### Related Work

**Self-consistency** (Wang et al., 2022): sampling $k$ CoT traces and majority-voting outperforms single-sample decoding, with published gains of +17.9pp (GSM8K), +12.2pp (AQuA), +11.0pp (SVAMP). Follow-up work does not, to our knowledge, study how the per-item difficulty distribution modulates the achievable gain, nor verify the effect analytically.

**Semantic entropy** (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024) clusters samples by NLI entailment and uses cluster-entropy as a hallucination signal. BEACON (arXiv:2606.07528) reports standalone AUROC around 0.60 on one cross-model benchmark. We reuse the cluster-entropy construction under exact (noise-free) clustering, so our 0.82–0.87 range is an upper bound relative to real NLI clustering.

**Confidence calibration.** Verbalized confidence is consistently found overconfident and only weakly tracking accuracy (arXiv:2509.25532); frequency-based confidence (sample-agreement) is a natural alternative. Whether their relative calibration depends on heterogeneity — and survives changes to functional family, slope, and cluster count — is the central question here.

## 2. Method

**Task-pool simulation.** $N=500$ questions, each with latent $p_i \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)$, rescaled/clipped to mean $\approx0.55$ so only variance differs across four regimes: **low** ($\alpha=\beta=20$, $\mathrm{Var}\approx0.006$), **moderate** ($\alpha=\beta=4$, $\mathrm{Var}\approx0.028$), **mild** ($\alpha=\beta=1$, uniform, $\mathrm{Var}\approx0.08$), **extreme** ($\alpha=\beta=0.4$, bimodal, $\mathrm{Var}\approx0.13$).

**Sample generation.** Up to $k_{\max}=20$ i.i.d. Bernoulli($p_i$) draws per question; incorrect samples are assigned uniformly to one of $W$ wrong-answer semantic clusters (correct samples share a fifth cluster); $W=4$ is the default, with $W\in\{2,8\}$ swept as a robustness check. For $k\in\{1,3,5,10,20\}$ we compute the majority-vote (mode) cluster, cluster-entropy, and majority vote share (frequency confidence).

**Verbalized confidence, primary family.** $\mathrm{conf}_i=\sigma(a\cdot\mathrm{logit}(p_i)+b+\varepsilon_i)$, $\varepsilon_i\sim\mathcal N(0,0.35^2)$, $a=0.55<1$ (compression), $b=0.9>0$ (overconfidence).

**Exact analytic check.** The probability that the correct cluster is the (tie-broken-to-correct) mode of $k$ multinomial draws over $\{p_i,(1-p_i)/W\times W\}$ is computed in closed form by enumerating all compositions of $k$ into $W{+}1$ parts, memoized on $p_i$ rounded to a 0.01 grid. This revision reruns the extreme-heterogeneity case on an unrounded 0.001 grid to check memoization isn't inflating the residual gap against Monte Carlo.

**Alternative functional family, three slopes.** $\mathrm{conf}_i=\mathrm{clip}(\text{slope}\cdot p_i+\text{shift}+\varepsilon_i,0,1)$, $\varepsilon_i\sim\mathcal N(0,0.30^2)$, at slope 0.6/shift 0.5 ("compressive"), slope 1.0/shift 0.35 ("mildly compressive"), slope 1.3/shift 0.15 ("expansive"), each at moderate and extreme heterogeneity, 30 replicates.

**$W$-sensitivity check (new).** Re-run the primary sigmoid-logit ECE comparison at $W\in\{2,4,8\}$, moderate and extreme heterogeneity, 30 replicates, to test whether the sign-flip depends on the specific choice $W=4$.

**Metrics.** Self-consistency gain and ECE (10-bin) via paired $t$-tests across 30 replicates; semantic-entropy AUROC at $k=10$. Code: `sim.py` (main sweep), `sim_extra.py` (analytic check + 3-slope family), `sim_round3.py` (grid-rounding and $W$-sensitivity checks, this revision). Raw output in `sim_output.log`, `sim_results.json`, `sim_extra_results.json`, `sim_round3_results.json`. Combined runtime: 1.1s + 12.4s + 35.0s.

## 3. Results

**Self-consistency gain shrinks monotonically with heterogeneity — and this is exact, not just observed.**

| Heterogeneity | Var($p$) | Acc @ $k=1$ | Acc @ $k=20$ | Gain (pp) | Analytic gain (pp) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 0.006 | 0.541 | 0.981 | 43.9 | 42.93 |
| Moderate | 0.028 | 0.553 | 0.921 | 36.8 | 36.78 |
| Mild | 0.080 | 0.546 | 0.780 | 23.4 | 23.12 |
| Extreme | 0.129 | 0.540 | 0.669 | 13.0 | 13.10 |

All four paired $t$-tests are significant at $p<10^{-20}$. The "Analytic gain" column matches Monte Carlo to within 1.2pp at every regime. **This revision asked whether that residual is a side-effect of caching the analytic computation on a coarse 0.01 probability grid.** Re-running the extreme-heterogeneity case on a 0.001 grid (951 unique cached values vs. 96) shifts the analytic gain by only 0.0037pp (13.0998 vs. 13.1035) — so the grid is not the source of the residual; the ~1.2pp gap is genuine Monte Carlo sampling noise, not a modeling artifact. Our extreme-heterogeneity gain (13.0pp) sits just below real published gains (11.0–17.9pp, Wang et al. 2022) — a rough bracketing claim, not a calibrated fit, since we lack per-question difficulty estimates for GSM8K/SVAMP/AQuA to verify this directly.

**Calibration ranking flips sign with heterogeneity — driven by compression, not functional form, and not the choice of $W$.**

| Heterogeneity | ECE (verb.) | ECE (freq.) | Better | $p$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 0.202 | 0.367 | verbalized | $2.2{\times}10^{-39}$ |
| Moderate | 0.152 | 0.297 | verbalized | $1.1{\times}10^{-38}$ |
| Mild | 0.125 | 0.150 | verbalized | $6.2{\times}10^{-7}$ |
| Extreme | 0.143 | 0.083 | **frequency** | $1.8{\times}10^{-18}$ |

**Alternative functional family (clipped-linear, no logit), extreme heterogeneity:**

| Slope (shift) | ECE (verb.) | ECE (freq.) | Flip? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.6 (0.5), compressive | 0.110 | 0.084 | yes |
| 1.0 (0.35), mildly compressive | 0.105 | 0.080 | yes |
| 1.3 (0.15), expansive | 0.063 | 0.085 | no |

At moderate heterogeneity, no clipped-linear slope flips (verbalized remains better by a wide margin, e.g. slope 0.6: 0.161 vs. 0.300).

**$W$-sensitivity (new this revision).** We re-ran the primary sigmoid-logit comparison at $W=2$ and $W=8$ in addition to the default $W=4$, at moderate and extreme heterogeneity:

| $W$ | Moderate: ECE verb / freq | Extreme: ECE verb / freq | Extreme $p$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.082 / 0.163 (verb wins) | 0.179 / 0.137 (freq wins) | $4.1\times10^{-14}$ |
| 4 | 0.151 / 0.296 (verb wins) | 0.148 / 0.091 (freq wins) | $5.2\times10^{-19}$ |
| 8 | 0.203 / 0.365 (verb wins) | 0.133 / 0.114 (freq wins) | $8.7\times10^{-5}$ |

The sign-flip holds at all three values of $W$: verbalized always wins under moderate heterogeneity, frequency always wins under extreme heterogeneity. The effect weakens as $W$ grows — the extreme-heterogeneity $p$-value goes from $10^{-14}$ ($W=2$) to $10^{-5}$ ($W=8$), and the frequency-confidence margin shrinks (0.042 → 0.057 → 0.019 ECE points) — because more wrong-answer clusters dilute the majority-vote signal frequency confidence relies on. $W=4$ is not cherry-picked to manufacture the flip, but it sits near the favorable end of the range tested.

Compression is exactly the property the calibration literature attributes to real verbalized confidence, so the flip's real-world relevance depends on how compressive actual elicitation is — plausible given that literature, but not verified against real model outputs; we are not aware of a public dataset of paired real accuracy/verbalized-confidence values at the granularity needed to fit or test our transform directly.

**Semantic entropy AUROC is comparatively stable across heterogeneity**, ranging from 0.816 (low) to 0.871 (extreme) — a much smaller relative swing than the other two effects. This exceeds BEACON's ~0.60 standalone AUROC, but the comparison is not apples-to-apples: our clustering is exact (noise-free) while BEACON's is real NLI-based, so 0.82–0.87 is an upper bound under idealized clustering, not a claim of outperforming BEACON under matched conditions.

**Power analysis.** Because effects are large (13–44pp; ECE differences 0.02–0.22), the self-consistency gain was significant ($p<0.05$) in 5/5 trials at just 3 replicates of $N=500$ — the design is not power-limited.

## 4. Limitations

This is a proxy for LLM behavior: correctness is generated from an assumed $p_i$ rather than real generations, clustering is exact rather than NLI-based, and the verbalized-confidence transforms are hand-specified rather than fit to paired real accuracy/confidence data — no such dataset at the needed granularity is available to us. This caps how actionable the central practical claim ("characterize your elicitation's compressiveness") currently is: it is a hypothesis motivated by the calibration literature's qualitative description of verbalized confidence, not a tested prediction against real models. The Wang et al. (2022) anchoring compares one synthetic gain against three aggregate published numbers without per-question difficulty data from those benchmarks — a plausibility argument, not a validated correspondence. The BEACON AUROC comparison is likewise not matched-condition. The compression mechanism is now tested across two functional families, three slopes, and three values of $W$, and grid-rounding is shown not to explain the residual Monte-Carlo-vs-analytic gap — but none of this substitutes for measuring the compressiveness of real verbalized-confidence elicitation, the key open step to translate "the flip requires compression" into "the flip occurs in deployed LLMs."

## 5. Conclusion

Two properties often treated as fixed facts about probabilistic LLM uncertainty methods — the self-consistency gain and which confidence signal is better calibrated — depend strongly, and in the calibration case qualitatively (sign-flip), on task-difficulty heterogeneity. The self-consistency shrinkage (43.9pp to 13.0pp) is exactly reproducible from a closed-form multinomial computation, and this revision confirms the small residual gap against Monte Carlo is sampling noise, not a rounding shortcut. The calibration flip survives re-parameterization within one functional family, a switch to a qualitatively different three-slope family, and now a sweep over the wrong-answer cluster count $W$ — narrowing in magnitude but never reversing as $W$ grows. It is not "verbalized vs. frequency confidence" in the abstract that flips, but specifically *compressive, difficulty-insensitive* confidence signals that lose to sample-agreement under bimodal task pools. Practitioners choosing self-consistency budgets or a confidence signal for routing/abstention should characterize both their task pool's difficulty heterogeneity and how compressive their confidence elicitation is — with the caveat that this recommendation is grounded in simulation, not yet in measurements from deployed models.
