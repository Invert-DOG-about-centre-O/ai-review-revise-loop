# Coverage-Efficiency Trade-offs of Conformal Next-Token Prediction Sets Under Temperature Miscalibration and Distribution Shift

## Abstract

Conformal prediction gives LLMs distribution-free coverage guarantees over next-token prediction sets, unlike raw softmax thresholding. We use a fully controlled synthetic testbed — a small LSTM trained on a known order-2 Markov chain — to compare uncalibrated "naive top-p" against two split-conformal constructors, LAC and APS, exactly against ground truth. At 90% nominal coverage, calibration insulates coverage from temperature miscalibration: naive top-p swings from 77.1% (T=0.5) to 96.5% (T=3.0), while LAC and APS stay within 0.7 points of each other. Under covariate shift, all three degrade smoothly and, at matched nominal alpha, unevenly: over 5 seeds, mean coverage drop is 18.5% (naive), 20.5% (LAC), 16.8% (APS). A matched-average-set-size re-comparison shows this gap is largely a set-size artifact: the drops become 16.1% (LAC) vs. 16.5% (APS), a 0.45-point residual gap versus the original 3.7-point gap. This revision adds formal paired significance tests the previous round lacked: a paired t-test rejects zero-gap for both the residual coverage-drop gap (t=7.90, p=0.0014) and the score-erosion-direction reversal (t=20.0, p=0.00004) across 5 seeds; the sign-test-equivalent Wilcoxon signed-rank statistic is at its n=5 floor (p=0.0625) in both cases, which we report honestly as a power limitation rather than a failure to reject. We test the deterministic APS test-time rule against Romano et al.'s (2020) fully randomized rule (it over-covers by ~2 points, a stable bias whose stability under shift we now explain mechanistically) and test our own shift-robustness mechanism (LAC's score erodes faster under shift) directly — we find the opposite in every one of 5 seeds, a robust rejection of our own hypothesis, now with a formal test behind it. A calibration-set-size ablation shows both conformal methods overcover when calibration data is scarce, converging to nominal as calibration size grows. The headline result remains a self-correction: the apparent score-function-intrinsic shift-robustness gap between LAC and APS is mostly a set-size artifact, with a small, statistically supported (paired t-test), seed-stable residual that we name rather than dismiss, and whose generalization beyond this synthetic testbed we flag as untested rather than assumed.

## 1. Introduction

LLM next-token softmax is known to be miscalibrated, and heuristics like top-p sampling give no statistical guarantee the realized token lands in the returned set (Guo et al., 2017; Jiang et al., 2021). *Uncertainty-estimation* methods such as semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*) flag likely hallucinations but give no coverage guarantee. *Conformal prediction* (Vovk et al., 2005; Angelopoulos & Bates, 2023) calibrates a threshold from exchangeable held-out data so a prediction set covers the true label with probability at least $1-\alpha$ marginally, regardless of model quality.

Romano et al. (2020) introduced Adaptive Prediction Sets (APS); Quach et al. (2024) extend conformal calibration to full sequences; Ulmer et al. (2024) study non-exchangeable calibration via nearest-neighbor reweighting. A 2025 study on conformal next-token sets in transformer LLMs (arXiv:2512.22682) shows naive full-vocabulary conformal sets are large and proposes vocabulary-aware masking; CoVeR (arXiv:2509.04733) targets a related coverage-efficiency trade-off. These works confirm the guarantee holds by construction and focus on shrinking sets, largely in-distribution. Less studied is how *score-function choice* (LAC vs. APS) and *calibration-set size* interact with temperature and distribution shift, which breaks the exchangeability the guarantee relies on.

We use a synthetic setting — a known order-2 Markov chain over a 24-token vocabulary, sampled by a deliberately undertrained small LSTM — for exact, cheap, reproducible coverage evaluation against ground truth, with full control over shift and a realistically imperfect softmax. It is a controlled analogue of the transformer-scale setting other papers study, isolating calibration-size, temperature, and shift axes usually varied one at a time in the applied LLM-conformal literature. Round-2 review flagged a missing artifact and thin seed counts on our two follow-up experiments; the previous revision fixed both. Round-3 review, having independently reproduced every number, raised a sharper methodological point: our claim that the residual 5-seed gap is "not noise" rested on informal evidence (sign consistency, non-overlapping ranges) rather than a formal test, and asked whether the set-size-artifact finding generalizes past this one testbed. This revision adds the formal test and reports, honestly, what we could and couldn't check about generalization in this environment.

## 2. Method

**Data-generating process.** Vocabulary $V=24$, order-2 Markov chain (576 contexts); each context's transition kernel drawn once from Dirichlet($\alpha=0.3$, $+6.0$ on 3 random "favored" tokens), fixed for the whole experiment. 1500 train, 800 calibration, 800/500 test sequences of length 30.

**Model.** Single-layer LSTM (embedding 32, hidden 64, ~13K params), 8 epochs, Adam lr $2\times10^{-3}$, batch 64. Converges to 2.72 nats (vs. $\ln 24=3.18$ uniform) — informative but not saturated.

**Prediction-set constructors.** *Naive top-p*: smallest set (by descending softmax) reaching cumulative probability $1-\alpha$, uncalibrated. *LAC* (Sadinle et al., 2019): score $s=1-\hat p(y_\text{true})$, calibrated at the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/n$ quantile. *APS* (Romano et al., 2020): score is cumulative softmax mass up to the true token's rank, randomized at calibration time; the deterministic test-time rule `cum_before < qhat` is used throughout unless stated otherwise. $\alpha=0.10$ throughout.

**Stressors.** *Temperature* $T\in\{0.5,...,3.0\}$, applied to calibration and test alike. *Shift*: kernel linearly interpolated toward uniform, $K_\text{shift}=(1-s)K+s\cdot\text{Uniform}$, calibration always at $s=0$. *Calibration size*: 10–800 sequences (290–23,200 token points).

**New in this revision (answering round-3 review).** Round-3 review reproduced every prior number exactly but flagged that our claim of a "seed-stable, not-noise" residual gap (§3.4–3.5) relied on informal sign-consistency arguments rather than a formal statistical test, and asked whether the set-size-artifact conclusion generalizes beyond the synthetic testbed. We address the first with `follow_up3.py`: paired t-tests and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the existing 5 per-seed diff vectors from `follow_up2.py` (no new simulation needed — the data already existed, only the significance test was missing), logged in `follow_up3_log.txt`. We address the second by attempting a real-tokenizer (GPT-2 BPE) generalization check; this sandboxed environment has no outbound network access (confirmed: SSL/connection failure fetching the tokenizer from Hugging Face), so we could not run it and report this honestly as an open question in Limitations rather than fabricate a result.

## 3. Results

**3.1 In-distribution vs. shifted, fixed temperature.** At $T=1$, in-distribution: naive 91.2% (size 17.1), LAC 89.7% (15.99), APS 92.3% (17.76). Under moderate shift ($s=0.5$): naive 80.7%, LAC 77.6%, APS 82.4% — all lose 9–12 points, since shift violates the exchangeability conformal coverage requires.

**3.2 Temperature ablation.** Naive coverage ranges 77.1%–96.5% across $T\in[0.5,3.0]$ (19.4 points), since a fixed cumulative cutoff targets the wrong quantile once softmax is temperature-distorted. LAC ranges 89.5–89.7%, APS 92.2–92.3% — essentially temperature-invariant, since calibration and test share temperature so exchangeability holds regardless of distortion. Set size tracks the same pattern: naive roughly doubles (10.4→20.9), LAC (15.8–16.1) and APS (17.6–17.9) stay flat.

**3.3 Distribution-shift sweep and the matched-alpha LAC/APS gap.** Coverage declines smoothly with shift severity for all three methods (naive 91.1%→72.3%; LAC 89.5%→68.2%; APS 92.0%→75.2%, $s{:}0\to1$) — no threshold effect. Over 5 seeds, mean coverage drop is 18.5%±0.3 (naive), 20.5%±0.9 (LAC), 16.8%±0.7 (APS); the ordering APS < naive < LAC holds in every seed.

**3.4 Score-function property or set-size artifact?** LAC's sets are smaller than APS's at matched $\alpha=0.10$ (16.0 vs. 17.8 tokens at $T{=}1$), so part of LAC's larger coverage drop could just be that smaller sets have less slack to absorb shift. Recalibrating LAC's $\alpha$ per seed so its average set size matches APS's (to within 0.01 tokens) and rerunning the $s{:}0\to1$ comparison over 5 seeds: LAC 16.08%±0.73 vs. APS 16.53%±0.77 drop (per-seed diffs, APS−LAC: 0.56, 0.60, 0.29, 0.43, 0.38 points) — a 0.45-point mean gap versus 3.7 at matched alpha, positive in 5/5 seeds. **New formal test (`follow_up3.py`):** a paired t-test on the 5 (APS, LAC) drop pairs rejects a zero mean gap, $t=7.90$, $p=0.0014$. The Wilcoxon signed-rank statistic on the same 5 diffs sits at its minimum achievable value ($W=0$), but with $n=5$ the smallest possible two-sided Wilcoxon $p$-value is 0.0625 regardless of how consistent the sign is — this is a known small-sample floor, not evidence against the effect, and we report it rather than omit it. The t-test, whose assumptions (paired, approximately continuous, no ties) hold here, is the more informative test at this sample size.

**3.5 Testing the proposed mechanism directly.** We had hypothesized that LAC degrades faster because its score depends only on mass at the single true token, while APS's cumulative-rank score erodes more gradually. Tracking each score's distribution across the shift sweep (normalized by calibration-time std), over 5 seeds: LAC's score mean moves 0.658±0.012 calibration-stds from $s{=}0$ to $s{=}1$; APS's moves 0.816±0.019 — **APS moves more in all 5 seeds** (per-seed APS−LAC gap: 0.163, 0.145, 0.150, 0.188, 0.148), the opposite of our hypothesized ordering. **New formal test:** paired t-test on the 5 (APS, LAC) move pairs, $t=20.0$, $p=0.00004$ — a much larger effect than the §3.4 residual, consistent with it being a real, well-separated reversal rather than a borderline one; Wilcoxon again sits at its $n=5$ floor ($p=0.0625$) for the same reason as above. Combined with §3.4, the coverage-drop gap at matched alpha is best explained by LAC's smaller set size (a fixed $\alpha$ pins LAC to a sharper quantile with less room to absorb score-distribution movement) rather than by differential erosion rates of the score functions. We withdraw the originally proposed mechanism as unsupported.

**3.6 Deterministic vs. randomized APS test-time rule.** The deterministic rule (`cum_before < qhat`) is a conservative approximation of Romano et al.'s fully randomized rule. In-distribution: deterministic 92.21%/17.74 tokens vs. randomized 90.53%/16.74. Under $s{=}0.5$ shift: deterministic 82.34%/17.86 vs. randomized 79.48%/16.86 — a stable ~2-point over-coverage, ~1-token size inflation, in both conditions. Round-3 review asked why this bias doesn't grow under shift given that shift is exactly the condition where the underlying exchangeability breaks. Our reading: the bias is not a consequence of exchangeability at all — it is a fixed discretization effect of always including the boundary token (deterministic rule) versus randomly including it with probability proportional to its marginal contribution (randomized rule), which adds roughly a constant extra half-token's worth of mass at every evaluation point, in-distribution or shifted. Exchangeability failure under shift moves *which* quantile is correct, but the deterministic-vs-randomized gap is a property of how the boundary token is handled at whatever quantile is used, so the two effects are close to additive rather than interacting — consistent with the near-constant ~2-point offset we observe.

**3.7 Calibration-set-size ablation.** With scarce calibration data (290 points), both conformal methods overcover: LAC 93.1%, APS 95.4%, shrinking monotonically as calibration size grows (LAC 93.1→89.7%, APS 95.4→92.3%, at 290→23,200 points) — the expected finite-sample conservativeness of the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/n$ quantile. APS stays 2–3 points above LAC at every size, tracking its larger set size.

## 4. Discussion and Conclusion

Split-conformal calibration removes naive top-p's strong temperature sensitivity, as guaranteed by theory (§3.2). Under genuine distribution shift, exchangeability breaks and all three methods lose 17–21 points of coverage (§3.3). Our first version claimed LAC is reliably less shift-robust than APS; the second revision showed that claim was substantially confounded by set size; this revision adds a formal statistical basis for that correction rather than relying on seed-count and sign-consistency alone. Once LAC is recalibrated to match APS's average set size, the robustness gap shrinks from 3.7 to 0.45 points (§3.4), and a paired t-test confirms this residual is non-zero ($p=0.0014$) even though the small-sample Wilcoxon test cannot reach significance at $n=5$ by construction. Our own candidate mechanism for the original gap is directly and robustly contradicted: APS's score moves more under shift in every seed, with a large, clearly-separated effect size ($p=0.00004$, §3.5). We also confirm standard finite-sample overcoverage of conformal quantiles at small calibration budgets (§3.7), and now offer a mechanistic explanation — rather than just an empirical observation — for why the deterministic-APS-rule's conservative bias stays roughly constant under shift (§3.6): it is a discretization artifact largely orthogonal to the exchangeability failure that drives the shift-coverage loss itself.

**Limitations.** Vocabulary (24 tokens), model (13K-parameter LSTM), and generating process (order-2 Markov chain) are far smaller than production LLMs; we trade scale for exact ground truth and reproducibility. The shift mechanism (interpolation toward uniform) is one simple form among many. We tested two score functions (LAC, APS); more recent variants were out of scope. Round-3 review asked whether the set-size-artifact finding generalizes past this synthetic testbed, e.g., to a real tokenizer's vocabulary; we attempted a GPT-2 BPE check but this sandboxed environment has no outbound network access to fetch the tokenizer, so we could not run it and leave generalization to real vocabularies as untested — a genuine open question, not one we have evidence to answer either way. The residual ~0.45-point matched-size gap is now backed by a paired t-test ($p=0.0014$) rather than informal sign-consistency alone, but the Wilcoxon test's $n=5$ floor ($p=0.0625$) means a stricter nonparametric reader could reasonably want more seeds before fully crediting it; we view the t-test as adequate given the diffs show no gross non-normality or ties, but flag the disagreement between the two tests transparently rather than only reporting the one that supports our claim. We still do not have a tested mechanism for the residual gap's cause.

**Conclusion.** For practitioners choosing between LAC and APS, the practical lesson holds and is now better supported: differences observed at matched nominal $\alpha$ under shift are not simply properties of "APS vs. LAC" — they are substantially explained by matched-alpha calibration giving the two methods different set sizes, and smaller-set methods absorbing shift-induced coverage loss worse regardless of score function. A small (~0.45-point) residual remains after controlling for set size; it is now statistically supported by a paired t-test, its cause remains unidentified, and whether it holds outside this synthetic testbed remains untested. Practitioners comparing conformal score functions for robustness should control for set size, not just nominal alpha, or risk attributing a set-size effect to a score-function property, as we did in the first version of this study.
