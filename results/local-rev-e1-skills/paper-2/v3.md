# Coverage-Efficiency Trade-offs of Conformal Next-Token Prediction Sets Under Temperature Miscalibration and Distribution Shift

## Abstract

Conformal prediction gives LLMs distribution-free coverage guarantees over next-token prediction sets, unlike raw softmax thresholding. We use a fully controlled synthetic testbed — a small LSTM trained on a known order-2 Markov chain — to compare uncalibrated "naive top-p" against two split-conformal constructors, LAC and APS, exactly against ground truth. At 90% nominal coverage, calibration insulates coverage from temperature miscalibration: naive top-p swings from 77.1% (T=0.5) to 96.5% (T=3.0), while LAC and APS stay within 0.7 points of each other. Under covariate shift, all three degrade smoothly (no threshold effect) and, at matched nominal alpha, unevenly: over 5 seeds, mean coverage drop is 18.5% (naive), 20.5% (LAC), 16.8% (APS), an ordering holding in every seed. A matched-average-set-size re-comparison — recalibrating LAC's alpha so its set size equals APS's — shows this gap is largely a set-size artifact: over 5 seeds (extended from 3 in the previous revision) the drops become 16.1% (LAC) vs. 16.5% (APS), a 0.45-point gap versus the original 3.7-point gap, and this residual is now itself consistent (APS drops more in 5/5 seeds, std 0.12 pts) rather than plausibly noise. We test the deterministic APS test-time rule against Romano et al.'s (2020) fully randomized rule (it over-covers by ~2 points, a stable conservative bias) and test our own shift-robustness mechanism (LAC's score erodes faster under shift) directly — we find the opposite in every one of 5 seeds (extended from 1), a robust rejection of our own hypothesis. A calibration-set-size ablation shows both conformal methods overcover when calibration data is scarce, converging to nominal as calibration size grows. The headline result remains a self-correction, now on firmer footing: the apparent score-function-intrinsic shift-robustness gap between LAC and APS is mostly a set-size artifact, with a small (~0.45-point), seed-stable residual that we name rather than dismiss.

## 1. Introduction

LLM next-token softmax is known to be miscalibrated, and heuristics like top-p sampling give no statistical guarantee the realized token lands in the returned set (Guo et al., 2017; Jiang et al., 2021). *Uncertainty-estimation* methods such as semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*; Kuhn et al., 2024) flag likely hallucinations but give no coverage guarantee. *Conformal prediction* (Vovk et al., 2005; Angelopoulos & Bates, 2023) calibrates a threshold from exchangeable held-out data so a prediction set covers the true label with probability at least $1-\alpha$ marginally, regardless of model quality.

Romano et al. (2020) introduced Adaptive Prediction Sets (APS); Quach et al. (2024) extend conformal calibration to full sequences; Ulmer et al. (2024) study non-exchangeable calibration via nearest-neighbor reweighting. A 2025 study on conformal next-token sets in transformer LLMs (arXiv:2512.22682) shows naive full-vocabulary conformal sets are large and proposes vocabulary-aware masking; CoVeR (arXiv:2509.04733) targets a related coverage-efficiency trade-off for decoding trajectories. These works confirm the guarantee holds by construction and focus on shrinking sets, largely in-distribution. Less studied is how *score-function choice* (LAC vs. APS) and *calibration-set size* interact with temperature and distribution shift, which breaks the exchangeability the guarantee relies on.

We use a synthetic setting — a known order-2 Markov chain over a 24-token vocabulary, sampled by a deliberately undertrained small LSTM — for exact, cheap, reproducible coverage evaluation against ground truth, with full control over shift and a realistically imperfect (not saturated) softmax. It is a controlled analogue of the transformer-scale setting other papers study, isolating calibration-size, temperature, and shift axes usually varied one at a time (or not at all) in the applied LLM-conformal literature. In the previous revision we probed a mechanism we had speculated about but not tested, and found our own hypothesis did not survive testing. This revision strengthens that self-correction with more seeds and closes a reproducibility gap flagged in round-2 review.

## 2. Method

**Data-generating process.** Vocabulary $V=24$, order-2 Markov chain (576 contexts); each context's transition kernel is drawn once from Dirichlet($\alpha=0.3$, $+6.0$ on 3 random "favored" tokens), fixed for the whole experiment. We sample 1500 train, 800 calibration, 800/500 test sequences of length 30.

**Model.** Single-layer LSTM (embedding 32, hidden 64, ~13K params), 8 epochs, Adam lr $2\times10^{-3}$, batch 64. Converges to 2.72 nats (vs. $\ln 24=3.18$ uniform) — informative but deliberately not saturated.

**Prediction-set constructors.** *Naive top-p*: smallest set (by descending softmax) reaching cumulative probability $1-\alpha$, uncalibrated. *LAC* (Sadinle et al., 2019): score $s=1-\hat p(y_\text{true})$, calibrated at the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/n$ quantile, include tokens with $\hat p \ge 1-\hat q$. *APS* (Romano et al., 2020): score is cumulative softmax mass up to the true token's rank, randomized at calibration time; the deterministic test-time rule `cum_before < qhat` is used throughout unless stated otherwise. $\alpha=0.10$ throughout.

**Stressors.** *Temperature* $T\in\{0.5,...,3.0\}$, applied consistently to calibration and test. *Shift*: kernel linearly interpolated toward uniform, $K_\text{shift}=(1-s)K+s\cdot\text{Uniform}$, calibration always at $s=0$. *Calibration size*: 10–800 sequences (290–23,200 token points).

**New in this revision (answering round-2 review).** Round-2 review flagged that (a) `follow_up_log.txt`, referenced in the previous revision's text, was missing from the submission, and (b) the two experiments driving our central self-correction — matched-set-size shift comparison and the score-distribution mechanism test — ran on only 3 and 1 seed respectively, leaving open whether their orderings were seed-stable, and whether the small residual matched-size gap (APS dropping more than LAC in all 3 original seeds) was signal or noise. We address both: `follow_up_log.txt` is now included (regenerated by re-running `follow_up.py` fresh, output verified to reproduce the previous revision's numbers exactly), and a new `follow_up2.py` extends both experiments to 5 seeds each, logged in `follow_up2_log.txt`. All code is deterministic and reuses the identical generator/model/calibration routines as `experiment.py`/`multiseed_check.py`/`follow_up.py`.

## 3. Results

**3.1 In-distribution vs. shifted, fixed temperature.** At $T=1$, in-distribution: naive 91.2% (size 17.1), LAC 89.7% (15.99), APS 92.3% (17.76). Under moderate shift ($s=0.5$): naive 80.7%, LAC 77.6%, APS 82.4% — all lose 9–12 points, since shift violates the exchangeability conformal coverage requires.

**3.2 Temperature ablation.** Naive coverage ranges 77.1%–96.5% across $T\in[0.5,3.0]$ (19.4 points), since a fixed cumulative cutoff targets the wrong quantile once softmax is temperature-distorted. LAC ranges 89.5–89.7%, APS 92.2–92.3% — essentially temperature-invariant, since calibration and test share temperature so exchangeability holds regardless of distortion. We report this as an implementation validation, not a novel finding. Set size tracks the same pattern: naive roughly doubles (10.4→20.9), LAC (15.8–16.1) and APS (17.6–17.9) stay flat.

**3.3 Distribution-shift sweep and the matched-alpha LAC/APS gap.** Coverage declines smoothly with shift severity for all three methods (naive 91.1%→72.3%; LAC 89.5%→68.2%; APS 92.0%→75.2%, $s{:}0\to1$) — no threshold effect. Over 5 seeds, mean coverage drop is 18.5%±0.3 (naive), 20.5%±0.9 (LAC), 16.8%±0.7 (APS); the ordering APS < naive < LAC holds in every seed, gaps (1.7–3.7 pts) exceeding cross-seed std (≤0.9 pts).

**3.4 Score-function property or set-size artifact? — now on 5 seeds.** LAC's sets are smaller than APS's at matched $\alpha=0.10$ (16.0 vs. 17.8 tokens at $T{=}1$), so part of LAC's larger coverage drop could just be that smaller sets have less slack to absorb shift, unrelated to the score function. We recalibrate LAC's $\alpha$ per seed (bisection) so its average in-distribution set size matches APS's set size at nominal $\alpha=0.10$ (matches to within 0.01 tokens in every seed) and rerun the $s{:}0\to1$ comparison. Extending from 3 to 5 seeds per round-2 review: coverage-drop gap nearly disappears — LAC 16.08%±0.73 vs. APS 16.53%±0.77 (per-seed drops: LAC/APS = 16.82/17.38, 15.57/16.17, 15.91/16.20, 17.00/17.43, 15.07/15.45) — a 0.45-point mean gap versus 3.7 at matched alpha. Unlike the original 3-seed run, the sign of this residual is now checkable for stability: APS drops more than LAC in **5/5 seeds** (per-seed APS−LAC: 0.56, 0.60, 0.29, 0.43, 0.37 points; mean 0.45, std 0.12). This still confirms the headline self-correction — the 3.7-point matched-alpha gap is mostly a set-size artifact — but the small residual is consistent enough across seeds that we now treat it as a real, if minor, effect rather than dismiss it as noise (see §3.5, §4).

**3.5 Testing the proposed mechanism directly — now on 5 seeds.** We hypothesized (previous revision) that LAC degrades faster because its score depends only on mass at the single true token, "exactly what shift corrupts first," while APS's cumulative-rank score erodes more gradually. We track each score's distribution across the shift sweep, normalized by its own calibration-time std. Extending from 1 to 5 seeds per round-2 review: from $s{=}0$ to $s{=}1$, LAC's score mean moves 0.658±0.012 calibration-stds while APS's moves 0.816±0.019 — **APS moves more in all 5 seeds** (per-seed LAC/APS: 0.676/0.839, 0.668/0.813, 0.649/0.799, 0.648/0.836, 0.646/0.794), the opposite of our hypothesized ordering, and now clearly not a fluke of the single seed we originally tested (the two distributions of per-seed moves do not overlap). Combined with §3.4, the coverage-drop gap at matched alpha is best explained by LAC's smaller set size (fixed $\alpha$ pins LAC to a higher, sharper quantile with less room to absorb any score-distribution movement) rather than by differential erosion rates of the score functions themselves. We withdraw the originally proposed mechanism as unsupported, now with cross-seed confirmation rather than a single data point.

**3.6 Deterministic vs. randomized APS test-time rule.** The deterministic rule used throughout (`cum_before < qhat`, no boundary randomization) is a conservative approximation of Romano et al.'s fully randomized rule. In-distribution: deterministic 92.21%/17.74 tokens vs. randomized 90.53%/16.74 — near-exact nominal coverage once randomization is added, at one token smaller. Under $s{=}0.5$ shift: deterministic 82.34%/17.86 vs. randomized 79.48%/16.86. The bias is a stable ~2-point over-coverage and ~1-token size inflation across both conditions; it does not change the qualitative shift results in §3.3–3.5 (which compare APS to itself and to LAC under the same rule throughout), but absolute APS numbers should be read as slightly conservative.

**3.7 Calibration-set-size ablation.** With scarce calibration data (290 points, 10 sequences), both conformal methods overcover: LAC 93.1%, APS 95.4%, shrinking monotonically as calibration size grows (LAC 93.1→89.7%, APS 95.4→92.3%, at 290→23,200 points) — the expected finite-sample conservativeness of the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/n$ quantile. APS stays 2–3 points above LAC at every size, tracking its larger set size (19.9→17.8 vs. 18.0→16.0).

## 4. Discussion and Conclusion

Split-conformal calibration removes naive top-p's strong temperature sensitivity, as guaranteed by theory (§3.2). Under genuine distribution shift, exchangeability breaks and all three methods lose 17–21 points of coverage (§3.3). Our first version claimed LAC is reliably less shift-robust than APS; the previous revision showed that claim was substantially confounded by set size, and this revision confirms it on more seeds: once LAC is recalibrated to match APS's average set size, the robustness gap shrinks from 3.7 to 0.45 points, with APS dropping marginally more in all 5 seeds tested (§3.4) — a small, seed-stable residual, not the large score-function-identity effect we originally reported. Our own candidate mechanism for the original gap (LAC's single-token score erodes faster) is directly and now robustly contradicted: APS's score moves more under shift in every seed we tested (§3.5). We also confirm standard finite-sample overcoverage of conformal quantiles at small calibration budgets (§3.7) and quantify the deterministic-APS-rule bias as a stable, small conservative offset (§3.6).

**Limitations.** Vocabulary (24 tokens), model (13K-parameter LSTM), and generating process (order-2 Markov chain) are far smaller than production LLMs; we trade scale for exact ground truth and reproducibility. The shift mechanism (interpolation toward uniform) is one simple form among many. We tested two score functions (LAC, APS); more recent variants were out of scope. The matched-set-size comparison (§3.4) and score-distribution test (§3.5) now use 5 seeds each (up from 3 and 1 in the previous revision), matching the main shift-robustness claim's seed count; the qualitative conclusions (gap shrinks sharply at matched size; APS's score moves more than LAC's) are consistent across all 5 seeds in both cases. The residual ~0.45-point matched-size gap is small relative to effect sizes elsewhere in the paper and we do not have a tested mechanism for it — we flag it as a real but minor and currently unexplained effect rather than claim to have identified its cause. As before, this paper dissects existing score functions rather than proposing a new one; its contribution is the controlled, ground-truth-exact comparison, including catching and correcting our own initial over-interpretation of an alpha-matched comparison, and now stress-testing that correction itself across more seeds per reviewer request.

**Conclusion.** For practitioners choosing between LAC and APS, the practical lesson holds and is now better supported: differences observed at matched nominal $\alpha$ under shift are not simply properties of "APS vs. LAC" — they are substantially explained by matched-alpha calibration giving the two methods different set sizes, and smaller-set methods absorbing shift-induced coverage loss worse regardless of which score function produced the small set. A small (~0.45-point), seed-stable residual remains after controlling for set size, whose source we have not identified. Practitioners comparing conformal score functions for robustness should control for set size, not just nominal alpha, or risk attributing a set-size effect to a score-function property, as we did in the first version of this study.
