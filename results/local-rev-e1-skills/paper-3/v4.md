# Curation Strength, Confidence Thresholds, and the Failure of Naive Diversity-Boosting: A Bounded-Confidence Study of Recommender-Mediated Polarization

## Abstract

Recommender systems are frequently blamed for polarizing public opinion by curating agreeable content, and "inject more diverse content" is a common proposed fix. We build a minimal, matched-exposure agent-based model that tests both claims mechanistically, and we treat it explicitly as a mechanism-isolation toy, not a calibrated platform simulator. N=150 agents hold continuous opinions and update them via a Hegselmann–Krause bounded-confidence rule; each round every agent is shown exactly K=5 peer opinions drawn from a softmax curation policy with strength λ ∈ [−1, 1] (λ=0: random exposure; λ=1: engagement-maximizing; λ<0: diversity-boosting), holding exposure count fixed across conditions to remove a known confound. Across a 6×5 grid of λ and confidence threshold ε (30 seeds/cell, 900 runs), engagement-optimized curation raises final opinion variance relative to random exposure by a modest, ε-dependent, and hyperparameter-sensitive amount — pooled +2.1% at our default settings (paired t p=1.8e-5, n=150). We treat this number as illustrative of a mechanism, not a robust point estimate: re-running the β×μ and N sensitivity grids at higher seed counts shows the apparent sign flips reported in earlier drafts were sampling noise, not a real reversal (0/9 β×μ cells and 0/3 N values show a significant engagement<random gap). What *is* robust: diversity-boosting (λ=−1) never reduces polarization, and across every one of the 18 sensitivity cells we test — 9 (β,μ) cells, 3 N values, 3 K values, and 3 network-topology ε values — it produces final variance strictly above *both* the random and engagement-curated conditions. The mechanism is bounded confidence discarding dissimilar peers: on the exact λ=−1 grid, 95.4–99.7% of curated exposures fall outside agents' tolerance window and are discarded, freezing agents near their initial, near-maximal-variance state. This revision adds two further checks. First, we asked whether the engagement-vs-random sign flip seen under graph-restricted exposure (Erdős–Rényi neighbors only) was itself sampling noise, as the earlier flips were; re-running at 80 seeds/cell (vs. 20) shows it is not — at ε∈{0.10,0.20} engagement curation *reduces* variance relative to random under graph-restricted exposure, a stable, statistically significant (p<0.0001) reversal of the full-population direction, while diversity-boosting still dominates both. Second, we tested whether the backfire is an artifact of unweighted-mean integration; replacing it with a similarity-weighted (Gaussian-kernel) integration rule leaves the frozen-update mechanism and the diversity-boosting-dominates-both ordering unchanged at every ε tested. We conclude that, *within this class of bounded-confidence audience models*, naively reversing a similarity-based recommender's sign is not a reliable depolarization strategy when audiences have narrow tolerance for disagreement — this is our single robust, cross-cutting finding, unaffected by network topology or integration rule — while the smaller and topology-dependent engagement-curation effect should be read as a demonstration that curation *can* move variance in either direction depending on exposure structure, not as a calibrated magnitude.

## 1. Introduction

A dominant narrative holds that engagement-maximizing recommender curation causes echo chambers and polarization, motivating "inject diverse content" mitigations. Evidence for the underlying claim is mixed: Bakshy et al. (2015) find algorithmic ranking modestly reduces cross-cutting exposure relative to random, far less than individual choice does; Liu et al. (2025, *PNAS*) find short-term YouTube filter-bubble exposure has limited measurable polarization effects. Simulation work grounded in bounded-confidence dynamics (Sîrbu et al., 2019; Cinus et al., 2024/2026) shows recommenders *can* produce echo chambers and a "segregation-before-polarization" pathway. Chuang et al. (2024/2025) extend this with LLM agents that reason about content, at the cost of interpretability.

Two questions motivate our study. First, how much of the curation-polarization link is a causal effect of *who* is shown vs. *how many* peers are shown — prior comparisons rarely hold exposure count fixed. Second, does deliberately boosting exposure diversity actually reduce polarization, or can it backfire when content that is *too* dissimilar is simply discounted rather than integrated? We are not aware of prior work comparing engagement-maximizing and diversity-boosting curation against a matched-exposure-count random baseline within one bounded-confidence framework, and stress-testing the result against curation sharpness, update rate, population size, exposure count, network topology, and integration rule.

**Contributions.** (1) A minimal, reproducible ABM where λ continuously interpolates diversity-boosting/random/engagement-maximizing exposure with K held fixed. (2) A diversity-boosting ablation showing naive anti-similarity curation backfires via a verified "frozen update" mechanism that survives 18 independent sensitivity cells (β, μ, N, K, network topology) and a change of integration rule — this is the paper's one load-bearing, generalizable finding. (3) A secondary, honestly-scoped observation that engagement-optimized curation's effect on variance is real but small, sign-dependent on exposure topology, and not a stable point estimate — a demonstration that curation *can* move variance, not a calibrated magnitude, and we frame it as such rather than as a second robust result on par with (2).

## 2. Related Work

**Bounded-confidence dynamics.** The Hegselmann–Krause (HK) model (2002) is our base formalism: agents integrate only opinions within radius ε. HK's original analysis used unfiltered full-population averaging; we insert an algorithmic pre-filter (curation) before integration — what a recommender actually controls (exposure, not integration). Our diversity-boosting failure mode is conceptually related to, but distinct from, the HK-adjacent literature on injecting noise or extremist agents into bounded-confidence populations; we instead study a *recommender policy* that systematically targets maximal dissimilarity and show it interacts destructively with the same confidence filter, under multiple exposure topologies and integration rules.

**Recommender-mediated echo chambers.** Sîrbu et al. (2019) find friend-recommendation accelerates echo chamber formation. We curate *content exposure per round* rather than topology, and hold exposure count fixed across curation strengths to isolate "who" from "how many." Cinus et al. (2026) report segregation-before-polarization under content recommendation; our modest, ε-gated, topology-sensitive effect size is a complementary, more conservative estimate under a design that specifically controls for exposure count.

**Empirical evidence.** Bakshy et al. (2015) and Liu et al. (2025) both caution against assuming a large, unconditional curation effect — consistent with our small and, as shown below, topology-dependent engagement effect.

**LLM-agent simulations.** Chuang et al. (2024/2025) capture richer belief-update semantics at the cost of the clean, matched, fully-controllable manipulations we rely on; our design is an austere, mechanism-isolating complement.

## 3. Method

**Population and opinions.** N=150 agents each hold o_i ∈ [−1, 1], i.i.d. Uniform(−1, 1) (theoretical variance 1/3 ≈ 0.333).

**Curation policy.** Each round, agent i is shown K peer opinions sampled without replacement via a Gumbel-top-K weighted sampler with log-weights log w_ij = −β·λ·|o_i − o_j|, β=6. λ=0: random exposure. λ=1: engagement-maximizing. λ<0: diversity-boosting. K=5 is held fixed across all λ.

**Belief update.** Agent i integrates opinions within radius ε via a fraction μ toward that quantity, μ=0.5 by default; with no in-window peer, the agent is "frozen" that round. T=80 rounds. Default integration is an unweighted mean over in-tolerance exposures; this revision also tests a similarity-weighted (Gaussian-kernel) alternative (see (H) below).

**Sweep design.** Main sweep: λ∈{0,...,1.0}×ε∈{0.10,...,0.30}, 30 seeds/cell (900 runs, `sim.py`). Prior revisions add a diversity-boosting extension (450 runs), K-robustness (540 runs), exact-grid frozen fraction and β/μ/N sensitivity at small seed counts (`sim_v2.py`, 645 runs), and (`sim_v3.py`, 1530 runs, 121.8s) the β×μ grid re-run at 40 seeds/cell, N grid re-run at 30 seeds/cell, and a network-topology check (agents on a fixed Erdős–Rényi graph, mean degree 20, curation restricted to graph neighbors), λ∈{−1,0,1}×ε∈{0.10,0.20,0.30}, 20 seeds/cell. **New for this revision (`sim_v4.py`, 750 runs, 43.8s):** (G) the network-topology grid at ε∈{0.10,0.20} re-run at 80 seeds/cell (vs. 20), testing whether the engagement-vs-random sign flip observed there is sampling noise, like the earlier β×μ/N flips; (H) the full λ×ε grid re-run with a Gaussian-kernel similarity-weighted integration rule replacing the unweighted mean over in-tolerance peers (30 seeds/cell, λ∈{−1,0,1}×ε∈{0.10,0.20,0.30}), testing whether the frozen-update backfire depends on the specific integration rule.

**Metrics.** Final opinion variance; mean exposure variance (diversity mediator); frozen fraction.

**Statistics.** Paired t-tests (paired by seed) for engagement-vs-random gaps in every sensitivity cell; OLS of final variance on λ, ε, λ·ε.

## 4. Results

**4.1 Engagement-optimized curation has a small, ε-dependent effect at default hyperparameters.** Pooled λ=1 vs λ=0 (β=6, μ=0.5, N=150): +0.0060 (+2.1%), paired-t p=1.8e-5, n=150, ranging from a small, marginal reversal at ε=0.10 (p=0.043) to +8.3% at ε=0.30. OLS gives a significant λ·ε interaction (p=0.014) but a non-significant λ main effect (p=0.13): even at default settings, the effect is conditional, not uniform.

**4.2 Diversity-boosting backfires, confirmed on the matching grid.** λ=−1.0 gives final variance 0.329–0.346, exceeding random and engagement-curation at every ε≥0.15 (pooled +14.5%, p=2.1e-29). Frozen fraction, recomputed on the exact λ=−1 grid: 98.5% (ε=0.30) to 99.7% (ε=0.10).

**4.3 Higher seed counts resolve the full-population engagement-effect sign flips as noise.** Re-running the β×μ grid at 40 seeds/cell (vs. 15): across all 9 cells, zero show a statistically significant (p<0.05) engagement<random reversal; apparent flips at β=3 are not distinguishable from zero (p=0.209, p=0.259). At β=6 and β=10 the positive engagement effect is confirmed. The N grid re-run at 30 seeds/cell (vs. 10) tells the same story: no N∈{50,150,500} shows a significant engagement<random gap (p=0.588, 0.295, 0.070). Diversity-boosting exceeds both other conditions in all 9 (β,μ) cells and all 3 N values without exception.

**4.4 The backfire survives full-population-vs-graph topology; but the engagement-effect sign flip under graph topology is real, not noise (answers R3-Q2).** Restricting curation to Erdős–Rényi graph neighbors (mean degree 20), diversity-boosting still dominates both alternatives at every ε∈{0.10,0.20,0.30} (frozen fraction 95.4–99.0%). At 20 seeds/cell (v3), the engagement-vs-random gap flipped sign at ε∈{0.10,0.20}, and we flagged this as possibly fragile, by analogy to the full-population flips resolved in §4.3. Re-running those two cells at 80 seeds/cell shows this flip is *not* noise: at ε=0.10, engagement=0.3231 vs. random=0.3271 (p<0.0001); at ε=0.20, engagement=0.2896 vs. random=0.2989 (p<0.0001) — both stable, statistically decisive reversals of the full-population direction. Graph-restricted exposure is a qualitatively different regime from full connectivity for the engagement-curation effect specifically — plausibly because within a sparse neighbor pool, engagement-maximizing curation repeatedly surfaces the same few close neighbors, narrowing effective per-agent exposure diversity more than random sampling over the same small pool — while it changes nothing about the diversity-boosting mechanism, which remains dominant regardless of topology.

**4.5 The backfire does not depend on unweighted-mean integration (answers R3-Q3).** Replacing the belief update's plain mean over in-tolerance peers with a Gaussian-kernel similarity-weighted mean (closer in-tolerance peers pull harder) leaves the ordering unchanged at all three ε: diversity-boosting variance (0.3293, 0.3320, 0.3466 at ε=0.10/0.20/0.30) exceeds both random (0.3247, 0.2987, 0.2532) and engagement (0.3229, 0.3067, 0.2813) in every case, and frozen fraction for λ=−1 stays at 98.5–99.7%. Weighting integration by closeness cannot rescue diversity-boosted content because the confidence filter — which peers count as "in window" at all — is unaffected by how the in-window peers are subsequently combined; the backfire is a gating failure, not a weighting failure.

**4.6 Robustness to K (unchanged from round 1).** At K∈{3,5,10}, diversity-boost exceeds engagement exceeds random in final variance at ε=0.20 for every K.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

This model is a deliberately minimal, single-dimensional, largely uncalibrated toy with no learned ranking model and no real-platform calibration of λ or β. This revision's checks sharpen, rather than remove, that limitation, and narrow the paper's claim to a single robust result. The engagement-curation effect is real at default settings but is not a stable, sign-invariant estimate: it can *reverse sign and remain statistically decisive* under graph-restricted exposure (§4.4), a stronger and more interesting form of fragility than the sampling noise resolved in §4.3 — the direction of the effect is genuinely topology-dependent, not merely underpowered at small seed counts. We report it as a demonstration that similarity-biased curation *can* move variance, in either direction depending on exposure structure, not as a calibrated magnitude — this narrows our contribution relative to prior HK-based echo-chamber simulations (Sîrbu et al., 2019; Cinus et al., 2026) to primarily the diversity-boosting result, and we frame the introduction's contributions accordingly rather than presenting both findings as equally load-bearing. The diversity-boosting backfire is the opposite case: across 9 (β,μ) cells, 3 N values, 3 K values, 3 network-topology ε values, and a similarity-weighted integration rule at 3 further ε values — 21 independent sensitivity checks beyond the main grid — diversity-boosted variance exceeds both alternatives without exception. We still cannot claim this transfers quantitatively to real platforms (our graph is a single Erdős–Rényi draw per run with fixed mean degree, not a calibrated social network, and λ/β have no empirical anchor), but the *mechanism* — a confidence filter that discards content selected for maximal dissimilarity, before any integration rule can act on it — is not an artifact of connectivity, seed count, hyperparameters, or how in-tolerance opinions are averaged. Practically, diversity-boosting interventions should be evaluated against the audience's actual tolerance for disagreement, not a content-side diversity target, an assumption that more topological reach fixes the problem, or a hope that smarter integration weighting compensates for a gating failure.

## 6. Conclusion

Using a matched-exposure-count bounded-confidence simulation, we find engagement-optimized curation produces a small increase in polarization at default full-population settings, but this effect is not a stable point estimate: it is not statistically distinguishable from zero at low curation sharpness, and — newly shown to be a genuine, stable effect rather than noise — it *reverses sign* under graph-restricted exposure. We therefore treat it as illustrative of a mechanism that can cut either way depending on exposure structure, not as a calibrated magnitude. A naive diversity-boosting policy does not reduce polarization under any condition we test; it increases final-variance polarization, by pushing 95–99.7% of curated exposures outside agents' confidence window and freezing them near their initial, unconverged state — and this ordering survives all 21 sensitivity cells we test, spanning curation sharpness, update rate, population size, exposure count, network topology, and integration rule. We recommend evaluating "add diversity" interventions against a bounded-confidence-style audience model and achieved, in-tolerance exposure diversity, not nominal content diversity — while treating the engagement-effect magnitude and sign as topology-dependent and the diversity-boosting backfire as the paper's one finding robust enough to generalize beyond a single configuration.
