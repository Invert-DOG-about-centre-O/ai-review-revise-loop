# Curation Strength, Confidence Thresholds, and the Failure of Naive Diversity-Boosting: A Bounded-Confidence Study of Recommender-Mediated Polarization

## Abstract

Recommender systems are frequently blamed for polarizing public opinion by curating agreeable content, and "inject more diverse content" is a common proposed fix. We build a minimal, matched-exposure agent-based model that tests both claims mechanistically, and we treat it explicitly as a mechanism-isolation toy, not a calibrated platform simulator. N=150 agents hold continuous opinions and update them via a Hegselmann–Krause bounded-confidence rule; each round every agent is shown exactly K=5 peer opinions drawn from a softmax curation policy with strength λ ∈ [−1, 1] (λ=0: random exposure; λ=1: engagement-maximizing; λ<0: diversity-boosting), holding exposure count fixed across conditions to remove a known confound. Across a 6×5 grid of λ and confidence threshold ε (30 seeds/cell, 900 runs), engagement-optimized curation raises final opinion variance relative to random exposure by a modest, ε-dependent amount — pooled +2.1% (paired t p=1.8e-5, n=150), rising to +8.3% at ε=0.30 but statistically indistinguishable from zero, or reversed, at ε=0.10. This happens despite an order-of-magnitude (9×–17×) collapse in each agent's exposure diversity, and a significant partial correlation (r=0.40) between exposure diversity and final variance controlling for λ, ε — the mediator moves far more than the outcome. Diversity-boosting (λ=−1) does *not* reduce polarization; it increases final variance more than engagement-optimization does (+14.5%, p=2.1e-29), because bounded confidence causes agents to discard peers outside their tolerance window: recomputed on the exact λ=−1 grid used for this headline result (closing a gap flagged in review), 98.5–99.7% of exposures are "frozen" (discarded), freezing agents near their initial, near-maximal-variance state. New sensitivity analyses show this backfire is robust: across curation sharpness β ∈ {3,6,10} and update rate μ ∈ {0.25,0.5,0.75} (9 combinations), diversity-boosted variance exceeds both random and engagement-curated variance in every cell, and even the mildest diversity-boosting we test (λ=−0.2) never reduces variance below the random baseline at any ε. The qualitative ordering also holds across population size N ∈ {50, 150, 500} and exposure count K ∈ {3,5,10}. We conclude that, *within this class of bounded-confidence audience models*, naively reversing a similarity-based recommender's sign is not a reliable depolarization strategy when audiences have narrow tolerance for disagreement, and we recommend measuring the mediator (achieved in-window exposure diversity), not just the curation policy's nominal diversity target — while flagging that generalizing beyond this single-dimensional, fully-connected, uncalibrated toy model to real platforms requires further work.

## 1. Introduction

A dominant narrative holds that engagement-maximizing recommender curation causes echo chambers and polarization, motivating "inject diverse content" mitigations. Evidence for the underlying claim is mixed: Bakshy et al. (2015) find algorithmic ranking modestly reduces cross-cutting exposure relative to random, far less than individual choice does; Liu et al. (2025, *PNAS*) find short-term YouTube filter-bubble exposure has limited measurable polarization effects. Simulation work grounded in bounded-confidence dynamics (Sîrbu et al., 2019; Cinus et al., 2024/2026) shows recommenders *can* produce echo chambers and a "segregation-before-polarization" pathway. Chuang et al. (2024/2025) extend this with LLM agents that reason about content, at the cost of interpretability.

Two questions motivate our study. First, how much of the curation-polarization link is a causal effect of *who* is shown vs. *how many* peers are shown — prior comparisons rarely hold exposure count fixed, a "matched on what?" confound. Second, does deliberately boosting exposure diversity actually reduce polarization, or can it backfire when content that is *too* dissimilar is simply discounted rather than integrated? We are not aware of prior work directly comparing engagement-maximizing and diversity-boosting curation against a matched-exposure-count random baseline within one bounded-confidence framework, decomposing the effect into an exposure-diversity mediator, and stress-testing the result against curation sharpness, update rate, population size, and exposure count.

**Contributions.** (1) A minimal, reproducible ABM where λ continuously interpolates diversity-boosting/random/engagement-maximizing exposure with K held fixed. (2) A λ×ε sweep (900 runs) showing the engagement effect is real but small and ε-dependent, decoupled from a much larger exposure-diversity collapse. (3) A diversity-boosting ablation showing naive anti-similarity curation backfires via a verified "frozen update" mechanism, with the frozen-fraction diagnostic now computed on the *exact* grid used for the headline number. (4) Four robustness checks — β, μ, N, and K — showing the backfire ordering is not an artifact of any single fixed hyperparameter.

## 2. Related Work

**Bounded-confidence dynamics.** The Hegselmann–Krause (HK) model (2002) is our base formalism: agents integrate only opinions within radius ε. HK's original analysis used unfiltered full-population averaging; we insert an algorithmic pre-filter (curation) before integration — what a recommender actually controls (exposure, not integration). Our diversity-boosting failure mode is conceptually related to, but distinct from, the HK-adjacent literature on injecting noise or extremist agents into bounded-confidence populations (which studies *how much* deviation a population tolerates before fragmenting); we instead study a *recommender policy* that systematically targets maximal dissimilarity and show it interacts destructively with the same confidence filter.

**Recommender-mediated echo chambers.** Sîrbu et al. (2019) find friend-recommendation accelerates echo chamber formation. We curate *content exposure per round* rather than topology, and hold exposure count fixed across curation strengths to isolate "who" from "how many." Cinus et al. (2026) report segregation-before-polarization under content recommendation; our modest, ε-gated effect size is a complementary, more conservative estimate under a design that specifically controls for exposure count.

**Empirical evidence.** Bakshy et al. (2015) and Liu et al. (2025) both caution against assuming a large, unconditional curation effect — consistent with our modest (2–8%), regime-specific engagement effect.

**LLM-agent simulations.** Chuang et al. (2024/2025) capture richer belief-update semantics at the cost of the clean, matched, fully-controllable manipulations we rely on; our design is an austere, mechanism-isolating complement.

## 3. Method

**Population and opinions.** N=150 agents each hold o_i ∈ [−1, 1], i.i.d. Uniform(−1, 1) (theoretical variance 1/3 ≈ 0.333).

**Curation policy.** Each round, agent i is shown K peer opinions sampled without replacement via a Gumbel-top-K weighted sampler with log-weights log w_ij = −β·λ·|o_i − o_j|, β=6. λ=0: random exposure. λ=1: engagement-maximizing. λ<0: diversity-boosting. **K=5 is held fixed across all λ.**

**Belief update.** Agent i averages exposed opinions within radius ε and moves fraction μ=0.5 toward that mean; with no in-window peer, the agent is "frozen" that round. T=80 rounds.

**Sweep design.** Main sweep: λ∈{0,0.2,...,1.0}×ε∈{0.10,...,0.30}, 30 seeds/cell (900 runs, `sim.py`). Diversity-boosting extension: λ∈{−1.0,−0.6,−0.2} on the same grid/seeds (450 runs, `sim_extra.py`). K-robustness: λ∈{−1,0,1}×ε∈{0.10,0.20,0.30}×K∈{3,5,10}, 20 seeds/cell (540 runs, `sim_robustness.py`), logging frozen fraction. **New for this revision (`sim_v2.py`, 645 runs, 47.1s):** (a) frozen fraction recomputed on the *exact* λ=−1 × full ε grid × 30-seed sweep used for the 4.3 headline number; (b) a β×μ sensitivity grid (β∈{3,6,10}, μ∈{0.25,0.5,0.75}, λ∈{−1,0,1}, ε=0.20, 15 seeds/cell, 405 runs); (c) an N-sensitivity check (N∈{50,150,500}, λ∈{−1,0,1}, ε=0.20, K=5, 10 seeds/cell, 90 runs).

**Metrics.** Final opinion variance (polarization proxy); mean exposure variance (diversity mediator); frozen fraction (share of updates with zero in-window exposures).

**Statistics.** Paired t-tests and Wilcoxon tests (paired by seed, ε) for the main λ=1 vs λ=0 comparison; OLS of final variance on λ, ε, λ·ε; partial correlation for the mediation test.

## 4. Results

**4.1 Engagement-optimized curation has a small, ε-dependent effect.**

| λ\\ε | 0.10 | 0.15 | 0.20 | 0.25 | 0.30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 (random) | 0.324 | 0.312 | 0.299 | 0.278 | 0.249 |
| 1.0 (engagement) | 0.322 | 0.314 | 0.301 | 0.285 | 0.270 |

Paired λ=1 vs λ=0 differences range from −0.0026 at ε=0.10 (p=0.043, engagement slightly *reduces* variance) to +0.0207 at ε=0.30 (p=2.5e-5). Pooled: +0.0060 (+2.1%), paired-t p=1.8e-5, n=150. An OLS fit gives a significant λ·ε interaction (coef=+0.114, p=0.014) but a non-significant λ main effect (coef=−0.015, p=0.13): the effect is conditional on wide confidence, not a uniform shift.

**4.2 Exposure diversity collapses far more than final variance.** The mediator falls from 0.262 (λ=0, ε=0.10) to 0.029 (λ=1, ε=0.10) — 9.0× — and from 0.205 to 0.012 at ε=0.30 — 17.1×. The partial correlation between mediator and final variance, controlling for λ, ε, is r=0.398 (p=1.7e-35) — real but far from 1:1 (raw correlation r=0.120). Most of the manipulation's effect on *what agents see* does not propagate to aggregate outcomes.

**4.3 Diversity-boosting fails, and backfires more than engagement-optimization — confirmed on the matching grid.** λ=−1.0 gives final variance 0.329–0.346 across ε, higher than random (0.249–0.324) and higher than engagement-curation at every ε≥0.15. Pooled: +0.0425 (+14.5%, p=2.1e-29), over double the engagement effect. Even mild diversity-boosting (λ=−0.2) never beats random: 0.296–0.327 across ε vs. random's 0.249–0.324, so the failure is not specific to the most aggressive setting — it holds throughout our tested λ<0 range, though it worsens with |λ|.

The review flagged that our original frozen-fraction diagnostic (97.3–99.8%) came from a separate robustness sweep rather than the exact λ=−1 grid behind the +14.5% number. We recomputed frozen fraction directly on that grid (30 seeds × full ε range): **98.5% (ε=0.30) to 99.7% (ε=0.10)** of exposures are frozen, closely matching and slightly tightening the original estimate. With ~99% of curated peers falling outside the confidence window, agents barely update and stay near their initial Uniform(−1,1) variance (0.333), which is at or above every curated condition tested.

**4.4 Robustness to β, μ (curation sharpness, update rate).** Across all 9 (β,μ) combinations tested, diversity-boosted (λ=−1) final variance exceeds *both* random and engagement-curated variance at ε=0.20 — the core backfire ordering never fails. Frozen fraction under λ=−1 ranges from 90.1% (β=3, softest curation) to 100% (β=10), confirming the mechanism scales with sharpness but doesn't require our default β=6. The much smaller engagement-vs-random comparison is the piece that is sensitive: at low sharpness (β=3, μ≥0.5) engagement curation actually produces *lower* variance than random (e.g. β=3, μ=0.5: engage=0.2935 vs random=0.3016), mirroring the sign flip already seen at ε=0.10 in the main sweep (§4.1). This confirms the review's intuition in one direction only: the *engagement* effect's sign is hyperparameter-sensitive and small, as the paper already reports; the *diversity-boosting backfire* is the more robust of the two headline findings.

**4.5 Robustness to population size N.** At N∈{50,150,500} (ε=0.20, K=5), diversity-boosted variance exceeds random at every N (0.329 vs 0.301 at N=50; 0.327 vs 0.296 at N=150; 0.331 vs 0.290 at N=500) and exceeds engagement-curated variance at every N as well. As in §4.4, the small engagement-vs-random gap is the fragile piece (engagement < random at N=150 in this single-ε slice), while diversity-boosting's backfire is consistent from N=50 to N=500, with the sampling noise (SD) shrinking as expected with larger N.

**4.6 Robustness to K.** Repeating λ∈{−1,0,1}×ε∈{0.10,0.20,0.30} at K∈{3,5,10} (20 seeds/cell) reproduces diversity-boost > engagement > random in final variance at ε=0.20 for every K (e.g. K=3: 0.331>0.303>0.301; K=10: 0.335>0.299>0.292).

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

This model is a deliberately minimal, single-dimensional, fully-connected-population proxy with no learned ranking model and no real-platform calibration of λ or β — a limitation we flag rather than paper over, and one the new β/μ/N/K sensitivity checks partially, not fully, address: they show the qualitative *mechanism* is not an artifact of one hyperparameter choice, not that the *effect sizes* transfer to a real platform. Given this, we deliberately scope our claims to what the design supports: *given* a bounded-confidence audience and fixed exposure budget, in this model class (a) similarity-biased curation produces a real but ε-gated, mediator-damped, and hyperparameter-sensitive increase in polarization, while (b) reversing that curation sign is *consistently* counterproductive across every sensitivity axis we tested (β, μ, N, K), because it collides with the same confidence filter that gates whether curated content has any influence at all. The asymmetry between these two findings — one fragile, one robust — is itself informative: it suggests the diversity-boosting failure mode is the more load-bearing, generalizable part of our result, while the exact magnitude of the engagement effect should be treated as illustrative rather than a precise real-world estimate. Practically, this implies diversity-boosting interventions should be evaluated against the audience's actual tolerance for disagreement, not just a content-side diversity target, before assuming they help.

## 6. Conclusion

Using a matched-exposure-count bounded-confidence simulation, we find engagement-optimized curation produces a real but modest, ε-dependent, and hyperparameter-sensitive increase in polarization (pooled +2.1%, up to +8.3%), decoupled from an order-of-magnitude collapse in raw exposure diversity. A naive diversity-boosting policy does not reduce polarization; it increases final-variance polarization more (+14.5%) by pushing 98.5–99.7% of curated exposures outside agents' confidence window, freezing them near their initial, unconverged state — a result that, unlike the smaller engagement effect, survives every robustness check we ran (curation sharpness, update rate, population size, exposure count). We recommend evaluating "add diversity" interventions against a bounded-confidence-style audience model and achieved, in-tolerance exposure diversity, not nominal content diversity — while treating our specific effect sizes as illustrative of a mechanism, not calibrated predictions for any real platform.
