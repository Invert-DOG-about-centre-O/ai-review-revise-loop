# When Does Censored Feedback Trap a Group? Feature Informativeness as the Governing Lever in Algorithmic Allocation

## Abstract

Algorithmic allocators (loans, patrols, screening) typically observe an outcome label *only for the people they act on*. This "selective labels" structure creates a feedback loop: a model that under-scores a group approves fewer of its members, observes fewer of their labels, and can stay wrong indefinitely. Much socio-technical work treats such traps as an intrinsic hazard of feedback loops. We ask a sharper, more actionable question: *when* does the trap actually bite, and *what governs its severity*? Using a controlled lending simulation in which two demographic groups are **identical in ground truth** — so every disparity that emerges is a pure loop artifact — we find the loop is trapping only when the individual-level feature is weakly informative, forcing the model to lean on group identity. Within this environment, **feature informativeness acts as a smooth, monotone governing lever**: as feature noise rises, the persistent approval-rate gap of a greedy allocator grows from 0.04 to 0.26 and its group-B calibration error quadruples; the same monotone trend holds when noise is *heteroskedastic* (degrading only group B's signal). Against baselines (ε-greedy, UCB optimism, demographic-parity), evaluated over 32 seeds with paired-bootstrap tests, we find: targeted exploration (UCB) *matches* random exploration on fairness gaps (Δ=0.004, p=0.35) while delivering significantly higher qualified-approval (+0.029, p<0.001), buying fairness at strictly lower efficiency cost; this "free lunch" is bounded, eroding utility past a moderate optimism bonus; a parity constraint erases the visible gap but leaves miscalibration intact; and, counterintuitively, adding *more* seed data from the biased region worsens the trap — robust across four seed-restriction thresholds. Code and raw logs accompany the paper.

## 1. Introduction

A defining socio-technical property of deployed decision systems is that they generate the data they later learn from. A lender records repayment only for approved applicants; predictive policing observes crime only where patrols are sent. This *selective-labels* / censored-feedback structure has been repeatedly identified as a driver of runaway disparity: past decisions bias future training data, and disparities compound over time [1,2,4].

Most of this literature establishes *that* feedback loops can amplify bias and argues, correctly, that purely technical fixes are insufficient [1,3]. Less attention is paid to a question a practitioner actually needs answered: **under what conditions does the loop trap a group, and which design knob most controls the damage?** A blanket "feedback loops are dangerous" gives no guidance on where to spend a limited fairness budget.

We isolate one answer empirically, with a deliberately strong null: the two groups are statistically identical in ground truth and feature distribution, so any group gap is *manufactured entirely by the learning loop*. Our central finding is that the trap is not automatic. When the model has a genuinely predictive *individual* feature, it rescues high-quality members of the disadvantaged group and the initial disparity decays on its own. The trap emerges only as the individual signal weakens and the model is pushed to rely on group identity as a proxy. Feature informativeness thus acts as a continuous dial on trap severity — a lever often more addressable in practice (better features, richer applications, human review of borderline cases) than the loop structure itself.

### Related Work

Simulation studies of predictive policing show patrol loops amplify racial disparities via biased observation [2,7], and self-training on model outputs amplifies representational disparity [4]; we instead characterize the *regime boundary* separating self-correcting from self-trapping loops. Lakkaraju et al. formalized selective labels for policy evaluation [6]; we use the same censoring as the *mechanism* of harm. A recurring proposal is that uncertainty-aware exploration prevents amplification [1]; we test ε-greedy and UCB head-to-head with significance tests. Finally, a critical literature argues metric-satisfying fixes can entrench power [3,5]; our parity baseline shows exactly such a fix masking, not curing, an epistemic deficit about a group.

## 2. Method

**World.** Applicants arrive in rounds of 400. Each has scalar quality `x ~ N(0,1)` and group `g ∈ {A,B}` (50/50). True repayment probability is `p(x) = σ(1.6x)`, **identical for both groups**. If approved, an applicant repays (label 1) with probability `p(x)`; utility is `+1` for a repaid loan, `−1` for a default (0 if not approved).

**Model.** Group-aware logistic regression on `[1, x_obs, g, x_obs·g]`, fit by gradient descent (numpy only), so it *can* recover the (identical) truth but must estimate each group's parameters from that group's observed labels — hence vulnerable to censoring. It observes a **noisy** feature `x_obs = x + N(0, σ_noise)` while outcomes depend on the clean `x`; `σ_noise` sets how much individual signal is available versus how much the model must fall back on group identity.

**Censored feedback.** Labels are appended to the training set **only for approved applicants**; the model retrains every round (40 rounds).

**Seed bias.** Group A's seed labels span all `x`; group B's few seed labels come only from a restricted low-`x` slice (`x < −0.3` by default) — a plausible historical artifact ("B was only ever approved when very safe"). This is the initial perturbation from which any trap must grow or decay.

**Allocators.**
- **greedy**: approve iff predicted `p ≥ τ=0.5` (no exploration).
- **eps_explore**: greedy, but with probability `ε` approve a uniformly random applicant (untargeted exploration).
- **ucb**: approve iff `pred + β·u ≥ τ`, `u = 1/√(n_g+1)` grows for the group with fewer observed labels (targeted exploration).
- **dp_parity**: enforce equal approval *rate* across groups each round.

**Metrics** ("final window" = mean of last 10 rounds). *rate_gap*: |approval-rate A − B| (should be 0 since groups are identical). *tpr_gap*: gap in approval among the truly-creditworthy (`p≥τ`). *qual_appr*: fraction of truly-creditworthy applicants approved (allocative efficiency). *util*: mean per-applicant net return. *calibErr_B*: |mean predicted − mean true| for B. **Statistics.** The main allocator comparison uses **32 seeds**; allocator-vs-allocator differences are tested with a **paired bootstrap over seeds** (20 000 resamples, two-sided). Ablation sweeps use 8–16 seeds (noted per table).

## 3. Results

**The trap is not automatic; a strong feature self-corrects.** With an informative feature (`σ_noise=0.5`) the greedy allocator's approval-rate gap settles at only 0.044 despite the biased seed — the individual signal lets it approve high-`x` B applicants, whose labels correct the model. The manufactured disparity decays on its own.

**Feature informativeness is a smooth governing lever.** Sweeping `σ_noise` for greedy (Table 1) shows every harm metric worsening monotonically as the individual signal degrades and the model leans on group identity:

*Table 1 — greedy, final window vs feature noise (8 seeds).*

| σ_noise | rate_gap | tpr_gap | qual_appr | calibErr_B | util |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.044 | 0.054 | 0.839 | 0.021 | 0.219 |
| 1.0 | 0.047 | 0.069 | 0.730 | 0.022 | 0.169 |
| 2.0 | 0.084 | 0.105 | 0.602 | 0.030 | 0.102 |
| 3.0 | 0.142 | 0.170 | 0.509 | 0.033 | 0.068 |
| 4.0 | 0.262 | 0.293 | 0.331 | 0.080 | 0.029 |

The persistent gap grows ~6× and B-calibration error ~4× as the feature becomes uninformative: *the loop traps a group to the extent the model must proxy through group identity.*

**The lever survives heteroskedastic noise (new).** A natural objection: the Table-1 trend uses noise applied equally to both groups. We re-ran greedy with **group-dependent** noise, `σ_B = het·σ_A`, base `σ_A=2.0`, degrading *only* B's observability while ground truth stays identical (Table 5). The `het=1.0` row reproduces Table 1's `σ_noise=2.0` row exactly (rate_gap 0.084, calibErr_B 0.030) — an internal consistency check — and every harm metric again rises monotonically as B's individual signal degrades relative to A's. The mechanism is if anything *sharper* here: when only the disadvantaged group's signal is corrupted, the model has no choice but to proxy through group identity, and the tpr_gap reaches 0.52.

*Table 5 — greedy, heteroskedastic across-group noise, final window (8 seeds).*

| het = σ_B/σ_A | rate_gap | tpr_gap | calibErr_B |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 0.084 | 0.105 | 0.030 |
| 1.5 | 0.143 | 0.201 | 0.035 |
| 2.0 | 0.257 | 0.361 | 0.049 |
| 3.0 | 0.366 | 0.518 | 0.057 |

**Allocator comparison, with significance (32 seeds, trap regime `σ_noise=2.0`; Table 2).** At 32 seeds the greedy gap point-estimate settles to 0.063±0.040 (the 8-seed 0.084 was inflated by one unlucky seed), still the widest and most variable. Both exploration methods reduce it: paired bootstrap gives greedy−ε_greedy Δrate_gap = +0.014 (p=0.049) and Δtpr_gap = +0.015 (p=0.040); greedy−UCB Δrate_gap = +0.010 (p=0.085, marginal). `dp_parity` drives the visible gap to ~0.002 (vs greedy, Δ=−0.061, p<0.001).

*Table 2 — allocators, final window, mean±sd over 32 seeds.*

| method | rate_gap | tpr_gap | qual_appr | util | calibErr_B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| greedy | 0.063±0.040 | 0.079±0.043 | 0.611 | 0.098±0.010 | 0.026 |
| eps_explore | 0.050±0.015 | 0.064±0.019 | 0.620 | 0.094±0.011 | 0.019 |
| ucb | 0.054±0.020 | 0.068±0.022 | 0.650 | 0.098±0.009 | 0.022 |
| dp_parity | 0.002±0.001 | 0.041±0.011 | 0.616 | 0.098±0.010 | 0.023 |

**A multiple-comparisons caveat.** We report nine paired-bootstrap tests. A formal Bonferroni correction sets the family-wise threshold at α=0.05/9≈0.0056. Two headline claims clear it decisively: UCB's efficiency edge over ε-greedy (qual_appr +0.029, p<0.001) and dp_parity closing the visible gap (p<0.001), both with p far below 0.0056. The greedy−ε_greedy rate_gap result (p=0.049) does **not** clear 0.0056 — we therefore treat "exploration narrows the gap" as *suggestive*, load-bearing only in combination with the same-direction tpr_gap (p=0.040, also above 0.0056) and UCB results, not as a standalone significant finding. The null we most want to defend — UCB vs ε-greedy on fairness (p=0.35) — is a non-rejection and is only strengthened by a stricter threshold.

**Targeted exploration matches random exploration on fairness but is strictly more efficient.** We previously claimed UCB "Pareto-dominates" ε-greedy; the paired tests refine this. On the fairness gaps the two are **statistically indistinguishable** (UCB−ε_greedy Δrate_gap = +0.004, p=0.35). UCB's genuine, significant advantage is *efficiency*: qualified-approval +0.029 (p<0.001) and utility +0.005 (p=0.050), because directing exploration at the under-sampled group approves *deserving* B applicants rather than random low-quality ones. Random exploration reaches the same fairness only by wasting approvals — its utility (0.094) is the lowest of all methods. The honest statement: **UCB attains ε-greedy's fairness at strictly lower utility cost**, not that it dominates on fairness itself.

**The free lunch is bounded, and utility is the binding constraint.** Extending the optimism bonus (Table 3) shows gaps and qualified-approval keep improving with β, but **utility peaks near β≈2 then declines monotonically** (0.096→0.090→0.081→0.071 as β goes 2→4→6→8). Qualified-approval itself never reverses in the computed range — it keeps climbing to 0.886 at β=8 — so the harm from over-optimism shows up first in *utility* (approving genuinely low-quality under-sampled applicants), not in a collapse of qualified access. Utility is thus the binding constraint that ends the free lunch; the intervention holds only in a bounded, tunable regime.

*Table 3 — UCB vs β, trap regime (16 seeds).*

| β | rate_gap | qual_appr | util |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 | 0.051 | 0.722 | 0.096 |
| 4.0 | 0.046 | 0.788 | 0.090 |
| 6.0 | 0.045 | 0.845 | 0.081 |
| 8.0 | 0.040 | 0.886 | 0.071 |

**A parity constraint hides the trap without curing it.** `dp_parity` zeroes the visible gap by construction, yet its group-B calibration error (0.023) is statistically comparable to greedy's (0.026): the tiny difference (Δ=−0.003, p=0.026) is significant only because of its small variance, and is negligible in magnitude — the model is *still* epistemically wrong about B; the constraint merely overrides its scores. The deficit would resurface the moment the constraint is relaxed or applied to a new sub-population, mirroring the sociotechnical critique that metric-satisfying fixes can mask rather than remedy harm [3,5].

**Representativeness beats quantity, robustly across the seed restriction.** Sweeping the group-B seed fraction (trap regime) shows *more* B seed data yielding a *larger* final gap: because the B seed is region-restricted (only low-`x`, low-repayment), piling on more of it makes the model *more confident that B is bad*. This is not an artifact of one restriction threshold. Re-running across four thresholds (Table 4) shows the slope (gap at seed_B_frac 0.5 minus at 0.05) is **positive at every threshold**; it only *attenuates* as the restriction loosens toward `x<0.3` (a nearly representative seed), exactly as the mechanism predicts. The fix for a censored-feedback trap is representative exploration, not raw data volume.

*Table 4 — greedy rate_gap vs seed restriction threshold (16 seeds).*

| seed_thresh | gap@frac=0.05 | gap@frac=0.5 | slope |
|---|---|---|---|
| x<−0.8 | 0.052 | 0.148 | +0.096 |
| x<−0.3 | 0.053 | 0.141 | +0.088 |
| x<0.0 | 0.057 | 0.149 | +0.092 |
| x<0.3 | 0.080 | 0.129 | +0.049 |

**Reproducibility.** The full 32-seed allocator sweep in `sim_ext.py` is the expensive artifact (>20 min wall-clock, dominated by the 20 000-resample bootstrap). For faster third-party verification, the companion scripts `_focus.py` (Tables 3–4) and `_het.py` (Table 5) reproduce their targeted results in ~1–2 min each by running only the needed configurations; the bootstrap resample count and seed set are the two knobs that trade verification time for precision. Table 1 reproduces exactly against the accompanying `run_output.txt`.

## 4. Conclusion

Feedback loops in algorithmic allocation are widely flagged as bias amplifiers, but our controlled study — groups identical in ground truth, so every gap is a pure artifact — shows the trap is conditional, not automatic, and its severity is governed by a single interpretable lever: **feature informativeness**, whether that signal is degraded uniformly or only for the disadvantaged group. When the model can rely on genuine individual signal it self-corrects; when it must proxy through group identity the loop traps the disadvantaged group, monotonically worse as signal degrades. Among interventions, uncertainty-directed exploration (UCB) matches random exploration's fairness at significantly lower utility cost — a free lunch, but only up to a moderate optimism level, beyond which utility (not qualified access) is bought back; a demographic-parity constraint cosmetically closes the gap while leaving the model's beliefs about the group uncorrected; and adding more data from a biased region actively backfires across every restriction threshold tested.

**Limitations.** This is a toy simulation (scalar feature, logistic model, 40 rounds, two groups, identical ground truth). We deliberately assumed identical groups to isolate the loop; real settings add genuine base-rate differences, strategic applicants, and label noise. The monotonic informativeness trend is now verified for both homoskedastic and heteroskedastic-across-group additive-Gaussian noise on one functional form for `p(x)`; the "governing lever" claim should still be read as demonstrated *within this environment* rather than proven in general. The UCB uncertainty proxy is a per-group count, not a calibrated posterior, so the *magnitude* of its efficiency edge is tied to that design even though its direction is significant. The transferable claims — informativeness governs trap severity; targeted exploration is more efficient than random at equal fairness (within a bounded β); parity ≠ calibration; representativeness ≠ quantity — are the ones we stand behind.
