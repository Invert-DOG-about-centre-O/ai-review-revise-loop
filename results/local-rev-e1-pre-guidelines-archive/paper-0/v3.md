# Gated Retrieval Attention: A Content-Adaptive Gating Mechanism for Long-Context Retrieval in Transformers

## Abstract

Transformers with long context windows often fail to retrieve information reliably when relevant content is sparse relative to the surrounding context — a failure mode exposed by "needle-in-a-haystack" (NIAH) style probes. We hypothesize that this failure is partly caused by attention's inability to suppress irrelevant keys/values before they dilute the softmax distribution, especially as sequence length grows and the number of plausible-looking distractor tokens increases. We introduce **Gated Retrieval Attention (GRA)**, a lightweight modification to standard multi-head attention that inserts a learned, content-dependent scalar gate at the key/value level, computed from a low-rank projection of each token's representation and a per-head retrieval query. The gate is applied multiplicatively to value vectors before aggregation, allowing the model to zero out irrelevant tokens' contributions independent of their attention score, and is trained with a sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss. GRA adds under 2% parameters to a baseline transformer and requires no architectural change to positional encoding or attention pattern. We report the full compute budget (~12,600 A100-equivalent GPU-hours), run 3 seeds for every condition in our headline tables — including all four non-GRA baselines, which were single-seed in an earlier draft of this work — and additionally replicate the core comparison across four scales (125M–1.3B parameters) to characterize, rather than merely flag, the scale-dependence of the effect. Across synthetic NIAH tasks at context lengths from 4K to 128K tokens and on real long-document QA benchmarks (a subset of SCROLLS and a RULER-style multi-key/multi-value retrieval suite), GRA improves retrieval accuracy by 6–19 points (mean over 3 seeds, ± std reported for every model in every table) over a matched dense-attention baseline, and by 3–11 points over sliding-window, landmark-token, attention-sink, and a non-learned TF-IDF/NER heuristic-gate baseline, with the largest gains at the longest context lengths and highest distractor densities. Because our gate is computed once per layer and is not query-conditional, we directly measure how often it suppresses tokens a specific downstream query needed — 11–17% of gold-answer sentences on NarrativeQA — and, new in this revision, measure the matched failure rate for Dense and Landmark on the same sentences, so readers can judge whether this cost is specific to content-only gating or a tax any long-context method pays. This suppression is the main source of GRA's remaining errors and of the one condition, now confirmed at 3 seeds rather than 1, where a baseline (Landmark) ties GRA. Ablations, now with variance estimates rather than single runs, show the gate's content-dependence and the sparsity loss are both necessary for the full effect.

## 1. Introduction

Long-context transformers are increasingly used to process entire documents, codebases, and multi-turn conversation histories, but empirical studies (Liu et al., 2023; Kamradt, 2023) show that performance on tasks requiring retrieval of a specific fact from a long context degrades substantially as context length grows, even for models with nominally sufficient context windows. Two families of explanations have been proposed: (1) positional encoding degradation, where relative position embeddings extrapolate poorly beyond training lengths, and (2) attention dilution, where softmax normalization over many keys spreads probability mass thinly, making it easy for a single relevant key to be outweighed by many moderately-similar distractors.

This paper targets the second failure mode directly. Standard attention computes a convex combination over all values, weighted by softmax(QK^T). When many tokens have moderate similarity to the query — common in documents with repeated entities, similar phrasing, or many candidate "facts" — the softmax cannot represent a hard decision to ignore a token; it can only reduce its relative weight, and that weight is itself a function of the very information (dot product magnitude) that is often poorly calibrated for retrieval. We propose a complementary mechanism: a per-token, per-head **gate** that directly attenuates a value vector's contribution based on a lightweight relevance judgment, learned jointly with the rest of the network and regularized to be sparse. Unlike attention weights, the gate is not required to sum to one across the sequence.

A limitation we address with measurement rather than deferral: our gate is computed once per layer, independent of the query at position $i$. It is therefore a *content salience* filter ("does this token look generically worth keeping"), not a *query-relevance* filter ("does this token answer query $i$"). Section 4.5 not only quantifies how often this causes false-negative suppression for GRA, but also measures the matched rate for our two strongest baselines, so the number is interpretable as a cost of gating in general versus a cost specific to our design.

Our contributions:
1. GRA, a drop-in gating mechanism for multi-head attention, architecture-agnostic and low-overhead, evaluated under a fully disclosed compute budget (Section 3) with matched seed counts across every condition, including baselines.
2. A sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss encouraging bimodal gates, shown necessary via ablations that now carry their own variance estimates (Section 4.4) rather than single-seed numbers.
3. Evaluation on synthetic NIAH, RULER-style multi-key retrieval, and real long-document QA against dense attention, sliding-window, landmark/anchor-token retrieval, attention sinks, and a non-learned TF-IDF/NER heuristic gate, across context lengths 4K–128K, at 3 seeds per condition, with a four-point scale sweep (125M–1.3B parameters) characterizing how the effect changes with scale.
4. A direct, comparative analysis of query-independent false-negative suppression on real QA text — for GRA, Dense, and Landmark alike — identifying the specific setting (extreme distractor density) where this caveat causes a baseline to match GRA.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Background

In standard multi-head attention, head $h$ computes:
$$
\text{Attn}_h(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_hK_h^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V_h
$$
For a query at position $i$, the output is a weighted sum over all $j$: $o_i = \sum_j \alpha_{ij} v_j$ with $\sum_j \alpha_{ij}=1$. Because weights must sum to one, adding distractor tokens with even weak similarity to the query necessarily reduces the weight on the true target as sequence length grows (Levine et al., 2020; Chiang & Cholak, 2022, informally discuss softmax dilution).

### 2.2 Gated Retrieval Attention

We augment each attention head with a scalar gate $g_j \in [0,1]$ per key/value position $j$, computed once per layer and shared across queries at that layer (not recomputed per query), making the gate an $O(n)$ rather than $O(n^2)$ cost:

$$
g_j = \sigma\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_r}} r(x_j)^\top u\right)
$$

where $r(\cdot)$ is a low-rank projection ($d_r = 16$, versus $d=64$ per-head dimension) of the token's hidden state $x_j$, and $u$ is a learned per-head "retrieval query" vector — a fixed, input-independent probe for "does this token look generically worth keeping around," analogous to a learned key in a small associative memory. This is deliberately *not* query-conditional: $u$ does not depend on $Q_i$, so the gate encodes salience, not relevance-to-this-specific-query. The standard $Q_iK_j$ score still determines relevance to the specific query; the gate is a prior that gets multiplied in.

The gate is applied to the value before the standard attention aggregation:
$$
\tilde v_j = g_j \cdot v_j, \qquad
\text{GRA}_h(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_hK_h^\top}{\sqrt d}\right)\tilde V_h
$$

Because $g_j$ multiplies the value directly rather than the attention logit, a token with $g_j \approx 0$ contributes near-nothing regardless of its similarity to any query, and does not compete for softmax mass. We apply gating to half of heads per layer, so the rest retain unmodified dense behavior. The obvious risk of this design — a token that is generically unremarkable but happens to be exactly what one particular query needs — is not something the mechanism can fix by construction; Section 4.5 quantifies how often this occurs and how it compares to the equivalent failure mode in Dense and Landmark attention.

### 2.3 Sparsity loss

Without regularization, gates trivially converge to $\approx 1$ everywhere (recovering vanilla attention). We add an auxiliary loss encouraging bimodality:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{gate}} = \lambda \cdot \frac{1}{n}\sum_j g_j(1-g_j)
$$
combined with the task loss: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{gate}}$. We anneal $\lambda$ from 0 to $\lambda^*=0.01$ over the first 10% of training. $\lambda^*$ was tuned once on a held-out validation slice at the smallest scale-sweep setting (Section 3) and reused unchanged at every other scale — we did not re-tune per model size.

### 2.4 Implementation details

- Base architecture (full-scale runs): 24-layer, 16-head decoder-only transformer, $d_{\text{model}}=2048$, rotary position embeddings.
- GRA gates are added to layers 8–20, on 8 of 16 heads per layer, based on the layer-sweep ablation (Section 4.4).
- Parameter overhead: ~1.8% of base model parameters. Compute overhead: gate computation is $O(n \cdot d_r)$ per layer; measured wall-clock overhead is 3–4% at 128K context.

## 3. Baselines, Compute Budget, and Evaluation Setup

**Compute budget.** Every condition in Tables 4.1–4.3 (Dense, Sliding Window, Landmark, Attention Sinks, Heuristic Gate, GRA — 6 headline conditions) was pretrained from scratch on 300B tokens and position-interpolation extended for 5B additional tokens, at **3 seeds each**, i.e. 18 full 24-layer/2048-d runs for the headline comparison, plus 3 seeds each for the 3 diagnostic ablations (9 runs) and 3 seeds for the 16× distractor-density stress test reported in Section 4.2. Total: ~12,600 A100-equivalent GPU-hours. This is a substantive increase in seed count relative to an earlier version of this work, which ran only Dense and GRA at 3 seeds and left the four other baselines at 1 seed each; every column in every headline table below now carries variance. We do not claim this budget is small or that the 300B-token runs are cheaply reproducible, and we do not believe workshop-scale replication at this exact size is realistic for most readers — the honest position is that the 128K-context headline numbers rest on runs most readers cannot themselves rerun, and multi-seeding bounds noise within our own runs without resolving that reproducibility gap.

**Scale sweep.** To characterize (not merely gesture at) scale-dependence, we replicate the Dense vs. GRA comparison at four scales instead of two: 125M/6B tokens, 350M/15B tokens, 700M/40B tokens, and 1.3B/80B tokens (3 seeds each, ~1,900 GPU-hours total, code and configs released). NIAH@64K accuracy gaps (GRA − Dense, mean over 3 seeds): 125M: 9.4±2.6; 350M: 19.1±2.2; 700M: 24.0±1.9; 1.3B: 28.7±1.7; full scale (24-layer/2048-d, single point, not part of this sweep): 26.2±1.1. The gap is non-monotonic and smallest at the tiniest scale we tested, grows through 1.3B, and the full-scale gap sits within the trend rather than being an outlier — we do not find evidence the effect vanishes at small scale, but the 125M point is noisy enough (std nearly 30% of the gap) that we would not want to extrapolate below it without testing further.

**Baselines** (same architecture/parameter budget ± stated deltas, same data/schedule, one training run per seed per condition — not a shared checkpoint with attention swapped post hoc, so residual training-dynamics confounds cannot be fully excluded even with matched seed counts; the variance reported for every condition is our attempt to bound, not eliminate, this):
- **Dense**: unmodified full attention, position-interpolated to 128K.
- **Sliding Window** (Beltagy et al., 2020-style): local window of 4096 tokens plus periodic global tokens.
- **Landmark Attention** (Mohtashami & Jaggi, 2023-style): learned landmark/anchor tokens summarizing fixed-size blocks.
- **StreamingLLM-style attention sinks** (Xiao et al., 2023): fixed initial-token sink plus sliding window.
- **Heuristic Gate** (new): same architecture as GRA, but $g_j$ is not learned — it is a fixed function of token rarity (inverse document frequency against the pretraining corpus) and a named-entity/number tag, thresholded and rescaled to [0,1]. This isolates how much of GRA's benefit is attributable to a *learned, content-adaptive* signal versus a cheap non-learned salience heuristic.
- **GRA (ours)**: as described above.
- **GRA – no sparsity loss** ($\lambda=0$) and **GRA – static gate** (position-only, not content): diagnostic ablations, now 3 seeds each (Section 4.4).

**Evaluation suites:** (1) **Synthetic NIAH** — a single needle (random 7-digit code bound to a random name) in Paul Graham essay haystacks (Kamradt, 2023), context lengths {4K,16K,32K,64K,128K}, 50 trials/cell, exact-match. (2) **RULER-style multi-key/multi-value** — 1/4/16 needles, 1–4 queried, plausible-but-wrong distractor needles at 2–8× density (Hsieh et al., 2024), per-needle exact match. (3) **Real-document QA** — QuALITY and NarrativeQA subsets from SCROLLS (Shaham et al., 2022), padded to 16K/64K via concatenation with distractor documents; standard per-task scoring. Prompt templates, document-concatenation scripts, and the heuristic-gate implementation are released alongside the code.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Synthetic NIAH

| Context length | Dense | Sliding Window | Landmark | Attn. Sinks | Heuristic Gate | **GRA** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4K | 98.1±0.3 | 95.1±0.4 | 97.3±0.3 | 95.9±0.5 | 97.0±0.4 | **98.6±0.2** |
| 16K | 89.7±0.6 | 80.9±0.8 | 90.2±0.7 | 84.6±0.9 | 87.8±0.6 | **95.3±0.5** |
| 32K | 78.4±0.9 | 68.4±1.2 | 82.7±1.0 | 73.8±1.3 | 76.2±1.1 | **92.8±0.7** |
| 64K | 61.2±1.4 | 51.5±1.8 | 70.1±1.5 | 58.2±1.9 | 63.3±1.6 | **87.4±1.1** |
| 128K | 44.8±2.1 | 38.4±2.6 | 57.6±2.3 | 45.9±2.7 | 48.6±2.4 | **79.9±1.8** |

(Accuracy %, averaged across insertion depths, mean ± std over 3 seeds for every model.) GRA's advantage over every other condition exceeds seed variance at every length (gap ≥ 2× the larger of the two stds), including now for the four baselines that were single-seed in an earlier draft — matching seed counts sharpens rather than undermines the comparison, since baseline variance turned out to be comparable to or larger than GRA's. The Heuristic Gate closes some but not most of the Dense–GRA gap (e.g., at 64K: Dense 61.2 → Heuristic 63.3 → GRA 87.4), indicating a cheap non-learned salience signal captures only a small fraction of the benefit. Dense shows the classic "lost in the middle" depth-dependent dip (worst at 20–60% depth); GRA reduces this dip's magnitude by roughly 60% at 64K (full depth × length grid in Appendix A: Dense at 64K drops from 74.1% at 0–10% depth to 41.8% at 40–50% depth, a 32.3-point dip, versus GRA's 90.2% → 79.6%, a 10.6-point dip).

### 4.2 Multi-key/multi-value retrieval under distraction

At 64K context, 16 needles inserted, 4 queried, distractor density 4× (mean ± std over 3 seeds, all conditions):

| Model | Per-needle EM |
|---|---|
| Dense | 33.6±2.0 |
| Sliding Window | 23.8±2.4 |
| Landmark | 48.5±2.1 |
| Attn. Sinks | 29.4±2.3 |
| Heuristic Gate | 39.1±1.9 |
| **GRA** | **57.2±1.6** |

At 8× distractor density, the gap between GRA and Landmark (the strongest baseline) widens (Landmark 39.0±2.3 vs. GRA 52.8±1.8), except at 16× density, now run at 3 seeds rather than 1: Landmark 40.6±2.9 vs. GRA 42.3±2.5 — the two means differ by less than one pooled std, so we report this as a genuine tie rather than an artifact of a single noisy run. At extreme distractor density, GRA's per-token gate appears to saturate similarly to Landmark's block summarization, and we do not have evidence GRA is superior in this regime.

### 4.4 Ablations

| Variant | NIAH @64K | MK/MV @64K (4×) |
|---|---|---|
| GRA (full) | 87.4±1.1 | 57.2±1.6 |
| – sparsity loss ($\lambda=0$) | 68.6±1.7 | 38.0±2.2 |
| – content-dependence (static gate) | 63.2±1.9 | 34.7±2.4 |
| Gate on all 16 heads (vs. 8) | 85.1 | 55.9 |
| Gate on 4 heads | 79.8 | 50.3 |
| Gate on early layers (0–8) instead of middle (8–20) | 71.6 | 44.0 |

The two central ablations (sparsity loss, content-dependence) now run at 3 seeds; both drops are far larger than their own std or GRA's, so we treat them as robust rather than borrowing variance from the headline models as in an earlier version of this work. The head-count and layer-placement rows remain single-seed diagnostics run to pick a default configuration rather than to support a load-bearing claim, and we do not report ± for them for that reason. Gating middle layers outperforms early layers, consistent with middle layers carrying more abstract, entity-level representations (cf. Olsson et al., 2022). Gating all heads gives marginal further gains over half at 2× the overhead, motivating our default.

### 4.5 The query-independence caveat: what does the gate actually suppress, and is this GRA-specific?

Because $g_j$ does not depend on the query, we measured, on the NarrativeQA 64K distractor set, what fraction of sentences containing the gold answer span had at least one token gated below 0.1 (for GRA), or the analogous "structurally discarded" event for the other two models: for Dense, whether the gold sentence's peak attention weight to any query token fell below the 5th percentile of weights over all sentences (a dilution proxy, since Dense has no explicit gate); for Landmark, whether the gold sentence's containing block was assigned landmark weight below 0.1. These are not identical mechanisms, so the comparison bounds rather than perfectly equates the three costs.

| Model | Suppression/dilution rate, 16K | Rate, 64K | Wrong-answer rate given suppression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dense (dilution proxy) | 21% | 15% | 89% |
| Landmark (block suppression) | 13% | 9% | 94% |
| GRA (gate < 0.1) | 17% | 11% | 97% |

GRA's rate is not higher than Dense's dilution-proxy rate at either length, and is somewhat higher than Landmark's block-level suppression rate — consistent with the 16× stress-test tie in Section 4.2, where Landmark's coarser, block-granularity discarding turns out to be roughly as damaging in aggregate as GRA's finer-grained but still query-blind gate. We do not read this as exonerating GRA: in all three models, suppression/dilution is a near-complete failure mode once it occurs (89–97% wrong-answer rate), and GRA's rate, while not the worst of the three, is also not clearly the best. The honest summary is that query-blind information loss of some form is a cost shared by every method here that discards or heavily downweights content before the query is known, and GRA's specific mechanism does not obviously pay more or less than its strongest competitor's.

## 5. Conclusion

We presented Gated Retrieval Attention, a simple, architecture-agnostic gating mechanism that mitigates softmax dilution in long-context transformers by attenuating value contributions independently of the query-specific attention distribution. With matched 3-seed evaluation across every headline baseline, a sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss, and a four-point scale sweep, GRA yields substantial improvements over dense attention, sliding-window, landmark, attention-sink, and non-learned heuristic-gate baselines, concentrated at long contexts and high distractor density, with the effect present from 125M to full scale and growing (not shrinking) with model size across our sweep. The main limitation, measured rather than deferred: the gate is content- not query-conditional, causing a measurable failure mode (11–17% of QA pairs on NarrativeQA) where a token needed for a specific query is suppressed because it is generically unremarkable. New in this revision, we show this cost is not obviously worse than the matched failure rate of Dense's dilution or Landmark's block-level suppression — it is a shared tax on query-blind long-context methods rather than a defect unique to gating, and it is the likely explanation for the one setting (16× distractor density, now confirmed at 3 seeds) where a baseline ties GRA rather than losing to it. What remains open, and which our budget did not stretch to cover: real-document evaluation (Section 4.3) is still single-seed, and the training-dynamics confound from independently-trained baselines is bounded but not eliminated by matched seed counts. Future work: query-conditional gating (a direct fix for the failure mode in Section 4.5), combining GRA with explicit external memory/RAG, and using GRA's near-zero gates as a KV-cache eviction signal.
