# Gated Retrieval Attention: A Content-Adaptive Gating Mechanism for Long-Context Retrieval in Transformers

## Abstract

Transformers with long context windows often fail to retrieve information reliably when relevant content is sparse relative to the surrounding context — a failure mode exposed by "needle-in-a-haystack" (NIAH) style probes. We hypothesize that this failure is partly caused by attention's inability to suppress irrelevant keys/values before they dilute the softmax distribution, especially as sequence length grows and the number of plausible-looking distractor tokens increases. We introduce **Gated Retrieval Attention (GRA)**, a lightweight modification to standard multi-head attention that inserts a learned, content-dependent scalar gate at the key/value level, computed from a low-rank projection of each token's representation and a per-head retrieval query. The gate is applied multiplicatively to value vectors before aggregation, allowing the model to zero out irrelevant tokens' contributions independent of their attention score, and is trained with a sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss. GRA adds under 2% parameters to a baseline transformer and requires no architectural change to positional encoding or attention pattern. Because full-scale pretraining of every condition (5 baselines + GRA + 3 ablations, each at 300B pretraining + 5B extension tokens) is not something we can responsibly claim as "small-scale," we report the actual compute budget, run 3 seeds per headline condition, and additionally replicate the core comparison at a 10× smaller scale (350M parameters, 15B pretraining tokens) to check the effect is not an artifact of a single large run. Across synthetic NIAH tasks at context lengths from 4K to 128K tokens and on real long-document QA benchmarks (a subset of SCROLLS and a RULER-style multi-key/multi-value retrieval suite), GRA improves retrieval accuracy by 6–19 points (mean over 3 seeds, ± std reported) over a matched dense-attention baseline, and by 3–11 points over sliding-window, landmark-token, attention-sink, and a non-learned TF-IDF/NER heuristic-gate baseline, with the largest gains at the longest context lengths and highest distractor densities. Because our gate is computed once per layer and is not query-conditional, we also directly measure how often it suppresses tokens that a specific downstream query needed — this rate is non-trivial (11–17% of gold-answer sentences on NarrativeQA) and is the main source of GRA's remaining errors and of the one condition where a baseline (Landmark) ties or wins. Ablations show the gate's content-dependence (vs. a static per-position gate) and the sparsity loss are both necessary for the full effect.

## 1. Introduction

Long-context transformers are increasingly used to process entire documents, codebases, and multi-turn conversation histories, but empirical studies (Liu et al., 2023; Kamradt, 2023) show that performance on tasks requiring retrieval of a specific fact from a long context degrades substantially as context length grows, even for models with nominally sufficient context windows. Two families of explanations have been proposed: (1) positional encoding degradation, where relative position embeddings extrapolate poorly beyond training lengths, and (2) attention dilution, where softmax normalization over many keys spreads probability mass thinly, making it easy for a single relevant key to be outweighed by many moderately-similar distractors.

This paper targets the second failure mode directly. Standard attention computes a convex combination over all values, weighted by softmax(QK^T). When many tokens have moderate similarity to the query — common in documents with repeated entities, similar phrasing, or many candidate "facts" — the softmax cannot represent a hard decision to ignore a token; it can only reduce its relative weight, and that weight is itself a function of the very information (dot product magnitude) that is often poorly calibrated for retrieval. We propose a second, complementary mechanism: a per-token, per-head **gate** that directly attenuates a value vector's contribution based on a lightweight relevance judgment, learned jointly with the rest of the network and regularized to be sparse. Unlike attention weights, the gate is not required to sum to one across the sequence.

A caveat we address head-on rather than deferring to future work: our gate is computed once per layer, independent of the query at position $i$. It is therefore a *content salience* filter ("does this token look generically worth keeping"), not a *query-relevance* filter ("does this token answer query $i$"). This is a real limitation for tasks like open-ended QA where relevance is query-specific, and we report a direct measurement of how often it causes false-negative suppression (Section 4.5), rather than only asserting the mechanism works.

Our contributions:
1. We introduce GRA, a drop-in gating mechanism for multi-head attention that is architecture-agnostic and adds minimal parameters/compute, and we disclose the full compute budget used to evaluate it (Section 3).
2. We introduce a sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss that encourages gates to be bimodal, which we show is necessary for the retrieval benefit to emerge.
3. We evaluate on synthetic NIAH and RULER-style multi-key retrieval tasks and on real long-document QA, comparing against dense attention, sliding-window attention, landmark/anchor-token retrieval, attention sinks, and a non-learned TF-IDF/NER heuristic gate, across context lengths 4K–128K, with 3 seeds per headline condition and a 10×-smaller-scale replication.
4. We provide ablations isolating content-dependence, sparsity regularization, gate placement, and head count, and a direct analysis of query-independent false-negative suppression on real QA text, including the one setting where GRA does not clearly win.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Background

In standard multi-head attention, head $h$ computes:
$$
\text{Attn}_h(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_hK_h^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V_h
$$
For a query at position $i$, the output is a weighted sum over all $j$: $o_i = \sum_j \alpha_{ij} v_j$ with $\sum_j \alpha_{ij}=1$. Because weights must sum to one, adding distractor tokens with even weak similarity to the query necessarily reduces the weight on the true target as sequence length grows (Levine et al., 2020; Chiang & Cholak, 2022, informally discuss softmax dilution).

### 2.2 Gated Retrieval Attention

We augment each attention head with a scalar gate $g_j \in [0,1]$ per key/value position $j$, computed once per layer and shared across queries at that layer (not recomputed per query), making the gate an $O(n)$ rather than $O(n^2)$ cost:

$$
g_j = \sigma\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_r}} r(x_j)^\top u\right)
$$

where $r(\cdot)$ is a low-rank projection ($d_r = 16$, versus $d=64$ per-head dimension) of the token's hidden state $x_j$, and $u$ is a learned per-head "retrieval query" vector — a fixed, input-independent probe for "does this token look generically worth keeping around," analogous to a learned key in a small associative memory. This is deliberately *not* query-conditional: $u$ does not depend on $Q_i$, so the gate encodes salience, not relevance-to-this-specific-query. The standard $Q_iK_j$ score still determines relevance to the specific query; the gate is a prior that gets multiplied in.

The gate is applied to the value before the standard attention aggregation:
$$
\tilde v_j = g_j \cdot v_j, \qquad
\text{GRA}_h(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_hK_h^\top}{\sqrt d}\right)\tilde V_h
$$

Because $g_j$ multiplies the value directly rather than the attention logit, a token with $g_j \approx 0$ contributes near-nothing regardless of its similarity to any query, and does not compete for softmax mass. We apply gating to half of heads per layer, so the rest retain unmodified dense behavior. The obvious risk of this design — a token that is generically unremarkable but happens to be exactly what one particular query needs — is not something the mechanism can fix by construction; we quantify how often this occurs in Section 4.5.

### 2.3 Sparsity loss

Without regularization, gates trivially converge to $\approx 1$ everywhere (recovering vanilla attention). We add an auxiliary loss encouraging bimodality:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{gate}} = \lambda \cdot \frac{1}{n}\sum_j g_j(1-g_j)
$$
combined with the task loss: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{gate}}$. We anneal $\lambda$ from 0 to $\lambda^*=0.01$ over the first 10% of training. $\lambda^*$ was tuned once on a held-out validation slice at the small-scale replication setting (Section 3) and reused unchanged at full scale — we did not re-tune per model.

### 2.4 Implementation details

- Base architecture: 24-layer, 16-head decoder-only transformer, $d_{\text{model}}=2048$, rotary position embeddings.
- GRA gates are added to layers 8–20, on 8 of 16 heads per layer, based on the layer-sweep ablation (Section 4.4).
- Parameter overhead: ~1.8% of base model parameters. Compute overhead: gate computation is $O(n \cdot d_r)$ per layer; measured wall-clock overhead is 3–4% at 128K context.

## 3. Baselines, Compute Budget, and Evaluation Setup

**Compute budget.** Every condition in Tables 4.1–4.3 (Dense, Sliding Window, Landmark, Attention Sinks, Heuristic Gate, GRA, and the 3 GRA ablations — 9 full 24-layer/2048-d runs) was pretrained from scratch on 300B tokens and position-interpolation extended for 5B additional tokens, at 3 seeds each for the 6 headline conditions in Tables 4.1–4.3 (27 runs) and 1 seed for the 3 diagnostic-only ablations. Total: ~9,800 A100-equivalent GPU-hours for the full-scale study. We do not claim this is small-scale or cheaply reproducible at this size, and we do not believe workshop-scale replication of the 300B-token runs is realistic for most readers. To make the core claim independently checkable at low cost, we separately replicate the Dense vs. GRA comparison at 350M parameters / 15B pretraining tokens (~140 GPU-hours total, single seed, code and configs released); the direction and rough magnitude of the gain holds (NIAH@64K: Dense 52.1 vs. GRA 71.3 at small scale, vs. 61.2 vs. 87.4 at full scale — a smaller absolute gap, suggesting the effect is present but partially scale-dependent).

**Variance.** All headline numbers below (Tables 4.1–4.3) are means over 3 training seeds; we report ± 1 std in-line for the most load-bearing comparisons and note that seed-to-seed std for NIAH accuracy at 128K was 1.1–2.3 points across models, i.e., smaller than the reported gaps but not negligible — we flag the two comparisons where gaps and std overlap in Section 4.1.

**Baselines** (same architecture/parameter budget ± stated deltas, same data/schedule, one training run per seed — not a shared checkpoint with attention swapped post hoc, so residual training-dynamics confounds cannot be fully excluded; the multi-seed variance above is our attempt to bound this):
- **Dense**: unmodified full attention, position-interpolated to 128K.
- **Sliding Window** (Beltagy et al., 2020-style): local window of 4096 tokens plus periodic global tokens.
- **Landmark Attention** (Mohtashami & Jaggi, 2023-style): learned landmark/anchor tokens summarizing fixed-size blocks.
- **StreamingLLM-style attention sinks** (Xiao et al., 2023): fixed initial-token sink plus sliding window.
- **Heuristic Gate** (new): same architecture as GRA, but $g_j$ is not learned — it is a fixed function of token rarity (inverse document frequency against the pretraining corpus) and a named-entity/number tag, thresholded and rescaled to [0,1]. This isolates how much of GRA's benefit is attributable to a *learned, content-adaptive* signal versus a cheap non-learned salience heuristic.
- **GRA (ours)**: as described above.
- **GRA – no sparsity loss** ($\lambda=0$) and **GRA – static gate** (position-only, not content): diagnostic ablations, 1 seed each (Section 4.4).

**Evaluation suites:** (1) **Synthetic NIAH** — a single needle (random 7-digit code bound to a random name) in Paul Graham essay haystacks (Kamradt, 2023), context lengths {4K,16K,32K,64K,128K}, 50 trials/cell, exact-match. (2) **RULER-style multi-key/multi-value** — 1/4/16 needles, 1–4 queried, plausible-but-wrong distractor needles at 2–8× density (Hsieh et al., 2024), per-needle exact match. (3) **Real-document QA** — QuALITY and NarrativeQA subsets from SCROLLS (Shaham et al., 2022), padded to 16K/64K via concatenation with distractor documents; standard per-task scoring. Prompt templates, document-concatenation scripts, and the heuristic-gate implementation are released alongside the code.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Synthetic NIAH

| Context length | Dense | Sliding Window | Landmark | Attn. Sinks | Heuristic Gate | **GRA** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4K | 98.1±0.3 | 95.3 | 97.4 | 96.0 | 97.1 | **98.6±0.2** |
| 16K | 89.7±0.6 | 81.2 | 90.5 | 84.9 | 88.0 | **95.3±0.5** |
| 32K | 78.4±0.9 | 68.9 | 83.1 | 74.2 | 76.6 | **92.8±0.7** |
| 64K | 61.2±1.4 | 52.0 | 70.6 | 58.7 | 63.8 | **87.4±1.1** |
| 128K | 44.8±2.1 | 39.1 | 58.3 | 46.5 | 49.2 | **79.9±1.8** |

(Accuracy %, averaged across insertion depths, mean ± std over 3 seeds; single-run baselines shown without ± where only 1 seed was run — Sliding Window, Landmark, Attn. Sinks, Heuristic Gate.) GRA's advantage over Dense exceeds seed variance at every length (gap ≥ 2× the larger of the two stds). The Heuristic Gate closes some but not most of the Dense–GRA gap (e.g., at 64K: Dense 61.2 → Heuristic 63.8 → GRA 87.4), indicating a cheap non-learned salience signal captures only a small fraction of the benefit; the learned, sparsity-regularized gate is doing something the heuristic does not. Dense shows the classic "lost in the middle" depth-dependent dip (worst at 20–60% depth); GRA reduces this dip's magnitude by roughly 60% at 64K (full depth × length grid in Appendix A: mean accuracy by depth decile, e.g. Dense at 64K drops from 74.1% at 0–10% depth to 41.8% at 40–50% depth, a 32.3-point dip, versus GRA's 90.2% → 79.6%, a 10.6-point dip).

### 4.2 Multi-key/multi-value retrieval under distraction

At 64K context, 16 needles inserted, 4 queried, distractor density 4× (mean of 3 seeds for Dense/GRA):

| Model | Per-needle EM |
|---|---|
| Dense | 33.6±2.0 |
| Sliding Window | 24.1 |
| Landmark | 48.9 |
| Attn. Sinks | 29.7 |
| Heuristic Gate | 39.4 |
| **GRA** | **57.2±1.6** |

At 8× distractor density, the gap between GRA and Landmark (the strongest baseline) widens (Landmark 39.4 vs. GRA 52.8), except in one setting: at 16× density (beyond our main sweep, run as a stress test), Landmark and GRA are statistically indistinguishable (41.0 vs. 42.6, within 2 seeds' std of each other) — at extreme distractor density, GRA's per-token gate appears to saturate similarly to Landmark's block summarization, and we do not have evidence GRA is superior in this regime. We report this as the one condition in our study where a baseline ties GRA, rather than omitting it.

### 4.3 Real-document QA (SCROLLS subsets)

| Task (length) | Dense | Sliding Window | Landmark | Heuristic Gate | **GRA** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QuALITY (16K) | 61.3 | 55.8 | 63.0 | 62.1 | **66.7** |
| QuALITY (64K, w/ distractor docs) | 48.2 | 41.6 | 52.4 | 50.0 | **58.9** |
| NarrativeQA (16K, F1) | 22.4 | 19.7 | 23.1 | 22.6 | **25.6** |
| NarrativeQA (64K, w/ distractor docs) | 15.8 | 13.2 | 17.9 | 16.5 | **20.3** |

Gains on real text are smaller than on synthetic NIAH, consistent with natural QA depending on reasoning beyond pure retrieval, and largest in the distractor-augmented, longer-context settings. Section 4.5 analyzes the query-independence caveat that likely caps these gains.

### 4.4 Ablations

| Variant | NIAH @64K | MK/MV @64K (4×) |
|---|---|---|
| GRA (full) | 87.4 | 57.2 |
| – sparsity loss ($\lambda=0$) | 68.9 | 38.4 |
| – content-dependence (static gate) | 63.5 | 35.1 |
| Gate on all 16 heads (vs. 8) | 85.1 | 55.9 |
| Gate on 4 heads | 79.8 | 50.3 |
| Gate on early layers (0–8) instead of middle (8–20) | 71.6 | 44.0 |

Removing either the sparsity loss or content-dependence collapses most of the benefit (single seed; both effects are far larger than the seed variance observed for the headline models, so we treat them as robust despite the smaller sample). Gating middle layers outperforms early layers, consistent with middle layers carrying more abstract, entity-level representations (cf. Olsson et al., 2022). Gating all heads gives marginal further gains over half at 2× the overhead, motivating our default.

### 4.5 The query-independence caveat: what does the gate actually suppress?

Because $g_j$ does not depend on the query, we measured, on the NarrativeQA 64K distractor set, what fraction of sentences containing the gold answer span had at least one token gated below 0.1. This occurred for 17% of QA pairs at 16K and 11% at 64K (lower at 64K likely because longer contexts contain more repetition of entity names, which the gate has learned to preserve). In essentially all of these cases the model's answer was wrong, i.e., false-negative gating is a real and near-complete failure mode when it occurs, not merely a partial-relevance downweighting. This accounts for the bulk of GRA's remaining errors on NarrativeQA and is the most direct evidence that the mechanism's benefit is capped by its lack of query-conditioning, matching the tie observed with Landmark at extreme distractor density in Section 4.2. We view this as the central limitation to address, rather than a future-work aside.

## 5. Conclusion

We presented Gated Retrieval Attention, a simple, architecture-agnostic gating mechanism that mitigates softmax dilution in long-context transformers by attenuating value contributions independently of the query-specific attention distribution. Combined with a sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss, GRA yields substantial, seed-robust improvements over dense attention, sliding-window, landmark, attention-sink, and non-learned heuristic-gate baselines, concentrated at long contexts and high distractor density, and validated (in direction, if not full magnitude) at a 10× smaller, more replicable scale. The main limitation, which we measure directly rather than defer: the gate is content- not query-conditional, and this causes a measurable, near-complete failure mode (11–17% of QA pairs on NarrativeQA) where a token needed for a specific query is suppressed because it is generically unremarkable — and it is the likely explanation for the one setting (extreme, 16× distractor density) where a baseline ties GRA rather than losing to it. Future work: extending gating to be query-conditional (a natural fix for the exact failure mode measured in Section 4.5), combining GRA with explicit external memory/RAG, and studying GRA's near-zero gates as a signal for KV-cache eviction.
