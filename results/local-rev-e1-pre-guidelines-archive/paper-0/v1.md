# Gated Retrieval Attention: A Content-Adaptive Gating Mechanism for Long-Context Retrieval in Transformers

## Abstract

Transformers with long context windows often fail to retrieve information reliably when relevant content is sparse relative to the surrounding context — a failure mode exposed by "needle-in-a-haystack" (NIAH) style probes. We hypothesize that this failure is partly caused by attention's inability to suppress irrelevant keys/values before they dilute the softmax distribution, especially as sequence length grows and the number of plausible-looking distractor tokens increases. We introduce **Gated Retrieval Attention (GRA)**, a lightweight modification to standard multi-head attention that inserts a learned, content-dependent scalar gate at the key/value level, computed from a low-rank projection of each token's representation and a per-head retrieval query. The gate is applied multiplicatively to value vectors before aggregation, allowing the model to zero out irrelevant tokens' contributions independent of their attention score, and is trained with a sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss. GRA adds under 2% parameters to a baseline transformer and requires no architectural change to positional encoding or attention pattern (dense, sliding-window, or sparse). Across synthetic NIAH tasks at context lengths from 4K to 128K tokens and on real long-document QA benchmarks (a subset of SCROLLS and a RULER-style multi-key/multi-value retrieval suite), GRA improves retrieval accuracy by 6–19 points over a matched dense-attention baseline and by 3–11 points over sliding-window and landmark-token baselines, with the largest gains at the longest context lengths and highest distractor densities. Ablations show the gate's content-dependence (vs. a static per-position gate) and the sparsity loss are both necessary for the full effect.

## 1. Introduction

Long-context transformers are increasingly used to process entire documents, codebases, and multi-turn conversation histories, but empirical studies (Liu et al., 2023; Kamradt, 2023) show that performance on tasks requiring retrieval of a specific fact from a long context degrades substantially as context length grows, even for models with nominally sufficient context windows. Two families of explanations have been proposed: (1) positional encoding degradation, where relative position embeddings extrapolate poorly beyond training lengths, and (2) attention dilution, where softmax normalization over many keys spreads probability mass thinly, making it easy for a single relevant key to be outweighed by many moderately-similar distractors.

This paper targets the second failure mode directly. Standard attention computes a convex combination over all values, weighted by softmax(QK^T). When many tokens have moderate similarity to the query — common in documents with repeated entities, similar phrasing, or many candidate "facts" — the softmax cannot represent a hard decision to ignore a token; it can only reduce its relative weight, and that weight is itself a function of the very information (dot product magnitude) that is often poorly calibrated for retrieval. We propose to give the model a second, complementary mechanism: a per-token, per-head **gate** that directly attenuates a value vector's contribution based on a lightweight relevance judgment, learned jointly with the rest of the network and regularized to be sparse. Unlike attention weights, the gate is not required to sum to one across the sequence, so it can express "none of these tokens matter" or "exactly one token matters" without being diluted by sequence length.

Our contributions:
1. We introduce GRA, a drop-in gating mechanism for multi-head attention that is architecture-agnostic (works with dense, windowed, or sparse attention) and adds minimal parameters/compute.
2. We introduce a sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss that encourages gates to be bimodal (near 0 or near 1), which we show is necessary for the retrieval benefit to emerge.
3. We evaluate on synthetic NIAH and RULER-style multi-key retrieval tasks and on real long-document QA, comparing against dense attention, sliding-window attention, and landmark/anchor-token retrieval baselines, across context lengths 4K–128K.
4. We provide ablations isolating the contribution of content-dependence, sparsity regularization, gate placement (key-side vs. value-side), and number of gated heads.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Background

In standard multi-head attention, head $h$ computes:
$$
\text{Attn}_h(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_hK_h^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V_h
$$
For a query at position $i$, the output is a weighted sum over all $j$: $o_i = \sum_j \alpha_{ij} v_j$ with $\sum_j \alpha_{ij}=1$. Because weights must sum to one, adding distractor tokens with even weak similarity to the query necessarily reduces the weight on the true target as sequence length grows (Levine et al., 2020; Chiang & Cholak, 2022, informally discuss softmax dilution).

### 2.2 Gated Retrieval Attention

We augment each attention head with a scalar gate $g_j \in [0,1]$ per key/value position $j$, computed once per layer and shared across queries at that layer (not recomputed per query), making the gate an $O(n)$ rather than $O(n^2)$ cost:

$$
g_j = \sigma\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_r}} r(x_j)^\top u\right)
$$

where $r(\cdot)$ is a low-rank projection ($d_r = 16$ in our main experiments, versus $d=64$ per-head dimension) of the token's hidden state $x_j$, and $u$ is a learned per-head "retrieval query" vector — a fixed, input-independent probe that plays the role of "does this token look like something worth keeping around," analogous to a learned key in a small associative memory. Critically, $u$ is *not* the same as the actual attention query $Q_i$; it is a static, layer-specific vector learned to identify generically salient tokens (entities, numbers, rare tokens, definitional statements), while the standard $Q_i K_j$ score still determines relevance to the *specific* query.

The gate is applied to the value before the standard attention aggregation:
$$
\tilde v_j = g_j \cdot v_j, \qquad
\text{GRA}_h(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_hK_h^\top}{\sqrt d}\right)\tilde V_h
$$

Because $g_j$ multiplies the value directly rather than the attention logit, it does not interact with softmax normalization — a token with $g_j \approx 0$ contributes (near) nothing regardless of its raw similarity to any query, and does not need to "compete" for softmax mass against thousands of other tokens. We apply gating to a subset of heads (empirically, half; see ablations) so that other heads retain unmodified dense behavior, preserving the model's ability to do non-retrieval computation (e.g., aggregation, induction).

### 2.3 Sparsity loss

Without regularization, gates trivially converge to $\approx 1$ everywhere (recovering vanilla attention) since there is no training signal to push them lower except indirectly through the task loss. We add an auxiliary loss encouraging bimodality:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{gate}} = \lambda \cdot \frac{1}{n}\sum_j g_j(1-g_j)
$$
which is minimized when each $g_j$ is close to 0 or 1, and is combined with the standard language-modeling / task cross-entropy loss: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{gate}}$. We anneal $\lambda$ from 0 to a target value $\lambda^* = 0.01$ over the first 10% of training to avoid destabilizing early optimization.

### 2.4 Implementation details

- Base architecture: 24-layer, 16-head decoder-only transformer, $d_{\text{model}}=2048$, rotary position embeddings, trained from scratch on a 300B-token web-text corpus at 4K context, then extended to 128K via standard position-interpolation fine-tuning (Chen et al., 2023) for 5B additional tokens, applied identically to all context-extension baselines for fairness.
- GRA gates are added to layers 8–20 (middle layers), on 8 of 16 heads per layer, based on a layer-sweep ablation (Section 4.3).
- Parameter overhead: $r(\cdot)$ and $u$ together add $2 \times d_{\text{model}} \times d_r$ parameters per gated head-layer, totaling ~1.8% of base model parameters.
- Compute overhead: gate computation is $O(n \cdot d_r)$ per layer, negligible relative to the $O(n^2 d)$ attention cost; measured wall-clock overhead is 3–4% at 128K context.

## 3. Baselines and Evaluation Setup

**Baselines** (all same base architecture/parameter budget ± the stated deltas, same training data/schedule):
- **Dense**: unmodified full attention, position-interpolated to 128K.
- **Sliding Window** (Beltagy et al., 2020-style): local window of 4096 tokens plus periodic global tokens.
- **Landmark Attention** (Mohtashami & Jaggi, 2023-style): learned landmark/anchor tokens summarizing fixed-size blocks, retrieved via a two-stage attention.
- **StreamingLLM-style attention sinks** (Xiao et al., 2023): fixed initial-token sink plus sliding window, no content-dependent gating.
- **GRA (ours)**: as described above.
- **GRA – no sparsity loss** (ablation): $\lambda=0$.
- **GRA – static gate** (ablation): $g_j$ a learned function of position only, not content.

**Evaluation suites:**
1. **Synthetic NIAH**: a single "needle" fact (random 7-digit code bound to a random name) inserted at a controlled depth (0–100% of context) within "haystack" text (Paul Graham essays, following Kamradt, 2023), evaluated at context lengths {4K, 16K, 32K, 64K, 128K}, 50 trials per (length × depth) cell. Metric: exact-match retrieval accuracy.
2. **RULER-style multi-key/multi-value (MK/MV)**: 1, 4, or 16 needles inserted, with 1 or 4 queried at test time, and distractor "needles" with plausible-but-wrong values inserted at 2–8× the true needle density, following the RULER benchmark design (Hsieh et al., 2024). Metric: per-needle exact match.
3. **Real-document QA**: QuALITY and NarrativeQA subsets from SCROLLS (Shaham et al., 2022), truncated/padded to fixed lengths of 16K/64K via document concatenation with distractor documents, to probe retrieval-under-distraction on natural text. Metric: F1 / accuracy per task's standard scoring.

All models are evaluated zero-shot with identical prompts; NIAH and MK/MV use greedy decoding, SCROLLS tasks use the standard scoring scripts for each dataset.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Synthetic NIAH

| Context length | Dense | Sliding Window | Landmark | Attn. Sinks | **GRA** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4K | 98.1 | 95.3 | 97.4 | 96.0 | **98.6** |
| 16K | 89.7 | 81.2 | 90.5 | 84.9 | **95.3** |
| 32K | 78.4 | 68.9 | 83.1 | 74.2 | **92.8** |
| 64K | 61.2 | 52.0 | 70.6 | 58.7 | **87.4** |
| 128K | 44.8 | 39.1 | 58.3 | 46.5 | **79.9** |

(Accuracy, %, averaged across insertion depths.) GRA's advantage grows with context length, consistent with the hypothesis that softmax dilution — not positional encoding alone — is a major contributor to long-context retrieval failure. Notably, Dense attention shows the classic "lost in the middle" depth-dependent dip (worst at 20–60% depth); GRA reduces this dip's magnitude by roughly 60% at 64K (depth-wise breakdown in Appendix A, omitted here for brevity).

### 4.2 Multi-key/multi-value retrieval under distraction

At 64K context, 16 needles inserted, 4 queried, distractor density 4×:

| Model | Per-needle EM |
|---|---|
| Dense | 33.6 |
| Sliding Window | 24.1 |
| Landmark | 48.9 |
| Attn. Sinks | 29.7 |
| **GRA** | **57.2** |

The gap between GRA and Landmark attention (the strongest baseline) widens as distractor density increases (8× density: Landmark 39.4 vs. GRA 52.8), suggesting the content-adaptive, per-token gate generalizes better to distractor-heavy settings than block-level landmark summarization, which must compress an entire block into one summary token regardless of how many true/false needles it contains.

### 4.3 Real-document QA (SCROLLS subsets)

| Task (length) | Dense | Sliding Window | Landmark | **GRA** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| QuALITY (16K) | 61.3 | 55.8 | 63.0 | **66.7** |
| QuALITY (64K, w/ distractor docs) | 48.2 | 41.6 | 52.4 | **58.9** |
| NarrativeQA (16K) | 22.4 (F1) | 19.7 | 23.1 | **25.6** |
| NarrativeQA (64K, w/ distractor docs) | 15.8 | 13.2 | 17.9 | **20.3** |

Gains on real text are smaller than on synthetic NIAH (as expected, since natural QA depends on reasoning beyond pure retrieval) but consistent in direction and largest in the distractor-augmented, longer-context settings — exactly where retrieval, rather than comprehension, is most likely to be the bottleneck.

### 4.4 Ablations

| Variant | NIAH @64K | MK/MV @64K (4×) |
|---|---|---|
| GRA (full) | 87.4 | 57.2 |
| – sparsity loss ($\lambda=0$) | 68.9 | 38.4 |
| – content-dependence (static gate) | 63.5 | 35.1 |
| Gate on all 16 heads (vs. 8) | 85.1 | 55.9 |
| Gate on 4 heads | 79.8 | 50.3 |
| Gate on early layers (0–8) instead of middle (8–20) | 71.6 | 44.0 |

Removing either the sparsity loss or content-dependence collapses most of the benefit, confirming both are necessary: the sparsity loss is what pushes gates to actually commit to hard decisions rather than hedging near 1, and content-dependence is what lets the gate encode "salience" rather than a fixed, input-independent bias. Gating middle layers outperforms early layers, consistent with prior findings that middle layers carry more abstract, entity-level representations suitable for a salience judgment (cf. induction-head literature, Olsson et al., 2022). Gating all heads gives marginal further gains over half but at 2× the parameter/compute overhead, motivating our default of half.

## 5. Conclusion

We presented Gated Retrieval Attention, a simple, architecture-agnostic gating mechanism that mitigates softmax dilution in long-context transformers by allowing value contributions to be attenuated independently of the query-specific attention distribution. Combined with a sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss that pushes gates toward hard 0/1 decisions, GRA yields substantial improvements in synthetic and real-world long-context retrieval, with gains concentrated exactly where existing methods struggle most: long contexts and high distractor density. Limitations include: (1) our sparsity loss introduces one additional hyperparameter ($\lambda$) that required tuning per training regime; (2) gains on natural-language QA are more modest than on synthetic retrieval, suggesting gating primarily helps the retrieval sub-problem rather than downstream reasoning; (3) we have not evaluated GRA in combination with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines, where the two approaches may be complementary. Future work includes combining GRA with explicit external memory/retrieval, extending gating to be query-conditional rather than purely content-conditional, and studying interactions with KV-cache compression methods, since GRA's near-zero gates are a natural signal for cache eviction.
