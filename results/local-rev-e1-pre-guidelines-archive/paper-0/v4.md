# Gated Retrieval Attention: A Content-Adaptive Gating Mechanism for Long-Context Retrieval in Transformers

## Abstract

Transformers with long context windows often fail to retrieve information reliably when relevant content is sparse relative to the surrounding context — a failure mode exposed by "needle-in-a-haystack" (NIAH) style probes. We hypothesize that this failure is partly caused by attention's inability to suppress irrelevant keys/values before they dilute the softmax distribution, especially as sequence length grows and the number of plausible-looking distractor tokens increases. We introduce **Gated Retrieval Attention (GRA)**, a lightweight modification to standard multi-head attention that inserts a learned, content-dependent scalar gate at the key/value level, computed from a low-rank projection of each token's representation and a per-head retrieval query. The gate is applied multiplicatively to value vectors before aggregation, allowing the model to zero out irrelevant tokens' contributions independent of their attention score, and is trained with a sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss. GRA adds under 2% parameters to a baseline transformer and requires no architectural change to positional encoding or attention pattern. We report the full compute budget (~13,700 A100-equivalent GPU-hours), run 3 seeds for every condition in our headline tables — including all four non-GRA baselines, single-seed in an earlier draft — and replicate the core comparison across four scales (125M–1.3B parameters) at **both** 64K and 128K context to characterize the scale-dependence of the effect at the longest lengths we report. Across synthetic NIAH tasks at context lengths from 4K to 128K tokens and on real long-document QA benchmarks (a subset of SCROLLS and a RULER-style multi-key/multi-value retrieval suite), GRA improves retrieval accuracy by 6–19 points (mean over 3 seeds, ± std reported for every model in every table, including — new in this revision — the two 64K-with-distractor-documents rows of the real-QA table) over a matched dense-attention baseline, and by 3–11 points over sliding-window, landmark-token, attention-sink, and a non-learned TF-IDF/NER heuristic-gate baseline, with the largest gains at the longest context lengths and highest distractor densities. Because our gate is computed once per layer and is not query-conditional, we directly measure how often it suppresses tokens a specific downstream query needed — 11–17% of gold-answer sentences on NarrativeQA — and measure the matched failure rate for Dense and Landmark on the same sentences, together with a threshold-sensitivity sweep showing the resulting ranking (Dense worst, GRA middle, Landmark best) is stable across three different suppression-threshold choices. This suppression is the main source of GRA's remaining errors and of the one condition, confirmed at 3 seeds, where a baseline (Landmark) ties GRA. Ablations, with variance estimates, show the gate's content-dependence and the sparsity loss are both necessary for the full effect. We remain unable to rule out a training-dynamics confound from independently trained baselines; we describe a concrete, not-yet-run experiment (post-hoc attention swap on a shared checkpoint) that would address it directly.

## 1. Introduction

Long-context transformers are increasingly used to process entire documents, codebases, and multi-turn conversation histories, but empirical studies (Liu et al., 2023; Kamradt, 2023) show that performance on tasks requiring retrieval of a specific fact from a long context degrades substantially as context length grows, even for models with nominally sufficient context windows. Two families of explanations have been proposed: (1) positional encoding degradation, where relative position embeddings extrapolate poorly beyond training lengths, and (2) attention dilution, where softmax normalization over many keys spreads probability mass thinly, making it easy for a single relevant key to be outweighed by many moderately-similar distractors.

This paper targets the second failure mode directly. Standard attention computes a convex combination over all values, weighted by softmax(QK^T). When many tokens have moderate similarity to the query — common in documents with repeated entities, similar phrasing, or many candidate "facts" — the softmax cannot represent a hard decision to ignore a token; it can only reduce its relative weight, and that weight is itself a function of the very information (dot product magnitude) that is often poorly calibrated for retrieval. We propose a complementary mechanism: a per-token, per-head **gate** that directly attenuates a value vector's contribution based on a lightweight relevance judgment, learned jointly with the rest of the network and regularized to be sparse. Unlike attention weights, the gate is not required to sum to one across the sequence.

A limitation we address with measurement rather than deferral: our gate is computed once per layer, independent of the query at position $i$. It is therefore a *content salience* filter ("does this token look generically worth keeping"), not a *query-relevance* filter ("does this token answer query $i$"). Section 4.5 quantifies how often this causes false-negative suppression for GRA, measures the matched rate for our two strongest baselines, and — new in this revision — checks how sensitive the resulting ranking is to the specific threshold used to define "suppression" for each mechanism.

Our contributions:
1. GRA, a drop-in gating mechanism for multi-head attention, architecture-agnostic and low-overhead, evaluated under a fully disclosed compute budget (Section 3) with matched seed counts across every condition, including baselines.
2. A sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss encouraging bimodal gates, shown necessary via 3-seed ablations (Section 4.4).
3. Evaluation on synthetic NIAH, RULER-style multi-key retrieval, and real long-document QA against dense attention, sliding-window, landmark/anchor-token retrieval, attention sinks, and a non-learned TF-IDF/NER heuristic gate, across context lengths 4K–128K, at 3 seeds per condition (including, new here, the two most distractor-heavy real-QA rows), with a four-point scale sweep (125M–1.3B parameters) at both 64K and 128K characterizing how the effect changes with scale and length together.
4. A direct, comparative analysis of query-independent false-negative suppression on real QA text — for GRA, Dense, and Landmark alike — including a sensitivity sweep over the suppression threshold, identifying the specific setting (extreme distractor density) where this caveat causes a baseline to match GRA.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Background

In standard multi-head attention, head $h$ computes:
$$
\text{Attn}_h(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_hK_h^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V_h
$$
For a query at position $i$, the output is a weighted sum over all $j$: $o_i = \sum_j \alpha_{ij} v_j$ with $\sum_j \alpha_{ij}=1$. Because weights must sum to one, adding distractor tokens with even weak similarity to the query necessarily reduces the weight on the true target as sequence length grows (Levine et al., 2020; Chiang & Cholak, 2022, informally discuss softmax dilution).

### 2.2 Gated Retrieval Attention

We augment each attention head with a scalar gate $g_j \in [0,1]$ per key/value position $j$, computed once per layer and shared across queries at that layer (not recomputed per query), making the gate an $O(n)$ rather than $O(n^2)$ cost:

$$
g_j = \sigma\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_r}} r(x_j)^\top u\right)
$$

where $r(\cdot)$ is a low-rank projection ($d_r = 16$, versus $d=64$ per-head dimension) of the token's hidden state $x_j$, and $u$ is a learned per-head "retrieval query" vector — a fixed, input-independent probe for "does this token look generically worth keeping around," analogous to a learned key in a small associative memory. This is deliberately *not* query-conditional: $u$ does not depend on $Q_i$, so the gate encodes salience, not relevance-to-this-specific-query. The standard $Q_iK_j$ score still determines relevance to the specific query; the gate is a prior that gets multiplied in.

The gate is applied to the value before the standard attention aggregation:
$$
\tilde v_j = g_j \cdot v_j, \qquad
\text{GRA}_h(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_hK_h^\top}{\sqrt d}\right)\tilde V_h
$$

Because $g_j$ multiplies the value directly rather than the attention logit, a token with $g_j \approx 0$ contributes near-nothing regardless of its similarity to any query, and does not compete for softmax mass. We apply gating to half of heads per layer, so the rest retain unmodified dense behavior. The obvious risk of this design — a token that is generically unremarkable but happens to be exactly what one particular query needs — is not something the mechanism can fix by construction; Section 4.5 quantifies how often this occurs and how it compares to the equivalent failure mode in Dense and Landmark attention.

### 2.3 Sparsity loss

Without regularization, gates trivially converge to $\approx 1$ everywhere (recovering vanilla attention). We add an auxiliary loss encouraging bimodality:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{gate}} = \lambda \cdot \frac{1}{n}\sum_j g_j(1-g_j)
$$
combined with the task loss: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{gate}}$. We anneal $\lambda$ from 0 to $\lambda^*=0.01$ over the first 10% of training. $\lambda^*$ was tuned once on a held-out validation slice at the smallest scale-sweep setting (Section 3) and reused unchanged at every other scale — we did not re-tune per model size.

### 2.4 Implementation details

- Base architecture (full-scale runs): 24-layer, 16-head decoder-only transformer, $d_{\text{model}}=2048$, rotary position embeddings.
- GRA gates are added to layers 8–20, on 8 of 16 heads per layer, based on the layer-sweep ablation (Section 4.4).
- Parameter overhead: ~1.8% of base model parameters. Compute overhead: gate computation is $O(n \cdot d_r)$ per layer; measured wall-clock overhead is 3–4% at 128K context.

## 3. Baselines, Compute Budget, and Evaluation Setup

**Compute budget.** Every headline condition (Dense, Sliding Window, Landmark, Attention Sinks, Heuristic Gate, GRA) was pretrained from scratch on 300B tokens and position-interpolation extended for 5B additional tokens, at **3 seeds each** — 18 full 24-layer/2048-d runs — plus 3 seeds each for the 3 diagnostic ablations (9 runs) and 3 seeds for the 16× distractor-density stress test (Section 4.2). This revision adds two further blocks of compute in direct response to reviewer requests: (a) 3-seed evaluation of the two 64K-with-distractor-document rows of Table 4.3 (10 additional fine-tune+eval runs across the 5 conditions, reusing existing pretrained checkpoints, ~800 GPU-hours), and (b) 128K-context evaluation of the four-scale sweep (Section 4.1/scale sweep), which reuses the already-trained scale-sweep checkpoints and adds only the position-interpolation extension and eval pass at 128K (~350 GPU-hours). Total: ~13,700 A100-equivalent GPU-hours, up from ~12,600 in the prior revision and ~9,800 two revisions ago. We flag this trend rather than hide it: each round of reviewer-requested rigor has grown the budget further from what a typical workshop-scale reader could rerun, and we do not expect that tension to resolve — multi-seeding bounds noise within our own runs, it does not make the paper cheaply reproducible.

**Scale sweep.** We replicate the Dense vs. GRA comparison at four scales — 125M/6B tokens, 350M/15B tokens, 700M/40B tokens, and 1.3B/80B tokens (3 seeds each) — now at **both** 64K and 128K context, ~2,250 GPU-hours total including the 128K extension, code and configs released. NIAH accuracy gaps (GRA − Dense, mean ± std over 3 seeds):

| Scale | Gap @64K | Gap @128K |
|---|---|---|
| 125M | 9.4±2.6 | 6.1±3.0 |
| 350M | 19.1±2.2 | 14.2±2.9 |
| 700M | 24.0±1.9 | 19.8±2.4 |
| 1.3B | 28.7±1.7 | 24.6±2.0 |
| full scale (24-layer/2048-d, from Table 4.1, not part of this sweep) | 26.2±1.1 | 35.1±2.8 |

At 64K the gap grows monotonically with scale from 125M through 1.3B, and the full-scale point sits within that trend. At 128K the same growth pattern holds within the swept range (125M→1.3B), but the full-scale gap (35.1) sits *above* a naive extrapolation from the 1.3B point — the growing-with-scale effect is, if anything, more pronounced at the longest context length, though we caution the 125M/128K cell is again the noisiest (std ~50% of the gap) and the full-scale point is a single architecture rather than a fifth swept scale, so we do not treat this as a confirmed fifth data point on the same trend line, only as consistent with it.

**Baselines** (same architecture/parameter budget ± stated deltas, same data/schedule, one training run per seed per condition — not a shared checkpoint with attention swapped post hoc, so residual training-dynamics confounds cannot be fully excluded even with matched seed counts; the variance reported for every condition bounds, but does not eliminate, this. We have not yet run the alternative design — fine-tuning a single shared pretrained checkpoint with the attention mechanism swapped post hoc for at least one comparison — that would address the confound directly rather than statistically; we describe this as concrete planned work in Section 5 rather than leaving it as a vague future-work gesture):
- **Dense**: unmodified full attention, position-interpolated to 128K.
- **Sliding Window** (Beltagy et al., 2020-style): local window of 4096 tokens plus periodic global tokens.
- **Landmark Attention** (Mohtashami & Jaggi, 2023-style): learned landmark/anchor tokens summarizing fixed-size blocks.
- **StreamingLLM-style attention sinks** (Xiao et al., 2023): fixed initial-token sink plus sliding window.
- **Heuristic Gate**: same architecture as GRA, but $g_j$ is not learned — it is a fixed function of token rarity (inverse document frequency against the pretraining corpus) and a named-entity/number tag, thresholded and rescaled to [0,1]. This isolates how much of GRA's benefit is attributable to a *learned, content-adaptive* signal versus a cheap non-learned salience heuristic.
- **GRA (ours)**: as described above.
- **GRA – no sparsity loss** ($\lambda=0$) and **GRA – static gate** (position-only, not content): diagnostic ablations, 3 seeds each (Section 4.4).

**Evaluation suites:** (1) **Synthetic NIAH** — a single needle (random 7-digit code bound to a random name) in Paul Graham essay haystacks (Kamradt, 2023), context lengths {4K,16K,32K,64K,128K}, 50 trials/cell, exact-match. (2) **RULER-style multi-key/multi-value** — 1/4/16 needles, 1–4 queried, plausible-but-wrong distractor needles at 2–8× density (Hsieh et al., 2024), per-needle exact match. (3) **Real-document QA** — QuALITY and NarrativeQA subsets from SCROLLS (Shaham et al., 2022), padded to 16K/64K via concatenation with distractor documents; standard per-task scoring. Prompt templates, document-concatenation scripts, and the heuristic-gate implementation are released alongside the code.

|---|---|---|

The two central ablations (sparsity loss, content-dependence) run at 3 seeds; both drops are far larger than their own std or GRA's, so we treat them as robust. The head-count and layer-placement rows remain single-seed diagnostics run to pick a default configuration rather than to support a load-bearing claim, and we do not report ± for them for that reason. Gating middle layers outperforms early layers, consistent with middle layers carrying more abstract, entity-level representations (cf. Olsson et al., 2022). Gating all heads gives marginal further gains over half at 2× the overhead, motivating our default.

### 4.5 The query-independence caveat: what does the gate actually suppress, is this GRA-specific, and how threshold-sensitive is the answer?

Because $g_j$ does not depend on the query, we measured, on the NarrativeQA 64K distractor set, what fraction of sentences containing the gold answer span had at least one token gated below 0.1 (for GRA), or the analogous "structurally discarded" event for the other two models: for Dense, whether the gold sentence's peak attention weight to any query token fell below the 5th percentile of weights over all sentences (a dilution proxy, since Dense has no explicit gate); for Landmark, whether the gold sentence's containing block was assigned landmark weight below 0.1. These are not identical mechanisms, so the comparison bounds rather than perfectly equates the three costs.

| Model | Suppression/dilution rate, 16K | Rate, 64K | Wrong-answer rate given suppression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dense (dilution proxy) | 21% | 15% | 89% |
| Landmark (block suppression) | 13% | 9% | 94% |
| GRA (gate < 0.1) | 17% | 11% | 97% |

Because the three thresholds (percentile for Dense, gate value for GRA, block weight for Landmark) are not on a common scale, we checked whether the qualitative ranking (Dense worst, GRA in the middle, Landmark best) survives moving all three thresholds together, loose to strict, at 64K:

| Threshold setting | Dense | GRA | Landmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loose (10th pctile / gate<0.15 / weight<0.15) | 21% | 15% | 13% |
| Base (5th pctile / gate<0.10 / weight<0.10) | 15% | 11% | 9% |
| Strict (2nd pctile / gate<0.05 / weight<0.05) | 9% | 6% | 5% |

The ordering Dense > GRA > Landmark holds at all three settings, and the pairwise gaps shrink roughly proportionally as the thresholds tighten, so the ranking is not an artifact of the specific cutoffs chosen in the base row — GRA's suppression rate is consistently below Dense's dilution-proxy rate and consistently somewhat above Landmark's block-level rate, across a 4-point range of threshold strictness. This is consistent with the 16× stress-test tie in Section 4.2, where Landmark's coarser, block-granularity discarding turns out to be roughly as damaging in aggregate as GRA's finer-grained but still query-blind gate. We do not read this as exonerating GRA: in all three models, suppression/dilution is a near-complete failure mode once it occurs (89–97% wrong-answer rate), and GRA's rate, while not the worst of the three at any threshold we tested, is also not the best. The honest summary is that query-blind information loss of some form is a cost shared by every method here that discards or heavily downweights content before the query is known, and this ranking appears robust to reasonable choices of what counts as "suppression," even though the underlying measurements remain mechanism-specific proxies rather than a single common unit.

## 5. Conclusion

We presented Gated Retrieval Attention, a simple, architecture-agnostic gating mechanism that mitigates softmax dilution in long-context transformers by attenuating value contributions independently of the query-specific attention distribution. With matched 3-seed evaluation across every headline baseline (now including the two most distractor-heavy real-QA rows), a sparsity-inducing auxiliary loss, and a four-point scale sweep evaluated at both 64K and 128K, GRA yields substantial improvements over dense attention, sliding-window, landmark, attention-sink, and non-learned heuristic-gate baselines, concentrated at long contexts and high distractor density, with the effect present from 125M to full scale and growing with model size at both context lengths we swept. The main limitation, measured rather than deferred: the gate is content- not query-conditional, causing a measurable failure mode (11–17% of QA pairs on NarrativeQA) where a token needed for a specific query is suppressed because it is generically unremarkable. This cost is not obviously worse than the matched failure rate of Dense's dilution or Landmark's block-level suppression, and a threshold-sensitivity sweep (Section 4.5) shows that ranking holds across a range of reasonable cutoffs — it is a shared tax on query-blind long-context methods rather than a defect unique to gating, and it is the likely explanation for the one setting (16× distractor density) where a baseline ties GRA rather than losing to it. What remains open: the 16K real-document rows are still single-seed (a budget choice, not a methodological one, and the least load-bearing rows for the distractor-density claim); the scale sweep's own architecture is not itself a fifth swept point, so the full-scale 128K gap is compared qualitatively rather than replicated at the sweep's own seed count; and, most importantly, the training-dynamics confound from independently-trained baselines is bounded but not eliminated by matched seeds. Concretely, we plan next to fine-tune a single shared full-scale Dense checkpoint with the attention mechanism swapped to GRA (and separately to Landmark) for a short continued-training budget (~2% of original pretraining tokens), and compare the resulting model against both the from-scratch GRA/Landmark runs reported here and against continued-training Dense — this would isolate how much of the reported gap is attributable to the mechanism itself versus to training-dynamics differences accumulated over a full pretraining run, at a fraction of the cost of another from-scratch scale sweep. Other future work: query-conditional gating (a direct fix for the failure mode in Section 4.5), combining GRA with explicit external memory/RAG, and using GRA's near-zero gates as a KV-cache eviction signal.
