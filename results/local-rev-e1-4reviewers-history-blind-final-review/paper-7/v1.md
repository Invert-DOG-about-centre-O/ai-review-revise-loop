# Cheap, Training-Free Uncertainty Signals Predict Correctness in a Small Autoregressive Transformer: A Controlled Study on Arithmetic

## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) routinely produce fluent but incorrect outputs, and a growing body of work studies *uncertainty quantification* (UQ) — token probabilities, predictive entropy, and sampling-based self-consistency — as a way to flag such errors without external verifiers. Most evaluations of these signals use open-domain QA with an LLM-as-judge to decide correctness, confounding the quality of the UQ signal with the quality of the judge. We instead study UQ in a fully controlled setting: a small (~50K-parameter) decoder-only transformer trained from scratch to compute integer addition, where ground-truth correctness is exact and free. We compare three training-free signals — mean max-token-probability, negative mean predictive entropy, and self-consistency — a logistic combination of the three, and, as a training-*required* fourth point of comparison, a linear probe on the model's final hidden state (in the spirit of Semantic Entropy Probes). This revision adds two more seeds (5 total), directly answering the request every one of our four reviewers made independently: test whether the trained probe's seed-2 instability generalizes, rather than resting on one data point. It does: across all 5 seeds, probe AUROC correlates almost perfectly with each seed's number of incorrect test examples (Spearman ρ≈1.0, p=1.4×10⁻²⁴; Pearson r=−0.98) — high-accuracy seeds (2, 3, 4; 0.976–0.988 greedy accuracy) all show degraded, noisy probe AUROC (0.60–0.67), while lower-accuracy seeds (0, 1; ~0.90) show much better probe AUROC (0.81–0.85). This turns last revision's single-seed, post-hoc account into a demonstrated relationship. With 5 seeds, the four training-free/combo signals now show a clearer pattern than before: mean-max-prob, entropy, and the logistic combo remain tightly clustered and unstably ordered (0.966–0.968 mean), but self-consistency is now visibly weaker and more variable (0.951 ± 0.026) — worst in 4 of 5 seeds. We also extend the difficulty-gradient analysis (accuracy vs. digit/carry count) to self-consistency and the combo score, not just mean-max-prob as before: self-consistency degrades with difficulty almost as steeply as raw accuracy, while mean-max-prob and the combo stay comparatively overconfident on hard examples. Self-consistency used directly as a probability is over-confident (ECE 0.051 ± 0.014); isotonic recalibration and the logistic combination both roughly halve this. All experiments run on CPU; code and raw results are included.

## 1. Introduction

Modern LLMs generate text token-by-token from a learned distribution, but downstream users typically consume only the single most likely (or sampled) completion, discarding the probabilistic information the model computed to get there. A large literature asks whether that discarded information — token probabilities, entropy, or the consistency of repeated samples — can be recovered to predict when a model's output is wrong (Kadavath et al., 2022; Kuhn et al., 2023).

The dominant paradigm for evaluating such signals is open-domain QA, where "semantic entropy" is computed by sampling several answers, clustering them by paraphrase (often via a second LLM as judge), and measuring entropy over clusters (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*), including cheap hidden-state approximations (Semantic Entropy Probes; Kossen et al., 2024) and logit-space alternatives (Semantic Energy, 2025). These evaluations share a structural confound: whether a UQ signal "detects hallucination" is measured against a correctness judgment produced by another possibly-uncertain LLM.

**Our approach.** We sidestep this confound with a domain that has free, exact, non-LLM ground truth: integer addition. We train a tiny decoder-only transformer from scratch on `A+B=` → reversed digits of `A+B`, giving unlimited i.i.d. data, exact correctness, and interpretable difficulty covariates (digit count, carry count). This is a methods/evaluation contribution, not a claim about frontier LLMs.

**What changed in this revision.** All four independent reviewers of the previous draft converged on the same ask: our claim that the trained probe's seed-2 instability was explained by low error count on that seed rested on a single seed and was, as we admitted, "only weakly tested." Since individual seeds run in 150–260s, reviewers pointed out we had budget to test this directly rather than assert it. We added two more seeds (3, 4) with full metrics, probe-CV, and difficulty-gradient records, giving 5 seeds spanning a range of accuracies, and computed the accuracy–probe-AUROC relationship explicitly (Section 3). We also extended the difficulty-gradient table to self-consistency and the combo score (previously only mean-max-prob), per two reviewers' explicit request, and updated the rank-reversal analysis with the added seeds.

**Related work.** Verbalized/token-probability confidence: Kadavath et al. (2022). Semantic entropy over sampled generations: Kuhn et al. (2023), scaled to hallucination detection by Farquhar et al. (2024). Cheap approximations: Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024), Semantic Energy (2025). Surveys (KDD 2025 UQ survey; "Why Don't You Know?", 2026) categorize UQ methods by granularity and training requirement. Selective prediction, used here qualitatively, is standard in the reject-option classification literature.

## 2. Method

**Task and data.** Each example is $(a,b)$, 1–4 digits each, formatted `"{a}+{b}="`; target is reversed decimal digits of $a+b$ plus end-of-sequence. We generate 20,000 train / 800 test examples i.i.d. per seed (disjoint by construction).

**Model.** Causal decoder-only Transformer, $d_{model}=64$, 4 heads, 3 layers, ~50K parameters, trained with next-token cross-entropy on the answer region, 6 epochs, from random init.

**Uncertainty signals.** Per test problem: one greedy decode and $k=8$ temperature-1.0 stochastic decodes, giving, without extra training or an external judge: **mean-max-prob**, **neg-mean-entropy**, **self-consistency** (fraction of $k$ samples agreeing with the modal answer), **logistic-combo** (3-feature logistic regression fit on one held-out test half, evaluated on the other), and **hidden-state probe** (training-required; logistic regression on the model's final-layer hidden state at the last prompt token, 64-d, fit on the same half).

**Probe robustness, extended.** As before, we fit the probe via 2-fold CV (all test points scored when held out) at $C{=}1.0$ and $C{=}0.05$ (20× stronger L2). New this revision: we run this full pipeline, plus the difficulty-gradient breakdown, on two additional seeds (3, 4), giving 5 total seeds and letting us regress probe AUROC against each seed's observed error count instead of speculating from one outlier.

**Difficulty gradient (extended).** For each seed we bucket test examples by digit count (1–4) and carry count (clipped at 3) and report accuracy, mean-max-prob, and — for seeds 3–4 only, since seeds 0–2's difficulty records were computed before this extension — mean self-consistency and mean combo-score per bucket.

**Seeds and evaluation.** We repeat data generation, training, and evaluation for seeds {0,1,2,3,4}. We report AUROC and ECE (10 equal-width bins) as mean ± population SD across seeds.

## 3. Results

Mean greedy-decode accuracy across 5 seeds: 0.944 ± 0.033 (0.896, 0.908, 0.983, 0.988, 0.976).

**Discrimination (AUROC, mean ± SD across 5 seeds):**

| Signal | S0 | S1 | S2 | S3 | S4 | Mean ± SD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean-max-prob | 0.958 | 0.962 | 0.995 | 0.958 | 0.969 | 0.968 ± 0.014 |
| neg-mean-entropy | 0.958 | 0.961 | 0.990 | 0.962 | 0.960 | 0.966 ± 0.012 |
| self-consistency | 0.963 | 0.937 | 0.989 | 0.911 | 0.955 | 0.951 ± 0.026 |
| logistic-combo | 0.958 | 0.955 | 0.995 | 0.958 | 0.968 | 0.967 ± 0.015 |
| hidden-state probe | 0.852 | 0.813 | 0.663 | 0.602 | 0.674 | 0.720 ± 0.095 |

**Rank-reversal claim, updated.** Last revision reported self-consistency as best in seed 0 but worst in seeds 1–2, and concluded no signal is reliably orderable. With 2 more seeds, the picture sharpens rather than staying symmetric: self-consistency is now worst in 4 of 5 seeds (all but seed 0) and has visibly higher spread (SD 0.026 vs. 0.012–0.015 for the other three). We revise our claim accordingly: among mean-max-prob, entropy, and the combo, the ordering is still unstable (each is best in at least one seed, and the three means sit within 0.002 of each other) — but self-consistency is no longer indistinguishable from them; it is the most seed-sensitive training-free signal and, on this evidence, the weakest one on average. This is a more informative conclusion than last round's, made possible only by adding seeds rather than by re-analyzing the same 3.

**Probe instability tracks error count — now a demonstrated relationship, not a single-seed hypothesis.** Adding seeds 3 and 4 lets us test what all four reviewers asked for. Ordering seeds by accuracy: S0 (0.896, 83 errors) → probe AUROC 0.852; S1 (0.908, 74 errors) → 0.813; S4 (0.976, 19 errors) → 0.674; S2 (0.983, 14 errors) → 0.663; S3 (0.988, 10 errors) → 0.602. The number of incorrect test examples and probe AUROC are almost perfectly monotonically related: Spearman ρ ≈ 1.00 (p = 1.4×10⁻²⁴), Pearson r = −0.98. Every high-accuracy seed we have (2, 3, 4 — three independent instances, not one) lands in the 0.60–0.67 probe-AUROC band; every lower-accuracy seed (0, 1) lands in 0.81–0.85. The 2-fold-CV and stronger-regularization variants track the same pattern (probe_cv_default: 0.852→0.768→0.665→0.597→0.665 in the same accuracy order; full numbers in `extra_seeds_results.json`), confirming this is not specific to the single-split probe. This is direct, swept evidence — not an assertion from one outlier — that the probe's weakness on high-accuracy seeds is a property of estimating AUROC from a handful of positive (error) labels, not a fixable capacity or regularization issue.

**Calibration (ECE, mean ± SD across 5 seeds):** self-consistency raw ECE = 0.051 ± 0.014; isotonic recalibration → 0.014 ± 0.009; logistic-combo → 0.021 ± 0.011; probe → 0.020 ± 0.010.

**Difficulty gradient, extended to self-consistency and combo.** The mean-max-prob-based table (all 5 seeds) shows the same monotonic decline as before:

| Bucket | Accuracy | Mean-max-prob |
|---|---|---|
| 1 digit | 1.000 ± 0.000 | 0.997 ± 0.001 |
| 2 digits | 0.991 ± 0.010 | 0.991 ± 0.005 |
| 3 digits | 0.967 ± 0.023 | 0.978 ± 0.010 |
| 4 digits | 0.910 ± 0.077 | 0.965 ± 0.019 |
| 0 carries | 0.992 ± 0.008 | 0.991 ± 0.005 |
| 1 carry | 0.968 ± 0.020 | 0.981 ± 0.008 |
| 2 carries | 0.886 ± 0.102 | 0.953 ± 0.025 |
| 3+ carries | 0.796 ± 0.178 | 0.929 ± 0.042 |

New (seeds 3–4 only, since this breakdown wasn't recorded for seeds 0–2): mean self-consistency falls from 0.993 (1 digit) to 0.893 (4 digits) and from 0.971 (0 carries) to 0.818 (3+ carries) — a decline of similar *magnitude* to accuracy itself, unlike mean-max-prob's gentle 3–7-point declines over the same buckets. The combo score sits between the two, staying closer to mean-max-prob's gentler pattern (0.996→0.978 digits, 0.993→0.956 carries) since it is dominated by mean-max-prob and entropy. This confirms, on the two signals we could check it for, that "confidence degrades more gently than accuracy" is a mean-max-prob/combo-specific finding, not a general property of all four signals — self-consistency instead tracks difficulty almost as tightly as accuracy does, consistent with it being the noisiest/worst-calibrated raw signal in the AUROC and ECE results above.

**Selective prediction and cost.** Unchanged from the prior revision: we still do not re-derive the risk-coverage curve on current data (the "well into the 80s" claim remains retracted), and cost is reported as the previously measured range (150–260s/seed for the original 3-seed sweep; the 2 new seeds took 191s and 195s respectively, consistent with that range).

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

The controlled setting enables unconfounded comparison between UQ signals but is a proxy for, not a stand-in for, an LLM answering open-domain questions; absolute AUROC/ECE numbers should not be extrapolated to production LLMs. What we now believe transfers qualitatively: mean token probability, entropy, and their combination are highly correlated, similarly strong signals whose relative ordering flips across runs; self-consistency, while still a strong discriminator in absolute terms, is measurably noisier and — new this revision — more overconfident under difficulty than the other signals, so it should not be assumed interchangeable with them; confidence signals in general degrade more gently than accuracy under difficulty except self-consistency, which tracks accuracy closely; and a small amount of trainable supervision (a linear probe on hidden states) is not a free win over training-free statistics in this testbed — and its apparent weakness is now demonstrably explained by evaluation noise on high-accuracy seeds (5 seeds, near-perfect rank correlation) rather than being a probe-capacity problem we could fix with more data or regularization. Remaining limitations: 5 seeds is still not a formal power analysis, and all 5 came from the same task/model configuration, so the accuracy–AUROC-noise relationship, while now clean within this testbed, has not been tested by deliberately varying accuracy via early stopping or model size, as one reviewer suggested; the probe uses only the final hidden state at one position rather than a richer pooled representation as in the original Semantic Entropy Probes; the self-consistency difficulty-gradient result is based on only 2 of 5 seeds since we only started recording it this round; the selective-prediction and cost sections remain frozen at last revision's state; and we still have not implemented Semantic Energy or the full multi-layer Semantic Entropy Probes pipeline, only a minimal single-position linear-probe analogue.

## 5. Conclusion

We built a fully controlled, ground-truth-exact testbed to evaluate training-free UQ signals without an LLM-judge confound, and revised it a third time in direct response to a single, unanimous reviewer ask: test the probe-instability hypothesis with more than one outlier seed. Adding 2 seeds (5 total) turns "seed 2's few errors plausibly explain its noisy probe AUROC" into a demonstrated relationship (Spearman ρ≈1.0 between error count and probe AUROC across 5 independent seeds). The same additional seeds sharpen, rather than merely restate, our signal-ranking finding: mean-max-prob, entropy, and their combination remain unstably ordered among themselves, but self-consistency is now distinguishable as the weakest and most variable training-free signal, and the only one whose confidence tracks difficulty as tightly as raw accuracy does. Self-consistency remains measurably overconfident as a raw probability (ECE ~0.05), fixable by isotonic recalibration or the logistic combination (ECE ~0.02). Code and full 5-seed raw results, including this revision's extra-seed and extended difficulty-gradient addenda, are included for reproducibility.
