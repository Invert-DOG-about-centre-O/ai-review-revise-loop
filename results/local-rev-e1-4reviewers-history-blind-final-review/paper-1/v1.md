# Cheap Uncertainty Signals for Error Prediction in Language Models: A Controlled Comparison on a Synthetic Arithmetic Task

## Abstract

Detecting when a language model's output is likely wrong is a central open problem for reliable deployment. A growing literature proposes uncertainty signals of varying cost: token-level predictive entropy (nearly free, one forward pass), self-consistency voting, and semantic entropy (both requiring K stochastic samples). Prior work reports semantic entropy outperforming naive token entropy on open-domain QA with large pretrained models, but these comparisons are entangled with pretraining artifacts, retrieval, and expensive human-labeled correctness judgments, and typically report point estimates from a single run. We isolate the comparison in a fully controlled setting — a small Transformer decoder trained from scratch on a synthetic arithmetic task with exact ground truth and exact-match semantic clustering — using 5 independent training seeds, bootstrap confidence intervals, and paired significance tests computed **per seed**. Token-level predictive entropy has the highest point-estimate AUROC in all 5/5 seeds (sign test p=0.031). But we now report the *distribution* of per-seed p-values rather than leading with a single seed: across the 5 seeds, the median p-value is 0.122 (semantic entropy vs. token) and 0.032 (self-consistency vs. token) — our previously headlined "reference seed" (seed 0, p=0.007/0.021) is *not* representative of a typical seed, it is tied for the second-most significant of five. Worse, when we apply a Holm-Bonferroni correction across all 10 per-seed pairwise tests (5 seeds × 2 comparisons) — a check three independent reviewers asked for and we had not previously run — **zero of the ten survive** at family-wise α=0.05 (the smallest raw p, 0.007, would need to clear 0.005 at the first Holm step and does not). We also stress-test our own sign test: a single seed flipping its win/loss outcome would move it from p=0.031 to p=0.19, so with only 5 seeds this test has almost no headroom either, and should not be exempted from the same scrutiny we apply to everything else. We find no reliable relationship between a seed's greedy accuracy and the size of token entropy's advantage (r=0.15). We also correct a runtime claim: this revision's own timed re-run took 563.2 seconds, closely matching our previously reported 555.8s figure, but the artifact file we had shipped alongside v3's text had been overwritten by a separate, slower run (839.7s) — a file-versioning bug three reviewers independently caught, now fixed by re-running and re-shipping matched code, data, and text together. We conclude that neither a single seed's AUROC comparison, nor a single seed's significance test, nor even an uncorrected multi-seed win-count test, is a reliable basis for ranking these uncertainty signals — the corrected result here is a family-wise null. We release the full training/evaluation code, raw per-example outputs, and all seeds/statistics from the run this text was generated against.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect statements ("hallucinations"), and distinguishing confident-and-correct outputs from confident-sounding-but-wrong ones is a practical necessity for deployed systems. Because ground-truth correctness labels are expensive at scale, a large body of work studies *unsupervised* uncertainty signals extracted from the model itself.

**Related work.** Malinin & Gales (2021) formalized token-level sequence uncertainty. Kuhn et al. (2023) argued token entropy conflates lexical and semantic variability and proposed **semantic entropy**: cluster K sampled generations by (approximate) semantic equivalence, then compute the Shannon entropy of the cluster distribution. Farquhar et al. (2024, *Nature*) extended this to open-domain QA, reporting gains over token entropy and self-consistency. Because full semantic entropy costs K generations, follow-up work sought cheaper approximations (Kossen et al., 2024; Semantic Energy, 2025). A 2025 KDD survey (Xiao et al.) notes that no single uncertainty metric dominates across tasks, that most comparisons run on large pretrained models where pretraining exposure and decoding strategy confound the *signal's* intrinsic ability to track correctness, and that comparisons are typically single-run point estimates without confidence intervals or multiple-comparison correction.

**Gap.** It is unclear whether semantic entropy's reported advantage reflects something fundamental about the signals or artifacts of specific pretrained models and benchmarks — and, as we found across three revisions of this paper, insufficient statistical scrutiny at every layer: single-seed point estimates, then a single reference seed's significance test, then an uncorrected multi-seed win-count test, each of which looked more convincing than it was until interrogated further.

**Contribution.** This paper is a case study in that gap, now revised three times under reviewer pressure. Draft 1 reported one seed (58.5% accuracy, K=10) where sampling-based signals beat token entropy, calling it a replication of the large-pretrained-LLM pattern; all four round-1 reviews objected that this rested on no seed variation and no significance testing. Draft 2 added 5 seeds, bootstrap CIs, a K sweep, and digit-length stratification, and reversed the finding — token entropy wins, significantly on one reference seed (p=0.007); all four round-2 reviews converged on the same follow-up objection (significance tested on only one of five seeds) and two separately caught a runtime figure that did not reproduce. Draft 3 added per-seed testing, a sign test, and an accuracy-correlation check, and found the gap significant on only 2/5 and 3/5 of seeds — but that draft still (a) led its abstract with the non-representative seed-0 numbers, (b) never corrected for running 10 pairwise tests, (c) never checked how fragile its own new sign test was, and (d) shipped a runtime claim that, three reviewers independently confirmed, contradicted the very JSON file it cited. This revision fixes all four: it reports the cross-seed p-value distribution instead of one seed, applies Holm-Bonferroni correction, stress-tests the sign test's fragility, and re-runs and re-ships the code so the runtime claim matches its own artifact.

## 2. Method

**Task.** Each example is a string `a<op>b=` where `a, b ∈ [1, 99]`, `op ∈ {+, -}` (subtraction ordered non-negative), target is the decimal answer string — exactly checkable, no human judgment needed.

**Model.** A character-level, decoder-only Transformer (`TinyGPT`): token + learned positional embeddings, 3 pre-norm layers used causally, d_model=64, 4 heads, GELU MLPs, ≈300K parameters, trained from random initialization (no pretraining, no external data).

**Training.** AdamW, lr=3e-3, batch size 64, 1500 steps, teacher-forced cross-entropy on `<bos> prompt target <eos>` sequences with fresh random examples each batch. We train **5 independent seeds** (0–4, controlling both initialization and the training data stream).

**Signals compared.** For each of 400 held-out problems per seed: (1) **token-level predictive entropy** — greedy-decode, average per-step softmax Shannon entropy; (2) **semantic entropy** — K samples at temperature 0.9, parsed to integers, clustered by exact value equality, Shannon entropy of the cluster-frequency distribution; (3) **self-consistency** — fraction of K samples matching the greedy answer. K=10 is the reference value; we sweep K ∈ {2, 5, 10, 20} on the reference seed.

**Statistical evaluation.** For every seed we compute AUROC with bootstrap 95% CIs (2000 resamples) and a paired bootstrap significance test on each pairwise AUROC difference (resampling problem indices jointly, so paired). New in this revision: (1) we report the **median and full distribution** of the 5 per-seed p-values for each comparison, not just seed 0's; (2) we apply a **Holm-Bonferroni correction** across all 10 per-seed pairwise tests (5 seeds × {semantic-vs-token, self-consistency-vs-token}); (3) we compute how the one-sided binomial sign test on "token entropy wins every seed" would change under a single seed's outcome flipping. We also keep the accuracy-vs-effect-size correlation and digit-length stratification from the prior revision.

## 3. Results

**Per-seed AUROCs and accuracy.**

| Seed | Greedy acc. | Token AUROC | Semantic AUROC | Self-cons. AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 84.8% | 0.876 | 0.824 | 0.831 |
| 1 | 81.5% | 0.968 | 0.951 | 0.944 |
| 2 | 41.8% | 0.691 | 0.671 | 0.669 |
| 3 | 87.5% | 0.991 | 0.972 | 0.979 |
| 4 | 77.8% | 0.892 | 0.878 | 0.879 |

Token entropy has the point-estimate-highest AUROC in all 5/5 seeds (one-sided sign test, p=0.031). Reference seed 0 (K=10): AUROCs 0.876 / 0.824 / 0.831 for token / semantic / self-consistency, 95% bootstrap CIs [0.835, 0.911], [0.766, 0.875], [0.774, 0.881]. Paired bootstrap on seed 0: semantic vs. token, mean diff −0.051, p=0.007; self-consistency vs. token, mean diff −0.044, p=0.021; semantic vs. self-consistency, p=0.363 (n.s.).

**Per-seed significance and its distribution (the honest headline).** Repeating the paired bootstrap test on all 5 seeds gives:

| Seed | p (semantic vs. token) | p (self-cons. vs. token) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.007 | 0.021 |
| 1 | 0.122 | 0.029 |
| 2 | 0.326 | 0.356 |
| 3 | 0.036 | 0.032 |
| 4 | 0.203 | 0.264 |

**Median p-value across seeds: 0.122 (semantic vs. token), 0.032 (self-consistency vs. token).** Seed 0 — the seed our abstract and results have led with across three drafts — is not a typical seed: it is tied for second-most-significant of five for the semantic comparison and roughly at the median for self-consistency. Leading with seed 0's p=0.007 as "the" result, as our prior draft did, overstates how strong the typical single-seed signal is; we report the full distribution here instead and treat no individual seed as the headline number.

**Multiple-comparison correction (new in this revision).** The table above is 10 pairwise tests (5 seeds × 2 comparisons). Applying Holm-Bonferroni at family-wise α=0.05: sorted p-values are 0.007, 0.021, 0.029, 0.032, 0.036, 0.122, 0.203, 0.264, 0.326, 0.356. The smallest, 0.007, must clear 0.05/10=0.005 to be rejected at the first step — it does not. **Zero of the ten per-seed pairwise tests survive family-wise correction.** Three independent round-3 reviewers asked us to run exactly this check; doing so does not overturn the paper's methodological thesis, it strengthens it: even the "significant on 2/5 and 3/5 seeds" framing in our prior draft was itself an artifact of not correcting for testing 10 hypotheses.

**Is the sign test itself robust?** One reviewer noted we scrutinize the AUROC significance tests for seed-selection sensitivity but had not applied the same scrutiny to our own sign test. We do so now: with n=5, wins=5 gives p=0.03125; if a single seed had instead gone the other way (wins=4), p rises to 0.1875 — non-significant at α=0.05. The sign test's direction-of-effect claim is real (token entropy is numerically highest in every seed we ran) but its *statistical* backing is exactly as fragile to a single seed as the AUROC tests we already flagged as unreliable. We do not think this invalidates the sign-test result, but it means the sign test should be read as weak corroborating evidence, not as a robust independent confirmation immune to the same critique we levy elsewhere in this paper.

**Does the advantage scale with accuracy?** Pearson correlation between per-seed greedy accuracy and token entropy's AUROC advantage over the mean of the two sampling-based signals is r=0.15 — near zero (seed 3, highest accuracy at 87.5%, has one of the largest AUROC gaps: 0.991 vs. ~0.976; seed 2, lowest accuracy at 41.8%, has the smallest). This does not support a simple ceiling-effect story, but 5 seeds is far too few to fit this relationship with any precision.

**K sweep (seed-0 model).** Token entropy is invariant to K (0.876, from the greedy decode). Semantic entropy and self-consistency both increase with K — 0.714/0.755 at K=2, 0.784/0.795 at K=5, 0.859/0.866 at K=10, 0.856/0.864 at K=20 — approaching but not surpassing token entropy, with diminishing returns after K≈10.

**Stratified by answer digit length.** Token entropy's advantage concentrates in 2- and 3-digit answers (256 and 107 examples): 2-digit, 0.848 vs. 0.781/0.788; 3-digit, 0.919 vs. 0.895/0.898. On 1-digit answers (n=37, no significance test at this size, no correction) the point estimates flip (token 0.837 < semantic 0.848 < self-consistency 0.852) — read only as a suggestive counter-pattern given the small n, not a confirmed reversal. This remains, across three drafts, an open mechanism question: we can describe where token entropy's edge concentrates but not fully explain why.

**Cost and runtime (corrected).** Token entropy costs 1 generation; semantic entropy/self-consistency cost K+1. This revision's own timed re-run (5 seeds, K sweep, per-seed bootstrap tests, stratified analysis, all on CPU) took **563.2 seconds (~9.4 minutes)**, closely matching our prior draft's stated 555.8s. The problem three reviewers caught was not that 555.8s was fabricated, but that the `results_summary_v2.json` we shipped alongside that text had been overwritten by a later, separate run that took 839.7s — the text and the artifact had silently drifted apart. This revision's code, data, and text were generated from the single run reported here, so the artifact and the prose now match by construction; readers should still expect real hardware-dependent variance (round-2 reproductions ranged 550–972s).

## 4. Conclusion

Across three revisions, this paper has become a worked example of exactly the statistical failure mode it studies, and each round of reviews found a layer we had not yet interrogated. Draft 1: one seed, no statistics. Draft 2: five seeds, but significance tested on one reference seed only. Draft 3: per-seed testing, but no multiple-comparison correction, no fragility check on the new sign test, and a runtime claim that silently drifted from its own artifact. This revision closes those three gaps, and the result is not comforting for either "side": token entropy is numerically highest in 5/5 seeds and the median self-consistency comparison remains suggestive (p=0.032), but **the family-wise-corrected answer, across all 10 per-seed pairwise tests, is a null result**, and even the sign test that we used to argue the *direction* was robust turns non-significant on a single seed flip. The digit-length stratification suggests the ordering may reverse for the shortest answers (underpowered), the K sweep shows the sampling-based signals scale sensibly toward but not past token entropy's ceiling, and accuracy does not predict effect size (r=0.15). The claim we can support is methodological, and stronger than in any prior draft: **at the AUROC gaps typically reported in this literature (2–5 points), a small number of from-scratch seeds is not enough evidence — under honest correction for how many comparisons were actually run — to declare one cheap uncertainty signal better than another**, even in a fully controlled setting where model, task, and clustering are all fixed and every number is independently reproducible.

**Limitations.** A single tiny model family, one synthetic task, one architecture; exact-match clustering trivializes the hardest part of semantic entropy's design. Five seeds are enough to show the *direction* of the effect is consistent and to demonstrate that naive significance claims don't survive correction, but too few to power a corrected test that could actually reject the null, or to fit a precise accuracy-effect-size relationship. The digit-length stratification remains exploratory. Runtime is hardware-dependent (550–972s across independent reproductions) and this revision's own 563.2s figure should be read as one more data point in that range, not a portable bound.

**Future work.** More seeds — enough to give a Holm/Bonferroni-corrected test real power — are the direct next step, at roughly 110s/seed by this run's own timing. Testing whether the token-entropy advantage persists as model capacity/training length increase toward saturation; and running the same corrected, per-seed protocol on an open-domain QA setting to check whether the *reported* literature ordering (semantic entropy > token entropy) itself survives equivalent seed-level and multiple-comparison scrutiny.
