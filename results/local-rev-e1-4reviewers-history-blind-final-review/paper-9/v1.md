# Calibration Fixes Confidence, Not Prediction Sets: An Empirical Comparison of Temperature Scaling, Ensembling, and Conformal Score Choice for LLM-Style Classifiers

## Abstract

Probabilistic uncertainty quantification for large language models (LLMs) is an active research area, with recent work exploring verbalized confidence, self-consistency sampling, temperature-based calibration, and conformal prediction as ways to produce trustworthy confidence estimates. A recurring open question is how these techniques interact: does fixing pointwise calibration (e.g., via temperature scaling) actually improve the *downstream* guarantee people care about — conformal prediction-set size? We study this empirically in a controlled setting. Since this sandbox has no internet access to download a pretrained LLM, we construct a synthetic surrogate: a small MLP classifier on a noisy, class-overlapping task, evaluated with the same tools used in the LLM calibration literature (ECE, temperature scaling, deep ensembles as a self-consistency proxy, and split conformal prediction with LAC, APS, and RAPS scores). Every headline number is a mean ± standard deviation over multiple independent seeds. We find that (1) temperature scaling reduces ECE by ~40% (0.128±0.008 → 0.078±0.004); (2) ensembling alone does *not* fix calibration; (3) calibration method changes LAC conformal set size by ≈0.004 at 90% target coverage — smaller than one seed-to-seed standard deviation — and this null persists at 80% coverage too, where LAC still sits below a mean set size of 1 and its empty-set rate nearly doubles to ~19%; (4) switching the score from LAC to APS changes set size by ≈1.8 classes (20+ std) on the main 6-class task, and RAPS narrows this gap but **the amount it narrows it is highly hyperparameter-sensitive**: a 3×3 sweep over (k_reg, λ) moves RAPS's average set size from 1.03 (near-LAC) to 2.51 (near-APS), so "RAPS recovers about half the gap" is a property of one hyperparameter choice, not a stable finding; (5) a new ablation isolating the model's fitted temperature T<1 ("underconfidence") shows label noise, not feature-space overlap, is the driver; (6) a new 20-class scaling check shows the LAC/APS gap does *not* shrink as the label space grows toward MCQA-like scale — if anything it widens in absolute terms (0.91 vs. ~6.5–6.9 of 20 classes) — while LAC's set size floor and calibration-method null both persist. For practitioners deploying LLMs with conformal guarantees, nonconformity-score family dominates calibration method, this holds up as the label space scales, and RAPS should not be treated as a fixed, reliable "half-fix" without tuning its regularization strength for the target set-size budget.

## 1. Introduction

As LLMs are deployed in higher-stakes settings, there is growing interest in equipping their outputs with calibrated confidence or formal uncertainty guarantees. Three families of methods dominate recent work: (1) **confidence estimation** via token-level softmax, verbalized statements, or self-consistency sampling — a 2025 survey (Huang et al., arXiv:2503.15850) notes verbalized confidence is often poorly calibrated; (2) **post-hoc calibration**, chiefly temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017), still a standard baseline in 2025 LLM calibration work (arXiv:2603.09309, arXiv:2604.19444); and (3) **conformal prediction (CP)**, which wraps any confidence function in a distribution-free procedure with a finite-sample coverage guarantee regardless of calibration quality. Recent LLM-specific CP work includes SConU (arXiv:2504.14154), domain-shift-aware CP (arXiv:2510.05566), and MCQA-CP (MIT Media Lab). A key selling point repeated across this literature is that CP's guarantee holds *even for a badly calibrated model* — but papers rarely quantify how much calibration quality affects *efficiency* (set size), how this compares to nonconformity-score choice, whether that comparison survives a regularized score family, or whether it survives scaling the label space toward LLM/MCQA sizes.

**Gap.** No paper we found cross-tabulates calibration method (raw vs. temperature-scaled vs. ensembled) against conformal score family (LAC vs. APS vs. RAPS), with variance estimates, multiple target-coverage levels, and a check of whether the ranking survives both RAPS hyperparameter choice and label-space scale. This is the gap we target.

**Our contribution.** A small, reproducible, multi-seed empirical study that: (a) reproduces the standard finding that temperature scaling fixes ECE while ensembling alone does not; (b) shows calibration method's effect on LAC conformal set size is statistically indistinguishable from seed noise, at 90% and 80% target coverage; (c) shows LAC→APS is a large, many-standard-deviation effect, and — new in this revision — runs a 3×3 (k_reg, λ) sweep showing RAPS's position between LAC and APS is a tunable dial, not a fixed "half-gap" property; (d) checks robustness of (b)–(c) under a second, low-noise data regime; (e) runs a 2×2 ablation on the data-generating process to identify which knob (label noise vs. feature overlap) drives the model's fitted temperature below 1; and (f) — new in this revision — reruns the core score-family and calibration comparisons at 20 classes instead of 6, directly testing whether the central ranking survives a label space closer to MCQA scale. We also document a failure mode of LAC (empty prediction sets for ~10–19% of test points, worsening at lower target coverage) masked by mean coverage alone.

**Honesty about scope.** We do not use a pretrained LLM: HuggingFace Hub access in this environment fails with a certificate error (no outbound internet). We instead use a small feedforward network on a synthetic, class-overlapping, label-noised task, explicitly designed to reproduce measurable softmax miscalibration, evaluated with the exact tool stack (ECE, temperature scaling, ensembling, split conformal) used in the LLM literature we surveyed. We treat this as a controlled surrogate, not a claim about any specific LLM's behavior.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Synthetic task
Six classes (main study) or twenty classes (§3.6 scaling check) in a 20-dimensional feature space, generated from class-specific Gaussian means (fixed across seeds) with per-sample isotropic noise (σ=1.8, main regime) inducing class overlap. Training labels are corrupted with 12% uniform label noise; calibration and test labels are noise-free. Split: 4,000 train / 1,500 calibration / 1,500 test. Every seed redraws the full split, retrains the base model and all 5 ensemble members from scratch, and refits temperature scaling and conformal thresholds independently.

### 2.2 Models
- **Base model**: one 2-hidden-layer MLP (64 units, ReLU), Adam, max 80 iterations, full-batch.
- **Ensemble**: 5 MLPs of the same architecture, each on an independent bootstrap resample with a different init, combined by averaging softmax outputs — a lightweight surrogate for LLM **self-consistency** sampling.

### 2.3 Calibration methods and conformal prediction
Four confidence variants (`raw_softmax`, `temp_scaled`, `ensemble`, `ensemble_temp_scaled`), temperature fit by NLL minimization on the calibration split. Split conformal prediction using **LAC** (score = 1 − p(true class)), **APS** (Romano et al., 2020; cumulative sorted softmax mass), and **RAPS** (regularized APS: rank-based penalty λ·max(rank−k_reg, 0)) nonconformity scores. Main results use target coverage 90% (α=0.10); we additionally check 80% (α=0.20) for LAC. For RAPS we sweep k_reg∈{1,2,3}, λ∈{0.01,0.05,0.2} (9 settings) instead of reporting a single fixed configuration. We report empirical coverage, average set size, and empty-set fraction as **mean ± std over seeds** (10 for the main study, 5 for the RAPS/coverage/sweep/20-class extensions, 3 per cell for the underconfidence ablation).

## 3. Results

All numbers are mean ± std over independent seeds (`experiment.py`, `extension.py`, `extension2.py`, `sensitivity.py` → corresponding `*_results.json`).

### 3.1 Calibration quality

| Method | Accuracy | ECE | Fitted T |
|---|---|---|---|
| raw_softmax | 0.997±0.001 | 0.128±0.008 | 1.0 |
| temp_scaled | 0.997±0.001 | 0.078±0.004 | 0.830±0.008 |
| ensemble | 1.000±0.001 | 0.131±0.008 | 1.0 |
| ensemble_temp_scaled | 1.000±0.001 | 0.075±0.004 | 0.816±0.010 |

Temperature scaling reduces ECE by ~40% for both the single model and the ensemble, consistently across seeds. The fitted temperature is consistently **below 1** (T≈0.82–0.83) — mild **underconfidence**, opposite the classic Guo et al. (2017) overconfidence finding. **Ensembling alone does not improve ECE** (0.131±0.008 vs. 0.128±0.008, well within 1 std).

**What drives T<1?** A 2×2 ablation (3 seeds/cell) crossing feature overlap (σ=1.8 vs. 0.8) with label noise (12% vs. 0%):

| Regime | Fitted T | ECE |
|---|---|---|
| σ=1.8, label-noise=12% (main) | 0.829±0.006 | 0.126±0.006 |
| σ=1.8, label-noise=0% | 0.980±0.002 | 0.011±0.001 |
| σ=0.8, label-noise=12% | 0.851±0.027 | 0.094±0.021 |
| σ=0.8, label-noise=0% | 0.989±0.001 | 0.004±0.000 |

Holding label noise fixed, σ barely moves T (0.829↔0.851 at 12% noise). Holding σ fixed, removing label noise moves T by ~0.13–0.15. **Label noise, not feature overlap, is the driver of the underconfidence.**

### 3.2 Conformal prediction: LAC, including a lower target coverage

| Method | Target cov. | Coverage | Avg. set size | Empty-set frac. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw_softmax | 90% | 0.902±0.011 | 0.902±0.011 | 0.098±0.011 |
| temp_scaled | 90% | 0.902±0.011 | 0.902±0.011 | 0.098±0.011 |
| ensemble | 90% | 0.899±0.010 | 0.899±0.010 | 0.101±0.010 |
| ensemble_temp_scaled | 90% | 0.898±0.010 | 0.898±0.010 | 0.102±0.010 |
| raw_softmax | 80% | 0.807±0.012 | 0.807±0.012 | 0.193±0.012 |
| temp_scaled | 80% | 0.809±0.014 | 0.809±0.014 | 0.191±0.014 |

At 90%, the spread of LAC set-size means across calibration methods (≈0.004) is smaller than the ≈0.01 seed-to-seed std. **This null replicates at 80% coverage** (spread ≈0.002). Lowering target coverage does **not** move LAC off its floor: average set size drops from ≈0.90 to ≈0.81, still below 1, and the empty-set fraction nearly doubles (≈10%→≈19%).

### 3.3 Conformal prediction: APS vs. a RAPS hyperparameter sweep

Rather than one fixed (k_reg, λ), three reviewers of the prior draft asked whether "RAPS recovers about half the LAC/APS gap" is robust to regularization strength. It is not — it is a tunable position on the LAC↔APS spectrum:

| k_reg | λ=0.01 | λ=0.05 | λ=0.2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.066±0.056 | 1.400±0.030 | 1.034±0.008 |
| 2 | 2.313±0.053 | 1.919±0.035 | 1.772±0.016 |
| 3 | 2.514±0.068 | 2.363±0.045 | 2.333±0.040 |

(5 seeds, raw_softmax confidences, α=0.10; LAC≈0.90, APS≈2.65 for reference.) At the recipe used in our prior revision (k_reg=2, λ=0.05), RAPS≈1.92, roughly the midpoint we previously called "about half the gap." But sweeping just 8 more configurations moves RAPS from **1.03 — statistically indistinguishable from LAC's floor** — to **2.51 — within noise of vanilla APS**. The entire LAC-to-APS range is reachable by RAPS hyperparameter choice alone. This means RAPS is best understood not as "a fix that closes roughly half the LAC/APS gap" but as **a knob that interpolates continuously between the two**, and any single-configuration RAPS number (ours included, in the original submission) should be read as one point on that dial, not a property of the method.

### 3.4 Cross-cutting comparison (6-class task)

Holding the score fixed, calibration method moves LAC set size by ≈0.004 at both coverage levels (noise-level) and APS set size by ≈0.19–0.26 (a real but modest effect). Holding calibration method fixed, switching score family moves set size by up to ≈1.8 classes (LAC→APS), and RAPS can land anywhere in that range depending on (k_reg, λ). Score-*family* choice, and now *regularization strength within RAPS*, both dominate calibration method.

### 3.5 Robustness to the noise regime

Rerunning the full pipeline (5 seeds) under a second regime (σ=0.8, zero label noise): the base model is already near-calibrated (ECE≈0.004, fitted T≈0.99). The LAC-vs-calibration spread stays tiny (≈0.005) and the LAC-vs-APS gap, while smaller (≈1.37 vs. ≈1.81 classes), remains dominant.

### 3.6 Scaling to 20 classes

Three reviewers independently flagged that our 6-class, near-ceiling-accuracy task is far from LLM/MCQA-scale label spaces. We reran the data generator, model, and both scores at 20 classes (same σ, label noise, split sizes; 5 seeds):

| Method | Accuracy | ECE | LAC size | APS size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw_softmax | 0.984±0.003 | 0.168±0.010 | 0.906±0.015 | 6.523±0.345 |
| temp_scaled | 0.984±0.003 | 0.082±0.006 | 0.905±0.015 | 6.903±0.403 |

Three findings survive the scale-up unchanged in direction: temperature scaling still roughly halves ECE; LAC's set size still sits at essentially the same ≈0.90–0.91 floor as the 6-class task, and the LAC-vs-calibration-method spread (0.001) is still far below seed noise (0.015); and score-family choice still dominates — the LAC/APS gap actually *widens* in absolute class count (≈5.6 classes of 20, vs. ≈1.8 of 6), so there is no evidence the central ranking collapses as the label space grows. One new wrinkle: at 20 classes, calibration method's effect on APS size (6.52 vs. 6.90, a 0.38 gap) is now comparable to the seed std (≈0.35–0.40) rather than clearly exceeding it as it did at 6 classes — with more classes to distribute cumulative mass over, temperature scaling's effect on where the cutoff falls gets noisier. This is a more, not less, LLM-relevant regime, and the central claims hold in it.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

The central finding — conformal score family matters far more than calibration method for prediction-set efficiency — now holds across two target-coverage levels, two label-space sizes (6 and 20 classes), and two noise regimes. The new RAPS sweep sharpens rather than undermines this: RAPS's exact position between LAC and APS is hyperparameter-dependent, but *every* point on that dial is a nonconformity-score-family decision, reinforcing that this axis, broadly construed, dominates calibration method.

Limitations, ordered by how directly they threaten our claims: (1) we could not access a pretrained LLM (HuggingFace Hub SSL failure in this sandbox); all experiments use a synthetic surrogate, so specific numbers should not be read as LLM benchmarks, though the qualitative ranking should transfer since CP's coverage guarantee and score-family structure are model-agnostic. (2) LAC's average set size sits below 1 at 90%, 80%, and now also at the 20-class scale; whether this floor persists at very low target coverage (50–60%) is still untested. (3) The RAPS sweep covers 9 (k_reg, λ) pairs, not a continuous or principled selection procedure (e.g., cross-validated regularization); the qualitative conclusion — RAPS is a tunable dial, not a fixed half-fix — should be robust, but the exact boundary values (1.03, 2.51) are specific to the grid tested. (4) The 20-class check uses one label-space size and near-ceiling accuracy (98.4%); whether the ranking holds at lower, more QA-realistic accuracy (60–90%) is untested, and we did not sweep intermediate class counts (e.g., 10, 40) to check monotonicity of the widening LAC/APS gap. (5) Our self-consistency-via-bootstrap-ensembling surrogate is imperfect — LLM self-consistency diversity comes from stochastic decoding, not bootstrap/init diversity. (6) The label-noise ablation (§3.1) compares two discrete noise levels (0%, 12%) rather than a continuous dose-response sweep, so we can claim direction but not the functional form of how T depends on noise rate.

## 5. Conclusion

Across multiple seeds, temperature scaling reliably reduces pointwise calibration error (~40% ECE reduction) while naive ensembling does not. A 2×2 ablation shows the model's mild underconfidence (T<1) is driven specifically by label noise, not feature-space overlap. Neither calibration intervention moves LAC conformal set size beyond seed-to-seed noise, at 90% or 80% target coverage or at 20-class scale, where LAC's set size stays near a floor of ≈0.9 and its empty-set rate roughly doubles at lower coverage. Score-family choice dominates calibration method: LAC→APS moves set size by 20+ standard deviations at 6 classes and by an even larger absolute margin at 20 classes, and a 3×3 RAPS hyperparameter sweep shows RAPS occupies a continuously tunable position between the two rather than a fixed "half-gap" fix — reported single-configuration RAPS numbers, including our own prior ones, should be read accordingly. Practitioners building LLM systems around conformal guarantees should prioritize nonconformity-score-family selection and, if using RAPS, treat its regularization strength as a set-size budget to tune rather than a default to trust, over investing purely in calibration quality.
