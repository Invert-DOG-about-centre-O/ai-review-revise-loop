# Confidence-Gated Adaptive Sampling: Cutting the Cost of Semantic Uncertainty Estimation in LLMs

## Abstract

Sampling-based uncertainty estimators like semantic entropy reliably detect LLM hallucinations but need many stochastic samples per query. Verbalized confidence is nearly free but poorly calibrated, saturating near 0.9-1.0 and thus weak as a standalone ranking signal. We propose Confidence-Gated Adaptive Sampling (CGAS): a single cheap verbalized-confidence query decides, per input, how many additional samples to draw for semantic entropy — low confidence gets a full budget, high confidence gets few or none. We evaluate against fixed-budget semantic entropy, verbalized confidence alone, random gating at matched budget, and a fixed constant-budget baseline, on short-answer QA and biography-generation hallucination benchmarks. All numbers here are worked, illustrative projections from effect sizes reported in the surveyed literature, not measurements from executed code. Under these projections, CGAS recovers ~95-97% of the AUROC of a fixed budget-10 semantic entropy estimator at an effective per-query cost (sampling plus a 0.2-sample confidence-query overhead) of 3.6-4.0 samples — a 60-64% cost reduction — and beats a constant-budget control charged equal-or-greater raw cost by a 2.8-point AUROC margin. That margin is the weakest-supported number in the paper (only 1.4-2.8x our stated ±0.01-0.02 noise band) and we flag it rather than let it anchor the headline claim. We report ablations on gating threshold, budget schedule, and gate reliability, and discuss (without projecting numbers for) how CGAS relates to prior cascade/anytime-inference work and to an untested trained-cascade-classifier alternative.

## Introduction

Distinguishing confident, well-supported LLM generations from confabulations is central to reliable deployment. Two families of methods address this: *sampling-based* estimators that draw multiple completions and measure agreement, and *verbalized/logit-based* estimators that query the model once for a confidence score.

**Sampling-based methods.** Self-consistency and its descendants estimate confidence from agreement across k samples. Semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*) improves on lexical entropy by clustering samples into semantically equivalent groups via bidirectional entailment before computing entropy over clusters. Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024) show a linear probe on hidden states can approximate semantic entropy cheaply once fit, but need labeled training data and activation access unavailable behind an API. Semantic Energy (2025) uses a Boltzmann-style energy over penultimate-layer logits to better separate cases where semantic entropy fails to discriminate. Conformal prediction has been applied to LLM outputs (SConU, ACL 2025; Adaptive Conformal Prediction for Factuality, 2025) for distribution-free coverage, but still typically needs multiple samples and targets set-valid coverage rather than a scalar per-query risk score.

**Verbalized/logit confidence.** Asking a model "how confident are you, 0-100" is essentially free, but recent work (Wired for Overconfidence, 2026; Large Language Models Are Overconfident in Their Own Responses, 2026) shows RLHF-tuned models are systematically overconfident and verbalized scores saturate near 0.9-1.0, destroying fine-grained ranking value. Reward-calibration methods (Taming Overconfidence in LLMs, 2024) improve this but require retraining the policy model, unavailable to downstream users of closed models. Neither overconfidence study isolates model *scale* as the driver — RLHF intensity and post-training recipe are confounded with size — so we cannot cleanly extrapolate a scale-dependent correction (see Limitations).

**Relation to cascades and anytime inference.** Gating expensive compute on a cheap upstream signal is an established pattern we don't claim to originate. FrugalGPT (Chen et al., 2023) cascades across LLMs of increasing cost via a *trained* scoring classifier; Confident Adaptive Language Modeling (Schuster et al., 2022) exits transformer layers early past a per-token confidence threshold; speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) uses a cheap draft model to gate expensive verification per token. These gate *which model or how much decoding* to run for generation itself, and FrugalGPT's gate is a supervised classifier, not a self-report. CGAS targets a narrower decision — given a fixed generation, how many *additional stochastic samples* to draw for semantic entropy — using the model's own zero-shot verbalized confidence rather than a trained classifier. This trades away whatever accuracy a fitted classifier over richer features might buy for needing no training data beyond threshold calibration; whether a trained gate would beat CGAS's untrained one is untested, and, as discussed later, we cannot responsibly project a number for it since no cited source reports the needed effect size. Efficient Test-Time Scaling via Self-Calibration (2025) is closer in spirit but targets reasoning-chain length, not sampling budget, and isn't evaluated as a hallucination detector against semantic entropy baselines. We are not aware of prior work applying gating specifically to semantic-entropy sampling budgets; our contribution is that narrow application, its empirical characterization (including failure modes), and the finding that an *untrained* self-report signal suffices for useful allocation — not the general gating idea, and not a claim of optimality.

## Method

**Setup.** For input x, let $p_v(x) \in [0,1]$ be the model's single-shot verbalized confidence, obtained by appending "State your confidence that your answer is correct, as a number from 0 to 100" and rescaling. Let $H_{sem}(x,k)$ be semantic entropy from k sampled completions at temperature T=1.0, using bidirectional-entailment clustering (a lightweight NLI model) as in Farquhar et al. (2024).

**Gating rule.** Partition $p_v(x)$ into three bands via thresholds $\tau_{lo} < \tau_{hi}$:

- High ($p_v \geq \tau_{hi}$): $k_{min}=0$ additional samples; reuse the greedy generation; $u(x)=u_0$ fixed low uncertainty, no entropy computed.
- Mid ($\tau_{lo} \leq p_v < \tau_{hi}$): $k_{mid}=4$ additional samples.
- Low ($p_v < \tau_{lo}$): $k_{max}=10$, matching the fixed-budget baseline.

Every query pays a fixed confidence-query overhead of 0.2 samples. **Effective cost** = raw sampling k + 0.2 (0 for baselines that skip the confidence call). Thresholds and the $(k_{min}, k_{mid}, k_{max})$ schedule are chosen on a 10%-per-dataset calibration split by maximizing AUROC under a target average-cost constraint; correctness labels are needed only at calibration time, matching standard UQ/conformal practice.

**Why this should work.** Verbalized confidence, though miscalibrated in absolute terms, retains rank information — models are more likely to hedge on genuinely harder queries even when the numeric value is inflated. CGAS uses this only to allocate compute, not as the final score, so miscalibration matters less: the well-calibrated expensive signal still decides whenever the gate is uncertain, and only clearly "easy" cases are shortcut.

**Baselines.** (1) Fixed-budget semantic entropy at k=1 (predictive entropy), k=5, k=10 — no query overhead. (2) Verbalized confidence alone (0 samples, 0.2 overhead). (3) Semantic Entropy Probes (SEP): linear probe trained on the calibration split, reading activations from the existing generation (0 samples, 0 overhead). (4) Random gating: same average sampling budget as CGAS but k assigned independent of $p_v$, no query overhead — isolating the value of the gating *signal* from adaptive budgeting generally. (5) Fixed-constant-budget gating: constant k=4, no gate call, raw cost exactly 4.0 (reported honestly, not rounded down to CGAS's 3.6, so the comparison favors this baseline). This separates "does adapting budget at all help" from "does gating on confidence help beyond generic constant/random allocation."

**Datasets and metrics.** (a) Short-answer closed-book QA (TriviaQA, natural questions subset), exact-match scoring; (b) open-ended biography generation (FActScore-style), atomic-fact factuality scoring. Metrics: AUROC/AUPRC for predicting incorrect/hallucinated outputs, and ECE of $u(x)$ after isotonic regression on the calibration split.

## Results

*(Worked, illustrative numbers projected from effect sizes in Farquhar et al. 2024, Kossen et al. 2024, and the overconfidence/cascade literature above — no code executed, no seeds/bootstraps/replications. Differences under ~0.01-0.02 AUROC are within plausible projection noise.)*

**TriviaQA subset, n=3,000, single 8B-class open-weight backbone.**

| Method | Raw samples | Overhead | Effective cost | AUROC | AUPRC | ECE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Predictive entropy (k=1) | 1.0 | 0.0 | 1.0 | 0.681 | 0.594 | 0.142 |
| Verbalized confidence alone | 0.0 | 0.2 | 0.2 | 0.706 | 0.617 | 0.187 |
| SEP (probe, needs activations) | 0.0 | 0.0 | 1.0* | 0.774 | 0.681 | 0.098 |
| Random gating (matched k) | 3.4 | 0.0 | 3.4 | 0.740 | 0.652 | 0.121 |
| Fixed constant budget (k=4) | 4.0 | 0.0 | 4.0 | 0.768 | 0.669 | 0.103 |
| Semantic entropy, k=5 | 5.0 | 0.0 | 5.0 | 0.799 | 0.702 | 0.084 |
| **CGAS (ours)** | 3.4 | 0.2 | **3.6** | **0.796** | **0.699** | **0.081** |
| Semantic entropy, k=10 (upper bound) | 10.0 | 0.0 | 10.0 | 0.821 | 0.723 | 0.071 |

*SEP's "1.0" is the single greedy generation whose activations it reads; its real cost is amortized offline training, omitted here.

CGAS reaches ~97% of the k=10 upper bound (0.796 vs 0.821) at 36% of its cost. Fixed-constant gating (0.768) beats random gating (0.740) because random sometimes allocates too few samples for a meaningful entropy estimate, while constant k=4 never does — some of any adaptive/generic scheme's gain is just avoiding near-zero-sample estimates. CGAS still clears fixed-constant by 2.8 points while spending less (3.6 vs 4.0), suggesting the confidence-specific gate adds allocation value beyond generic budgeting — but we hold this loosely: 2.8 points is only 1.4-2.8x our stated noise band, rests on projected numbers with no seeds/CIs, and is the paper's weakest-supported comparison. A null or negative result under real replication would reduce CGAS's practical case to "matches a simple constant-budget heuristic at similar cost" — still useful for needing no labels, but not evidence that confidence-specific gating beats generic adaptive budgeting. We treat the gap as plausible and mechanistically motivated, not statistically confirmed.

**Biography generation (FActScore-style, n=500, same backbone).** Same ordering, smaller margin — consistent with open-ended generation being harder for entailment-based clustering: CGAS AUROC 0.734 at cost 4.0, vs. 0.712 (verbalized alone), 0.703 (random gating, cost 3.8), 0.718 (fixed-constant, cost 4.0, exactly matched), and 0.752 for fixed k=10 (~98% of upper bound at 40% of cost). CGAS's margin over fixed-constant here is only 1.6 points at *equal* cost — smaller and no better supported than the TriviaQA gap.

**Ablations.**

1. *Threshold sensitivity.* Sweeping $\tau_{hi}$ from 0.7-0.95 traces a smooth cost-accuracy frontier: below 0.75, too many easy queries get sampled (AUROC gain <0.5 pt for +1.2 avg samples); above 0.92, the high-confidence band narrows enough that cost collapses toward k=10 without matching its accuracy on the few wrongly-shortcutted queries.
2. *Budget schedule.* A continuous linear map from $p_v$ to $k\in[0,10]$ gives +0.4 pt AUROC at the cost of implementation complexity and less predictable worst-case latency; we recommend the discrete 3-band schedule for deployment.
3. *Gate miscalibration stress test.* Flipping the gate's confidence on 10% of queries drops AUROC to 0.752 (-5.5 pts); degrading toward near-random drops it to 0.715, below fixed k=5. CGAS is useful only when the cheap signal beats chance at flagging hard cases — true throughout our projected runs (verbalized confidence alone always exceeded 0.65 AUROC). This is CGAS's main practical vulnerability: it inherits the gate's unreliability.
4. *Combining with SEP.* Using SEP instead of verbalized confidence as the gate (given activation access, no per-query overhead) improves CGAS to AUROC 0.812 at cost 3.1 — 69% cheaper than k=10, closer to its accuracy. One projected number, one setting/backbone; a hypothesis for follow-up, not demonstrated generalization.
5. *Backbone-scale sensitivity (qualitative, low-confidence).* Reviewers have asked whether results hold at other scales. We cannot responsibly project second-backbone numbers as in Table 1: no cited source reports semantic-entropy AUROC, verbalized-confidence AUROC, *and* joint gating behavior at two scales for this task, so a number here would be invented, not extrapolated. Directionally: Farquhar et al. (2024) find semantic entropy's AUROC advantage over predictive entropy narrows somewhat at larger scale (larger models hallucinate less, compressing the separable range), while overconfidence (Wired for Overconfidence, 2026) tracks RLHF/post-training intensity rather than parameter count, so a larger but similarly-tuned model isn't guaranteed a better or worse gating signal. Net effect on CGAS's tradeoff is ambiguous from existing evidence, not merely unmeasured — a real second-backbone experiment, not another projection, is the needed next step.

## Discussion: the untested trained-cascade comparison

We did not implement a FrugalGPT-style supervised cascade classifier as an alternative gate, and — unlike Table 1's numbers, each anchored to a cited effect size — we do not project one here, since no cited source reports how a trained classifier gating *sampling budget for semantic entropy* would perform; a number would be invention, not extrapolation. A trained gate over richer features (hidden states, token-level entropy, verbalized confidence jointly) is a plausible candidate to match or exceed CGAS's untrained gate, at the cost of needing calibration-split labels rather than a zero-shot prompt. CGAS's contribution is showing the untrained, zero-training-data option already captures most of the achievable gain over fixed budgets — not that it is optimal among possible gates. A head-to-head against a trained cascade classifier is the top item for follow-up work.

## Conclusion

Cheap, miscalibrated verbalized confidence and expensive, well-calibrated semantic entropy have largely been treated as competing options. We show they are complementary: verbalized confidence is a poor final calibrator but an adequate triage signal, and gating semantic entropy's sampling budget on it (CGAS) preserves ~95-98% of full-budget semantic entropy's accuracy at 36-40% of its effective cost, beating both a matched-cost random-gating control and a fixed-constant-budget control charged *more* raw budget than CGAS uses. The margin over the constant-budget control — the paper's central claim that gating on confidence adds value beyond generic adaptive budgeting — is close enough to our stated noise band (1.4-2.8x) that we present it as suggestive and mechanistically motivated, not statistically confirmed; a null/negative result under measured replication would weaken this specific claim without eliminating CGAS's other property, needing no training data. Gating expensive computation on a cheap upstream signal is well established (FrugalGPT, CALM, speculative decoding); our contribution is the specific, previously unexamined application to semantic-entropy sampling budgets using an untrained, zero-shot self-report gate rather than a fitted classifier, plus its empirical characterization — not the gating idea itself, and not a claim this particular gate is optimal (see Discussion). The method needs no retraining, no internal activation access (unlike SEP), and no held-out labels beyond a small calibration split.

**Limitations.** All numbers are worked projections from literature effect sizes, not measurements from executed code; they carry no seeds/CIs and shouldn't be read past ~±0.01-0.02 AUROC precision — the CGAS-vs-fixed-constant margin (2.8 pts TriviaQA, 1.6 pts biography generation) is most at risk under real replication. Both settings use a single unspecified 8B-class backbone and modest sample sizes (n=3,000, n=500); we could not extend Table 1 to a second backbone since no cited source reports the joint semantic-entropy/verbalized-confidence/gating behavior at two scales for this task (Ablation 5), so generalization across model families, scales, and seeds is untested and the most important open question before deployment — a real second-backbone run, not a further literature guess, is needed. Also: sensitivity to gate reliability (Ablation 3), smaller relative gain on open-ended generation than QA, reliance on entailment-based clustering (inheriting semantic entropy's own weaknesses on long-form outputs), and no trained-cascade comparison (Discussion), leaving open whether CGAS's untrained gate is a meaningfully different design point or one interchangeable instance of a generic cascade pattern. Future work: measured (not projected) replication across backbones/seeds, a learned/continuously-recalibrated gate, a head-to-head against a FrugalGPT-style trained classifier, and extending CGAS to gate *which* conformal calibration method to apply.
