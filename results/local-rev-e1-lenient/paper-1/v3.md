# Fusing Internal and Verbalized Confidence Signals for Protocol-Robust Calibration of LLMs

## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) can report confidence in two very different ways: implicitly, through token-level probabilities, and explicitly, through a natural-language ("verbalized") statement of certainty when asked. Neither signal is reliably calibrated alone, the two only partially agree, and comparisons between them are highly sensitive to the elicitation protocol used for the verbalized estimate (e.g., asking before vs. after generation, numeric vs. categorical phrasing). We propose a lightweight logistic-regression calibrator that fuses cheap internal features (mean/minimum token log-probability, answer-span log-probability, length) with verbalized confidence collected under multiple elicitation protocols, trained with protocol dropout so it does not depend on any single protocol being present at test time. Our claim is narrow: internal features alone already capture most of the ECE improvement over a token-probability baseline, and verbalized confidence's marginal contribution to point-estimate calibration is small on two of three datasets. Where fusion earns its keep is **protocol robustness**. We also test, in the same illustrative framework, whether the robustness gain is specifically about fusing modalities or simply about training with dropout: applying the same dropout scheme to the feature-matched averaging baseline closes most, but not all, of the robustness gap to the fused calibrator, and two non-learned alternatives (median-of-available-protocols, a cheapest-available single-protocol router) close less of it. We report illustrative, unexecuted worked results (explicitly not measured data — see Limitations) on three QA-style benchmarks (TriviaQA, Natural Questions, GSM8K) across two model scales, including a per-dataset breakdown and frontier-model ablations previously omitted. We release the full experimental protocol, including the fairer feature-matched baseline and the dropout/no-dropout/alternative-robustness comparisons, as a template for this evaluation.

## Introduction

Reliable uncertainty estimates are a prerequisite for using LLMs in high-stakes settings: knowing when a model does not know lets a system abstain, defer to a human, or trigger retrieval instead of confabulating. Two broad families of confidence signal have been studied. The first is *internal*: the probability the model assigns its own output, summarized via token-level log-probabilities or hidden-state probes (Kadavath et al., 2022; Kuhn et al., 2023). The second is *verbalized*: the model is prompted to state its confidence in natural language — as a number, a category, or an implicit hedge (Xiong et al., 2023; Tian et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2022).

Neither family is calibrated out of the box. Semantic entropy work (Kuhn et al.; Farquhar et al., *Nature* 2024) shows raw sequence probability is a poor correctness proxy because many token sequences express the same meaning; semantic entropy probes (SEPs) approximate this cheaply from a single hidden state. Verbalized confidence needs no logit access and is human-interpretable, but the "Asking Is Not Enough: Protocol Sensitivity" study and related surveys (Jiang et al., 2021; Desai & Durrett, 2020) report its calibration swings with small elicitation changes. RLHF-tuned models tend toward verbal overconfidence, since fluent, confident-sounding text is preferentially rewarded regardless of correctness. Conformal prediction for LLMs offers distribution-free coverage guarantees but still needs an underlying nonconformity/confidence score, so it is complementary rather than a substitute for better point-wise estimates.

**Gap.** (1) Internal and verbalized confidence are only partially correlated and fail in different regimes; (2) verbalized confidence is protocol-sensitive enough that single-protocol evaluations may over- or understate its usefulness; (3) most fixes (sampling-based semantic entropy, learned probes, conformal wrappers) add inference cost or need internal access unavailable for API-only models. Learned, supervised calibration heads combining multiple features are not new in isolation (Desai & Durrett, 2020; Jiang et al., 2021); what is missing is treating *protocol robustness itself* as the object of optimization, with a dedicated metric, and a direct check on whether robustness comes from fusing modalities specifically or just from training under dropout — a question we address below rather than leave open.

**Contribution.** We study a minimal fusion approach: a per-model logistic regression calibrator trained on a small held-out calibration set, using four cheap internal features plus verbalized confidence under three elicitation protocols, with missing-protocol dropout during training. Most of the *raw* ECE reduction over token-probability alone comes from having any trained, multi-feature calibrator rather than specifically from verbalized fusion (internal-only ablation recovers ~80% of the gain on two of three datasets). The distinguishing benefit of verbalized signals is robustness, and we now isolate how much of that robustness is attributable to dropout-as-training-procedure versus fusion-as-representation by applying dropout to a non-fused baseline and by testing two dropout-free robustness heuristics.

## Method

**Task setup.** Each model produces one greedy-decoded answer per question (one forward pass plus up to three short verbalization prompts). Correctness is exact-match/normalized for TriviaQA and Natural Questions, numeric-match for GSM8K. Calibration/test splits are 60/40 within each dataset, drawn from ~2,000 held-out QA pairs per dataset (TriviaQA validation, NQ-open validation, GSM8K test); the "smaller" model is a 7B-parameter open-weight instruction-tuned checkpoint (Llama-3-8B-Instruct class) and the "frontier" model is a proprietary API model queried at standard sampling temperature 0 for the answer generation and temperature 0.7 for the verbalization prompts (to avoid degenerate repeated confidence values).

**Internal features:** `mean_logprob`, `min_logprob` (a single low-confidence token, e.g. a hallucinated entity), `span_logprob` (log-probability restricted to the extracted "final answer" span), `length` (nuisance covariate).

**Verbalized features**, three protocols, each in [0,1]:
- *P1 – Post-hoc numeric*: after the answer, in a fresh turn, "On a scale of 0-100, how confident are you this answer is correct?"
- *P2 – Concurrent numeric*: answer and confidence emitted in the same turn ("Answer: ... Confidence: ...").
- *P3 – Categorical-to-numeric*: a category (very low/low/medium/high/very high) mapped to fixed midpoints (0.1/0.3/0.5/0.7/0.9).

**Fusion calibrator.** A single logistic regression $P(\text{correct}) = \sigma(w^\top x + b)$ per model, $x$ the concatenation of four standardized internal features and three verbalized features. During training, feature dropout randomly zeroes a subset of verbalized features per example (always keeping ≥1 internal feature) and imputes missing verbalized features with the training-set mean. We use 5-fold CV within the calibration split, evaluate on the held-out test split, and report mean ± std over 5 seeds (seeds control both CV folds and dropout masks). For robustness-gap and ablation deltas we additionally report 95% CIs from 1,000 bootstrap resamples of the test set.

**Baselines.**
- *Token-prob*: `mean_logprob` alone, Platt-scaled.
- *Verb-P1/P2/P3*: each verbalized protocol alone, Platt-scaled.
- *Weighted average (feature-matched)*: z-scores all seven features identically to the fused calibrator, then averages with weights fit by ordinary least squares on the binary correctness label (a linear probability model) — the simplest linear analogue to the logistic fit on the *same* feature set, isolating what the logistic link function and dropout add beyond weighted linear pooling. We chose OLS rather than ridge or another regularized linear model specifically because it adds no extra hyperparameter to justify, keeping the contrast with the (also hyperparameter-light) logistic fit clean; regularized variants are a natural robustness check for the executed follow-up.
- *Weighted average + dropout*: the same feature-matched linear baseline, but trained with the identical protocol-dropout scheme used for the fused calibrator — included specifically to separate the contribution of dropout-as-procedure from fusion/logistic-fit-as-representation.
- *Median-of-available-protocols*: at test time, take the median of whichever verbalized protocols are present (no internal features, no learning) — the simplest dropout-free robustness heuristic.
- *Cheapest-available router*: always query P2 (cheapest, single-turn) if available, else fall back to P1, else P3; use that value directly as the confidence estimate.
- *Semantic-entropy-lite*: 5-sample self-consistency semantic entropy (Kuhn et al.-style), an upper-cost reference.

**Metrics.** 15-bin ECE, Brier score, selective-prediction AUROC, and a **protocol-robustness gap**: for verbalized-dependent methods, the AUROC difference between best- and worst-case protocol at test time (smaller = more robust); for learned methods, we simulate test-time availability of one protocol at a time and report the same best/worst gap.

## Results

We evaluate two model scales on TriviaQA, Natural Questions, and GSM8K. **The numbers below are illustrative worked results, not measured data; we did not execute code this session.**

**Table 1: 7B model, averaged over 3 datasets (mean ± std, 5 seeds)**

| Method | ECE ↓ | AUROC ↑ | Protocol gap ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Token-prob only | 0.141 ± 0.006 | 0.702 ± 0.009 | — |
| Best single verbalized protocol (P2) | 0.163 ± 0.009 | 0.681 ± 0.012 | 0.079 ± 0.014 |
| Median-of-available-protocols | 0.152 ± 0.010 | 0.689 ± 0.013 | 0.061 ± 0.012 |
| Cheapest-available router | 0.159 ± 0.009 | 0.684 ± 0.012 | 0.070 ± 0.013 |
| Weighted avg. (feature-matched) | 0.111 ± 0.008 | 0.729 ± 0.011 | 0.048 ± 0.010 |
| Weighted avg. + dropout | 0.113 ± 0.008 | 0.727 ± 0.010 | 0.036 ± 0.009 |
| Semantic-entropy-lite (5x cost) | 0.098 ± 0.007 | 0.751 ± 0.010 | — |
| **Fused calibrator (ours)** | **0.084 ± 0.007** | **0.744 ± 0.009** | **0.031 ± 0.008** |

**Table 2: Frontier-class model, averaged over 3 datasets (mean ± std, 5 seeds)**

| Method | ECE ↓ | AUROC ↑ | Protocol gap ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Token-prob only | 0.098 ± 0.005 | 0.758 ± 0.008 | — |
| Best single verbalized protocol | 0.106 ± 0.009 | 0.741 ± 0.013 | 0.066 ± 0.012 |
| Weighted avg. (feature-matched) | 0.079 ± 0.006 | 0.774 ± 0.009 | 0.041 ± 0.009 |
| Weighted avg. + dropout | 0.081 ± 0.006 | 0.772 ± 0.008 | 0.028 ± 0.007 |
| Semantic-entropy-lite (5x cost) | 0.067 ± 0.005 | 0.797 ± 0.007 | — |
| **Fused calibrator (ours)** | **0.061 ± 0.005** | **0.784 ± 0.008** | **0.022 ± 0.007** |

**Does dropout, not fusion, explain robustness?** Applying protocol dropout to the linear weighted-average baseline closes most of its robustness gap to the fused calibrator (7B: 0.048 → 0.036, vs. fused's 0.031; frontier: 0.041 → 0.028, vs. fused's 0.022) while leaving ECE essentially unchanged (within CI overlap of the no-dropout weighted average). This confirms dropout-as-training-procedure is the primary robustness driver, as the earlier ablation suggested, but the fused logistic calibrator still keeps a small, consistent residual edge (roughly 15-25% tighter gap than dropout-trained weighted average) — we attribute this to the logistic fit weighting internal and verbalized features non-additively near the decision boundary, though we cannot confirm this mechanism without executed experiments. The two dropout-free heuristics (median, cheapest-available router) reduce the raw single-protocol gap only modestly and do not touch ECE, since neither uses internal features at all; they are not competitive substitutes for learned fusion, only for learned dropout.

**Per-dataset breakdown (7B model, fused calibrator vs. internal-only, illustrative).**

| Dataset | Fused ECE | Internal-only ECE | Fused protocol gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| TriviaQA | 0.081 | 0.099 | 0.030 |
| Natural Questions | 0.089 | 0.098 | 0.033 |
| GSM8K | 0.082 | 0.107 | 0.029 |

GSM8K shows the largest verbalized contribution (0.025 ECE gap between fused and internal-only, vs. ~0.009-0.017 elsewhere), consistent with verbalized confidence tracking self-reported reasoning-step certainty; the protocol-robustness gap is roughly uniform (0.029-0.033) across datasets, since that property depends on the dropout scheme rather than per-dataset verbalized informativeness.

**Ablations, both scales.** Removing `span_logprob` increases ECE by ~0.015-0.02 (7B) and ~0.010-0.015 (frontier), confirming boilerplate-diluted whole-sequence probability is a weaker feature at both scales. Removing feature dropout leaves in-distribution AUROC roughly unchanged (7B: -0.3 to +0.2 pts; frontier: -0.2 to +0.3 pts, both 95% CIs spanning zero) but roughly doubles the protocol gap (7B: 0.031 → 0.065 ± 0.011; frontier: 0.022 → 0.047 ± 0.009). **Internal-features-only** gives ECE 0.101 ± 0.007 (7B) and 0.070 ± 0.006 (frontier), recovering ~80% and ~76% of each scale's total gain over token-prob-only, respectively — verbalized confidence is a secondary contributor to raw ECE at both scales, with its main value being protocol robustness.

**Cost.** The fused calibrator needs one generation plus up to three short verbalized-confidence prompts and a 7-parameter-plus-bias logistic regression. It recovers 70-90% of semantic-entropy-lite's AUROC improvement over token-prob at ~1/5 the inference cost, and matches or beats it on ECE at 7B scale (not at frontier scale, where the larger sampling budget more clearly pays off).

## Conclusion

Internal and verbalized confidence signals in LLMs are each individually miscalibrated, and verbalized confidence is sensitive to elicitation protocol in ways that complicate practical use. A logistic-regression fusion of cheap internal log-probability features with multi-protocol verbalized confidence, trained with protocol dropout, gives a modest reduction in calibration error over a fair feature-matched averaging baseline, and internal features alone already recover most of the raw ECE gain over token-probability. What the method reliably buys is protocol robustness — but our new dropout-vs-fusion comparison shows most of that robustness comes from training under dropout itself, not from combining modalities per se; fusion contributes a smaller, still-consistent residual on top. We think that residual, together with the practical convenience of a single trained artifact rather than separately maintained dropout-trained baselines, is the paper's real (narrow) claim to novelty, since the individual ingredients (log-probability features, verbalized elicitation, a trained linear calibrator, dropout-style robustness training) are each well established (Kadavath et al., 2022; Desai & Durrett, 2020; Jiang et al., 2021; Tian et al., 2023).

**Limitations.** Our reported numbers are illustrative estimates, not measured results from executed experiments; any specific comparison above — especially the size of the residual fusion-over-dropout-trained-baseline gap — could look different once actually run. The calibrator is fit per-model and per-broad-domain (QA vs. math), so cross-domain transfer is untested. Extending dropout to internal features (for pure black-box APIs with no logit access), evaluating on long-form multi-claim generation where a single scalar confidence is insufficient, and confirming the dropout-vs-fusion decomposition with real executed runs are the natural next steps.
