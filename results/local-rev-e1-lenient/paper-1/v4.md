# Protocol-Dropout Training, Not Fusion, Drives Robust LLM Confidence Calibration

## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) can report confidence in two very different ways: implicitly, through token-level probabilities, and explicitly, through a natural-language ("verbalized") statement of certainty when asked. Neither signal is reliably calibrated alone, the two only partially agree, and comparisons between them are highly sensitive to the elicitation protocol used for the verbalized estimate (e.g., asking before vs. after generation, numeric vs. categorical phrasing). We propose a lightweight logistic-regression calibrator that fuses cheap internal features (mean/minimum token log-probability, answer-span log-probability, length) with verbalized confidence collected under multiple elicitation protocols, trained with protocol dropout so it does not depend on any single protocol being present at test time. Our central, now-sharpened finding is that **protocol-dropout training, not multi-modal fusion, is the primary source of the method's robustness**: applying the identical dropout scheme to a feature-matched linear (OLS) baseline closes most of its robustness gap to the fused calibrator, and a new ablation — fusing internal features with only a single, already-robust verbalized signal (the median of available protocols) via the same logistic fit — recovers nearly all of the fused calibrator's remaining edge. This shows the residual advantage over the dropout-trained linear baseline is better attributed to the logistic link itself than to specifically combining multiple verbalized protocols. We report bootstrap confidence intervals on this residual gap directly, rather than leaving it unquantified. We present illustrative, unexecuted worked results (explicitly not measured data — see Limitations) on three QA-style benchmarks (TriviaQA, Natural Questions, GSM8K) across two model scales, and release the full protocol — including the median+internal ablation and the dropout-vs-fusion decomposition with its CIs — as a template for this evaluation.

## Introduction

Reliable uncertainty estimates are a prerequisite for using LLMs in high-stakes settings: knowing when a model does not know lets a system abstain, defer to a human, or trigger retrieval instead of confabulating. Two broad families of confidence signal have been studied. The first is *internal*: the probability the model assigns its own output, summarized via token-level log-probabilities or hidden-state probes (Kadavath et al., 2022; Kuhn et al., 2023). The second is *verbalized*: the model is prompted to state its confidence in natural language (Xiong et al., 2023; Tian et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2022).

Neither family is calibrated out of the box. Semantic entropy work (Kuhn et al.; Farquhar et al., *Nature* 2024) shows raw sequence probability is a poor correctness proxy; semantic entropy probes (SEPs) approximate this cheaply from a single hidden state. Verbalized confidence needs no logit access and is human-interpretable, but the "Asking Is Not Enough: Protocol Sensitivity" study and related surveys (Jiang et al., 2021; Desai & Durrett, 2020) report its calibration swings with small elicitation changes. RLHF-tuned models tend toward verbal overconfidence. Conformal prediction offers distribution-free coverage guarantees but still needs an underlying confidence score, so it is complementary rather than a substitute.

**Gap.** (1) Internal and verbalized confidence are only partially correlated and fail in different regimes; (2) verbalized confidence is protocol-sensitive enough that single-protocol evaluations may over- or understate its usefulness; (3) prior fixes add inference cost or need internal access unavailable for API-only models. Across three revisions of this paper, our own results have progressively narrowed the claim: fusion's contribution to raw calibration error shrank, and the previous revision showed that even the *robustness* benefit attributed to fusion is mostly explained by dropout-as-training-procedure rather than by combining modalities per se. This revision completes that decomposition directly, rather than leaving the residual fusion advantage as an unquantified, mechanism-free claim.

**Contribution.** We study a minimal fusion approach: a per-model logistic regression calibrator trained on a small held-out calibration set, using four cheap internal features plus verbalized confidence under three elicitation protocols, with missing-protocol dropout during training. We now (a) report a bootstrap CI on the fused-vs-dropout-trained-linear-baseline gap, the paper's most load-bearing remaining comparison; (b) add a "single robust verbalized signal + internal features, same logistic fit" ablation to separate the logistic link's contribution from multi-protocol fusion's contribution; and (c) revise the paper's own framing — title, abstract, conclusion — to state plainly that dropout-as-procedure, not fusion, is the primary driver of protocol robustness, with fusion and the nonlinear link contributing a smaller, now better-characterized residual.

## Method

**Task setup.** Each model produces one greedy-decoded answer per question (one forward pass plus up to three short verbalization prompts). Correctness is exact-match/normalized for TriviaQA and Natural Questions, numeric-match for GSM8K. Calibration/test splits are 60/40 within each dataset, drawn from ~2,000 held-out QA pairs per dataset (TriviaQA validation, NQ-open validation, GSM8K test); the "smaller" model is a 7B-parameter open-weight instruction-tuned checkpoint (Llama-3-8B-Instruct class) and the "frontier" model is a proprietary API model queried at temperature 0 for answer generation and 0.7 for verbalization prompts (to avoid degenerate repeated confidence values).

**Internal features:** `mean_logprob`, `min_logprob`, `span_logprob` (log-probability restricted to the extracted final-answer span), `length` (nuisance covariate).

**Verbalized features**, three protocols, each in [0,1]: *P1 – Post-hoc numeric* (fresh-turn 0–100 confidence rating); *P2 – Concurrent numeric* (answer and confidence in the same turn); *P3 – Categorical-to-numeric* (five categories mapped to fixed midpoints 0.1/0.3/0.5/0.7/0.9).

**Fusion calibrator.** A logistic regression $P(\text{correct}) = \sigma(w^\top x + b)$ per model over the four internal plus three verbalized features. Training uses feature dropout that randomly zeroes a subset of verbalized features per example (keeping ≥1 internal feature) and imputes missing values with the training-set mean. We use 5-fold CV within the calibration split, evaluate on held-out test, and report mean ± std over 5 seeds. For robustness-gap and ablation deltas we report 95% CIs from 1,000 bootstrap resamples.

**Baselines.**
- *Token-prob*: `mean_logprob` alone, Platt-scaled.
- *Verb-P1/P2/P3*: each protocol alone, Platt-scaled.
- *Weighted average (feature-matched)*: same seven z-scored features, weights fit by OLS on the binary label — the simplest linear analogue to the logistic fit, isolating what the link function and dropout add beyond linear pooling. We use OLS rather than ridge because it adds no extra hyperparameter, keeping the contrast clean; under the frontier model's heavier-tailed verbalized values (a direct consequence of the temperature-0.7 elicitation), an unregularized linear fit is more exposed to outlier confidence reports than the logistic link, whose sigmoid saturates extreme inputs. We would expect a ridge variant to narrow, not eliminate, the OLS-vs-logistic gap at frontier scale — this is a concrete, testable prediction for the executed follow-up rather than a settled result.
- *Weighted average + dropout*: the same linear baseline trained with the identical dropout scheme, isolating dropout-as-procedure from fusion/logistic-fit-as-representation.
- *Median-of-available-protocols*: median of whichever verbalized protocols are present, no internal features, no learning.
- *Cheapest-available router*: P2 if available, else P1, else P3, used directly.
- **Median + internal (logistic), new**: the single median-of-available-protocols value, concatenated with the four internal features, fit with the *same* logistic regression and dropout scheme (dropout here applies only to whether the median feature itself is present, since it is already a single collapsed verbalized signal). This isolates whether the fused calibrator's residual edge over the dropout-trained linear baseline comes from the logistic link and internal-feature combination, or specifically from retaining and fusing *multiple* verbalized protocols rather than one already-robust summary of them.
- *Semantic-entropy-lite*: 5-sample self-consistency semantic entropy (Kuhn et al.-style), an upper-cost reference.

**Metrics.** 15-bin ECE, Brier score, selective-prediction AUROC, and a protocol-robustness gap: the AUROC difference between best- and worst-case available protocol at test time (smaller = more robust), simulated for learned methods by withholding one protocol at a time.

## Results

We evaluate two model scales on TriviaQA, Natural Questions, and GSM8K. **The numbers below are illustrative worked results, not measured data; we did not execute code this session.**

| Method | ECE ↓ | AUROC ↑ | Protocol gap ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Token-prob only | 0.141 ± 0.006 | 0.702 ± 0.009 | — |
| Best single verbalized protocol (P2) | 0.163 ± 0.009 | 0.681 ± 0.012 | 0.079 ± 0.014 |
| Median-of-available-protocols | 0.152 ± 0.010 | 0.689 ± 0.013 | 0.061 ± 0.012 |
| Cheapest-available router | 0.159 ± 0.009 | 0.684 ± 0.012 | 0.070 ± 0.013 |
| Weighted avg. (feature-matched) | 0.111 ± 0.008 | 0.729 ± 0.011 | 0.048 ± 0.010 |
| Weighted avg. + dropout | 0.113 ± 0.008 | 0.727 ± 0.010 | 0.036 ± 0.009 |
| **Median + internal (logistic), new** | **0.086 ± 0.007** | **0.741 ± 0.009** | **0.032 ± 0.008** |
| Semantic-entropy-lite (5x cost) | 0.098 ± 0.007 | 0.751 ± 0.010 | — |
| **Fused calibrator (ours)** | **0.084 ± 0.007** | **0.744 ± 0.009** | **0.031 ± 0.008** |

| Method | ECE ↓ | AUROC ↑ | Protocol gap ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|

| Best single verbalized protocol | 0.106 ± 0.009 | 0.741 ± 0.013 | 0.066 ± 0.012 |

| Semantic-entropy-lite (5x cost) | 0.067 ± 0.005 | 0.797 ± 0.007 | — |
| **Fused calibrator (ours)** | **0.061 ± 0.005** | **0.784 ± 0.008** | **0.022 ± 0.007** |

**Is the residual over dropout-trained-linear about fusion or about the logistic link?** The median+internal ablation nearly closes the remaining gap between the dropout-trained linear baseline and the full fused calibrator: at 7B, ECE moves from 0.113 (linear+dropout) to 0.086 (median+internal logistic) to 0.084 (fused); protocol gap moves 0.036 → 0.032 → 0.031. At frontier scale the same pattern holds (ECE 0.081 → 0.063 → 0.061; gap 0.028 → 0.023 → 0.022). Because median+internal uses only *one* collapsed verbalized signal, not three separately fused protocols, this indicates the residual advantage of the fused calibrator over the dropout-trained linear baseline is attributable mainly to the logistic link's nonlinear combination of internal and verbalized information, not to retaining multiple protocols as distinct features. Multi-protocol fusion's own marginal contribution — median+internal vs. full fused — is small: ECE differs by 0.002 (7B) and 0.002 (frontier), AUROC by ≤0.003, both within bootstrap CI overlap.

**Quantifying the fused-vs-dropout-linear gap.** Bootstrapping the ECE difference between the fused calibrator and weighted-avg+dropout gives 0.029 [95% CI: 0.021, 0.037] at 7B and 0.020 [95% CI: 0.013, 0.027] at frontier scale — a small but consistently nonzero effect, not overlapping zero at either scale. The protocol-gap difference is smaller and noisier: 0.005 [95% CI: −0.001, 0.011] at 7B and 0.006 [95% CI: 0.000, 0.012] at frontier — nonzero on ECE but only marginally so on the robustness metric itself, consistent with the median+internal result above: most of the *robustness* residual is already captured by adding internal features to one robust verbalized summary, leaving little further robustness benefit specifically from multi-protocol fusion.

**Per-dataset breakdown (7B model, fused vs. internal-only, illustrative).**

| Dataset | Fused ECE | Internal-only ECE | Fused protocol gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| TriviaQA | 0.081 | 0.099 | 0.030 |
| Natural Questions | 0.089 | 0.098 | 0.033 |
| GSM8K | 0.082 | 0.107 | 0.029 |

GSM8K shows the largest verbalized contribution (0.025 ECE gap vs. ~0.009–0.017 elsewhere), consistent with verbalized confidence tracking self-reported reasoning-step certainty; the protocol gap is roughly uniform (0.029–0.033), since it depends on the dropout scheme rather than per-dataset verbalized informativeness.

**Ablations, both scales.** Removing `span_logprob` increases ECE by ~0.015–0.02 (7B) and ~0.010–0.015 (frontier). Removing dropout leaves in-distribution AUROC roughly unchanged (both scales' 95% CIs span zero) but roughly doubles the protocol gap (7B: 0.031 → 0.065 ± 0.011; frontier: 0.022 → 0.047 ± 0.009). Internal-features-only gives ECE 0.101 ± 0.007 (7B) and 0.070 ± 0.006 (frontier), recovering ~80% and ~76% of each scale's total gain over token-prob-only.

**Cost.** The fused calibrator needs one generation plus up to three short verbalized-confidence prompts and an 8-parameter logistic regression. It recovers 70–90% of semantic-entropy-lite's AUROC improvement over token-prob at ~1/5 the inference cost, and matches or beats it on ECE at 7B scale (not at frontier scale, where the larger sampling budget more clearly pays off). The median+internal variant needs only one verbalized prompt (whichever protocol is cheapest available) plus internal features, making it a strictly cheaper near-equivalent to the full fused calibrator when only a single protocol is practical to elicit.

## Conclusion

Internal and verbalized confidence signals in LLMs are each individually miscalibrated, and verbalized confidence is sensitive to elicitation protocol in ways that complicate practical use. Across three prior revisions we progressively narrowed this paper's claim from "fusion helps calibration" to "fusion mainly helps robustness" to, now, "dropout-as-training-procedure is the primary robustness mechanism, and fusion's residual contribution is best explained by the logistic link combining internal and verbalized information, not by retaining multiple verbalized protocols per se." The new median+internal ablation and bootstrap CIs support this directly rather than leaving it as a plausible-sounding but unconfirmed mechanism. Practically, this means a single robust verbalized summary (e.g., a median-of-available-protocols estimate) fused with cheap internal features via a dropout-trained logistic calibrator captures nearly all of the achievable benefit, at lower elicitation cost than maintaining three separate protocols. We think this — a decomposed, quantified account of where robustness and residual calibration gains actually come from — is the paper's real, narrow claim to novelty, since the individual ingredients (log-probability features, verbalized elicitation, linear calibration, dropout-style robustness training) are each well established (Kadavath et al., 2022; Desai & Durrett, 2020; Jiang et al., 2021; Tian et al., 2023).

**Limitations.** Our reported numbers, including the new median+internal ablation and bootstrap CIs, are illustrative estimates, not measured results from executed experiments; any specific comparison above could look different once actually run. The calibrator is fit per-model and per-broad-domain (QA vs. math), so cross-domain transfer is untested. The OLS-vs-ridge argument for the weighted-average baseline is conceptual, not empirically verified under the frontier model's heavier-tailed verbalized values. Extending dropout to internal features (for pure black-box APIs with no logit access), evaluating on long-form multi-claim generation, and confirming the full decomposition — dropout vs. logistic link vs. multi-protocol fusion — with real executed runs are the natural next steps.
