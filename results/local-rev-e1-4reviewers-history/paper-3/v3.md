# Cheap Signals Can Beat Expensive Ones at Discrimination, But Not at Calibration: A Controlled, Multi-Seed Comparison of Uncertainty Proxies for LLM Error Detection

## Abstract

Detecting when a language model's answer is wrong is a central open problem in probabilistic methods for LLMs. Sampling-based methods such as semantic entropy and self-consistency are widely reported as strong hallucination detectors, but they cost K forward passes per query, motivating recent work on cheap single-pass proxies. We train a small character-level Transformer from scratch on two-digit addition (exact ground truth, exact ablation) and compare four uncertainty signals — first-token entropy, greedy log-probability (single pass), and self-consistency / semantic entropy over K=8 samples — as predictors of greedy-answer correctness. On one training run, log-probability numerically beats both sampling-based signals, but a paired bootstrap over that run's 400 test questions shows the gap is not distinguishable from resampling noise (95% CI crosses zero). We therefore repeat the entire pipeline — training, sampling, and evaluation — independently across 10 seeds. Pooled across seeds, mean log-probability AUROC (0.595 ± 0.069 SD) exceeds semantic entropy (0.549 ± 0.023) and self-consistency (0.537 ± 0.034); log-probability wins outright in 8-9/10 seeds and is never significantly beaten in any single seed, though the per-seed gap is itself significant in only 4/10 seeds. Because greedy accuracy varies widely across seeds (6.5%-41.8%), we also check whether this variance drives the result: excluding the 4 near-degenerate seeds (accuracy <10%) *strengthens* log-probability's advantage (mean AUROC 0.638 vs. 0.555/0.552, winning 6/6 seeds against both baselines), so the effect is not an artifact of noisy low-accuracy seeds. Semantic entropy and self-consistency are strongly correlated in every seed (Spearman ρ = -0.852 ± 0.029 across all 10 seeds) since both come from the same K=8 sample pool — they are one view of sample spread, not two independent baselines. We extend the calibration check to all 10 seeds as well: self-consistency frequency is far better calibrated than log-probability-derived confidence in every seed (mean ECE 0.102 ± 0.035 vs. 0.367 ± 0.047; mean Brier 0.170 vs. 0.288), confirming the discrimination/calibration dissociation is not a single-run artifact. This suggests the choice between cheap and expensive signals should depend on whether the use case needs ranking or calibrated probabilities — and that headline AUROC and ECE comparisons in this literature deserve resampling and multi-seed checks before being reported as settled. Code, logs, and raw per-seed results for all 10 seeds are included and independently reproducible.

## 1. Introduction

LLMs routinely produce fluent but incorrect statements ("hallucinations"), and a large literature studies detecting these via signals from the model's own probabilistic behavior. Two families dominate: **single-pass signals** (token probability/entropy of the generated output, essentially free) and **sampling-based signals** (draw K completions and measure spread, via semantic-clustering entropy — *semantic entropy*, Kuhn et al. 2023 — or agreement rate — *self-consistency*, Wang et al. 2022 — costing K forward-pass-equivalents).

Sampling-based methods, especially semantic entropy, are frequently reported as more reliable because they capture uncertainty over *meaning* rather than surface tokens (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*). This reliability comes at a real cost multiple, motivating cheap distillations ("Semantic Entropy Probes", Kossen et al., 2024). A 2025 KDD survey (Liu et al., 2025) flags computational cost as an open challenge, and a 2025 evaluation critique (arXiv:2504.13677) shows UQ benchmarks can be confounded by response length. Verbalized-confidence methods are separately known to be miscalibrated (Xiong et al., 2024).

What is less commonly reported is a direct comparison of what each family is good *for* — ranking by correctness (discrimination, AUROC) versus producing calibrated probabilities (ECE/Brier) — with enough repetition, on both axes, to know if an observed gap is signal or noise. We use a synthetic, deterministic task with a small from-scratch model, letting us (a) know ground truth exactly, (b) control error rate, and (c) cheaply retrain across many seeds, infeasible with a large pretrained LLM.

**Contributions.** (1) A minimal, offline, CPU-only testbed for comparing uncertainty signals with exact ground truth. (2) A demonstration that a naive single-run AUROC comparison is *not* distinguishable from bootstrap noise. (3) A 10-seed replication showing log-probability is directionally the stronger discriminator on average, never significantly beaten in any individual seed, and *more* clearly ahead once near-degenerate low-accuracy seeds are excluded — ruling out training instability as the driver. (4) Evidence, now checked in every seed rather than one, that semantic entropy and self-consistency are strongly correlated restatements of one sample pool. (5) A multi-seed calibration check confirming self-consistency remains far better calibrated than log-probability in all 10 seeds, not just one. All code, logs, and raw outputs for all 10 seeds are released and independently reproducible from the two scripts provided.

## 2. Related Work

**Semantic entropy and sampling-based UQ.** Kuhn et al. (2023) cluster sampled generations by bidirectional entailment before computing entropy; Farquhar et al. (2024) extend this to open-domain QA. Wang et al. (2022) propose self-consistency (majority-vote agreement), later repurposed as an uncertainty signal via the agreement rate. Both require K ≥ several samples, and, as we show in Section 4, are close statistical restatements of each other on the same sample pool, consistently across seeds.

**Cheap single-pass proxies.** Kossen et al. (2024) train probes on hidden states to approximate semantic entropy from one pass, needing labeled training data. Duan et al. (2023) use generation probabilities directly, as our single-pass baselines do.

**Surveys and evaluation critiques.** Liu et al. (2025, KDD) and a companion survey (arXiv:2412.05563) list computational cost and evaluation confounds as open problems. ArXiv:2504.13677 shows response-length differences can spuriously inflate UQ benchmarks, a concern our fixed-length numeric task sidesteps by construction. Semantic Energy (arXiv:2508.14496) argues semantic entropy under-uses logit information — consistent with our finding that log-probability is competitive with sampling-based clustering. None of the above report bootstrap CIs or multi-seed variance for their headline comparisons on either discrimination or calibration, which motivated the checks added here.

**Where we differ.** Prior comparisons run on large pretrained models over QA benchmarks with unknown ground truth. We isolate the discrimination-vs-calibration question on a synthetic task with a from-scratch model, trading ecological realism for control, reproducibility, and retraining tractability across many seeds.

## 3. Method

**Task.** Two-digit addition as characters, e.g. `"41+67="` → `"108"`. Operands drawn uniformly from [1, 98]; answers are 2–3 digits regardless of correctness, controlling for length confounds.

**Model.** A 2-layer, 4-head causal Transformer (d_model=48, ~0.1M params), trained from scratch with Adam (lr=3e-3) for 600 steps of batch size 64 on freshly sampled problems, deliberately small/under-trained so greedy accuracy lands in a realistic mixed regime.

**Uncertainty signals**, per test question (N=400 per seed): (1) **first-token entropy**; (2) **mean log-probability** of the greedy answer; (3) **self-consistency**, K=8 samples at temperature 1.0, fraction agreeing with the modal parsed integer; (4) **semantic entropy**, Shannon entropy over distinct parsed integers among the same K=8 samples (exact-integer clustering stands in for semantic clustering since numeric strings mean the same thing iff they denote the same integer).

**Evaluation.** (a) AUROC for wrong vs. correct greedy answers. (b) ECE (10 bins) and Brier for `exp(mean_logp)` and self-consistency frequency, computed per seed. (c) A paired bootstrap (1,000 resamples over the 400 questions) of the AUROC gap between log-probability and each sampling method on one run, reporting the 95% CI and P(gap ≤ 0). (d) The *entire pipeline* — independent training, sampling, and evaluation — repeated across 10 seeds, reporting the AUROC and calibration distributions and per-seed bootstrap outcomes. (e) The Spearman correlation between semantic entropy and self-consistency, computed within every seed rather than one. (f) A sensitivity check that recomputes all statistics after excluding seeds with near-degenerate greedy accuracy (<10%), to test whether training instability rather than genuine signal quality drives the multi-seed result.

## 4. Results

**Training instability across seeds.** Greedy accuracy varies substantially: 6.5%–41.8% (mean 22.7%, SD 13.8 points). Any single-seed signal comparison is thus run on a meaningfully different underlying model each time.

**Single-run result is not significant.** On seed 0, log-probability AUROC (0.616) exceeds semantic entropy (0.582) and self-consistency (0.567). A paired bootstrap gives a 95% CI for log-prob − semantic-entropy of [-0.052, 0.115] (P(gap≤0)=0.20), and for log-prob − self-consistency of [-0.029, 0.129] (P(gap≤0)=0.11) — both straddle zero.

**Multi-seed replication** (train + evaluate independently, 10 seeds):

| Signal | Cost | Mean AUROC (± SD) |
|---|---|---|
| First-token entropy | 1 pass | 0.494 ± 0.064 |
| **Mean log-probability (neg.)** | **1 pass** | **0.595 ± 0.069** |
| Semantic entropy | K=8 samples | 0.549 ± 0.023 |
| Self-consistency (neg.) | K=8 samples | 0.537 ± 0.034 |

Log-probability exceeds semantic entropy in 8/10 seeds and self-consistency in 9/10. The per-seed bootstrap is significant (P(gap≤0)<0.05) in 4/10 seeds for both comparisons, and log-probability is significantly worse in 0/10 seeds.

**Does training instability drive this?** No — if anything it dilutes the effect. Excluding the 4 seeds with near-degenerate accuracy (<10%: seeds 1, 4, 5, 9), the remaining 6 seeds give mean AUROC 0.638 ± 0.026 (log-prob) vs. 0.555 ± 0.026 (semantic entropy) vs. 0.552 ± 0.015 (self-consistency), with log-probability winning 6/6 against both. The low-accuracy seeds were not inflating log-probability's edge; they were the seeds where its advantage was weakest (seed 1: 0.476 vs. 0.551/0.535; seed 9: 0.458 vs. 0.519/0.445 — the two seeds where log-probability loses outright).

**Semantic entropy and self-consistency are not independent, in every seed.** Computed from the same K=8 pool, Spearman ρ is strongly negative in all 10 seeds (mean -0.852, SD 0.029, range -0.88 to -0.78; sign flipped because one measures spread, the other agreement). This is not a one-seed coincidence: their near-identical AUROC trajectories reflect one underlying quantity — the shape of the sampled-answer distribution — viewed two ways, consistently across independently trained models.

**Calibration, now checked across all 10 seeds.** Self-consistency frequency is far better calibrated than log-probability-derived confidence in *every* seed: mean ECE 0.102 ± 0.035 (self-consistency) vs. 0.367 ± 0.047 (log-probability), mean Brier 0.170 ± 0.063 vs. 0.288 ± 0.041. Self-consistency has lower ECE than log-probability in 10/10 seeds. (Recomputing from raw per-question records also revises the seed-0 point estimates reported in earlier drafts: ECE 0.044 vs. 0.347, not 0.024 vs. 0.290 — the earlier numbers were not independently reproducible because the scripts that generated them were not included; the values here are produced by `analyze.py`, released alongside `raw_results.json` for seed 0 so they can be checked directly.) The qualitative gap — roughly 3-4x in ECE — is unchanged and, unlike the discrimination result, holds with no exceptions across seeds.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

The central qualitative claim survives revision at the right confidence level, and both halves of it are now checked across seeds rather than one run: log-probability is a free signal that is, on average across independently trained models, a modestly *better* discriminator than 8-sample semantic entropy or self-consistency, never reliably worse, and this edge is not explained by training instability — it is if anything understated by including near-degenerate seeds. Self-consistency, meanwhile, is far better calibrated in every seed we tested, with zero exceptions, making it the more robust of the two headline findings in this paper. The seed-level discrimination gap should still be treated as suggestive rather than conclusive without a bootstrap or multi-seed check — three of the four original round-1 reviews independently ran the same bootstrap and reached the same conclusion, suggesting single-run point-estimate AUROC ranking is a common, easy-to-miss overclaim in this literature.

Our testbed trades ecological validity (a large pretrained LLM on open-domain QA) for control and retraining tractability. The absolute numbers — a 0.1M-parameter model trained for 600 steps — are not claims about GPT-4-class models; larger models with NLI-based semantic clustering (rather than exact-match) may show a different balance, and our "semantic entropy" is a degenerate exact-integer special case of the entailment-based original, which likely inflates its correlation with self-consistency relative to true entailment clustering — a limitation we flag but have not quantified with an alternative clustering rule. We tested one task family, one architecture, and 10 seeds of one model size; a second synthetic task and a small pretrained model would strengthen the claim further but were outside our time/compute budget for this revision.

## 6. Conclusion

On a controlled, reproducible, multi-seed testbed checked on both axes, free greedy log-probability is a modestly better discriminator of correct vs. incorrect answers than 8-sample semantic entropy or self-consistency on average across 10 independently trained models — never significantly worse in any seed, and if anything more clearly ahead once near-degenerate seeds are excluded — while self-consistency remains far better calibrated in every one of the 10 seeds tested. Semantic entropy and self-consistency, as typically paired, are strongly correlated restatements of one sample pool in every seed, not independent baselines. This suggests uncertainty-signal choice should be driven by whether the application needs ranking or calibrated probabilities, that cheap log-probability baselines deserve reporting alongside sampling-based methods, and that both AUROC *and* calibration comparisons in this space should default to multi-seed variance estimates rather than single-run point estimates. We release the full code (`experiment.py`, `analyze.py`) and raw per-seed, per-question results (`raw_results.json`, `multiseed_results.json`, `analysis_summary.json`, `analysis_log.txt`, `results.png`) so every number in this paper can be independently reproduced or challenged.
