# Cheap Signals Can Beat Expensive Ones at Discrimination, But Not at Calibration: A Controlled, Multi-Seed Comparison of Uncertainty Proxies for LLM Error Detection

## Abstract

Detecting when a language model's answer is wrong is a central open problem in probabilistic methods for LLMs. Sampling-based methods such as semantic entropy and self-consistency are widely reported as strong hallucination detectors, but they cost K forward passes per query, motivating recent work on cheap single-pass proxies. We train a small character-level Transformer from scratch on two-digit addition (exact ground truth, exact ablation) and compare four uncertainty signals — first-token entropy, greedy log-probability (single pass), and self-consistency / semantic entropy over K=8 samples — as predictors of greedy-answer correctness. On one training run, log-probability numerically beats both sampling-based signals, but a paired bootstrap over that run's 400 test questions shows the gap is not distinguishable from resampling noise (95% CI crosses zero) — a check the original version of this paper omitted. We therefore repeated the entire pipeline across 10 independently trained models. Pooled across seeds, mean log-probability AUROC (0.595 ± 0.069 SD) exceeds semantic entropy (0.549 ± 0.023) and self-consistency (0.537 ± 0.034); log-probability wins outright in 8-9/10 seeds and is never *significantly* beaten in any single seed (0/10), even though within any one seed the margin is often within bootstrap noise (significant in only 4/10 seeds). We also find semantic entropy and self-consistency are strongly correlated (Spearman ρ=-0.85, sign flipped because one measures spread and the other agreement) since both come from the same K=8 sample pool — they are one view of sample spread, not two independent baselines. Self-consistency frequency remains dramatically better calibrated (ECE=0.024) than log-probability-derived confidence (ECE=0.290). This dissociation between discrimination and calibration, now backed by both within-run and cross-seed uncertainty quantification, suggests the choice between cheap and expensive signals should depend on whether the use case needs ranking or calibrated probabilities — and that headline AUROC gaps in this literature deserve resampling and multi-seed checks before being reported as settled. Code, logs, and raw results for all 10 seeds are included.

## 1. Introduction

LLMs routinely produce fluent but incorrect statements ("hallucinations"), and a large literature studies detecting these via signals from the model's own probabilistic behavior. Two families dominate: **single-pass signals** (token probability/entropy of the generated output, essentially free) and **sampling-based signals** (draw K completions and measure spread, via semantic-clustering entropy — *semantic entropy*, Kuhn et al. 2023 — or agreement rate — *self-consistency*, Wang et al. 2022 — costing K forward-pass-equivalents).

Sampling-based methods, especially semantic entropy, are frequently reported as more reliable because they capture uncertainty over *meaning* rather than surface tokens (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*). This reliability comes at a real cost multiple, motivating cheap distillations ("Semantic Entropy Probes", Kossen et al., 2024). A 2025 KDD survey (Liu et al., 2025) flags computational cost as an open challenge, and a 2025 evaluation critique (arXiv:2504.13677) shows UQ benchmarks can be confounded by response length — underscoring that head-to-head comparisons need controlled testbeds *and* variance quantification. Verbalized-confidence methods are separately known to be miscalibrated (Xiong et al., 2024).

What is less commonly reported is a direct comparison of what each family is good *for* — ranking by correctness (discrimination, AUROC) versus producing calibrated probabilities (ECE/Brier) — with enough repetition to know if an observed gap is signal or noise. We use a synthetic, deterministic task with a small from-scratch model, letting us (a) know ground truth exactly, (b) control error rate, and (c) cheaply retrain across many seeds, infeasible with a large pretrained LLM.

**Contributions.** (1) A minimal, offline, CPU-only testbed for comparing uncertainty signals with exact ground truth. (2) A demonstration that a naive single-run AUROC comparison of log-probability vs. sampling-based signals is *not* distinguishable from bootstrap noise, and that this check matters. (3) A 10-seed replication showing log-probability is directionally the stronger discriminator on average and never significantly beaten in any individual seed — a more defensible discrimination claim. (4) Evidence that semantic entropy and self-consistency, as commonly paired, are strongly correlated restatements of one sample pool, not independent methods. (5) Confirmation that self-consistency remains ~12x better calibrated than log-probability. All code and raw outputs, including all 10 seeds, are released.

## 2. Related Work

**Semantic entropy and sampling-based UQ.** Kuhn et al. (2023) cluster sampled generations by bidirectional entailment before computing entropy; Farquhar et al. (2024) extend this to open-domain QA. Wang et al. (2022) propose self-consistency (majority-vote agreement), later repurposed as an uncertainty signal via the agreement rate. Both require K ≥ several samples, and, as we show in Section 4, are close statistical restatements of each other on the same sample pool.

**Cheap single-pass proxies.** Kossen et al. (2024) train probes on hidden states to approximate semantic entropy from one pass, needing labeled training data. Duan et al. (2023) use generation probabilities directly, as our single-pass baselines do.

**Surveys and evaluation critiques.** Liu et al. (2025, KDD) and a companion survey (arXiv:2412.05563) list computational cost and evaluation confounds as open problems. ArXiv:2504.13677 shows response-length differences can spuriously inflate UQ benchmarks, a concern our fixed-length numeric task sidesteps by construction. Semantic Energy (arXiv:2508.14496) argues semantic entropy under-uses logit information — consistent with our finding that log-probability is competitive with sampling-based clustering. None of the above report bootstrap CIs or multi-seed variance for their headline comparisons, which motivated the checks added here.

**Where we differ.** Prior comparisons run on large pretrained models over QA benchmarks with unknown ground truth. We isolate the discrimination-vs-calibration question on a synthetic task with a from-scratch model, trading ecological realism for control, reproducibility, and — new in this revision — retraining tractability across many seeds.

## 3. Method

**Task.** Two-digit addition as characters, e.g. `"41+67="` → `"108"`. Operands drawn uniformly from [1, 98]; answers are 2–3 digits regardless of correctness, controlling for length confounds.

**Model.** A 2-layer, 4-head causal Transformer (d_model=48, ~0.1M params), trained from scratch with Adam (lr=3e-3) for 600 steps of batch size 64 on freshly sampled problems, deliberately small/under-trained so greedy accuracy lands in a realistic mixed regime.

**Uncertainty signals**, per test question (N=400 per seed): (1) **first-token entropy**; (2) **mean log-probability** of the greedy answer; (3) **self-consistency**, K=8 samples at temperature 1.0, fraction agreeing with the modal parsed integer; (4) **semantic entropy**, Shannon entropy over distinct parsed integers among the same K=8 samples (exact-integer clustering stands in for semantic clustering since numeric strings mean the same thing iff they denote the same integer).

**Evaluation.** (a) AUROC for wrong vs. correct greedy answers. (b) ECE (10 bins) and Brier for `exp(mean_logp)` and self-consistency frequency. (c) **New: significance and multi-seed checks.** For a single run we bootstrap (1,000 paired resamples over the 400 questions) the AUROC gap between log-probability and each sampling method, reporting the 95% CI and P(gap ≤ 0). We then repeat the *entire pipeline* — independent training and evaluation — across 10 seeds, reporting the resulting AUROC distribution and per-seed bootstrap outcomes, the only way to distinguish "this signal is generally better" from "this one model happened to favor it." We also report the Spearman correlation between semantic entropy and self-consistency within a seed, to quantify how independent these two baselines actually are.

## 4. Results

**Training instability across seeds.** Greedy accuracy varies substantially across the 10 seeds: 6.5%–41.8% (mean 22.7%, SD 13.8 points). Any single-seed signal comparison is thus run on a meaningfully different underlying model each time — exactly why single-run comparisons need the checks below.

**Single-run result is not significant.** On seed 0, log-probability AUROC (0.616) exceeds semantic entropy (0.582) and self-consistency (0.567). A paired bootstrap (1,000 resamples) gives a 95% CI for log-prob − semantic-entropy of [-0.052, 0.115] (P(gap≤0)=0.20), and for log-prob − self-consistency of [-0.029, 0.129] (P(gap≤0)=0.11) — both straddle zero. The original version of this paper reported the point-estimate ranking as settled without this check; it does not survive resampling on a single run.

**Multi-seed replication** (train + evaluate independently, 10 seeds):

| Signal | Cost | Mean AUROC (± SD across seeds) |
|---|---|---|
| First-token entropy | 1 pass | 0.494 ± 0.064 |
| **Mean log-probability (neg.)** | **1 pass** | **0.595 ± 0.069** |
| Semantic entropy | K=8 samples | 0.549 ± 0.023 |
| Self-consistency (neg.) | K=8 samples | 0.537 ± 0.034 |

Log-probability's AUROC exceeds semantic entropy's in 8/10 seeds and self-consistency's in 9/10. Repeating the within-seed bootstrap at each seed, the log-probability advantage is significant (P(gap≤0)<0.05) in 4/10 seeds for both comparisons, and **log-probability is significantly worse in 0/10 seeds**. So while any individual run's gap is frequently within noise (as all four original reviews independently found via their own bootstraps on the seed-0-style run), the sign of the effect is consistent enough across independently trained models that it is not an artifact of one lucky run — a real but modest, seed-dependent advantage, not the "beats it at every budget" result we originally claimed.

**Semantic entropy and self-consistency are not independent.** Computed from the same K=8 pool, the two are strongly related: Spearman ρ = -0.85 (p≈2×10⁻¹¹⁴, seed 2, N=400; sign flipped because one measures spread, the other agreement). Their near-identical AUROC trajectories in a cost-accuracy sweep are therefore largely two views of one quantity — the shape of the sampled-answer distribution — not two independently corroborating methods.

**Calibration.** Unchanged, and the paper's most robust result: self-consistency frequency is far better calibrated than log-probability-derived confidence — ECE=0.024 vs. 0.290 (Brier 0.213 vs. 0.293) on the seed-0 run. `exp(mean_logp)` is systematically overconfident (e.g., 0.389 empirical accuracy in the [0.6,0.7) confidence bin), while self-consistency tracks accuracy closely in every bin. Some bins are sparse (n=1–3 in the tails), adding unquantified noise there, but the aggregate 12x gap dwarfs plausible bin-count noise.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

The central qualitative claim survives revision, now stated at the right confidence level: log-probability is a free signal that is, on average across independently trained models, a modestly *better* discriminator than 8-sample semantic entropy or self-consistency, and is never reliably worse — but the effect is small enough relative to model-to-model variance that any single run's comparison should be treated as suggestive, not conclusive, without a bootstrap or multi-seed check. This is as much a methodological point as an empirical one: three of the four original reviews independently ran the same bootstrap and reached the same conclusion, suggesting "single-run, point-estimate AUROC ranking" is a common, easy-to-miss overclaim in this literature.

Our testbed trades ecological validity (a large pretrained LLM on open-domain QA) for control and retraining tractability. The absolute numbers — a 0.1M-parameter model trained for 600 steps — are not claims about GPT-4-class models; larger models with NLI-based semantic clustering (rather than exact-match) may show a different balance, and our "semantic entropy" is a degenerate exact-integer special case of the entailment-based original. What we believe transfers: discrimination and calibration are different axes a sampling budget does not automatically improve together; log-probability deserves reporting as a baseline; and headline AUROC comparisons in UQ work — ours included — need resampling and ideally multi-seed checks before being treated as settled. We tested one task family, one architecture, and 10 seeds of one model size; a second synthetic task and a small pretrained model would strengthen the claim further but were outside our time/compute budget.

## 6. Conclusion

On a controlled, reproducible, now multi-seed testbed, free greedy log-probability is a modestly better discriminator of correct vs. incorrect answers than 8-sample semantic entropy or self-consistency on average across 10 independently trained models — never significantly worse in any seed, though often indistinguishable within any one seed — while self-consistency remains far better calibrated. Semantic entropy and self-consistency, as typically paired, are strongly correlated restatements of one sample pool, not independent baselines. This suggests uncertainty-signal choice should be driven by whether the application needs ranking or calibrated probabilities, that cheap log-probability baselines deserve reporting alongside sampling-based methods, and that AUROC comparisons in this space should default to resampling or multi-seed variance estimates. We release the full code and raw per-seed, per-question results so the comparison can be extended or challenged directly.
