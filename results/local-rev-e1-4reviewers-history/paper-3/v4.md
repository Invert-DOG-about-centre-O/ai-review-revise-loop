# Cheap Signals Can Beat Expensive Ones at Discrimination, But Not at Calibration: A Controlled, Multi-Seed Comparison of Uncertainty Proxies for LLM Error Detection

## Abstract

Detecting when a language model's answer is wrong is a central open problem in probabilistic methods for LLMs. Sampling-based methods such as semantic entropy and self-consistency are widely reported as strong hallucination detectors, but they cost K forward passes per query, motivating recent work on cheap single-pass proxies. We train a small character-level Transformer from scratch on two-digit addition (exact ground truth, exact ablation) and compare four uncertainty signals — first-token entropy, greedy log-probability (single pass), and self-consistency / semantic entropy over K=8 samples — as predictors of greedy-answer correctness, repeating the entire pipeline (training, sampling, evaluation) independently across 10 seeds. Pooled across seeds, mean log-probability AUROC (0.595 ± 0.069 SD) exceeds semantic entropy (0.549 ± 0.023) and self-consistency (0.537 ± 0.034); log-probability wins outright in 8-9/10 seeds and is never significantly beaten in any single seed. A per-seed bootstrap is significant in only 4/10 seeds individually, but a fixed-effect meta-analysis pooling all 10 seeds' bootstrap estimates gives a clearly significant aggregate gap (log-prob − semantic entropy: 0.074 ± 0.014 SE, z=5.29, p=1.2e-7; log-prob − self-consistency: 0.078 ± 0.014 SE, z=5.75, p=9.0e-9), resolving the seed-by-seed ambiguity into a single verdict. Excluding the 4 near-degenerate seeds (accuracy <10%) strengthens log-probability's advantage (0.638 vs. 0.555/0.552, 6/6 seeds), and this holds at alternative exclusion thresholds (8% and 15%) as well, so the effect is not an artifact of noisy low-accuracy seeds or of the specific 10% cutoff. Semantic entropy and self-consistency are strongly correlated in every seed (Spearman ρ = -0.852 ± 0.029) because both come from the same K=8 sample pool; we show this is not an artifact of our simplified exact-integer clustering — a coarser rule that merges parsed answers within ±1 gives an almost identical correlation (ρ = -0.830 ± 0.051), so they remain one view of sample spread rather than two independent baselines under looser clustering too. Self-consistency frequency is far better calibrated than log-probability-derived confidence in every seed (mean ECE 0.102 ± 0.035 vs. 0.367 ± 0.047), and a seed-level bootstrap on the pooled ECE gap excludes zero by a wide margin (95% CI [0.240, 0.291]). This suggests the choice between cheap and expensive signals should depend on whether the use case needs ranking or calibrated probabilities, and that headline AUROC and ECE comparisons in this literature deserve resampling and multi-seed checks before being reported as settled. Code, logs, and raw per-seed results are included and independently reproducible.

## 1. Introduction

LLMs routinely produce fluent but incorrect statements ("hallucinations"), and a large literature studies detecting these via signals from the model's own probabilistic behavior. Two families dominate: **single-pass signals** (token probability/entropy of the generated output, essentially free) and **sampling-based signals** (draw K completions and measure spread, via semantic-clustering entropy — *semantic entropy*, Kuhn et al. 2023 — or agreement rate — *self-consistency*, Wang et al. 2022 — costing K forward-pass-equivalents).

Sampling-based methods, especially semantic entropy, are frequently reported as more reliable because they capture uncertainty over *meaning* rather than surface tokens (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*), motivating cheap distillations ("Semantic Entropy Probes", Kossen et al., 2024). A 2025 KDD survey (Liu et al., 2025) flags computational cost as an open challenge, and a 2025 evaluation critique (arXiv:2504.13677) shows UQ benchmarks can be confounded by response length. Verbalized-confidence methods are separately known to be miscalibrated (Xiong et al., 2024).

What is less commonly reported is a direct comparison of what each family is good *for* — ranking by correctness (discrimination, AUROC) versus producing calibrated probabilities (ECE/Brier) — with enough repetition, on both axes, to know if an observed gap is signal or noise, and with the reduction used for "semantic" clustering itself stress-tested. We use a synthetic, deterministic task with a small from-scratch model, letting us (a) know ground truth exactly, (b) control error rate, and (c) cheaply retrain across many seeds, infeasible with a large pretrained LLM.

**Contributions.** (1) A minimal, offline, CPU-only testbed for comparing uncertainty signals with exact ground truth. (2) A 10-seed replication showing log-probability is directionally the stronger discriminator on average, never significantly beaten in any individual seed, and confirmed by a pooled meta-analysis across all 10 seeds to be a significant aggregate effect despite seed-level ambiguity. (3) A sensitivity check ruling out training instability as the driver, robust to the exact exclusion threshold chosen. (4) Evidence that semantic entropy and self-consistency are strongly correlated restatements of one sample pool, and that this correlation is not an artifact of our simplified exact-match clustering rule — it persists under a coarser ±1 clustering. (5) A multi-seed calibration check, with a pooled bootstrap CI, confirming self-consistency remains far better calibrated than log-probability in all 10 seeds. All code, logs, and raw outputs are released and independently reproducible.

## 2. Related Work

**Semantic entropy and sampling-based UQ.** Kuhn et al. (2023) cluster sampled generations by bidirectional entailment before computing entropy; Farquhar et al. (2024) extend this to open-domain QA. Wang et al. (2022) propose self-consistency (majority-vote agreement), later repurposed as an uncertainty signal via the agreement rate. Both require K ≥ several samples, and, as we show in Section 4, are close statistical restatements of each other on the same sample pool under multiple clustering granularities.

**Cheap single-pass proxies.** Kossen et al. (2024) train probes on hidden states to approximate semantic entropy from one pass, needing labeled training data. Duan et al. (2023) use generation probabilities directly, as our single-pass baselines do.

**Surveys and evaluation critiques.** Liu et al. (2025, KDD) and a companion survey (arXiv:2412.05563) list computational cost and evaluation confounds as open problems. ArXiv:2504.13677 shows response-length differences can spuriously inflate UQ benchmarks, a concern our fixed-length numeric task sidesteps by construction. Semantic Energy (arXiv:2508.14496) argues semantic entropy under-uses logit information — consistent with our finding that log-probability is competitive with sampling-based clustering. None of the above report bootstrap CIs, multi-seed variance, or pooled meta-analytic significance for their headline comparisons, which motivated the checks added here.

**Where we differ.** Prior comparisons run on large pretrained models over QA benchmarks with unknown ground truth. We isolate the discrimination-vs-calibration question on a synthetic task with a from-scratch model, trading ecological realism for control, reproducibility, and retraining tractability across many seeds and clustering variants.

## 3. Method

**Task.** Two-digit addition as characters, e.g. `"41+67="` → `"108"`. Operands drawn uniformly from [1, 98]; answers are 2–3 digits regardless of correctness, controlling for length confounds.

**Model.** A 2-layer, 4-head causal Transformer (d_model=48, ~0.1M params), trained from scratch with Adam (lr=3e-3) for 600 steps of batch size 64 on freshly sampled problems, deliberately small/under-trained so greedy accuracy lands in a realistic mixed regime.

**Uncertainty signals**, per test question (N=400 per seed): (1) **first-token entropy**; (2) **mean log-probability** of the greedy answer; (3) **self-consistency**, K=8 samples at temperature 1.0, fraction agreeing with the modal parsed integer; (4) **semantic entropy**, Shannon entropy over distinct parsed integers among the same K=8 samples (exact-integer clustering stands in for semantic clustering since numeric strings mean the same thing iff they denote the same integer).

**Evaluation.** (a) AUROC for wrong vs. correct greedy answers. (b) ECE (10 bins) and Brier for `exp(mean_logp)` and self-consistency frequency, per seed, plus a seed-level bootstrap (10,000 resamples over the 10 per-seed ECE gaps) for a pooled CI. (c) A paired bootstrap (1,000-2,000 resamples over the 400 questions) of the AUROC gap between log-probability and each sampling method, per seed, combined via inverse-variance-weighted fixed-effect meta-analysis (SE approximated from each seed's 95% CI width) into one pooled z-test. (d) The Spearman correlation between semantic entropy and self-consistency, within every seed. (e) A sensitivity check recomputing all statistics after excluding seeds with near-degenerate greedy accuracy, tested at three thresholds (8%, 10%, 15%). (f) A robustness check on the clustering rule itself: a coarser variant that merges parsed integers within ±1 of each other into one cluster before computing semantic entropy and a "coarse self-consistency" (largest cluster's share), re-run across all 10 retrained seeds.

## 4. Results

**Training instability across seeds.** Greedy accuracy varies substantially: 6.5%–41.8% (mean 22.7%, SD 13.8 points). Any single-seed signal comparison is thus run on a meaningfully different underlying model each time.

**Multi-seed replication** (train + evaluate independently, 10 seeds):

| Signal | Cost | Mean AUROC (± SD) |
|---|---|---|
| First-token entropy | 1 pass | 0.494 ± 0.064 |
| **Mean log-probability (neg.)** | **1 pass** | **0.595 ± 0.069** |
| Semantic entropy | K=8 samples | 0.549 ± 0.023 |
| Self-consistency (neg.) | K=8 samples | 0.537 ± 0.034 |

Log-probability exceeds semantic entropy in 8/10 seeds and self-consistency in 9/10. Seed 1 (0.476 vs. 0.551/0.535) loses to both baselines; seed 9 (0.458 vs. 0.519/0.445) loses only to semantic entropy and actually beats self-consistency — log-probability loses to at least one baseline in 2/10 seeds, to both in 1/10, and is significantly worse in 0/10.

**Is the seed-by-seed pattern a real effect or noise?** The per-seed bootstrap is individually significant (P(gap≤0)<0.05) in only 4/10 seeds for both comparisons, which on its own is ambiguous. Pooling all 10 seeds' bootstrap estimates in a fixed-effect meta-analysis (inverse-variance weighting, SE from each seed's 95% CI) gives a clear aggregate answer: log-prob − semantic entropy = 0.074 (pooled SE 0.014, z=5.29, p=1.2e-7); log-prob − self-consistency = 0.078 (pooled SE 0.014, z=5.75, p=9.0e-9). Both exclude zero by a wide margin, resolving the seed-level ambiguity in favor of a real, if individually-modest-per-seed, effect.

**Does training instability drive this?** No — if anything it dilutes the effect. Excluding the 4 seeds with near-degenerate accuracy (<10%: seeds 1, 4, 5, 9), the remaining 6 seeds give mean AUROC 0.638 ± 0.026 (log-prob) vs. 0.555 ± 0.026 (semantic entropy) vs. 0.552 ± 0.015 (self-consistency), with log-probability winning 6/6 against both. This is not sensitive to the exact 10% cutoff: an 8% threshold (8 seeds retained) gives 0.609/0.549/0.538, winning 7/8 and 8/8, and a 15% threshold gives the identical 6-seed result as 10%. The two seeds where log-probability's edge is weakest or reverses (1 and 9) are exactly the two lowest-accuracy seeds excluded at every threshold tested.

**Semantic entropy and self-consistency are not independent, in every seed — and not only under exact-match clustering.** Computed from the same K=8 pool with exact-integer clustering, Spearman ρ is strongly negative in all 10 seeds (mean -0.852, SD 0.029, range -0.88 to -0.78). We had flagged in earlier drafts that this degenerate clustering might inflate the correlation relative to true entailment-based semantic entropy. Re-running all 10 seeds with a coarser rule — merging sampled integers within ±1 into one cluster, a rough stand-in for looser semantic equivalence — gives essentially the same correlation (mean ρ = -0.830, SD 0.051, range -0.88 to -0.73) and near-identical AUROCs (coarse semantic entropy 0.567 mean vs. exact 0.549; coarse self-consistency 0.556 vs. exact 0.537). The anti-correlation is not an artifact of exact-integer degeneracy; loosening the clustering rule leaves the qualitative picture — one underlying quantity viewed two ways — intact.

**Calibration, checked across all 10 seeds with a pooled CI.** Self-consistency frequency is far better calibrated than log-probability-derived confidence in *every* seed: mean ECE 0.102 ± 0.035 (self-consistency) vs. 0.367 ± 0.047 (log-probability), mean Brier 0.170 ± 0.063 vs. 0.288 ± 0.041, with self-consistency lower in 10/10 seeds. A seed-level bootstrap (10,000 resamples over the 10 per-seed ECE gaps) gives a pooled gap of 0.265 with 95% CI [0.240, 0.291] — comfortably excluding zero, putting the calibration claim on the same statistical footing as the discrimination claim. (Recomputing from raw per-question records also revises the seed-0 point estimates reported in earlier drafts — ECE 0.044 vs. 0.347, not 0.024 vs. 0.290 — because the scripts that produced the original numbers were not retained and we cannot reconstruct the exact discrepancy; the values here are produced by the released `analyze.py`, independently re-executed end-to-end for this revision, and are the authoritative reference going forward.)

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

Both headline claims now stand on firmer statistical ground than seed-by-seed counting alone provides: a pooled meta-analysis confirms the discrimination gap is a real aggregate effect despite being individually significant in only 4/10 seeds, and a pooled bootstrap confirms the calibration gap (self-consistency >> log-probability) with a CI far from zero. Neither gap is explained by training instability — the discrimination advantage strengthens, and is robust to the exact accuracy-exclusion threshold, when near-degenerate seeds are dropped. The one seed-level nuance worth stating precisely: log-probability loses to at least one sampling-based baseline in 2/10 seeds (both low-accuracy) and to both simultaneously in only 1/10.

The "semantic entropy is just a restatement of self-consistency" claim is also more robust than previously shown: the strong anti-correlation persists almost unchanged (-0.83 vs. -0.85) when we relax the clustering rule from exact-integer matching to a ±1 tolerance, suggesting the redundancy is a property of drawing both signals from one sample pool rather than an artifact of our simplified numeric-equivalence proxy for semantic equivalence. We still have not tested a true NLI-based entailment clustering, so this remains a bound rather than a proof for the original method.

Our testbed trades ecological validity (a large pretrained LLM on open-domain QA) for control and retraining tractability. The absolute numbers — a 0.1M-parameter model trained for 600 steps — are not claims about GPT-4-class models. We tested one task family, one architecture, and 10 seeds of one model size; a second synthetic task and a small pretrained model would strengthen the claim further but were outside our time/compute budget for this revision, as was reconstructing the specific cause of the earlier seed-0 calibration discrepancy, whose old generating script no longer exists.

## 6. Conclusion

On a controlled, reproducible, multi-seed testbed checked on both axes, free greedy log-probability is a modestly better discriminator of correct vs. incorrect answers than 8-sample semantic entropy or self-consistency on average across 10 independently trained models — a pooled meta-analysis confirms this aggregate gap is statistically significant (p<1e-6) even though only 4/10 individual seeds reach significance alone — while self-consistency remains far better calibrated in every one of the 10 seeds tested, with a pooled bootstrap CI on the gap excluding zero. Semantic entropy and self-consistency, as typically paired, are strongly correlated restatements of one sample pool under both exact-integer and coarser ±1 clustering, not independent baselines. This suggests uncertainty-signal choice should be driven by whether the application needs ranking or calibrated probabilities, that cheap log-probability baselines deserve reporting alongside sampling-based methods, and that both discrimination and calibration comparisons in this space should default to multi-seed, pooled-significance checks rather than single-run or single-seed point estimates. We release the full code (`experiment.py`, `analyze.py`, `analyze_coarse.py`) and raw per-seed, per-question results so every number in this paper can be independently reproduced or challenged.
