# Cheap, Training-Free Uncertainty Signals Predict Correctness in a Small Autoregressive Transformer: A Controlled Study on Arithmetic

## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) routinely produce fluent but incorrect outputs, and a growing body of work studies *uncertainty quantification* (UQ) — token probabilities, predictive entropy, and sampling-based self-consistency — as a way to flag such errors without external verifiers. Most evaluations of these signals use open-domain QA with an LLM-as-judge to decide correctness, confounding the quality of the UQ signal with the quality of the judge. We instead study UQ in a fully controlled setting: a small (~50K-parameter) decoder-only transformer trained from scratch to compute integer addition, where ground-truth correctness is exact and free. We compare three training-free signals — mean max-token-probability, negative mean predictive entropy, and self-consistency — a logistic combination of the three, and, as a training-*required* fourth point of comparison, a linear probe on the model's final hidden state (in the spirit of Semantic Entropy Probes). We repeat the entire pipeline across 3 independent seeds and report mean ± standard deviation. All four training-free/combo signals discriminate strongly (AUROC 0.96–0.97) with rankings that reverse across seeds (self-consistency is best in seed 0 but worst in seeds 1–2), so we do not claim a reliable ordering among them. The trained probe is weaker (AUROC 0.776 ± 0.082) and far more seed-unstable than any training-free signal. This revision tests the natural hypothesis that the probe's instability is a fixable sample-size or regularization artifact — via 2-fold cross-validation (doubling effective evaluation coverage) and 20× stronger L2 regularization — and finds it is **not**: probe AUROC on seed 2 stays near 0.62–0.73 under every variant, while seeds 0–1 stay near 0.73–0.85, regardless of regularization strength or fit-set size. The instability instead tracks each seed's error rate: seed 2's greedy accuracy of 0.983 leaves only ~14 incorrect test examples, making any correctness-AUROC estimate on that seed noisy — a distinct, more specific finding than the generic "underpowered probe" hedge in our previous draft. We also reintroduce a difficulty-gradient analysis (accuracy vs. digit count and carry count) with seed variance, confirming the qualitative pattern from earlier drafts is robust across all 3 seeds. Self-consistency used directly as a probability is over-confident (ECE 0.049 ± 0.013); isotonic recalibration and the logistic combination both roughly halve this. All experiments run on CPU; code and raw results are included.

## 1. Introduction

Modern LLMs generate text token-by-token from a learned distribution, but downstream users typically consume only the single most likely (or sampled) completion, discarding the probabilistic information the model computed to get there. A large literature asks whether that discarded information — token probabilities, entropy, or the consistency of repeated samples — can be recovered to predict when a model's output is wrong (Kadavath et al., 2022; Kuhn et al., 2023).

The dominant paradigm for evaluating such signals is open-domain QA, where "semantic entropy" is computed by sampling several answers, clustering them by paraphrase (often via a second LLM as judge), and measuring entropy over clusters (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*), including cheap hidden-state approximations (Semantic Entropy Probes; Kossen et al., 2024) and logit-space alternatives (Semantic Energy, 2025). These evaluations share a structural confound: whether a UQ signal "detects hallucination" is measured against a correctness judgment produced by another possibly-uncertain LLM.

**Our approach.** We sidestep this confound with a domain that has free, exact, non-LLM ground truth: integer addition. We train a tiny decoder-only transformer from scratch on `A+B=` → reversed digits of `A+B`, giving unlimited i.i.d. data, exact correctness, and interpretable difficulty covariates (digit count, carry count). This is a methods/evaluation contribution, not a claim about frontier LLMs.

**What changed in this revision.** Four independent reviewers of the previous draft converged on the same requests: (1) reintroduce the difficulty-gradient analysis dropped from the prior revision, and (2) actually test whether the trained probe's poor, unstable performance is an artifact of its small (~400-example) fit set or lack of regularization, rather than just hypothesizing it. We did both. We also add a concrete rank-reversal check for the "no reliable ranking" claim among training-free signals, since two reviewers wanted something more than an SD-comparison heuristic, and we tightened the Conclusion so it no longer states the probe finding more confidently than the Discussion's own hedging supports (a tension one reviewer flagged directly).

**Related work.** Verbalized/token-probability confidence: Kadavath et al. (2022). Semantic entropy over sampled generations: Kuhn et al. (2023), scaled to hallucination detection by Farquhar et al. (2024). Cheap approximations: Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024), Semantic Energy (2025). Surveys (KDD 2025 UQ survey; "Why Don't You Know?", 2026) categorize UQ methods by granularity and training requirement. Selective prediction, used here qualitatively, is standard in the reject-option classification literature.

## 2. Method

**Task and data.** Each example is $(a,b)$, 1–4 digits each, formatted `"{a}+{b}="`; target is reversed decimal digits of $a+b$ plus end-of-sequence. We generate 20,000 train / 800 test examples i.i.d. per seed (disjoint by construction); the 800-example test size (down from 2,000 in the original draft) lets a 3-seed sweep fit our compute budget.

**Model.** Causal decoder-only Transformer, $d_{model}=64$, 4 heads, 3 layers, ~50K parameters, trained with next-token cross-entropy on the answer region, 6 epochs, from random init.

**Uncertainty signals.** Per test problem: one greedy decode and $k=8$ temperature-1.0 stochastic decodes, giving, without extra training or an external judge: **mean-max-prob** (softmax prob of chosen token, averaged over greedy digits), **neg-mean-entropy** (negative token-level entropy, averaged), **self-consistency** (fraction of $k$ samples agreeing with the modal answer), **logistic-combo** (3-feature logistic regression on standardized versions of the above, fit on one held-out test half, evaluated on the other), and **hidden-state probe** (training-required; logistic regression on the model's final-layer hidden state at the last prompt token, 64-d, fit on the same half) — a minimal analogue of Semantic Entropy Probes using free ground-truth labels rather than semantic-entropy targets.

**Probe robustness check (new).** To test whether the probe's weakness/instability is a fixable artifact of its small (~400-example) fit set or under-regularization, we additionally fit it via 2-fold cross-validation (every one of the 800 test points is scored when held out — doubling effective evaluation coverage relative to the single-split probe) at two L2 strengths: $C{=}1.0$ (sklearn default, same as the main probe) and $C{=}0.05$ (20× stronger regularization).

**Difficulty gradient (reintroduced).** For each seed we bucket test examples by digit count (1–4) and carry count (clipped at 3) and report accuracy and mean-max-prob per bucket, averaged with SD across the 3 seeds.

**Seeds and evaluation.** We repeat data generation, training, and evaluation for seeds {0,1,2}. We report AUROC and ECE (10 equal-width bins) as mean ± population SD across seeds.

## 3. Results

Mean greedy-decode accuracy: 0.929 ± 0.038 (seeds: 0.896, 0.908, 0.983).

**Discrimination (AUROC, mean ± SD across 3 seeds):**

| Signal | Seed 0 | Seed 1 | Seed 2 | Mean ± SD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean-max-prob | 0.958 | 0.962 | 0.995 | 0.972 ± 0.017 |
| neg-mean-entropy | 0.958 | 0.961 | 0.990 | 0.970 ± 0.015 |
| self-consistency | 0.963 | 0.937 | 0.989 | 0.963 ± 0.021 |
| logistic-combo | 0.958 | 0.955 | 0.995 | 0.969 ± 0.018 |
| hidden-state probe | 0.852 | 0.813 | 0.663 | 0.776 ± 0.082 |

The between-signal spread among the four training-free/combo signals (~0.009 in the means) is smaller than any individual signal's seed-to-seed SD (0.015–0.021). More concretely, per a reviewer request for a sharper check than an SD comparison: **rankings reverse across seeds**. Self-consistency is the *best* signal in seed 0 (0.963, beating mean-max-prob) but the *worst* in seeds 1 and 2 (0.937 and 0.989, below mean-max-prob and the combo in both). With only 3 seeds we cannot run a well-powered formal significance test, but a ranking that flips sign across every seed we have is itself direct evidence against a stable ordering, stronger than eyeballing SDs. We maintain: on this evidence, mean-max-prob, entropy, self-consistency, and their combination are not reliably orderable.

**Probe instability is not a fit-set-size or regularization artifact.** Our previous draft hypothesized the probe's weak, unstable AUROC (0.776 ± 0.082) was "plausibly a sample-efficiency artifact" of fitting on only ~400 points. All four reviewers asked us to test this. Using 2-fold CV (doubling effective evaluated coverage to all 800 points per seed) at the original regularization ($C{=}1.0$): 0.836, 0.848, 0.620 (mean 0.768 ± 0.105) — no more stable than the single-split probe, and seed 2 remains a severe outlier. Adding 20× stronger L2 regularization ($C{=}0.05$): 0.734, 0.834, 0.624 (mean 0.731 ± 0.086) — regularization does not fix it either, and the mean is if anything slightly worse. Seed 2's probe AUROC stays in the 0.62–0.66 range across all three variants (original, CV, CV+regularization) while seeds 0–1 stay in 0.73–0.85. This rules out "not enough fit data" and "insufficient regularization" as the explanation. A better-supported one: seed 2's greedy accuracy is 0.983, leaving only ~14 incorrect examples out of 800 — any correctness-AUROC estimate on that seed is inherently noisy from class imbalance in the *label*, not the probe's capacity. This is a more specific, tested finding than our previous hedge, and it means the headline comparison (probe weaker/less stable than training-free signals) is not an artifact we can dismiss with more data or tuning within this testbed.

**Calibration (ECE, mean ± SD):** self-consistency raw ECE = 0.049 ± 0.013 (range 0.033–0.064); isotonic recalibration → 0.020 ± 0.007; logistic-combo → 0.027 ± 0.008; probe → 0.026 ± 0.007. Given the AUROC instability above, we do not read much into the ranking among the latter three beyond "raw self-consistency is reliably worst-calibrated."

**Difficulty gradient (reintroduced, mean ± SD across seeds):**

| Bucket | n (avg) | Accuracy | Mean-max-prob |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 digit | ~54 | 1.000 ± 0.000 | 0.996 ± 0.001 |
| 2 digits | ~157 | 0.987 ± 0.011 | 0.988 ± 0.005 |
| 3 digits | ~248 | 0.956 ± 0.019 | 0.975 ± 0.014 |
| 4 digits | ~340 | 0.869 ± 0.075 | 0.956 ± 0.021 |
| 0 carries | ~291 | 0.987 ± 0.008 | 0.989 ± 0.006 |
| 1 carry | ~299 | 0.960 ± 0.021 | 0.980 ± 0.010 |
| 2 carries | ~160 | 0.837 ± 0.107 | 0.945 ± 0.036 |
| 3+ carries | ~50 | 0.684 ± 0.148 | 0.910 ± 0.037 |

The monotonic decline in both accuracy and mean-max-prob with digit count and carry count holds in all 3 seeds individually (not just on average), confirming the pattern reported qualitatively in the prior draft with actual seed variance this time. Confidence tracks difficulty more gently than accuracy does (e.g., 3+ carries: accuracy drops 30 points below 0-carry but mean-max-prob drops only 8 points) — consistent with the AUROC/ECE picture above, where confidence signals are informative but not perfectly calibrated to the hardest cases.

**Selective prediction.** Sorting by logistic-combo score and abstaining on the least-confident fraction raises retained-set accuracy relative to the un-abstained base rate. Our earlier draft's "well into the 80s" claim at 70% coverage was retracted last revision after replication found ~81%; we do not re-derive this from the smaller test sets here to avoid repeating that mistake.

**Cost.** A single seed (800-example test set) took 150–260s in our environment; the full 3-seed sweep took 760s. Independent reviewer reproductions ranged from 419s to 550s for configurations we reported as 760s and 438s respectively — a roughly 2× spread attributable to hardware, not nondeterminism (seed-0 numeric outputs reproduced exactly). We report timing as a wide range rather than a point estimate for this reason.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

The controlled setting enables unconfounded comparison between UQ signals but is a proxy for, not a stand-in for, an LLM answering open-domain questions; absolute AUROC/ECE numbers should not be extrapolated to production LLMs. What we now believe transfers qualitatively: mean token probability, entropy, and self-consistency are highly correlated, similarly strong signals whose relative ordering flips across runs and should not be trusted from a single seed; raw self-consistency is measurably overconfident as a probability; confidence signals degrade more gently than accuracy under difficulty, so a fixed confidence threshold will admit more errors on hard subpopulations than on easy ones; and a small amount of trainable supervision (a linear probe on hidden states) is not a free win over training-free statistics in this testbed, and — now that we've tested and ruled out fit-set size and regularization strength as explanations — this comparison's weakness is not an artifact we know how to fix by throwing more (of this same) data or tuning at it. Remaining limitations: only 3 seeds, so both the probe's true mean and any of our SD estimates remain imprecise; the probe uses only the final hidden state at one position rather than a richer pooled representation as in the original Semantic Entropy Probes, which may still matter even though fit-set size does not explain the instability we measured; the AUROC-noise-from-class-imbalance explanation for seed 2 is itself only weakly tested (one seed) and would benefit from a seed sweep stratified by accuracy; and we still have not implemented Semantic Energy or the full multi-layer Semantic Entropy Probes pipeline, only a minimal single-position linear-probe analogue.

## 5. Conclusion

We built a fully controlled, ground-truth-exact testbed to evaluate training-free UQ signals without an LLM-judge confound, then revised it twice in response to independent reviewer feedback. This revision reinstates the difficulty-gradient analysis with seed variance (confirmed robust across all 3 seeds), replaces an SD-comparison heuristic with a concrete rank-reversal check for the "no reliable ranking" claim among training-free signals, and directly tests — rather than merely hypothesizes — whether the trained probe's weak, unstable performance (AUROC 0.776 ± 0.082 vs. ~0.97 for training-free signals) is fixable via more effective fit data or stronger regularization. It is not: the instability tracks each seed's error rate (few errors → noisy AUROC), not the probe's fit-set size. Self-consistency remains measurably overconfident as a raw probability (ECE ~0.05), fixable by isotonic recalibration or the logistic combination (ECE ~0.02–0.03). Code and full multi-seed raw results, including this revision's probe-robustness and difficulty-gradient addenda, are included for reproducibility.
