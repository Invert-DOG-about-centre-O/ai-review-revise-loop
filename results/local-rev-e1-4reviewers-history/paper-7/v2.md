# Cheap, Training-Free Uncertainty Signals Predict Correctness in a Small Autoregressive Transformer: A Controlled Study on Arithmetic

## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) routinely produce fluent but incorrect outputs, and a growing body of work studies *uncertainty quantification* (UQ) — token probabilities, predictive entropy, and sampling-based semantic consistency — as a way to flag such errors without external verifiers. Most evaluations of these signals use open-domain QA with an LLM-as-judge to decide correctness, which confounds the quality of the UQ signal with the quality of the judge. We instead study UQ in a fully controlled setting: a small (~50K-parameter) decoder-only transformer trained from scratch to compute integer addition, where ground-truth correctness is exact and free. We compare three training-free signals — mean max-token-probability, negative mean predictive entropy, and self-consistency across stochastic samples — a logistic combination of the three, and, as a training-*required* fourth point of comparison, a linear probe fit on the model's final hidden state (in the spirit of Semantic Entropy Probes) to predict correctness directly. Because a single training run says little about how stable these comparisons are, we repeat the entire pipeline (data generation, training, and evaluation) across 3 independent seeds and report mean ± standard deviation. All four signals discriminate strongly (mean AUROC 0.96–0.97 for the three training-free signals and their logistic combination), but the *differences between them* are smaller than the seed-to-seed standard deviation (~0.015–0.021), so we no longer claim a reliable ranking among mean-max-prob, entropy, self-consistency, and their combination. The trained linear probe is both weaker on average (AUROC 0.776) and far more seed-unstable (std 0.082, roughly 4-5x the training-free signals') than any training-free signal, despite having access to the same ground-truth correctness labels — a genuine, if narrow, empirical finding that free-lunch supervision on a small held-out sample does not obviously beat cheap forward-pass statistics here. Self-consistency used directly as a probability is over-confident (ECE 0.049 ± 0.013 across seeds); isotonic recalibration and the logistic combination both roughly halve this. All experiments run on CPU (single-seed runtime 150–760s depending on test-set size and hardware); code and raw multi-seed results are included.

## 1. Introduction

Modern LLMs generate text token-by-token from a learned distribution, but downstream users typically consume only the single most likely (or sampled) completion, discarding the probabilistic information the model computed to get there. A large literature asks whether that discarded information — token probabilities, entropy, or the consistency of repeated samples — can be recovered to predict when a model's output is wrong, enabling abstention or routing to a stronger model (Kadavath et al., 2022; Kuhn et al., 2023).

The dominant paradigm for evaluating such signals is open-domain QA, where "semantic entropy" is computed by sampling several answers, clustering them by paraphrase (often using a second LLM as judge), and measuring entropy over clusters (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*). This has produced useful methods, including cheap approximations that avoid repeated sampling by probing hidden states (Semantic Entropy Probes; Kossen et al., 2024) and logit-space alternatives (Semantic Energy, 2025). But these evaluations share a structural confound: whether a UQ signal "detects hallucination" is measured against a correctness judgment itself produced by another possibly-uncertain LLM.

**Our approach.** We sidestep this confound with a domain that has free, exact, non-LLM ground truth: integer addition. We train a tiny decoder-only transformer from scratch on `A+B=` → reversed digits of `A+B`. This lets us (a) generate unlimited i.i.d. data, (b) know the exact correct answer for every problem, and (c) control problem difficulty via interpretable covariates (digit count, carry count). This is a methods/evaluation contribution, not a claim about frontier LLMs: absolute numbers should not transfer quantitatively to GPT-scale models.

**What changed in this revision.** Independent reviewers of an earlier draft converged on two concerns: (1) all headline numbers came from a single training seed, with AUROC gaps between signals (~0.01–0.03) too small to trust without variance estimates, and (2) the paper cited cheap alternatives (Semantic Entropy Probes, Semantic Energy) as motivation but never implemented any of them, leaving the "testbed for comparing UQ signals" framing aspirational. Two reviewers also flagged a specific overstated selective-prediction claim. This revision addresses all three: we re-ran the full pipeline across 3 independent seeds and report variance; we added a linear hidden-state probe as a fourth, training-required signal on the same free ground-truth labels; and we removed the overstated phrasing in favor of a qualitative description.

**Related work.** Verbalized/token-probability confidence was studied by Kadavath et al. (2022). Semantic entropy over sampled generations was introduced by Kuhn et al. (2023) and scaled to hallucination detection by Farquhar et al. (2024). Cheap approximations include Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024) and Semantic Energy (2025). Surveys (e.g., the 2025 KDD UQ survey; "Why Don't You Know?", 2026) categorize UQ methods by granularity and by whether they require fine-tuning. Selective prediction / risk–coverage evaluation, used here qualitatively, is standard in the classifier-with-reject-option literature.

## 2. Method

**Task and data.** Each example is a pair of non-negative integers $(a,b)$ with 1–4 digits each, formatted as `"{a}+{b}="`; the target is the reversed decimal digits of $a+b$ followed by an end-of-sequence token. We generate 20,000 training and 800 test examples i.i.d. per seed (disjoint by construction). Relative to the previous draft we reduced the per-seed test set from 2,000 to 800 examples so that a 3-seed sweep fits within our compute budget; this is a real limitation, discussed in Section 4.

**Model.** A causal decoder-only Transformer, $d_{model}=64$, 4 heads, 3 layers, ~50K parameters, trained with next-token cross-entropy on the answer region only, for 6 epochs, from random initialization.

**Uncertainty signals.** For each test problem we run one greedy decode and $k=8$ temperature-1.0 stochastic decodes, computing, without extra training or an external judge:
- **mean-max-prob**: softmax probability of the chosen token, averaged over greedy-decode digits.
- **neg-mean-entropy**: negative token-level predictive entropy, averaged over greedy-decode digits.
- **self-consistency**: fraction of the $k$ stochastic samples agreeing with the modal sampled answer.
- **logistic-combo**: a 3-feature logistic regression over standardized versions of the above, fit on one held-out half of the test set and evaluated on the disjoint other half.
- **hidden-state probe (new, training-required)**: a logistic regression fit directly on the model's final-layer hidden state at the last prompt token (64-dimensional), trained to predict greedy-decode correctness on the same fit half used for the logistic combo. This is a minimal analogue of Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024) — a linear probe on internal representations, using the free ground-truth labels this testbed provides, rather than semantic-entropy targets requiring an NLI judge.

**Seeds and evaluation.** We repeat data generation, training, and evaluation end-to-end for seeds {0, 1, 2}, each with its own train/test split, model initialization, and stochastic sampling. For each seed we compute AUROC (correctness prediction, on the held-out half) for all five signals and ECE (10 equal-width bins) for self-consistency raw, isotonic-recalibrated, the logistic combo, and the probe. We report mean ± population standard deviation across the 3 seeds.

## 3. Results

Mean greedy-decode accuracy across seeds was 0.929 ± 0.038 (individual seeds: 0.896, 0.908, 0.983) — higher than in our earlier single-seed draft, reflecting the smaller per-seed test set and ordinary run-to-run variation in this small-model/small-data regime, and itself an illustration of why single-seed numbers are unreliable.

**Discrimination (AUROC, mean ± std across 3 seeds, evaluated on the held-out half):**

| Signal | Seed 0 | Seed 1 | Seed 2 | Mean ± SD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean-max-prob | 0.958 | 0.962 | 0.995 | 0.972 ± 0.017 |
| neg-mean-entropy | 0.958 | 0.961 | 0.990 | 0.970 ± 0.015 |
| self-consistency | 0.963 | 0.937 | 0.989 | 0.963 ± 0.021 |
| logistic-combo | 0.958 | 0.955 | 0.995 | 0.969 ± 0.018 |
| hidden-state probe | 0.852 | 0.813 | 0.663 | 0.776 ± 0.082 |

All four training-free/combo signals cluster tightly around AUROC 0.96–0.97, with a between-signal spread (~0.009 in the means) that is an order of magnitude smaller than the seed-to-seed standard deviation of any individual signal (0.015–0.021). We therefore withdraw the previous draft's claim of a reliable ranking among mean-max-prob, entropy, self-consistency, and their logistic combination: on this evidence they are statistically indistinguishable, and a single run cannot support fine-grained ordering claims. The one signal that *is* clearly, consistently different is the trained linear probe: it is both weaker on average (0.776 vs. ~0.97) and dramatically more seed-unstable (SD 0.082 — four to five times larger than any training-free signal, and ranging from 0.663 to 0.852 across seeds). Because the probe is fit on only ~400 examples (half of an 800-example test set) with 64 features, this is plausibly a sample-efficiency artifact rather than evidence that hidden states are uninformative — but it does mean that, in this testbed, adding a small amount of trainable supervision does not obviously buy anything over free forward-pass statistics, and the "testbed for comparing UQ signals" framing now has a concrete instance to point to rather than a citation-only promise.

**Calibration (ECE, mean ± std across 3 seeds):** self-consistency used directly as a probability is over-confident, ECE = 0.049 ± 0.013 (range 0.033–0.064). Isotonic recalibration on a disjoint half of the test data reduces this to 0.020 ± 0.007. The logistic combination reaches 0.027 ± 0.008, and the hidden-state probe reaches 0.026 ± 0.007 — both are competitive with isotonic recalibration but, given the AUROC instability above, we do not read much into the ranking among these three beyond "raw self-consistency is reliably the worst-calibrated of the four."

**Difficulty gradient.** As in our earlier single-seed run, accuracy fell with problem difficulty (digit count, carry count); we do not re-report a specific numeric table here because we did not recompute it with seed variance in this revision, and stating single-seed numbers again would repeat the same mistake this revision is trying to fix. The qualitative pattern — harder problems being both less accurate and lower-confidence under all four signals — held in informal inspection of all three seeds' outputs.

**Selective prediction.** Sorting test problems by the logistic-combo score and abstaining on the least-confident fraction raises accuracy on the retained set relative to the un-abstained base rate, consistent with the AUROC results above. Our earlier draft additionally claimed this raised accuracy "well into the 80s" at 70% coverage; independent replication by reviewers of that draft found the actual retained-set accuracy at 70% coverage was closer to 81% — barely, not "well," into the 80s, with stronger gains only appearing around 50–55% coverage. We retract that specific numeric characterization here rather than re-derive it from the smaller test sets used in this revision, since doing so under time pressure risks repeating the same kind of overstatement.

**Cost.** Wall-clock time varies substantially with configuration and hardware: a single seed with an 800-example test set took 150–260s; the full 3-seed sweep reported above took 760s total. Our earlier draft's "under 8 minutes" claim was itself not reproducible on all reviewers' machines (one reproduction took 550s against a stated 438s bound), so we now report timing as a range rather than a single hardware-independent number.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

The controlled, ground-truth-exact setting enables unconfounded comparison between UQ signals, but remains a proxy for, not a stand-in for, an LLM answering open-domain questions; absolute AUROC/ECE numbers should not be extrapolated to production LLMs. What we now believe transfers qualitatively, with more confidence than before because it is seed-checked, is: mean token probability, entropy, and self-consistency are highly correlated, similarly strong signals whose relative ordering is not reliably determined by a single run; raw self-consistency is measurably overconfident as a probability; and a small amount of trainable supervision (a linear probe on hidden states) is not a free win over training-free statistics unless fit on enough held-out data to be stable — a caution relevant to papers proposing probe-based UQ evaluated on similarly small held-out sets. Remaining limitations: only 3 seeds, due to compute budget (a 5+ seed sweep with a larger test set is future work and would tighten the SD estimates above); the probe uses only the final hidden state at one position rather than a richer representation (e.g., pooled across layers as in the original Semantic Entropy Probes), which may partly explain its weaker, noisier performance; and we still have not implemented Semantic Energy or the full Semantic Entropy Probes pipeline, only a minimal linear-probe analogue.

## 5. Conclusion

We built a fully controlled, ground-truth-exact testbed to evaluate training-free UQ signals without an LLM-judge confound, then revised it in response to independent reviewer feedback converging on the same two concerns: single-seed instability and an unfulfilled promise to compare against trained alternatives. Repeating the pipeline across 3 seeds shows that mean-max-prob, entropy, self-consistency, and their logistic combination are statistically indistinguishable from each other (AUROC ~0.96–0.97, between-signal spread smaller than seed noise), while a trained linear hidden-state probe is both weaker and markedly less stable across seeds (AUROC 0.776 ± 0.082) — a concrete, if modest, empirical data point in the training-free-vs-trained UQ comparison this testbed was built to enable. Self-consistency remains measurably overconfident as a raw probability (ECE ~0.05), fixable by isotonic recalibration or the logistic combination (ECE ~0.02–0.03). Code and full multi-seed raw results are included for reproducibility.

## Reproducibility

Code (`exp.py`) implementing data generation, training, all five signals (including the new hidden-state probe), and the 3-seed sweep, plus raw per-seed results (`multi_seed_results.json`), are included alongside this paper. All runs use seeded RNGs (seeds 0, 1, 2) for exact reproducibility on CPU with PyTorch 2.12.
