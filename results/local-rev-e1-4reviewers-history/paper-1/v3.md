# Cheap Uncertainty Signals for Error Prediction in Language Models: A Controlled Comparison on a Synthetic Arithmetic Task

## Abstract

Detecting when a language model's output is likely wrong is a central open problem for reliable deployment. A growing literature proposes uncertainty signals of varying cost: token-level predictive entropy (nearly free, one forward pass), self-consistency voting, and semantic entropy (both requiring K stochastic samples). Prior work reports semantic entropy outperforming naive token entropy on open-domain QA with large pretrained models, but these comparisons are entangled with pretraining artifacts, retrieval, and expensive human-labeled correctness judgments, and typically report point estimates from a single run. We isolate the comparison in a fully controlled setting — a small Transformer decoder trained from scratch on a synthetic arithmetic task with exact ground truth and exact-match semantic clustering — and, following two rounds of reviewer feedback, use 5 independent training seeds, bootstrap confidence intervals and paired significance tests computed **per seed** (not just on one reference run), a binomial sign test on the cross-seed win pattern, a check of whether the effect size scales with model accuracy, a K sweep, and a stratified breakdown by answer length. Averaged over seeds, token-level predictive entropy has the highest AUROC in all 5 seeds (sign test p=0.031), and on the reference seed the gap over semantic entropy and self-consistency is statistically significant (bootstrap paired test, p=0.007 and p=0.021). Per-seed testing shows this significance is not uniform: the gap is significant on 2/5 seeds for semantic entropy and 3/5 seeds for self-consistency, so the *direction* of the effect is robust across seeds but its *statistical significance on any single seed* is not guaranteed — a distinction the original single-seed draft could not have made. We find no reliable relationship between a seed's greedy accuracy and the size of token entropy's advantage (r=0.15). We conclude that the token-entropy-vs-sampling-based-signal ordering reported for large pretrained LLMs does not automatically transfer to this small from-scratch setting, and that single-seed comparisons of these signals — including our own original draft — are not a reliable basis for claims about which signal is "better," nor is a single seed's significance test a reliable stand-in for testing across seeds. We release the full training/evaluation code, raw per-example outputs, and all seeds/statistics.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect statements ("hallucinations"), and distinguishing confident-and-correct outputs from confident-sounding-but-wrong ones is a practical necessity for deployed systems. Because ground-truth correctness labels are expensive at scale, a large body of work studies *unsupervised* uncertainty signals extracted from the model itself.

**Related work.** Malinin & Gales (2021) formalized token-level sequence uncertainty. Kuhn et al. (2023) argued token entropy conflates lexical and semantic variability and proposed **semantic entropy**: cluster K sampled generations by (approximate) semantic equivalence, then compute the Shannon entropy of the cluster distribution. Farquhar et al. (2024, *Nature*) extended this to open-domain QA, reporting gains over token entropy and self-consistency. Because full semantic entropy costs K generations, follow-up work sought cheaper approximations (Kossen et al., 2024; Semantic Energy, 2025). A 2025 KDD survey (Xiao et al.) notes that no single uncertainty metric dominates across tasks, that most comparisons run on large pretrained models where pretraining exposure and decoding strategy confound the *signal's* intrinsic ability to track correctness, and that comparisons are typically single-run point estimates without confidence intervals.

**Gap.** It is unclear whether semantic entropy's reported advantage reflects something fundamental about the signals or artifacts of specific pretrained models and benchmarks — and, as we found across two revisions of this paper, insufficient statistical scrutiny, including scrutiny of *which seed* a significance test happens to be run on.

**Contribution.** This paper is a case study in that gap, now revised twice under reviewer pressure. Our original draft reported a single-seed result (58.5% greedy accuracy, K=10) in which sampling-based signals beat token entropy by 2–3 AUROC points and called this a replication of the large-pretrained-LLM pattern. All four round-1 reviews objected: no seed variation, no significance testing. Our first revision added 5 seeds, bootstrap CIs, a K sweep, and a digit-length stratification, and found the *opposite* result — token entropy wins, significantly on the reference seed. All four round-2 reviews independently converged on a follow-up objection: significance was tested on only one of the five seeds, so it was unclear whether p=0.007 was typical or a favorable draw, and two reviewers separately flagged that our reported wall-clock runtime did not reproduce on their hardware. This revision adds per-seed paired significance tests, a sign test on the seed-consistency claim, an accuracy-vs-effect-size check, and corrects the runtime claim to reflect measured variance across machines.

## 2. Method

**Task.** Each example is a string `a<op>b=` where `a, b ∈ [1, 99]`, `op ∈ {+, -}` (subtraction ordered non-negative), target is the decimal answer string — exactly checkable, no human judgment needed.

**Model.** A character-level, decoder-only Transformer (`TinyGPT`): token + learned positional embeddings, 3 pre-norm layers used causally, d_model=64, 4 heads, GELU MLPs, ≈300K parameters, trained from random initialization (no pretraining, no external data).

**Training.** AdamW, lr=3e-3, batch size 64, 1500 steps, teacher-forced cross-entropy on `<bos> prompt target <eos>` sequences with fresh random examples each batch, kept short so the model is genuinely uncertain rather than saturated. We train **5 independent seeds** (0–4, controlling both initialization and the training data stream).

**Signals compared.** For each of 400 held-out problems per seed: (1) **token-level predictive entropy** — greedy-decode, average per-step softmax Shannon entropy; (2) **semantic entropy** — K samples at temperature 0.9, parsed to integers, clustered by exact value equality, Shannon entropy of the cluster-frequency distribution; (3) **self-consistency** — fraction of K samples matching the greedy answer. K=10 is the reference value; we additionally sweep K ∈ {2, 5, 10, 20} on the reference seed.

**Statistical evaluation.** For *every* seed we compute AUROC with bootstrap 95% CIs (2000 resamples) and a paired bootstrap significance test on each pairwise AUROC difference (resampling problem indices jointly for both signals, so paired rather than independent) — this revision extends what was previously computed only on the reference seed to all 5 seeds. We add a one-sided binomial sign test on the claim "token entropy has the highest AUROC in every seed" (5/5 successes against a null of 50/50). We compute the Pearson correlation between each seed's greedy accuracy and the size of token entropy's AUROC advantage over the mean of the two sampling-based signals, to check whether the effect is an artifact of a particular capability level. We also stratify AUROC by greedy-answer digit length (1, 2, 3 digits) to test the mechanism our first draft proposed.

## 3. Results

**Reference seed (seed 0, K=10).** Greedy accuracy 84.75% (339/400). AUROCs: token entropy **0.876**, semantic entropy 0.824, self-consistency 0.831.

| Signal | AUROC (seed 0) | 95% bootstrap CI |
|---|---|---|
| Token-level predictive entropy | 0.876 | [0.835, 0.911] |
| Self-consistency | 0.831 | [0.774, 0.881] |
| Semantic entropy | 0.824 | [0.766, 0.875] |

Paired bootstrap tests (seed 0): semantic entropy vs. token entropy, mean diff −0.051, 95% CI [−0.091, −0.013], **p=0.007**; self-consistency vs. token entropy, mean diff −0.044, CI [−0.086, −0.006], **p=0.021**; semantic entropy vs. self-consistency, mean diff −0.007, CI [−0.021, 0.007], p=0.363 (not significant).

**Per-seed significance (new in this revision).** Repeating the paired bootstrap test on all 5 seeds instead of only seed 0 shows the effect's *sign* is stable but its *significance* is not:

| Seed | Greedy acc. | Token AUROC | p (semantic vs. token) | p (self-cons. vs. token) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 84.8% | 0.876 | 0.007 | 0.021 |
| 1 | 81.5% | 0.968 | 0.122 | 0.029 |
| 2 | 41.8% | 0.691 | 0.326 | 0.356 |
| 3 | 87.5% | 0.991 | 0.036 | 0.032 |
| 4 | 77.8% | 0.892 | 0.203 | 0.264 |

Token entropy has the point-estimate-highest AUROC in all 5/5 seeds (one-sided binomial sign test against a 50/50 null, **p=0.031**), so the *direction* of the effect is unlikely to be chance. But the *magnitude* is only significant at α=0.05 on 2/5 seeds for semantic entropy and 3/5 seeds for self-consistency — seed 2, the lowest-accuracy run (41.8%), shows the weakest, non-significant separation for both comparisons. Had we happened to pick seed 2 as our single "reference seed" in the first revision, we would have reported a null result instead of p=0.007. This is exactly the seed-selection sensitivity the round-2 reviews asked us to check for, and it means: trust the sign (5/5, sign-test-significant), but do not treat any single seed's p-value, including our own reference seed's p=0.007, as representative of the effect's typical strength.

**Does the advantage scale with accuracy?** Pearson correlation between per-seed greedy accuracy and token entropy's AUROC advantage over the mean of the two sampling-based signals is r=0.15 across the 5 seeds — near zero, with no visible trend toward the advantage shrinking as accuracy approaches ceiling (seed 3, the highest-accuracy seed at 87.5%, has one of the largest AUROC gaps of any seed: 0.991 vs. ~0.976). This does not support a simple story where the effect is only a low-accuracy artifact, but 5 seeds is too few to fit this relationship precisely; it is a suggestive data point for the future-work question of whether the advantage persists as capacity/training length increase.

**K sweep (seed-0 model).** Token entropy is invariant to K (0.876, from the greedy decode). Semantic entropy and self-consistency both increase with K — 0.714/0.755 at K=2, 0.784/0.795 at K=5, 0.859/0.866 at K=10, 0.856/0.864 at K=20 — approaching but not surpassing token entropy, with diminishing returns after K≈10. The sampling-based signals are not mis-implemented; they behave as expected, just from a lower ceiling here.

**Stratified by answer digit length.** Token entropy's advantage is concentrated in 2- and 3-digit answers (256 and 107 examples), beating both sampling signals by 6–7 AUROC points (2-digit: 0.848 vs. 0.781/0.788; 3-digit: 0.919 vs. 0.895/0.898). On 1-digit answers (37 examples, the smallest and noisiest stratum, no multiple-comparison correction applied across the 3 strata × 2 comparisons here) the point estimates flip and match our original hypothesis (token 0.837 < semantic 0.848 < self-consistency 0.852), but with n=37 and no significance test at this stratum size, this should be read only as a suggestive counter-pattern, not a confirmed reversal. The 2- and 3-digit result is more trustworthy given larger n, and is consistent with the opposite of our original mechanism: accumulated per-token entropy along the greedy path becomes a richer signal as more decision points accumulate, while sampling-based clustering on a small discrete answer space saturates its information faster.

**Cost and runtime.** Token entropy costs 1 generation; semantic entropy/self-consistency cost K+1. In this revision's own run (5 seeds, the K sweep, per-seed bootstrap tests, and the stratified analysis, all on CPU), total wall-clock was 555.8 seconds (~9.3 minutes), matching `elapsed_seconds` in the released `results_summary_v2.json`. We previously claimed "under 11 minutes on CPU" as a fixed bound; three of four round-2 reviewers reproduced the identical code on their own hardware and measured 550–972 seconds (9.2–16.2 minutes) — a real spread, not a fabricated number, but our earlier phrasing understated how hardware-dependent it is. We now report the runtime as measured (~9–16 minutes across four independent CPU reproductions) rather than as a single fixed claim.

## 4. Conclusion

Across two revisions, this paper has become a worked example of exactly the statistical failure mode it studies. Draft 1 reported a single seed favoring sampling-based signals. Draft 2 added 5 seeds and bootstrap statistics on one reference seed and found the opposite — token entropy wins, p=0.007. This draft adds per-seed testing and finds a third layer of nuance: the *sign* of the effect (token entropy highest in 5/5 seeds) is itself statistically supported (sign test p=0.031), but the *significance* of the gap on any individual seed varies from p=0.007 to p=0.356, so a single reference seed's p-value — ours included — is not a stable estimate of how strong the effect typically is. We do not think this means token entropy is "better" in general: the digit-length stratification suggests the ordering may flip for very short answers (though underpowered), the K sweep shows the sampling-based signals scale sensibly, and accuracy does not predict the effect size (r=0.15). The main claim we can support is methodological, now sharpened by this revision: **at the AUROC gaps typically reported in this literature (2–5 points), neither a single-seed comparison nor a single-seed significance test is a reliable way to establish which uncertainty signal is better** — even seed selection alone can turn p=0.007 into p=0.36 within a fully controlled setting where model, task, and clustering are all fixed.

**Limitations.** Still a single tiny model family, one synthetic task, one architecture; exact-match clustering trivializes the hardest part of semantic entropy's design, so results should not be extrapolated to open-ended NLG. Five seeds establish that the sign of the effect is stable and sign-test-significant but remain too few to fit a precise per-seed variance model or a reliable accuracy-vs-effect-size trend. The digit-length stratification is exploratory (small, unequal strata, no multiple-comparison correction) and should be read as a hypothesis for follow-up, not a confirmed mechanism. Runtime is hardware-dependent (measured 9.3–16.2 minutes across reproductions) and should not be read as a portable performance claim.

**Future work.** More seeds to fit the per-seed significance and accuracy-correlation distributions precisely; testing whether the token-entropy advantage persists as model capacity/training length increase toward saturation, informed by but not resolved by our r=0.15 accuracy-advantage correlation; and running the same per-seed rigorous protocol on an open-domain QA setting to check whether the *reported* literature ordering (semantic entropy > token entropy) itself survives equivalent seed-level scrutiny.
