# Cheap Uncertainty Signals for Error Prediction in Language Models: A Controlled Comparison on a Synthetic Arithmetic Task

## Abstract

Detecting when a language model's output is likely wrong is a central open problem for reliable deployment. A growing literature proposes uncertainty signals of varying cost: token-level predictive entropy (nearly free, one forward pass), self-consistency voting, and semantic entropy (both requiring K stochastic samples). Prior work reports semantic entropy outperforms naive token entropy on open-domain QA with large pretrained models, but these comparisons are entangled with pretraining artifacts, retrieval, and expensive human-labeled correctness judgments, and typically report point estimates from a single run. We isolate the comparison in a fully controlled setting — a small Transformer decoder trained from scratch on a synthetic arithmetic task with programmatically exact ground truth and exact (not entailment-based) semantic clustering — and, following reviewer feedback on an earlier draft, replace the single-run comparison with a statistically rigorous one: 5 independent training seeds, bootstrap confidence intervals and paired significance tests on AUROC differences, a sweep over the number of samples K, and a stratified breakdown by answer length. The result reverses our earlier qualitative claim. Averaged over seeds, token-level predictive entropy is the *strongest*, not the weakest, of the three signals in this setting (AUROC 0.69–0.99 across seeds, always highest), and on our reference seed the gap over semantic entropy and self-consistency is statistically significant (bootstrap paired test, p=0.007 and p=0.021 respectively; 95% CIs exclude zero). Semantic entropy and self-consistency remain statistically indistinguishable from each other (p=0.36), consistent with our earlier caution. We conclude that the token-entropy-vs-sampling-based-signal ordering reported for large pretrained LLMs does *not* automatically transfer to this small from-scratch setting, and that single-seed comparisons of these signals — including our own original draft — are not a reliable basis for claims about which signal is "better." We release the full training/evaluation code, raw per-example outputs, and all seeds/statistics.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect statements ("hallucinations"), and distinguishing confident-and-correct outputs from confident-sounding-but-wrong ones is a practical necessity for any deployed system. Because ground-truth correctness labels are expensive at scale, a large body of work studies *unsupervised* uncertainty signals extracted from the model itself.

**Related work.** Malinin & Gales (2021) formalized token-level "sequence-level uncertainty." Kuhn et al. (2023) argued token entropy conflates lexical and semantic variability and proposed **semantic entropy**: cluster K sampled generations by (approximate) semantic equivalence, then compute the Shannon entropy of the cluster distribution. Farquhar et al. (2024, *Nature*) extended this to open-domain QA, reporting gains over token entropy and self-consistency. Because full semantic entropy costs K generations, follow-up work sought cheaper approximations (Kossen et al., 2024; Semantic Energy, 2025). A 2025 KDD survey (Xiao et al.) notes that no single uncertainty metric dominates across tasks and that most reported comparisons run on large pretrained models where pretraining exposure, decoding strategy, and task familiarity confound the *signal's* intrinsic ability to track correctness — and, notably, are typically reported as single-run point estimates without confidence intervals.

**Gap.** It is unclear whether semantic entropy's reported advantage reflects something fundamental about the signals or artifacts of specific pretrained models, benchmarks, and — as we found in revising this paper — insufficient statistical scrutiny of what are often small AUROC gaps. We are not aware of prior work replicating this comparison in a from-scratch setting with exact ground truth *and* seed/bootstrap-level statistical rigor.

**Contribution.** This paper is itself a case study in that gap. An earlier version of this work reported a single-seed result (58.5% greedy accuracy, K=10) in which semantic entropy and self-consistency outperformed token entropy by 2–3 AUROC points, and described this as "replicating" the qualitative pattern from large pretrained LLMs. Four independent reviews of that draft converged, without exception, on the same core objection: a single training run with no seed variation and no significance testing cannot support that claim. We agree, reran the comparison with 5 seeds, bootstrap CIs, a K sweep, and a stratified breakdown, and report what we actually found: the earlier ordering does not hold up, and in the opposite direction from what we expected.

## 2. Method

**Task.** Each example is a string `a<op>b=` where `a, b ∈ [1, 99]`, `op ∈ {+, -}` (subtraction ordered non-negative), target is the decimal answer string — exactly checkable, no human judgment needed.

**Model.** A character-level, decoder-only Transformer (`TinyGPT`): token + learned positional embeddings, 3 pre-norm Transformer layers used causally, d_model=64, 4 heads, GELU MLPs, ≈300K parameters, trained entirely from random initialization (no pretraining, no external data).

**Training.** AdamW, lr=3e-3, batch size 64, 1500 steps, teacher-forced cross-entropy on `<bos> prompt target <eos>` sequences with fresh random examples each batch. We deliberately keep training short so the model is genuinely uncertain rather than saturated or random. We train **5 independent seeds** (0–4, controlling both weight initialization and the training data stream) rather than one, and report the full spread rather than a single point estimate.

**Signals compared.** For each of 400 freshly sampled held-out problems per seed:
1. **Token-level predictive entropy**: greedy-decode; average per-step softmax Shannon entropy (nats).
2. **Semantic entropy**: draw K samples at temperature 0.9; parse to integers; cluster by exact value equality; Shannon entropy of the cluster-frequency distribution.
3. **Self-consistency**: fraction of K samples matching the greedy answer.

Signals are computed from the same generations. K=10 is the reference value (matching prior single-pass-vs-sampling cost comparisons); we additionally sweep **K ∈ {2, 5, 10, 20}** on the reference seed.

**Statistical evaluation.** For the reference seed (seed 0) we compute AUROC with **bootstrap 95% confidence intervals** (2000 resamples of the 400 problems) and a **paired bootstrap significance test** on each pairwise AUROC difference (resampling problem indices jointly for both signals in each replicate, so the test is paired rather than treating the two AUROC estimates as independent — addressing the same concern a McNemar-style or DeLong test would target). Across all 5 seeds we report each signal's AUROC per seed to characterize seed sensitivity directly, rather than averaging it away. We also stratify AUROC by the greedy answer's digit length (1, 2, 3 digits) to test the specific mechanism our earlier draft proposed (that token entropy under-weights multimodality that only appears in later digits).

## 3. Results

**Reference seed (seed 0, K=10, matching the original protocol).** Greedy accuracy 84.75% (339/400). AUROCs: token entropy **0.876**, semantic entropy 0.824, self-consistency 0.831 — token entropy is now the *strongest* signal, the reverse of our earlier draft's finding on a different reproduction of the same nominal setup.

| Signal | AUROC (seed 0) | 95% bootstrap CI |
|---|---|---|
| Token-level predictive entropy | 0.876 | [0.835, 0.911] |
| Self-consistency | 0.831 | [0.774, 0.881] |
| Semantic entropy | 0.824 | [0.766, 0.875] |

Paired bootstrap tests (seed 0): semantic entropy vs. token entropy, mean diff −0.051, 95% CI [−0.091, −0.013], **p=0.007**; self-consistency vs. token entropy, mean diff −0.044, CI [−0.086, −0.006], **p=0.021**; semantic entropy vs. self-consistency, mean diff −0.007, CI [−0.021, 0.007], p=0.363 (not significant — consistent with our earlier caution that these two closely related signals shouldn't be over-differentiated).

**Seed sensitivity.** Across 5 independently trained seeds, greedy accuracy ranges from 41.8% to 87.5% — training-run variance alone is large in this small, short-training regime — and **token entropy has the highest AUROC in every single seed** (range 0.69–0.99 across seeds, vs. 0.67–0.97 for semantic entropy and 0.67–0.98 for self-consistency). The direction of the token-entropy-vs-sampling-signal gap is therefore consistent across seeds, but it is consistently the *opposite* of what our original draft (and the large-pretrained-model literature) reported.

**K sweep (seed-0 model).** Token entropy is invariant to K (0.876, computed once from the greedy decode). Semantic entropy and self-consistency both *increase* with K — 0.714/0.755 at K=2, 0.784/0.795 at K=5, 0.859/0.866 at K=10, 0.856/0.864 at K=20 — approaching but not surpassing token entropy as K grows, with diminishing returns after K≈10. This shows the sampling-based signals are not simply mis-implemented; they are behaving as expected (more samples → better estimate of the true output distribution's entropy), they are just being compared against an unusually strong single-pass baseline in this setting.

**Stratified by answer digit length.** Token entropy's advantage is driven almost entirely by 2- and 3-digit answers (256 and 107 examples respectively), where it beats both sampling signals by 6–7 AUROC points (2-digit: 0.848 vs. 0.781/0.788; 3-digit: 0.919 vs. 0.895/0.898). On 1-digit answers (37 examples, smallest and noisiest stratum) the ordering flips and matches our original hypothesis (token 0.837 < semantic 0.848 < self-consistency 0.852). This directly contradicts the mechanism we proposed in the earlier draft — that per-step greedy entropy under-weights multimodality that only manifests in later digits. If anything, the opposite appears true here: when there are more decision points (more digits), the *accumulated* per-token entropy along the greedy path becomes a richer, better-calibrated signal, while sampling-based clustering on a small, discrete answer space saturates its information faster than token-level entropy does.

**Cost.** Token entropy costs 1 generation; semantic entropy/self-consistency cost K+1. The full experiment (5 seeds × training + eval, plus the K sweep and stratified analysis) completed in under 11 minutes on CPU.

## 4. Conclusion

Our original draft reported a single from-scratch training run in which sampling-based uncertainty signals modestly outperformed token-level entropy, and described this as replicating the pattern reported for large pretrained LLMs on open-domain QA. All four independent reviews of that draft flagged the same weakness: no seed variation, no significance testing, and headline AUROC gaps of only 2–3 points. Taking that feedback seriously and rerunning the comparison with 5 seeds and bootstrap statistics, we find the opposite qualitative result: token-level entropy is the strongest signal, consistently across seeds and with a statistically significant margin on our reference seed. We do not think this means token entropy is "better" in general — our stratified analysis shows the ordering flips on shorter answers, and the K sweep shows the sampling-based signals behave sensibly, just from a lower ceiling in this specific small-model, short-answer regime. The main scientific conclusion we can support is methodological: **at the AUROC gaps typically reported in this literature (2–5 points), single-seed, non-bootstrapped comparisons are not a reliable way to establish which uncertainty signal is better**, and this holds even in a fully controlled from-scratch setting where confounds like entailment-clustering noise and label noise have been removed. We would not have discovered this without the reviewers' insistence on statistical rigor, and we flag it as a caution for the broader signal-comparison literature this paper set out to probe.

**Limitations.** Still a single tiny model family, one synthetic task, and one architecture; the exact-match clustering that makes ground truth cheap here also trivializes the hardest part of semantic entropy's design, so these results should not be extrapolated to open-ended NLG without further work. Five seeds is enough to establish that the sign of the effect is stable but not enough to fit a precise variance model; the digit-length stratification is exploratory (small n per stratum, no correction for multiple comparisons) and should be read as a hypothesis for follow-up, not a confirmed mechanism.

**Future work.** Extending the seed count further, testing whether the token-entropy advantage persists as model capacity/training length increase toward saturation, and running the same rigorous multi-seed/bootstrap protocol on an open-domain QA setting to check whether the *reported* literature ordering (semantic entropy > token entropy) itself survives equivalent statistical scrutiny, are the most direct next steps raised by this revision.
