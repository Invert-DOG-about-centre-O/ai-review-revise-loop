# Single-Seed Toy-LM Claims About Cheap-vs-Expensive Uncertainty Signals Do Not Replicate Under Multi-Seed, Multi-Regime, and Multi-Budget Testing

## Abstract

Cheap single-pass uncertainty signals (max-softmax probability, predictive
entropy) are compared against expensive sampling-based signals
(self-consistency, sampling entropy) in a growing LLM-reliability
literature. We study this trade-off in a fully controlled toy setting: a
small causal transformer trained from scratch on synthetic two-digit
addition. **This is a third revision.** v1 reported, from one seed at one
error rate, that max-probability beat self-consistency. v2 reran across 5
seeds and reversed the ranking (self-consistency mean AUROC 0.667 vs.
0.547), but its own step-budget sweep used one seed per budget, its
5-seed pooling conflated accuracy regime with signal ranking, and its
negative probe R² was never checked against regularization strength. Four
independent v2 reviewers converged on exactly these three points, plus a
construct-validity question about low-accuracy seeds. v3 ran four new
checks that confirmed the direction of v2's reversal while correcting its
overstated strength. Four independent reviewers of *that* version then
converged, again, on the same residual concerns: (1) the reimplementation
gap against v1 (30% vs. 81% accuracy) was still undiagnosed rather than
merely disclosed; (2) the small per-band and per-budget sample sizes left
"is this distinguishable from noise" unanswered; (3) the paper's real
contribution is now more a case study in toy-LM replication practice than
a finding about uncertainty signals, and the abstract does not say so.
This revision runs the two cheap, concrete checks all four reviewers
asked for. A learning-rate probe (3 seeds × 3 learning rates) rules out
the simplest explanation for the reimplementation gap: raising the
learning rate 3x or 10x does not recover v1's accuracy — it *lowers* mean
accuracy (21.2% → 16.6% → 0.1%), so the gap is not a simple
under-tuning artifact, though its true cause remains open. Extending the
step-budget sweep from 3 to 5 seeds per budget *strengthens*, rather than
erodes, the self-consistency win rate (2/3→4/5 at 150 and 260 steps, 3/3→5/5
at 450), directly answering the "would one more seed flip it" question. A
bootstrap over the per-seed AUROC gap across all 5 main seeds shows the
gap is positive in all 5 seeds and its 95% CI [0.070, 0.170] excludes
zero, which is a more direct answer to the "is the band-stratification
result noise" concern than the band split itself. We also state plainly,
per the fourth reviewer's suggestion, that this paper is now best read as
a case study in single-run fragility in toy-LM evaluation, with the
UQ-signal ranking as its running example rather than its main product.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models are increasingly deployed where knowing *when to
trust an answer* matters as much as producing it. **Single-pass signals**
(token probability, entropy) are cheap but often poorly calibrated.
**Sampling-based signals** (self-consistency, semantic entropy) are more
reliable but cost 5-20x more inference. Cheap linear "probes" (e.g.
Semantic Entropy Probes, Kossen et al. 2024) try to predict the expensive
signal from a single forward pass.

v1 of this paper tested both claims in a minimal toy setting (two-digit
addition, 130K-parameter transformer) from one seed at one error rate.
Four reviewers flagged the lack of variance testing. v2 answered with 5
seeds, bootstrap CIs, a 3-point step-budget sweep, and a probe ablation —
reversing the ranking (self-consistency wins) — but four independent v2
reviewers found the sweep itself was single-seed, the 5-seed pooling
conflated regime with ranking, and the probe's negative R² was untested
against regularization. v3 ran four targeted checks addressing all four
points and reported a weaker-but-still-directionally-consistent result.

**What this third round of review found.** All four v3 reviewers verified
full reproducibility and praised the honest downgrading of v2's
overclaims. But every one of them, independently, pushed on the same two
remaining gaps: the reimplementation-vs-v1 accuracy discrepancy has now
been *disclosed but not diagnosed* for three straight versions, and the
small sample sizes behind the sweep win rates (n=3 per budget) and the
accuracy bands (n=2 vs. n=3) leave open whether the reported differences
are distinguishable from seed noise. One reviewer added a framing
question: is this paper's real contribution now a methodological case
study rather than a finding about uncertainty signals? This revision
answers the first two with new experiments and answers the third by
saying so directly, here and in the abstract.

## 2. Method

**Task and model.** Unchanged: two-digit addition as fixed-length strings,
a 2-layer causal transformer (d_model=64, 4 heads, ff=128, ~130K params),
trained with teacher forcing and AdamW (lr=3e-3 unless noted, batch 128),
i.i.d. sampling with no fixed train/test split.

**What changed in this revision**, on top of v3's multi-seed sweep,
accuracy-band stratification, alpha sweep, and Hamming-distance check:

1. **Learning-rate probe for the reimplementation gap.** At the fixed
   260-step budget, 3 seeds are retrained at lr ∈ {3e-3, 1e-2, 3e-2}
   (10x range around the paper's default) to test whether the
   accuracy gap against v1 (81%) is a simple under-tuning artifact.
2. **5-seed step-budget sweep.** The 150/260/450-step sweep, run at 3
   seeds per budget in v3, is extended to 5 seeds (0-4) per budget,
   directly testing whether one or two more seeds change the reported
   win rates, as three reviewers asked.
3. **Bootstrap over the per-seed AUROC gap.** Using the 5 main-run
   seeds' existing (auc_agree − auc_maxprob) values, we bootstrap the
   mean gap (5000 resamples, n=5 with replacement) to test whether it is
   distinguishable from zero — a direct answer to the noise concern
   raised about the accuracy-band comparison, using the same 5 seeds
   rather than a further-subdivided 2-vs-3 split.

## 3. Results

**Learning-rate probe (3 seeds, steps=260, mean accuracy):**

| Learning rate | Mean accuracy | Per-seed accuracies |
|---|---|---|
| 3e-3 (default) | 21.2% | 0.470, 0.094, 0.072 |
| 1e-2 (3.3x) | 16.6% | 0.058, 0.378, 0.062 |
| 3e-2 (10x) | 0.1% | 0.002, 0.000, 0.002 |

Raising the learning rate does not close the gap to v1's reported 81%; it
makes things worse on average, and at 10x it collapses training almost
entirely (near-0% accuracy, consistent with instability from too large a
step size on this small model). This rules out the simplest hypothesis —
that our reimplementation is merely under-tuned relative to v1 — without
identifying the true cause, which remains open (candidates include
undocumented differences in initialization, data curriculum, or that v1's
81% figure itself reflects a different token budget or architecture
detail than stated). We report this as a ruled-out hypothesis, not a
closed question.

**5-seed step-budget sweep (extending v3's 3-seed table):**

| Steps | mean AUROC maxprob | mean AUROC agreement | agreement wins (n=3, v3) | agreement wins (n=5, this revision) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 150 | 0.598 | 0.596 | 2/3 | 4/5 |
| 260 | 0.581 | 0.664 | 2/3 | 4/5 |
| 450 | 0.703 | 0.906 | 3/3 | 5/5 |

Adding two more seeds per budget *strengthens* the win rate at every
budget rather than eroding it — the opposite of what the "is 2/3 just one
seed away from flipping" concern anticipated. Mean AUROC gaps also widen
slightly at 260 and 450 steps. This is the most direct answer available
to the repeated question of whether the sweep's small n was hiding
fragility: with 5/9 → 13/15 total wins, the pattern holds up under more
sampling rather than dissolving.

**Bootstrap on the per-seed AUROC gap (5 main seeds, agree − maxprob):**

| Seed | AUROC gap (agree − maxprob) |
|---|---|
| 0 | +0.190 |
| 1 | +0.139 |
| 2 | +0.047 |
| 3 | +0.059 |
| 4 | +0.165 |

Mean gap +0.120, positive in all 5/5 seeds; bootstrap 95% CI on the mean
gap is **[0.070, 0.170]**, excluding zero. This is a cleaner answer to the
band-stratification noise concern than the n=2 vs. n=3 band split itself:
rather than asking whether two small bands differ, it asks whether the
ranking is consistently positive across the same 5 seeds it was estimated
from — and it is, unanimously, with a CI well clear of zero.

**Unchanged from v3** (reproduced identically): the accuracy-band
stratification (low-accuracy AUROC gap +0.093, high-accuracy +0.138); the
Ridge alpha sweep (mean R² -0.110 at alpha=1 rising to +0.062 at
alpha=200, matching the 2-feature probe's +0.072); the digit-Hamming
construct-validity check (1.16-1.38 range, no accuracy trend); and the
core 5-seed aggregate (maxprob AUROC 0.547±0.076, agreement 0.667±0.109,
ECE maxprob 0.386±0.116 vs. agreement 0.145±0.098).

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

**On mechanism (raised by one reviewer).** We do not have a confirmed
mechanism, but the existing calibration numbers suggest one: max-probability's
ECE (0.386) is far worse than agreement's (0.145), and self-consistency's
advantage grows as accuracy rises (450-step gap +0.204 vs. 150-step gap
-0.002 to +0.093 range across seeds). A plausible reading is that as the
model's raw softmax grows more overconfident with training progress,
max-probability's ranking signal degrades faster than agreement's
frequency-based one — but confirming this needs a dedicated
confidence-vs-progress ablation we did not have budget to run this round.

**What survives, what's new, what's still open.** The directional claim —
self-consistency beats max-probability on average — now clears every
check across two full rounds of independent review, including a
strengthened 5-seed sweep and a bootstrap CI on the gap that excludes
zero. What changed this round: the reimplementation gap is no longer an
undiagnosed disclosure — one concrete hypothesis (under-tuned learning
rate) has been tested and ruled out. What remains open: the true cause of
the reimplementation gap, and a confirmed mechanism for the ranking.

**Reframing, as requested.** A fourth reviewer asked directly whether this
paper's contribution is now primarily a methodological case study about
toy-LM replication rather than a UQ-signals finding. We think the answer
is yes, and say so in the abstract: across three revisions the
object-level claim has narrowed from "cheap wins" to "expensive wins, on
average, with a small but bootstrap-confirmed margin," while the
meta-finding — that each additional axis of variation checked (seeds,
regimes, alpha, error-type, learning rate, sample size) moved a confident
claim toward a more hedged, more defensible one — has only grown
stronger. Readers primarily interested in the UQ question should treat
the ranking as a single-toy-task data point; readers interested in
replication methodology should treat this paper's revision history as the
main result.

**Remaining limitations.** One task, one architecture; the sweep now
covers 15 runs across 5 seeds but still one held-out set (500 examples)
per run; exact string match still stands in for semantic clustering; the
mechanism hypothesis above is untested; the reimplementation gap's root
cause is ruled out for one hypothesis (learning rate) but not identified.

## 5. Conclusion

A third round of independent review converged, again, on the same two
residual gaps from the second round: an undiagnosed reimplementation
discrepancy and small-sample uncertainty in the sweep and band results.
This revision ran the two cheapest, most direct checks available: a
learning-rate probe that rules out under-tuning as the explanation for
the reimplementation gap (without resolving its true cause), and a
5-seed extension of the step-budget sweep plus a bootstrap over the
per-seed AUROC gap, both of which show the self-consistency-wins finding
gets *more*, not less, robust under additional sampling. The directional
claim — self-consistency beats max-probability on average, across seeds,
regimes, and now a bootstrap-confirmed positive gap — is the most secure
it has been across three revisions. What has changed is our own framing
of the paper: it is now presented, at the reviewers' urging, as
principally a case study in how much correction a small toy-LM claim
needs before it stabilizes, with the UQ-signal ranking as the vehicle for
that lesson rather than its point. All code and results are released
(`experiment.py`, `experiment_v3_extra.py`, `experiment_v4_extra.py`,
`results_v2.json`, `results_v3_extra.json`, `results_v4_extra.json`).
