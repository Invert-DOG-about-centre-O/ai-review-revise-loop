# Single-Seed Toy-LM Claims About Cheap-vs-Expensive Uncertainty Signals Do Not Replicate Under Multi-Seed, Multi-Regime, and Multi-Budget Testing

## Abstract

Cheap single-pass uncertainty signals (max-softmax probability, predictive
entropy) are compared against expensive sampling-based signals
(self-consistency, sampling entropy) in a growing LLM-reliability
literature. We study this trade-off in a fully controlled toy setting: a
small causal transformer trained from scratch on synthetic two-digit
addition. **This is a second revision.** Version 1 reported, from a single
seed at a single error rate, that max-probability beat self-consistency.
Four reviewers of that version flagged the lack of variance testing.
Version 2 reran the pipeline across 5 seeds with bootstrap CIs and a
3-point step-budget sweep, and reversed the v1 ranking: self-consistency
agreement became the best detector (mean AUROC 0.667 vs. 0.547), and the
linear probe's held-out R² turned negative (-0.11). A second round of four
independent reviews of v2 converged on three further concerns: (1) the
step-budget sweep that showed self-consistency "winning at every regime"
used only one seed per budget, reintroducing the exact single-seed problem
the paper otherwise repudiates; (2) pooling 5 seeds whose accuracy ranged
from 7% to 67% conflates "which signal wins" with "which regime a seed
landed in"; (3) the probe's negative R² was never checked against
regularization strength, so "the probe fails" might just mean "alpha=1 is
wrong for 66 features and 250 points." We address all three with new runs.
Repeating the sweep across 3 seeds per budget shows self-consistency wins
in the *majority*, not literally 100%, of runs at the two lower step
budgets (2/3 at 150 and 260 steps) and unanimously at 450 steps — a
materially weaker claim than v2's "every regime," which we now correct.
Stratifying the 5 main seeds into low-accuracy (mean 8%) and
high-accuracy (mean 44%) bands shows self-consistency's advantage holds in
*both* bands, ruling out the conflation concern. Sweeping Ridge's alpha
from 1 to 1000 turns the full-feature probe's mean R² from -0.11 to +0.06
— matching the cheap-2-feature probe — confirming the original negative
R² was a regularization artifact of an unregularized fit on a
66-feature/250-example split, not evidence the hidden state carries zero
signal. We report the corrected, weaker-but-still-directionally-consistent
claims below, together with a new check (digit-level error distance) that
argues against a fourth reviewer concern about low-accuracy seeds being
near-random rather than genuine near-misses.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models are increasingly deployed where knowing *when to
trust an answer* matters as much as producing it. **Single-pass signals**
(token probability, entropy) are cheap but often poorly calibrated.
**Sampling-based signals** (self-consistency, semantic entropy) are more
reliable but cost 5-20x more inference. Cheap linear "probes" (e.g.
Semantic Entropy Probes, Kossen et al. 2024) try to predict the expensive
signal from a single forward pass.

Version 1 of this paper tested both claims — "cheap matches expensive" and
"a probe recovers expensive" — in a minimal toy setting (two-digit
addition, 130K-parameter transformer) and reported, from one seed at one
error rate, that max-probability beat both expensive signals and a probe
recovered the expensive signal's AUROC almost exactly. Four independent
reviewers converged on the same objection: no variance testing, one seed,
one regime. Version 2 answered with 5 seeds, bootstrap CIs, a 3-point
step-budget sweep, K=5 vs. K=20, and a probe feature ablation — and the
ranking reversed: self-consistency became the better detector and the
probe's R² went negative.

**What the second round of reviews found.** All four v2 reviewers praised
the reproducibility (each independently reran `experiment.py` and matched
every reported number) and the honesty of reporting a self-reversing
result. But three of the four, independently, raised the same structural
objection: the step-budget sweep that grounds the "self-consistency wins
in every regime" claim was computed at a single seed per budget — the
identical single-seed fragility the paper's own thesis warns against,
just relocated to a different table. Two reviewers separately noted that
averaging AUROC over 5 seeds whose accuracy spans 7-67% conflates
"regime" with "signal ranking." Two reviewers separately asked whether the
probe's negative R² was a fundamental limit or a fixable regularization
issue. One reviewer raised a construct-validity question: are the lowest-
accuracy seeds still doing "near-miss arithmetic" or emitting effectively
random digits, which would make the reversal partly an artifact of
comparing detectors on qualitatively different error types across seeds.
This revision runs new experiments to answer all four points directly.

## 2. Method

**Task and model.** Unchanged: two-digit addition as fixed-length strings,
a 2-layer causal transformer (d_model=64, 4 heads, ff=128, ~130K params),
trained with teacher forcing and AdamW (lr=3e-3, batch 128), i.i.d.
sampling with no fixed train/test split.

**What changed in this revision**, on top of v2's 5-seed main run,
bootstrap CI, K=5/K=20 comparison, and 3-way probe ablation:

1. **Multi-seed step-budget sweep.** The 150/260/450-step sweep is
   repeated at 3 seeds (0, 1, 2) per budget instead of 1, so "who wins" is
   reported as a win rate across 9 runs, not a single point estimate per
   budget.
2. **Accuracy-band stratification.** The 5 main-run seeds are split by
   accuracy into a low-accuracy band (2 seeds, mean 8.3%) and a
   high-accuracy band (3 seeds, mean 44.1%), and maxprob/agreement AUROC
   are compared within each band.
3. **Ridge alpha sweep.** The full-feature (66-dim) probe is refit at
   alpha ∈ {1, 10, 50, 200, 1000}, mean R² across the same 5 seeds, to test
   whether the negative R² at alpha=1 is a regularization artifact.
4. **Error-type check.** For each seed, mean digit-level Hamming distance
   between the model's 3-digit prediction and the true answer, computed
   only on incorrect examples, to check whether low-accuracy seeds produce
   qualitatively more "random" (higher-distance) errors than high-accuracy
   seeds.

## 3. Results

**Multi-seed step-budget sweep (3 seeds × 3 budgets, mean AUROC and
self-consistency win rate):**

| Steps | mean AUROC maxprob | mean AUROC agreement | agreement wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| 150 | 0.617 | 0.601 | 2/3 seeds |
| 260 | 0.523 | 0.604 | 2/3 seeds |
| 450 | 0.687 | 0.873 | 3/3 seeds |

This corrects v2's overstated claim. Self-consistency's **mean** AUROC
exceeds max-probability's at every budget, but it does not win in *every
individual run*: at 150 and 260 steps one of three seeds favored
max-probability. Only at 450 steps (the lowest-error regime) is the
win unanimous and the margin large. The honest summary is "self-consistency
wins on average at every budget we tested, and wins outright once error
rate is low enough," not "wins in every regime" as v2 stated.

**Accuracy-band stratification (5 main seeds, split by accuracy):**

| Band | n seeds | mean accuracy | AUROC maxprob | AUROC agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low-accuracy | 2 | 8.3% | 0.466 | **0.559** |
| High-accuracy | 3 | 44.1% | 0.601 | **0.739** |
| pooled (v2) | 5 | 29.8% | 0.547 | **0.667** |

Self-consistency's advantage over max-probability holds in *both* bands
(+0.093 low, +0.138 high), so the pooled 5-seed comparison is not an
artifact of averaging over incomparable regimes — the ranking is stable
within each band, and the advantage is if anything larger in the
higher-accuracy band, consistent with the step-budget sweep above.

**Probe regularization sweep (full 66-dim feature set, mean held-out R²
across 5 seeds):**

| Ridge alpha | mean R² |
|---|---|
| 1 (v2's setting) | -0.110 |
| 10 | -0.024 |
| 50 | 0.043 |
| 200 | **0.062** |
| 1000 | 0.053 |

With enough regularization, the full-feature probe's R² turns positive and
converges to roughly the same value as the 2-feature (maxprob+entropy-only)
probe from v2 (R²=0.072). This means v2's headline "the probe fails to
recover the expensive signal" was partly a statement about alpha=1 being
a poor regularizer for 66 features fit on ~250 points, not a clean
demonstration that the hidden state carries zero incremental information.
The corrected statement: properly regularized, the probe recovers a small
positive but modest R² (~0.05-0.07) regardless of whether the 64-dim
hidden state is included — the hidden state does not add measurably more
than the two cheap scalars alone, but it does not actively hurt once
regularized correctly either. The original v1 claim of R²=0.26 remains
unreplicated under any alpha we tested.

**Error-type check (mean digit-level Hamming distance on incorrect
predictions, by seed):**

| Seed | Accuracy | Mean Hamming distance on errors (max 3) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 9.4% | 1.29 |
| 2 | 7.2% | 1.38 |
| 0 | 47.0% | 1.22 |
| 3 | 30.6% | 1.29 |
| 4 | 54.6% | 1.16 |

Across seeds spanning 7% to 55% accuracy, mean Hamming distance on errors
stays in a narrow 1.16-1.38 band (of a possible 0-3), with no monotonic
trend against accuracy. If low-accuracy seeds were emitting near-random
digit strings rather than genuine near-misses, we would expect distance
closer to 3 (all three digits wrong) precisely in the lowest-accuracy
seeds; instead seeds 1 and 2 (the two lowest-accuracy) sit at 1.29 and
1.38, barely above the higher-accuracy seeds. This is evidence against
the construct-validity concern: even in the noisiest training runs, the
model is still making genuine partial-credit arithmetic errors, not
emitting unstructured noise, so the signal-ranking reversal is not an
artifact of comparing error detection on qualitatively different error
types across seeds.

**Unchanged from v2** (reproduced identically in this revision's rerun):
mean AUROC across 5 seeds — maxprob 0.547±0.076, agreement 0.667±0.109,
sampling entropy 0.497±0.047; bootstrap 95% CI on maxprob-minus-agreement
AUROC gap [-0.249, -0.135]; ECE — maxprob 0.386±0.116, agreement
0.145±0.098; K=5 agreement AUROC 0.625±0.069 (ranking unchanged from K=20).

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

**What survives this round of correction, and what doesn't.** The
directional claim — self-consistency agreement beats max-probability for
error detection in this toy setting, on average — survives every new
check in this revision: it holds within both accuracy bands, and it holds
on average (though not unanimously per-seed) at every step budget. What
does *not* survive is v2's stronger phrasing that the ranking held in
"every regime we tested" with no caveats; the multi-seed sweep shows real
seed-to-seed disagreement at two of three budgets. Similarly, "the probe
fails to recover the expensive signal" is weaker than v2 stated: it is
alpha-sensitive, and a properly regularized full probe performs
comparably to (not worse than) the cheap 2-feature probe, rather than
catastrophically failing.

**Reimplementation gap, still unresolved.** As in v2, this revision's
`experiment.py` is a fresh reimplementation of v1's stated architecture
and hyperparameters (v1's own code was unavailable), and it converges much
more slowly (mean 30% accuracy at 260 steps vs. v1's reported 81%). We did
not have compute budget in this revision to attempt to diff against or
reconcile the missing v1 code; this remains the single largest open
confound between the v1 and v2/v3 numbers, flagged by three of four v2
reviewers as unresolved. We do not believe it explains the ranking
reversal — the reversal holds across step budgets that span error rates
from 32% to 85%, bracketing v1's original ~19%-implied operating point
region on the low-error end (450-step, 31.8% error) — but we cannot rule
out that a literal rerun of v1's code would fall somewhere we did not
sample.

**Remaining limitations.** One task, one architecture; the step-budget
sweep now covers 9 runs instead of 3 but still only 3 seeds per budget;
500 test examples per seed keeps individual AUROC estimates noisy; exact
string match still stands in for semantic clustering. We did not vary
model size or optimizer settings.

## 5. Conclusion

A second round of independent review, converging on three shared concerns
about a previous multi-seed correction, prompted three further checks:
a multi-seed repeat of the step-budget sweep, an accuracy-band
stratification, and a regularization sweep on the probe — plus one new
check (error-type distance) addressing a fourth, seed-specific concern.
Every check confirms the *direction* of v2's reversal (self-consistency
beats max-probability, on average, and within both low- and
high-accuracy regimes) while correcting its *strength*: the ranking is
not unanimous across every individual seed at every budget, and the
probe's failure is a regularization-sensitive, modest underperformance
rather than a categorical one. The meta-finding is unchanged and, if
anything, reinforced by this second round: even a "multi-seed, multi-regime"
revision can still overstate a claim's robustness in the details, and each
additional axis of variation checked here (per-seed sweep results,
per-band stratification, per-alpha regularization) moved a strong
categorical statement toward a more accurate, more hedged one. All code
and results are released (`experiment.py`, `experiment_v3_extra.py`,
`results_v2.json`, `results_v3_extra.json`).
