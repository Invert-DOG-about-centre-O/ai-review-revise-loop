# Calibration Fixes Confidence, Not Prediction Sets: An Empirical Comparison of Temperature Scaling, Ensembling, and Conformal Score Choice for LLM-Style Classifiers

## Abstract

Probabilistic uncertainty quantification for large language models (LLMs) is an active research area, with recent work exploring verbalized confidence, self-consistency sampling, temperature-based calibration, and conformal prediction as ways to produce trustworthy confidence estimates. A recurring open question is how these techniques interact: does fixing pointwise calibration (e.g., via temperature scaling) actually improve the *downstream* guarantee people care about — conformal prediction-set size? We study this empirically in a controlled setting. Since this sandbox has no internet access to download a pretrained LLM, we construct a synthetic surrogate: a small MLP classifier on a noisy, class-overlapping 6-way task, evaluated with the same tools used in the LLM calibration literature (ECE, temperature scaling, deep ensembles as a self-consistency proxy, and split conformal prediction with LAC, APS, and RAPS scores). Every headline number is a mean ± standard deviation over multiple independent seeds (fresh data draw, model init, and bootstrap resample each time). We find that (1) temperature scaling reduces ECE by ~40% (0.128±0.008 → 0.078±0.004); (2) ensembling alone does *not* fix calibration; (3) calibration method changes LAC conformal set size by ≈0.004 at 90% target coverage — smaller than one seed-to-seed standard deviation — and this null persists at 80% coverage too (0.807 vs. 0.809), where LAC still sits below a mean set size of 1 and its empty-set rate nearly doubles to ~19%; (4) switching the score from LAC to APS changes set size by ≈1.8 classes (20+ std), and regularized APS (RAPS) recovers roughly half of that gap back (LAC≈0.91 → RAPS≈1.9 → APS≈2.6–2.8), so score-family choice still dominates calibration method even after controlling for APS's known tendency toward oversized sets; (5) a new ablation isolating the model's fitted temperature T<1 ("underconfidence") shows label noise, not feature-space overlap, is the driver: removing label noise alone pushes T from ≈0.83 to ≈0.98 regardless of overlap level, while removing overlap alone barely moves T. For practitioners deploying LLMs with conformal guarantees, nonconformity-score family dominates calibration method — a distinction easy to lose in survey-level treatments that discuss the two as parallel, interchangeable remedies.

## 1. Introduction

As LLMs are deployed in higher-stakes settings, there is growing interest in equipping their outputs with calibrated confidence or formal uncertainty guarantees. Three families of methods dominate recent work: (1) **confidence estimation** via token-level softmax, verbalized statements, or self-consistency sampling — a 2025 survey (Huang et al., arXiv:2503.15850) notes verbalized confidence is often poorly calibrated; (2) **post-hoc calibration**, chiefly temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017), still a standard baseline in 2025 LLM calibration work (arXiv:2603.09309, arXiv:2604.19444); and (3) **conformal prediction (CP)**, which wraps any confidence function in a distribution-free procedure with a finite-sample coverage guarantee regardless of calibration quality. Recent LLM-specific CP work includes SConU (arXiv:2504.14154), domain-shift-aware CP (arXiv:2510.05566), and MCQA-CP (MIT Media Lab). A key selling point repeated across this literature is that CP's guarantee holds *even for a badly calibrated model* — but papers rarely quantify how much calibration quality affects *efficiency* (set size), how this compares to nonconformity-score choice, or whether that comparison survives switching to a regularized score family designed to fix APS's main weakness.

**Gap.** No paper we found cross-tabulates calibration method (raw vs. temperature-scaled vs. ensembled) against conformal score family (LAC vs. APS vs. RAPS), with variance estimates and multiple target-coverage levels, to ask which factor matters more for prediction-set size. This is the gap we target.

**Our contribution.** A small, reproducible, multi-seed empirical study that: (a) reproduces the standard finding that temperature scaling fixes ECE while ensembling alone does not; (b) shows calibration method's effect on LAC conformal set size is statistically indistinguishable from seed noise, at *both* 90% and 80% target coverage; (c) shows LAC→APS is a large, many-standard-deviation effect, and quantifies how much RAPS narrows that gap without eliminating the ranking; (d) checks robustness of (b)–(c) under a second, low-noise data regime; and (e) runs a 2×2 ablation on the data-generating process to identify *which* knob (label noise vs. feature overlap) drives the model's fitted temperature below 1. We also document a failure mode of LAC (empty prediction sets for ~10–19% of test points, worsening at lower target coverage) masked by mean coverage alone.

**Honesty about scope.** We do not use a pretrained LLM: HuggingFace Hub access in this environment fails with a certificate error (no outbound internet). We instead use a small feedforward network on a synthetic, class-overlapping, label-noised task, explicitly designed to reproduce measurable softmax miscalibration, evaluated with the exact tool stack (ECE, temperature scaling, ensembling, split conformal) used in the LLM literature we surveyed. We treat this as a controlled surrogate, not a claim about any specific LLM's behavior.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Synthetic task
Six classes in a 20-dimensional feature space, generated from class-specific Gaussian means (fixed across seeds) with per-sample isotropic noise (σ=1.8, main regime) inducing class overlap. Training labels are corrupted with 12% uniform label noise to induce memorization-driven miscalibration (Guo et al., 2017); calibration and test labels are noise-free. Split: 4,000 train / 1,500 calibration / 1,500 test. Every seed redraws the full split, retrains the base model and all 5 ensemble members from scratch, and refits temperature scaling and conformal thresholds independently.

### 2.2 Models
- **Base model**: one 2-hidden-layer MLP (64 units, ReLU), Adam, max 80 iterations, full-batch.
- **Ensemble**: 5 MLPs of the same architecture, each on an independent bootstrap resample with a different init, combined by averaging softmax outputs — a lightweight surrogate for LLM **self-consistency** sampling, where diversity comes from stochastic decoding rather than bootstrap/init diversity.

### 2.3 Calibration methods and conformal prediction
Four confidence variants (`raw_softmax`, `temp_scaled`, `ensemble`, `ensemble_temp_scaled`), temperature fit by NLL minimization on the calibration split. Split conformal prediction on the held-out test set, using **LAC** (score = 1 − p(true class)), **APS** (Romano et al., 2020; cumulative sorted softmax mass), and **RAPS** (regularized APS: a rank-based penalty λ·max(rank−k_reg, 0) added to the cumulative-mass score, k_reg=2, λ=0.05, following the standard RAPS recipe) nonconformity scores. Main results use target coverage 90% (α=0.10); we additionally check 80% (α=0.20) for LAC to test whether its below-1 average set size is a floor artifact specific to 90%. We report empirical coverage, average set size, and empty-set fraction, as **mean ± std over seeds** (10 for the main study, 5 for the RAPS/coverage extension and low-noise regime, 3 per cell for the underconfidence ablation — chosen so total compute stays under two minutes).

## 3. Results

All numbers are mean ± std over independent seeds (`experiment.py` → `results.json`; `extension.py` → `extension_results.json`; `sensitivity.py` → `sensitivity_results.json`).

### 3.1 Calibration quality

| Method | Accuracy | ECE | Fitted T |
|---|---|---|---|
| raw_softmax | 0.997±0.001 | 0.128±0.008 | 1.0 |
| temp_scaled | 0.997±0.001 | 0.078±0.004 | 0.830±0.008 |
| ensemble | 1.000±0.001 | 0.131±0.008 | 1.0 |
| ensemble_temp_scaled | 1.000±0.001 | 0.075±0.004 | 0.816±0.010 |

Temperature scaling reduces ECE by ~40% for both the single model and the ensemble, consistently across seeds. The fitted temperature is consistently **below 1** (T≈0.82–0.83) — mild **underconfidence**, opposite the classic Guo et al. (2017) overconfidence finding. **Ensembling alone does not improve ECE** (0.131±0.008 vs. 0.128±0.008, well within 1 std).

**What drives T<1?** We ran a 2×2 ablation (3 seeds/cell) crossing feature overlap (σ=1.8 vs. 0.8) with label noise (12% vs. 0%):

| Regime | Fitted T | ECE |
|---|---|---|
| σ=1.8, label-noise=12% (main) | 0.829±0.006 | 0.126±0.006 |
| σ=1.8, label-noise=0% | 0.980±0.002 | 0.011±0.001 |
| σ=0.8, label-noise=12% | 0.851±0.027 | 0.094±0.021 |
| σ=0.8, label-noise=0% | 0.989±0.001 | 0.004±0.000 |

Holding label noise fixed, σ barely moves T (0.829↔0.851 at 12% noise; 0.980↔0.989 at 0% noise). Holding σ fixed, removing label noise moves T by ~0.13–0.15, from clearly <1 to ≈1. **Label noise, not feature overlap, is the driver of the underconfidence**: it teaches the model to hedge on ambiguous-looking inputs even when calibration/test labels are clean, which the isotonic overlap knob alone does not reproduce.

### 3.2 Conformal prediction: LAC, including a lower target coverage

| Method | Target cov. | Coverage | Avg. set size | Empty-set frac. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw_softmax | 90% | 0.902±0.011 | 0.902±0.011 | 0.098±0.011 |
| temp_scaled | 90% | 0.902±0.011 | 0.902±0.011 | 0.098±0.011 |
| ensemble | 90% | 0.899±0.010 | 0.899±0.010 | 0.101±0.010 |
| ensemble_temp_scaled | 90% | 0.898±0.010 | 0.898±0.010 | 0.102±0.010 |
| raw_softmax | 80% | 0.807±0.012 | 0.807±0.012 | 0.193±0.012 |
| temp_scaled | 80% | 0.809±0.014 | 0.809±0.014 | 0.191±0.014 |

At 90%, the spread of LAC set-size means across calibration methods (≈0.004) is smaller than the ≈0.01 seed-to-seed std — not distinguishable from noise. **This null replicates at 80% coverage** (spread ≈0.002, still below the ≈0.013 seed std), addressing the concern that the 90%-coverage null was an artifact of the specific target level. However, lowering target coverage does **not** move LAC off its floor — average set size drops from ≈0.90 to ≈0.81, still below 1, and the empty-set fraction nearly doubles (≈10%→≈19%). The floor is a structural property of LAC's cutoff rule, not a threshold-specific artifact.

### 3.3 Conformal prediction: APS vs. RAPS

| Method | Coverage | LAC size | APS size | RAPS size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw_softmax | 1.00 | 0.90 | 2.65±0.08 | 1.92±0.03 |
| temp_scaled | 1.00 | 0.91 | 2.84±0.08 | 1.87±0.02 |

APS never produces empty sets and over-covers, averaging 2.6–2.8 of 6 classes. RAPS (k_reg=2, λ=0.05) is markedly smaller than vanilla APS (≈1.9 vs. ≈2.6–2.8) — it does what it is designed to do, controlling APS's large sets — but is still roughly twice LAC's average size and produces zero empty sets, unlike LAC. Calibration method still shifts RAPS non-trivially (temp_scaled RAPS 1.868 vs. raw RAPS 1.919, a ~0.05 gap against seed stds of ≈0.02–0.03), the same direction of shift seen for vanilla APS, though smaller in magnitude.

### 3.4 Cross-cutting comparison

Holding the score fixed, calibration method moves LAC set size by ≈0.004 at both 90% and 80% target coverage (noise-level) and APS set size by ≈0.19–0.26 (a real but modest effect). Holding calibration method fixed, switching score family moves set size by **≈1.0 class (LAC→RAPS)** to **≈1.7–1.9 classes (LAC→APS)** — RAPS recovers roughly half the LAC/APS gap but does not close it. Score-*family* choice remains far larger than either calibration effect, at every coverage level and regularization setting we tested.

### 3.5 Robustness to the noise regime

Rerunning the full pipeline (5 seeds) under a second regime (σ=0.8, zero label noise): the base model is already near-calibrated (ECE≈0.004, fitted T≈0.99, consistent with the ablation in 3.1 attributing underconfidence to label noise). The LAC-vs-calibration spread stays tiny (≈0.005) and the LAC-vs-APS gap, while smaller (≈1.37 vs. ≈1.81 classes), remains dominant.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

The central finding — conformal score family matters far more than calibration method for prediction-set efficiency — now holds across two target-coverage levels, a regularized score variant, and two noise regimes. It helps explain why LLM-focused CP papers invest in designing better nonconformity scores rather than upstream calibration. RAPS narrowing but not closing the LAC/APS gap suggests the gap is partly structural (LAC's hard cutoff vs. any cumulative-mass rule's tendency to include multiple classes) rather than purely an artifact of vanilla APS being unregularized.

Limitations, ordered by how directly they threaten our claims: (1) we could not access a pretrained LLM (HuggingFace Hub SSL failure in this sandbox); all experiments use a synthetic surrogate, so specific numbers should not be read as LLM benchmarks, though the qualitative ranking should transfer since CP's coverage guarantee and score-family structure are model-agnostic. (2) LAC's average set size sits below 1 at both 90% and 80% target coverage; we have not tested whether this floor persists at very low target coverage (e.g., 50–60%) or at lower classifier accuracy. (3) RAPS results use one fixed (k_reg, λ) setting rather than a sweep; different regularization strength could narrow the LAC/APS-family gap further. (4) All experiments use 6 balanced classes and near-ceiling accuracy (99.7%+); whether score-family dominance holds at LLM-scale vocabularies or lower, more QA-realistic accuracy (60–90%) is untested. (5) Our self-consistency-via-bootstrap-ensembling surrogate is imperfect — LLM self-consistency diversity comes from stochastic decoding, not bootstrap/init diversity.

## 5. Conclusion

Across multiple seeds, temperature scaling reliably reduces pointwise calibration error (~40% ECE reduction) while naive ensembling does not. A new ablation shows the model's mild underconfidence (T<1) is driven specifically by label noise, not feature-space overlap. Neither calibration intervention moves LAC conformal set size beyond seed-to-seed noise, at 90% *or* 80% target coverage, where LAC's set size stays below 1 and its empty-set rate roughly doubles at the lower coverage target. The choice of nonconformity score family dominates: LAC→APS moves set size by 20+ standard deviations, and while RAPS recovers about half of that gap versus vanilla APS, it does not eliminate the ranking. Practitioners building LLM systems around conformal guarantees should prioritize nonconformity-score-family selection — checking empty-set rates for LAC-style scores and considering regularized variants for APS-style scores — over investing purely in calibration quality.
