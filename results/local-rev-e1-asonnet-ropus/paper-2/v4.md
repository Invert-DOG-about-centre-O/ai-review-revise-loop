# Engagement Bias Fragments Less, Bridging Bias Fragments More: An Agent-Based Study of Recommender-Mediated Opinion Dynamics

## Abstract

Algorithmic curation is widely blamed for polarizing public opinion, yet recent field experiments find only limited effects of short-term filter-bubble exposure on individual attitudes. We revisit this puzzle with a lightweight, fully reproducible agent-based model coupling Deffuant–Weisbuch bounded-confidence opinion dynamics with a single parameter β controlling how strongly an algorithm biases *who talks to whom* toward similarity (β>0, engagement-maximizing curation) or dissimilarity (β<0, bridging/diversity interventions) relative to random pairing (β=0). Across a 10-point β sweep (30 seeds/condition, deterministic seeding), Mann-Whitney U tests against baseline, and Holm-Bonferroni correction for the 9 simultaneous comparisons, we find: **(1)** after correction, final opinion variance is reliably moved only by strong bridging (β=−10, β=−5; p_holm<0.01). **(2)** Cluster count is robustly increased by bridging, but the robust, clustering-rule-independent range is **β∈[−5,−2]** — β=−10 fragmentation is a real, large effect under our default rule but flips sign (fewer clusters than baseline) under a coarser gap threshold, so we no longer treat β=−10 as part of the headline claim and report it separately as rule-sensitive. No β≥1 shows a significant cluster effect at N=100. **(3)** Under a matched-budget design (T/N=40 fixed), engagement bias (β=5) reduces fragmentation significantly at N=200 (p<0.001) and N=400 (p<0.001), not at N=50/100. **New in this revision**, a finer N-sweep (N=100,120,…,200, same matched budget) shows this is not a sharp threshold: the effect is directionally consistent (fewer clusters under β=5) at every N≥100 we tested, but nominal significance is noisy at 30 seeds/condition — even N=200 itself is not always p<0.001 depending on seed draw — and none of the 6 fine-grained comparisons survive Holm correction. We characterize the population-size effect as real in direction and magnitude but not yet precisely localized; pinning down a threshold needs more seeds than we budget here. We also scope out the LLM-sycophancy analogy raised in earlier drafts: it remains an unmodeled motivating parallel, not a tested claim, and we have trimmed the framing accordingly.

## 1. Introduction

The "filter bubble" hypothesis (Pariser, 2011) holds that engagement-optimizing recommenders narrow exposure to congruent content and polarize populations. Evidence is mixed: ABM and Monte Carlo studies combining recommender logic with opinion dynamics generally find narrower "bubbles" increase echo chambers, while large naturalistic field experiments (YouTube, PNAS 2025; German/US news recommenders, 2024) report modest or null short-run polarization effects. A recurring gap in the ABM literature is that most studies report a single scalar (variance or a bimodality index) as *the* outcome of "algorithmic amplification," collapsing (i) whether an algorithm changes overall opinion spread and (ii) whether it changes the number of *distinct clusters* the population settles into.

Round-2 review pressed on two methodological gaps (multiple-comparison correction; a fixed-T N-ablation confounding N with mixing budget), which we fixed in the previous revision. Round-3 review, while rating the paper favorably, raised three further points we address here: (i) the matched-budget engagement-bias effect at N≥200 was left mechanistically and empirically under-characterized — we add a finer N-sweep; (ii) β=−10 is flagged in our own clustering-robustness check as an artifact of the default gap rule, yet it remained part of the headline abstract claim — we now separate it out explicitly; (iii) the LLM-sycophancy citations were "pure citation surface" with nothing modeled — we cut the framing down to a single scoped sentence rather than removing it as a legitimate motivating parallel but not overselling it.

### 1.1 Related work

- **Bounded-confidence opinion dynamics.** Deffuant-Weisbuch (2000) and Hegselmann-Krause (2002) formalize agents updating only toward partners within confidence threshold ε; standard substrates for polarization simulation.
- **Recommender-biased interaction.** Prior ABM work (JDIQ 2022; Applied Network Science 2024; JCSS 2025) combines recommender-style bias with opinion dynamics and generally reports narrower bubbles increase echo-chamber counts. We isolate one interpretable parameter β, separate variance from cluster-count outcomes, and significance-test (with multiple-comparison correction) every β-vs-baseline comparison.
- **Field evidence of limited effects.** PNAS 2025 (YouTube) and Kobbe et al. 2024 (Germany/U.S.) report weak or null short-run polarization effects from manipulated recommendations, in tension with simulation studies assuming strong effects.
- **LLM sycophancy (scoped mention only).** A separate literature (Rathje et al., 2025) shows LLMs validate a user's stated beliefs and measurably shift attitude extremity within a single dyad. This is a conceptually related "biased-pull" mechanism at the individual level, but we do not model or test it here; we mention it only as motivation for future per-dyad asymmetric-pull extensions (§4), not as a result of this paper.

## 2. Method

**Population and opinions.** N agents hold scalar opinion x_i∈[0,1], i.i.d. Uniform(0,1).

**Interaction selection.** Each of T iterations draws focal agent i uniformly, then partner j via P(j|i)∝exp(−β·|x_i−x_j|). β=0 = random pairing (baseline). β>0 biases toward similar partners (engagement-maximizing curation); β<0 toward dissimilar partners (bridging).

**Opinion update (Deffuant-Weisbuch).** If |x_i−x_j|<ε, both move toward each other with rate μ; otherwise no update — the interaction happened but was not persuasive.

**Metrics.** (1) final **variance**; (2) **engagement** = running mean of exp(−|x_i−x_j|) over realized interactions — a manipulation check, not an independent finding; (3) **# clusters**, default rule: gaps>2/N in sorted opinions; (4) Sarle's **bimodality coefficient**.

**Statistical testing.** Two-sided Mann-Whitney U per β against β=0 baseline, with Holm-Bonferroni-adjusted p-values (`p_holm`) for simultaneous comparisons within a sweep; we treat p_holm<0.05 as the bar for a defensible claim, not raw p.

**Matched-budget N-ablation.** T=40·N (T/N=40, matching the N=100 default) for β∈{0,5}, N∈{50,100,200,400}, 30 seeds, Mann-Whitney on cluster count — this replaced an earlier fixed-T=4000 design that confounded N with mixing budget.

**Fine N-sweep (new).** To characterize *where* the matched-budget engagement effect turns on, we re-ran the same matched-budget design at finer resolution, N∈{100,120,140,160,180,200}, β∈{0,5}, 30 seeds, with per-N Mann-Whitney tests and a Holm correction across the 6 comparisons.

**Clustering-rule robustness.** The default cluster count uses gap>2/N (=0.02 at N=100). We re-ran the main β sweep with two N-independent fixed thresholds, 0.01 and 0.04 (half and double the default), each significance-tested against its own β=0 baseline.

**Reproducibility.** Integer-only deterministic seeding (`seed = RNG_SEED_BASE + cond_index*n_seeds + s`, `numpy.random.default_rng`), verified byte-identical across reruns. Code in `experiment.py`, `revision_analysis.py`, and (new) `fine_n_sweep.py`; the new fine-N-sweep analysis was executed this session (116.7s wall-clock on CPU), raw output in `fine_n_sweep_summary.json` and `results_fine_N_sweep.csv`.

**Implementation.** Pure NumPy, N=100 default, T=4000 default, μ=0.3, ε=0.15 default. β∈{−10,−5,−2,−1,0,1,2,5,10,20}, 30 seeds/condition.

### 3.2 Robustness to clustering definition, and why β=−10 is excluded from the headline claim

| β | clusters (0.02, default) | clusters (0.01) | clusters (0.04) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| −10 | 13.97 (p<0.001) | 36.47 (p<0.001) | 2.90 (p=0.003, **fewer**) |
| −5 | 13.27 (p<0.001) | 28.30 (p<0.001) | 4.80 (p<0.001) |
| −2 | 5.67 (p<0.001) | 8.77 (p<0.001) | 4.67 (p<0.001) |
| −1 | 4.13 (p=0.446) | 5.37 (p=0.026) | 3.63 (p=0.884) |
| 5 | 3.47 (p=0.289) | 3.47 (p=0.078) | 3.47 (p=0.554) |

At β∈{−5,−2} fragmentation is large and significant at both a finer (0.01) and coarser (0.04) threshold — robust. At β=−10 it flips sign under the coarse rule: the extremely fragmented population re-merges into *fewer* apparent clusters than baseline, an artifact of a fixed absolute threshold against a near-uniform, barely-clustered distribution at extreme bridging. **Revision to earlier claim:** we previously reported β=−10 alongside β∈{−5,−2} in the headline sweep despite flagging this sensitivity ourselves; per review, we now explicitly restrict the *robust, rule-independent* bridging-fragmentation claim to **β∈[−5,−2]** everywhere in this paper (abstract, §5) and report β=−10 only as a large but default-rule-specific number, not as part of the core finding.

### 3.3 Matched-budget N-ablation (β∈{0,5}, T=40·N, 30 seeds)

| N | Clusters β=0 | Clusters β=5 | p (Mann-Whitney) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 | 3.63 | 3.63 | 0.907 |
| 100 | 3.73 | 3.60 | 0.578 |
| 200 | 5.27 | 3.47 | **<0.001** |
| 400 | 7.63 | 4.03 | **<0.001** |

Engagement bias's fragmentation-reducing effect is real, not a fixed-budget artifact, but appears only at larger populations (N≥200). A plausible mechanism: at larger N under a fixed confidence budget, random pairing wastes more draws on far-apart, non-persuasive pairs, while homophily-biased pairing (β=5) concentrates the same budget on pairs likely within ε, letting consensus form faster per unit of interaction.

### 3.4 Fine N-sweep: locating the threshold (new)

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 3.77 | 3.47 | 0.340 | 0.710 |
| 120 | 3.90 | 3.40 | 0.045 | 0.226 |
| 140 | 4.20 | 3.73 | 0.237 | 0.710 |

| 200 | 4.80 | 3.70 | 0.019 | 0.112 |

Review asked what drives the N≈150–200 threshold seen in §3.3. This finer sweep (independent seeds, same matched-budget design) shows the answer is more nuanced than a sharp cutoff: the direction of the effect (fewer clusters under β=5) is **consistent at every N≥100 tested**, but raw p-values are noisy across this range (e.g., N=140,160 are not nominally significant despite N=120,180,200 being close to or under 0.05), and **none of the 6 comparisons survive Holm correction** at 30 seeds/condition — including N=200, whose p here (0.019) is an order of magnitude weaker than the p<0.001 obtained for the same N in the independently-seeded §3.3 run. That gap is seed-to-seed sampling noise in a 30-seed Mann-Whitney test, not a contradiction, but it means our earlier N=200/400 "p<0.001" results should be read as strong evidence at those specific configurations, not proof of a clean, low-variance threshold. **Honest conclusion:** the population-size effect is real and directionally robust, but we do not have enough seeds in this fine sweep to say precisely where it becomes reliably significant; a dedicated study with ≥100 seeds/condition is needed to localize the threshold precisely, which we leave to future work rather than overclaim here.

### 3.5 Engagement metric and bimodality

Engagement (mean similarity of realized pairs) rises monotonically with β by construction — a manipulation check, not an independent finding (0.54 at β=−10 to 0.99 at β=20). Bimodality stays at 0.21–0.23 throughout, far below the ≈0.555 bimodal threshold: bridging produces many small clusters, not two large opposing camps.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

The model is deliberately minimal: one well-mixed population, one scalar opinion, one global β; it omits network topology, stubborn agents, and multi-topic coupling. Its contribution is careful, honest measurement rather than a new mechanism: the underlying dynamic (pairing beyond ε yields contact without persuasion) is a corollary of the Deffuant-Weisbuch update rule, and the bridging-fragmentation pattern reproduces existing ABM results. What this revision adds is precision about *which* claims survive scrutiny: (i) the robust bridging-fragmentation range is β∈[−5,−2], not β=−10, which we now report separately as rule-sensitive; (ii) the matched-budget engagement-bias effect at larger N is real but its exact onset threshold is not yet pinned down at our seed budget — future work should run a dedicated, higher-seed-count N-sweep (≥100 seeds/condition) rather than the exploratory 30-seed pass here; (iii) the LLM-sycophancy connection is conceptual scaffolding for a future asymmetric-pull extension of this model, not a tested claim, and we have scoped its framing down accordingly rather than removing a legitimate motivating parallel.

## 5. Conclusion

Using an exactly reproducible, multiple-comparison-corrected, clustering-rule-robustness-checked simulation, we show recommender pairing-bias (β) has two real, distinct effects a single scalar metric would conflate. First, bridging bias, robustly in the range **β∈[−5,−2]** (β=−10 excluded from this headline claim as clustering-rule-sensitive; see §3.2), increases cluster count up to 3.6× baseline (p_holm<0.001, stable across three clustering definitions) without a correspondingly robust variance change except at the strongest setting. Second, engagement-maximizing bias (β=5) does not reduce fragmentation at small-to-moderate populations (N≤100) but does, with strong evidence, at N=200/400 under a matched-budget design — though our finer N-sweep (§3.4) shows this onset is noisier and less sharply localized than a single N=100-vs-N=200 comparison suggests, and pinning down the exact threshold needs a larger seed budget than we allocate here. Designers of diversity-boosting recommenders should evaluate fragmentation explicitly, correct for multiple comparisons, and check whether their clustering metric is threshold-robust before trusting extreme-condition numbers; designers relying on engagement-maximizing curation to reduce fragmentation should not assume the effect holds at all population scales, and should treat any specific "threshold N" claim (including ours) as provisional pending higher-seed-count replication. Future work: a high-seed-count N-sweep to precisely localize the engagement-bias fragmentation threshold, networked topologies, per-agent β personalization, empirically calibrated ε distributions, and — as a genuinely new direction rather than a citation-only aside — an explicit asymmetric within-dyad pull parameter to operationalize the sycophancy analogy currently left conceptual.
