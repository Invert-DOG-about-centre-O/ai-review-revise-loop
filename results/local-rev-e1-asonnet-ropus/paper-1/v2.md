# Cheap Token-Entropy Matches (But Does Not Reliably Beat) Expensive Sampling for Error Detection in a Small Undertrained Transformer

## Abstract

Sampling-based uncertainty quantification (UQ) — semantic entropy, self-consistency — costs 5–10x extra inference compute versus single-pass alternatives (max softmax probability, token entropy). We run a fully controlled, from-scratch study: a 153K-parameter character-level GPT trained on synthetic integer addition until it reaches imperfect accuracy, comparing cheap single-pass entropy against expensive K=8 sampling-based semantic entropy and self-consistency for predicting greedy-answer errors. Across 3 seeds (600 test cases), mean-token-entropy achieves AUROC 0.624 ± 0.047, versus 0.572 ± 0.063 (semantic entropy) and 0.565 ± 0.077 (self-consistency) at 8x the cost. **We now test this gap for significance.** A seed-level bootstrap (n=3, acknowledged to be underpowered) shows a consistently positive gap, but a within-seed example-level bootstrap gives P(gap ≤ 0) of 0.11–0.30 per seed — not conventionally significant, so the honest claim is "matches or modestly exceeds," not "beats." **We also find the two expensive "semantic" signals are highly redundant** (Pearson r = 0.88–0.92 between self-consistency-disagreement and semantic entropy), meaning the three-signal expensive side effectively has ~2 independent degrees of freedom, not 3. **A new K/temperature ablation on seed 0 shows the ranking is not stable**: raising K from 8 to 16–24 lifts semantic entropy and self-consistency AUROC past the cheap baseline (0.625→0.712 and 0.641→0.750 vs. 0.690), but the same sweep on seed 1 shows the opposite — AUROC *falls* from K=8 to K=24 (0.587→0.525 and 0.614→0.526). Temperature has smaller, seed-dependent effects. So expensive sampling is not categorically worse than cheap entropy; it is noisy, redundant across its own signals, and its behavior as K grows is itself seed-dependent — an unstable operating point rather than a reliable win for either side. We report all of this, including the parts that complicate our own headline claim, because that instability is itself the finding.

## 1. Introduction

Uncertainty quantification (UQ) flags likely-wrong LLM outputs for deferral or double-checking. The dominant paradigm samples K generations per prompt and measures disagreement — lexically (self-consistency; Wang et al. 2022) or after semantic clustering (semantic entropy; Kuhn, Gal & Farquhar, *Nature* 2024) — at ~K× the inference cost of one greedy pass. Cheaper single-pass alternatives (max softmax probability, token entropy) require no extra generations; Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al. 2024) go further, learning to approximate sampling-based UQ from single-pass hidden states. Recent surveys (KDD 2025 UQ/calibration survey) note no single UQ metric dominates across settings.

**Gap.** Most cheap-vs-expensive comparisons run on large pretrained models on open-ended QA, where accuracy, difficulty, and temperature confound the comparison. We instead ask, with model/task/training fully controlled and reproducible from scratch: *does expensive K-sample sampling actually beat the cheapest single-pass entropy signal at detecting greedy-decode errors, and how robust is that comparison to statistical noise, signal redundancy, and the choice of K?*

**Contribution.** A minimal offline testbed (tiny GPT on synthetic arithmetic) comparing five UQ signals across 3 seeds, extended in this revision with (1) bootstrap significance tests at both the seed level and the within-seed example level, (2) a redundancy check among the expensive signals, and (3) a K∈{8,16,24}/temperature∈{0.5,0.9,1.3} sensitivity ablation — directly answering reviewer concerns that the original headline gap could be within noise, that the expensive signals might be near-degenerate, and that K=8/T=0.9 might understate what sampling can do.

## 2. Related Work

**Sampling-based UQ.** Semantic entropy clusters K sampled generations by bidirectional entailment before computing cluster entropy, addressing lexical overcounting of paraphrases. Self-consistency samples K chain-of-thought completions and majority-votes; the vote margin doubles as an uncertainty signal. Both require K ≫ 1 generations.

**Cheap alternatives.** Semantic Entropy Probes train a lightweight probe on single-generation hidden states to predict sampling-based semantic entropy without sampling. Token entropy and max-softmax-probability are the simplest single-pass baselines, standard in the calibration literature but often reported as inferior to sampling-based methods on open-ended generation.

**Positioning.** We do not claim cheap signals beat expensive ones in general — large-model open-ended-generation work shows sampling helps substantially, especially for lexical-form variation single-pass entropy cannot see. Our contribution is a controlled small-scale study of *how robust* a cheap-beats-expensive finding actually is once you check significance, signal redundancy, and K-sensitivity — a check this literature rarely applies because retraining is normally too expensive, but which is cheap here.

## 3. Method

**Task.** Synthetic addition as strings: `"{a}+{b}="` → `"{a+b}"`, a,b ∈ [0,40] uniform, disjoint train/test (a,b) pairs (700/200 per seed).

**Model.** Causal decoder-only `TinyGPT` (`nn.TransformerEncoderLayer` + causal mask): d_model=64, 4 heads, 3 layers, dropout 0.1, 153,039 parameters, 15-token character vocabulary. AdamW (lr=3e-4), 60 epochs, batch 32, loss masked to the answer span. Deliberately small/brief so accuracy is imperfect but non-trivial.

**UQ signals.** *Cheap (1x):* `mean_token_entropy`, `max_token_entropy` from the greedy pass. *Expensive (≈8x, K=8, T=0.9):* `lexical_diversity` (distinct raw strings / K), `self_consistency_disagreement` (1 − majority fraction), `semantic_entropy` (entropy over parsed-integer clusters — the correct semantic clustering here, since two strings are equivalent iff they parse to the same integer).

**Evaluation.** AUROC per seed for predicting greedy-answer incorrectness (200 test cases), plus logistic-regression probes on (a) cheap-only, (b) expensive-only, (c) all five signals, each fit on a random half of a seed's test set and evaluated on the other half. 3 seeds, mean ± std.

**New in this revision — significance, redundancy, and K/T sensitivity.** (i) *Seed-level bootstrap*: resample the 3 per-seed AUROC gaps with replacement (10,000 draws) — informative about direction but not a valid CI given n=3, which we flag rather than overstate. (ii) *Example-level bootstrap*: within each seed, resample the 200 test examples (2,000 draws) and recompute the mean-token-entropy vs. semantic-entropy AUROC gap, reporting P(gap ≤ 0) per seed — this is the more defensible test since our real unit of noise is which 200 (a,b) pairs land in the test set. (iii) *Redundancy*: Pearson/Spearman correlation between `self_consistency_disagreement` and `semantic_entropy` per seed, since both derive from the same K=8 sample set. (iv) *K/T ablation*: retrain fresh models for seeds 0 and 1, then re-run sampling-based signals at K ∈ {8, 16, 24} (T=0.9 fixed) and T ∈ {0.5, 0.9, 1.3} (K=8 fixed, seed 0 only, due to compute budget), holding the trained weights and test set fixed so only the sampling budget changes. All code (`experiment.py`, `aggregate.py`, `stats_check.py`, `ablation_k_temp.py`, `ablation_k_temp_seed1.py`) and raw outputs are in this directory.

## 4. Results

**Accuracy/cost.** Greedy accuracy: 48.5%/20.0%/16.5% (seeds 0/1/2), mean 28.3% — high seed-to-seed variance from small model/data. Majority-vote accuracy tracked (not reliably beat) greedy (59.0%/17.5%/23.0%; worse than greedy in 2/3 seeds). Measured cost ratio: 7.8x–8.6x (mean 8.2x), confirming K=8 sampling costs close to 8x.

**Individual signal AUROC** (mean ± std, 3 seeds; chance = 0.5):

| Signal | Cost | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| mean_token_entropy | 1x | **0.624 ± 0.047** |
| max_token_entropy | 1x | 0.586 ± 0.058 |
| semantic_entropy | 8x | 0.572 ± 0.063 |
| self_consistency_disagreement | 8x | 0.565 ± 0.077 |
| lexical_diversity | 8x | 0.563 ± 0.045 |

Mean token entropy wins individually in all 3 seeds (0.686 vs. 0.673; 0.571 vs. 0.531; 0.615 vs. 0.524).

**Is this gap real? (reviewer Q1)** Seed-level bootstrap (resampling which of our 3 seeds we "ran," 10,000 draws) gives mean gap +0.052 to +0.061 vs. each expensive signal with 95% CI excluding 0 (e.g., semantic entropy: [+0.025, +0.091]) — but with only 3 seeds this bootstrap is a directional heuristic, not a valid interval, and we do not want to overclaim it. The more defensible test is within-seed example-level bootstrap (2,000 resamples of the 200 test cases per seed) for mean-token-entropy vs. semantic entropy (the best expensive signal): mean gap is positive in all 3 seeds (+0.025, +0.040, +0.089) but P(gap ≤ 0) is 0.30, 0.27, and 0.11 respectively — not significant at conventional thresholds in any single seed. **Conclusion: the data support "cheap matches expensive at 1/8 the cost," consistently across seeds in direction, but not the stronger "cheap reliably beats expensive" framing our original abstract used.**

**Are the expensive signals redundant? (reviewer Q2)** Pearson correlation between `self_consistency_disagreement` and `semantic_entropy` is 0.92 (seed 0), 0.88 (seed 1), 0.89 (seed 2) — both derived from the same 8 samples, they are near-duplicates on this compact answer space, so the "three expensive signals" comparison effectively has ~2 independent degrees of freedom, making the expensive side's apparent underperformance partly a redundancy artifact rather than three independently weak signals.

**Learned probes:**

| Probe | Signals | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| cheap_probe | mean/max token entropy | **0.619 ± 0.053** |
| expensive_probe | lexical div., self-consistency, semantic entropy | 0.545 ± 0.089 |
| all_probe | all five | 0.586 ± 0.105 |

The cheap probe still clearly beats the expensive probe; `all_probe` doesn't help, consistent with the redundancy finding above — the expensive signals add noise, not orthogonal information.

**How sensitive is this to K and temperature? (reviewer Q3)** On seed 0, raising K at fixed T=0.9 *helps* the expensive signals past the cheap baseline (mean_token_entropy = 0.690 fixed): semantic entropy 0.625 (K=8) → 0.711 (K=16) → 0.712 (K=24); self-consistency 0.641 (K=8) → 0.708 (K=16) → 0.750 (K=24). Temperature sweeps (K=8, seed 0) show smaller, mixed effects: T=0.5 gives 0.656/0.689, T=1.3 gives 0.624/0.623, both still below the K=16+ results. But on seed 1 the *same* K sweep goes the other way: semantic entropy 0.587 (K=8) → 0.525 (K=24); self-consistency 0.614 (K=8) → 0.526 (K=24) — increasing K *hurt* on this seed. (Seed 1's own cheap baseline was also unusually low in this rerun, 0.511, versus 0.571 in the original run with nominally the same seed — CPU floating-point/thread nondeterminism appears to shift outcomes meaningfully in this small, high-variance regime, itself a finding.) **So K=8/T=0.9 does not systematically understate sampling's potential — sometimes more samples help a lot (seed 0), sometimes they hurt (seed 1), and neither expensive nor cheap signals are a stable bet at fixed compute in this regime.**

## 5. Limitations

One 153K-parameter model, one synthetic task, and (for the core 5-signal comparison) 3 seeds; the K/T ablation used fresh retrained models on only 2 of those seeds due to compute budget, so its qualitative disagreement between seeds is suggestive, not conclusive — more seeds are needed to know whether K-sensitivity itself has a stable sign. Per-seed, within-seed significance for the headline AUROC gap is weak (Section 4); readers should treat "cheap matches expensive at matched budget" as the supported claim, not "cheap beats expensive." The two expensive "semantic" signals are highly correlated, so the 3-vs-2 signal-count comparison is not an apples-to-apples independent-signal comparison. We did not evaluate Semantic Entropy Probes. None of this generalizes to large pretrained LLMs on open-ended QA, where lexical variation of correct answers — the failure mode semantic clustering targets — is far more prominent than in this compact, exactly-parseable answer space.

## 6. Conclusion

In a controlled, from-scratch, CPU-only study, the cheapest single-pass signal (mean token entropy) matches or modestly exceeds expensive 8x-cost sampling-based signals for detecting a small transformer's greedy-answer errors — but our own significance and sensitivity checks show this gap is not statistically decisive within any single seed, the expensive signals are largely redundant with each other, and the ranking is unstable as K grows (better than cheap at K≥16 on one seed, worse at K=24 on another). The methodological lesson is not "cheap always wins" but that comparisons like this need explicit significance testing, redundancy checks among "independent" signals, and a K-sensitivity sweep before any compute-vs-quality claim is trustworthy — checks nearly free here and worth insisting on even where retraining is expensive.
