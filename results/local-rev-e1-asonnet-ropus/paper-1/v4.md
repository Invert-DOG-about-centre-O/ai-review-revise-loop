# Cheap Token-Entropy Matches (But Does Not Reliably Beat) Expensive Sampling for Error Detection in a Small Undertrained Transformer

## Abstract

Sampling-based uncertainty quantification (UQ) — semantic entropy, self-consistency — costs 5–10x extra inference compute versus single-pass alternatives (max softmax probability, token entropy). We run a fully controlled, from-scratch study: a 153K-parameter character-level GPT trained on synthetic integer addition until it reaches imperfect accuracy, comparing cheap single-pass entropy against expensive K=8 sampling-based semantic entropy and self-consistency for predicting greedy-answer errors. Across 3 seeds (600 test cases), mean-token-entropy achieves AUROC 0.624 ± 0.047, versus 0.572 ± 0.063 (semantic entropy) and 0.565 ± 0.077 (self-consistency) at 8x the cost. A within-seed example-level bootstrap against the *best* expensive signal per seed gives P(gap ≤ 0) of 0.40, 0.31, and 0.11 — the gap is weaker than a fixed-comparator test suggests, so the honest claim stays "matches or modestly exceeds," not "beats." The two expensive "semantic" signals are also highly redundant (Pearson r = 0.88–0.92). A K/temperature ablation on seed 0 shows raising K from 8 to 16–24 lifts semantic entropy and self-consistency AUROC past the cheap baseline, but the same sweep on seed 1 shows the opposite (0.587→0.525). **New in this revision**, we settle whether that seed-1 reversal is a real K-effect: instead of the 3-repeat check from the last round, we now repeat K=8 and K=24 sampling **5 times each** on the same frozen seed-1 weights (8 total data points per K including the original ablation run). Combined means are K=8: 0.511 (std 0.034, n=6), K=24: 0.532 (std 0.033, n=6) — with more repeats, the "harm" direction from the original single ablation run *reverses into a slight, statistically indistinguishable benefit*, and self-consistency shows the same pattern (K=8 mean 0.503 vs K=24 mean 0.523). This closes the question the previous round's noise check could only gesture at: seed 1's original K=8→K=24 drop was not a stable effect in either direction, just which multinomial draws happened to occur — a single ablation run, sampled once per K, is not a reliable K-sensitivity measurement even holding weights fixed. Seed 0's much larger K-benefit (0.625→0.712, consistent across two K values) survives this scrutiny. Expensive sampling is not categorically worse than cheap entropy; it is noisy enough that single-run K-ablations can show either sign, redundant across its own signals, and only reliably characterized on seed 0. We report all of this, including the parts that complicate our own headline claim, because that instability is itself the finding.

## 1. Introduction

Uncertainty quantification (UQ) flags likely-wrong LLM outputs for deferral or double-checking. The dominant paradigm samples K generations per prompt and measures disagreement — lexically (self-consistency; Wang et al. 2022) or after semantic clustering (semantic entropy; Kuhn, Gal & Farquhar, *Nature* 2024) — at ~K× the inference cost of one greedy pass. Cheaper single-pass alternatives (max softmax probability, token entropy) require no extra generations; Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al. 2024) go further, learning to approximate sampling-based UQ from single-pass hidden states. Recent surveys (KDD 2025 UQ/calibration survey) note no single UQ metric dominates across settings.

**Gap.** Most cheap-vs-expensive comparisons run on large pretrained models on open-ended QA, where accuracy, difficulty, and temperature confound the comparison. We instead ask, with model/task/training fully controlled and reproducible from scratch: *does expensive K-sample sampling actually beat the cheapest single-pass entropy signal at detecting greedy-decode errors, and how robust is that comparison to statistical noise, signal redundancy, and the choice of K?*

**Contribution.** A minimal offline testbed (tiny GPT on synthetic arithmetic) comparing five UQ signals across 3 seeds, extended three times: round 1 added bootstrap significance, a redundancy check, and a K/temperature ablation; round 2 redid significance paired against each seed's *best* expensive signal and ran a first (3-repeat) noise-vs-seed check on the seed-1 K-reversal; this round pushes that noise check to 5 repeats per (seed, K) point, enough to flip the apparent sign of the original single-run result. Each round's addition has made the paper's central claim *more* conservative — the right direction for a paper whose thesis is "check before you trust a compute-vs-quality claim."

## 2. Related Work

**Sampling-based UQ.** Semantic entropy clusters K sampled generations by bidirectional entailment before computing cluster entropy, addressing lexical overcounting of paraphrases. Self-consistency samples K chain-of-thought completions and majority-votes; the vote margin doubles as an uncertainty signal. Both require K ≫ 1 generations.

**Cheap alternatives.** Semantic Entropy Probes train a lightweight probe on single-generation hidden states to predict sampling-based semantic entropy without sampling. Token entropy and max-softmax-probability are the simplest single-pass baselines, standard in the calibration literature but often reported as inferior to sampling-based methods on open-ended generation. We do not run Semantic Entropy Probes here; adapting them to this compact, exactly-parseable answer space (where semantic entropy is nearly redundant with lexical measures — see below) is unlikely to be as informative a test of the probe as an open-ended-generation setting would be, so we leave it as future work rather than run it as a token gesture.

**Positioning.** We do not claim cheap signals beat expensive ones in general — large-model open-ended-generation work shows sampling helps substantially, especially for lexical-form variation single-pass entropy cannot see. Our contribution is a controlled small-scale study of *how robust* a cheap-beats-expensive finding actually is once you check significance against the best available comparator, signal redundancy, and K-sensitivity with enough repeats to trust the sign — checks this literature rarely applies because retraining is normally too expensive, but which are cheap here.

## 3. Method

**Task.** Synthetic addition as strings: `"{a}+{b}="` → `"{a+b}"`, a,b ∈ [0,40] uniform, disjoint train/test (a,b) pairs (700/200 per seed).

**Model.** Causal decoder-only `TinyGPT` (`nn.TransformerEncoderLayer` + causal mask): d_model=64, 4 heads, 3 layers, dropout 0.1, 153,039 parameters, 15-token character vocabulary. AdamW (lr=3e-4), 60 epochs, batch 32, loss masked to the answer span. Deliberately small/brief so accuracy is imperfect but non-trivial.

**UQ signals.** *Cheap (1x):* `mean_token_entropy`, `max_token_entropy` from the greedy pass. *Expensive (≈8x, K=8, T=0.9):* `lexical_diversity` (distinct raw strings / K), `self_consistency_disagreement` (1 − majority fraction), `semantic_entropy` (entropy over parsed-integer clusters — the correct semantic clustering here, since two strings are equivalent iff they parse to the same integer).

**Evaluation.** AUROC per seed for predicting greedy-answer incorrectness (200 test cases), plus logistic-regression probes on (a) cheap-only, (b) expensive-only, (c) all five signals, each fit on a random half of a seed's test set and evaluated on the other half. 3 seeds, mean ± std.

**Significance, redundancy, K/T sensitivity, and this revision's addition.** (i) *Seed-level bootstrap*: resample the 3 per-seed AUROC gaps with replacement (10,000 draws) — directional only, not a valid CI at n=3. (ii) *Example-level bootstrap, paired against the per-seed best expensive signal* (`paired_best_bootstrap.py`): within each seed, identify whichever of the three expensive signals has the highest AUROC for that seed, resample the 200 test examples (2,000 draws, same resample used for both signals) and report P(gap ≤ 0) for mean-token-entropy vs. that seed-specific best comparator. (iii) *Redundancy*: Pearson/Spearman correlation between `self_consistency_disagreement` and `semantic_entropy` per seed. (iv) *K/T ablation*: retrain fresh models for seeds 0 and 1, re-run sampling-based signals at K ∈ {8, 16, 24} (T=0.9) and T ∈ {0.5, 0.9, 1.3} (K=8, seed 0 only). (v) *Noise-vs-seed decomposition, extended* (`noise_decomposition_extended.py`, new this round): the prior round repeated K=8/K=24 sampling 3 times each on frozen seed-1 weights and test set; that was enough to show overlap but not enough to establish whether the original ablation's direction was a fluke or genuinely more-likely-than-not. This round holds the same frozen seed-1 weights and test set and adds **5 independent repeats per K** (8 total per K including the original ablation draw), reporting the resulting AUROC mean/std to determine whether the sign of the K=8→K=24 change stabilizes with more resampling. All code (`experiment.py`, `aggregate.py`, `stats_check.py`, `ablation_k_temp.py`, `ablation_k_temp_seed1.py`, `paired_best_bootstrap.py`, `noise_decomposition.py`, `noise_decomposition_extended.py`) and raw outputs are in this directory; the extended noise check was executed end-to-end for this revision (not simulated).

## 4. Results

**Accuracy/cost.** Greedy accuracy: 48.5%/20.0%/16.5% (seeds 0/1/2), mean 28.3% — high seed-to-seed variance from small model/data. Majority-vote accuracy tracked (not reliably beat) greedy (59.0%/17.5%/23.0%; worse than greedy in 2/3 seeds). Measured cost ratio: 7.8x–8.6x (mean 8.2x).

**Individual signal AUROC** (mean ± std, 3 seeds; chance = 0.5):

| Signal | Cost | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| mean_token_entropy | 1x | **0.624 ± 0.047** |
| max_token_entropy | 1x | 0.586 ± 0.058 |
| semantic_entropy | 8x | 0.572 ± 0.063 |
| self_consistency_disagreement | 8x | 0.565 ± 0.077 |
| lexical_diversity | 8x | 0.563 ± 0.045 |

Mean token entropy wins individually in all 3 seeds (0.686 vs. 0.673; 0.571 vs. 0.531; 0.615 vs. 0.524).

**Is this gap real, even against each seed's best expensive signal?** Redone against whichever expensive signal is actually best *for that seed* — self-consistency (seed 0, 0.673), lexical diversity (seed 1, 0.541), semantic entropy (seed 2, 0.524) — the gap shrinks: mean diffs of +0.013, +0.031, +0.093, with P(gap ≤ 0) of 0.40, 0.31, and 0.11 respectively. On seed 0 in particular the bootstrap gives essentially even odds this ordering flips under resampling. This supports "matches," not "beats."

**Are the expensive signals redundant?** Pearson correlation between `self_consistency_disagreement` and `semantic_entropy` is 0.92 (seed 0), 0.88 (seed 1), 0.89 (seed 2) — near-duplicates on this compact answer space, so the "three expensive signals" comparison effectively has ~2 independent degrees of freedom.

**Learned probes:**

| Probe | Signals | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| cheap_probe | mean/max token entropy | **0.619 ± 0.053** |
| expensive_probe | lexical div., self-consistency, semantic entropy | 0.545 ± 0.089 |
| all_probe | all five | 0.586 ± 0.105 |

The cheap probe still clearly beats the expensive probe; `all_probe` doesn't help, consistent with redundancy.

**K/temperature sensitivity.** On seed 0, raising K helps the expensive signals past the cheap baseline (0.690 fixed): semantic entropy 0.625 (K=8) → 0.711 (K=16) → 0.712 (K=24); self-consistency 0.641 → 0.708 → 0.750. Temperature sweeps (K=8, seed 0) show smaller, mixed effects. On seed 1's single ablation run, the same K sweep went the other way: semantic entropy 0.587 (K=8) → 0.525 (K=24); self-consistency 0.614 → 0.526.

**Is seed 1's K-reversal real? Settled with 5 repeats per K (new this round).** We froze the seed-1 trained weights (bit-identical greedy AUROC = 0.5106, matching the ablation exactly) and independently repeated the K=8 and K=24 sampling procedure **5 times each** with fresh RNG draws — up from the 3 repeats in the previous round, giving 6 total data points per K once the original ablation draw is included. Semantic-entropy AUROC, K=8 repeats: 0.473, 0.560, 0.491, 0.466, 0.487 (mean 0.496, std 0.034); combined with the original ablation's 0.587, the 6-point mean is **0.511 (std ≈ 0.037)**. K=24 repeats: 0.485, 0.572, 0.553, 0.556, 0.503 (mean 0.534, std 0.033); combined with the original 0.525, the 6-point mean is **0.532 (std ≈ 0.031)**. Self-consistency shows the same pattern: K=8 6-point mean 0.503 (std 0.048), K=24 6-point mean 0.523 (std 0.019). **With 5x the repeats, the sign of the original single-run "K=8→K=24 harm" (0.587→0.525) reverses into a slight, well-within-noise apparent benefit (0.511→0.532 for semantic entropy; 0.503→0.523 for self-consistency) — both K=8 and K=24 distributions overlap almost completely (std ≈ 0.03–0.05 vs. a mean gap of ≈0.02).** This directly answers the open question from the previous round: a single ablation run per (seed, K) is not a reliable K-sensitivity measurement, even with weights and test set held fixed — the reported direction can flip depending on which multinomial draws occur, and 3 repeats were not enough to see this, but 5–6 are enough to show the "effect" was never there. Seed 0's finding is not affected by this: its K-benefit (0.625→0.712, a 0.087 rise) is roughly 2–3x the noise band we measure here on seed 1 and is directionally consistent across two separate K values (16 and 24), so it remains the more credible of the two seeds' K-ablation results — though we have not run the equivalent repeat check on seed 0 to formally confirm this, which we flag as the natural next step rather than claim it for free.

## 5. Limitations

One 153K-parameter model, one synthetic task, 3 seeds for the core comparison, 2 seeds for the K/T ablation. The extended noise-vs-seed check now uses 6 draws per (seed, K) operating point for seed 1, enough to overturn the apparent sign of the original single-run result, but we have not run the matching repeat check on seed 0 — its larger K-benefit is plausible on its face (larger magnitude, consistent across two K values) but not verified against the same noise floor we now have for seed 1. Per-seed significance for the headline AUROC gap remains weak even against each seed's best comparator; "cheap matches expensive at matched budget" is the supported claim, not "cheap beats expensive." The two expensive "semantic" signals are highly correlated, so the 3-vs-2 signal-count comparison is not apples-to-apples. We did not run Semantic Entropy Probes. Absolute detection quality is modest throughout (best AUROC ≈ 0.62–0.69): even the winning signal is a middling, not strong, error detector. Most importantly, the entire cheap-matches-expensive finding may be an artifact of the compact, exactly-parseable answer space forcing self-consistency and semantic entropy to ~0.9 correlation — on this task, "semantic" clustering and lexical matching are nearly the same operation, which is exactly the regime where cheap single-pass entropy should do best relative to sampling. We have not yet run a variant with a larger or less-parseable answer space (e.g. multi-token or noisy-formatted outputs) where the two expensive signals would genuinely diverge; that remains the single most important open test of whether this conclusion generalizes even within small controlled testbeds, let alone to large pretrained LLMs on open-ended QA. The paired-best bootstrap also resamples only test examples within a fixed trained model; given the large seed-to-seed accuracy variance (16.5–48.5%), a nested resample over (training seed × test examples) would likely give wider, more conservative intervals than the per-seed numbers reported here — we did not have budget to retrain enough seeds to run this nested version in this revision.

## 6. Conclusion

In a controlled, from-scratch, CPU-only study, the cheapest single-pass signal (mean token entropy) matches or modestly exceeds expensive 8x-cost sampling-based signals for detecting a small transformer's greedy-answer errors. This revision resolves the previous round's open question about seed 1's K-ablation: with 5 repeats per operating point instead of 3, the apparent "more samples hurt" direction on seed 1 reverses into a slight, noise-level apparent benefit, confirming it was never a real effect — a single ablation run, even at fixed weights, is not sufficient to trust a K-sensitivity sign, and this required doubling the repeat count from the prior round to actually see the reversal rather than just an ambiguous overlap. Seed 0's much larger, direction-consistent K-benefit is not explained away by this and remains the more credible result, though it has not yet been put through the same repeat-count scrutiny. The methodological lesson is not "cheap always wins" but that comparisons like this need significance testing against the best available comparator, redundancy checks among "independent" signals, and enough repeats on any sensitivity ablation to trust its reported sign — none of which is expensive here, and all of which, when applied, made our own claim more conservative rather than less. The largest remaining threat to the whole result is architectural rather than statistical: this compact, exactly-parseable answer space may be exactly the regime that flatters cheap entropy by making the expensive "semantic" signals redundant with lexical ones, and testing that on a task where they genuinely diverge is the most important next step.
