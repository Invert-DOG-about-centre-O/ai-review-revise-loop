# Does Semantic Clustering Actually Beat Lexical Entropy? A Controlled Toy Study of Uncertainty Signals in a Small Transformer

## Abstract

Semantic entropy — clustering sampled generations by meaning before computing
entropy — is widely presented as an improvement over naive lexical (surface-form)
entropy for detecting hallucinations in LLMs, on the intuition that surface-form
diversity inflates apparent uncertainty for questions that are actually
semantically determinate. We test this in a fully controlled synthetic
environment where ground-truth ambiguity is known by construction, removing
confounds (unknown ground truth, imperfect paraphrase clustering, uncontrolled
answer length) that make this comparison hard to isolate on real LLMs. We train
a small (≈0.4M-parameter) causal Transformer from scratch on a synthetic
"capital city" QA task with deterministic, genuinely ambiguous, and
out-of-distribution (OOD) question classes, and compare lexical vs.
oracle-semantic sampling entropy as ambiguity predictors — now across **5
independent seeds** rather than one, per reviewer feedback. Lexical entropy is
a consistently equal-or-better predictor of ambiguity than semantic entropy
(mean AUROC 1.000±0.000 vs. 0.987±0.011, lexical ≥ semantic in 5/5 seeds), and
this holds even under a new ablation in which alias diversity is deliberately
made class-correlated (more aliases for cities appearing in ambiguous
countries) — a design meant to break the "additive offset" mechanism we
proposed, which it narrows (semantic AUROC drops to 0.970±0.017) but does not
reverse. A second, revised finding: unlike our original single-seed run,
multi-seed replication shows the length–entropy correlation among
deterministic questions is **not** reliably near zero (lexical: mean r=0.29,
std=0.17 across seeds; semantic: mean r=−0.09, std=0.21) — with only 25
questions per class, single-seed correlation estimates are unstable, and we
now report this as a noisy null rather than a clean one. We also newly
quantify OOD detection: semantic entropy separates OOD from in-distribution
questions well (AUROC 0.888±0.074) but the "unmapped token" novelty rate,
while informative, is a much noisier signal (AUROC 0.584±0.156). We report all
of this as an honest, small-scale data point, with variance estimates now
attached to every headline number, and all code, logs, and raw results
included for inspection and re-run.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models frequently produce fluent but incorrect or unsupported
statements ("hallucinations"), and a growing line of work uses **sampling-based
uncertainty quantification (UQ)** — generating multiple completions and
measuring their diversity — as a training-free detector. The best-known method,
**semantic entropy** (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*),
clusters sampled answers into equivalence classes using an NLI model before
computing entropy, arguing that naive token/string-level entropy conflates
*how an answer is phrased* with *whether the model knows the answer*.
Follow-up work proposes cheaper approximations — Semantic Entropy Probes
(Kossen et al., 2024), Kernel Language Entropy (NeurIPS 2024), Semantic Energy
(2025), and Semantic Reformulation Entropy (2025).

A recent evaluation paper — *"Revisiting Uncertainty Quantification Evaluation
in Language Models: Spurious Interactions with Response Length"* (2025) — flags
that reported UQ benchmark gains may partly reflect a length confound rather
than genuine discrimination. Survey papers (KDD 2025; ACM CSUR 2025) note that
evaluating semantic vs. lexical clustering cleanly is hard in practice: real QA
datasets have unknown ground-truth ambiguity, clustering depends on an
imperfect NLI/judge model, and length is entangled with topic/difficulty.

**Gap.** We are not aware of small, fully controlled studies isolating the
lexical-vs-semantic comparison from these confounds via a synthetic world
where true ambiguity and semantic equivalence classes are known exactly. This
lets us ask: *holding clustering quality and ground truth fixed and known,
does semantic entropy separate ambiguous from deterministic questions better
than raw lexical entropy — reliably, across seeds and under an alias-diversity
design that specifically targets our own explanation for why it might not?*

**Contribution.** We build such an environment, train a small Transformer on
it from scratch (CPU-only), and, revising our initial single-seed submission
per reviewer feedback, report: (1) a null result replicated over 5 seeds —
lexical entropy is not inflated relative to oracle-semantic entropy, and
consistently is a marginally cleaner ambiguity signal; (2) a targeted ablation
in which alias diversity is made class-correlated, which narrows but does not
reverse this null result; (3) a corrected, noisier length-confound finding —
the near-zero correlation we originally reported was itself a single-seed
artifact; and (4) a newly quantified positive result — entropy detects
out-of-distribution questions well above chance (AUROC 0.89), while a cheaper
proxy (novel-token rate) is a much noisier detector.

## 2. Related Work

- **Semantic entropy family.** Kuhn, Gal & Farquhar (ICLR 2023) cluster
  sampled generations via NLI entailment before computing entropy; Farquhar et
  al. (*Nature*, 2024) scale this to confabulation detection. Kossen et al.
  (2024) show semantic uncertainty is largely linearly decodable from hidden
  states. Kernel Language Entropy (NeurIPS 2024) generalizes hard clustering
  to a continuous kernel. Semantic Energy (2025) clusters penultimate-layer
  logits instead of post-softmax probabilities. Semantic Reformulation Entropy
  (2025) perturbs the question instead of clustering outputs.
- **Confound / evaluation critiques.** The length-bias paper (arXiv:2504.13677,
  2025) shows several UQ benchmarks' gains correlate with answer length rather
  than genuine calibration; survey literature (KDD 2025; ACM CSUR 2025) frames
  UQ evaluation methodology itself as an open problem.
- **This paper's relation to that work.** We contribute a controlled
  instrument for testing claims from that literature (semantic > lexical;
  length confounds exist) at small scale with exact ground truth — a
  complement to, not a replacement for, large-scale benchmark evaluation. We
  do not implement real NLI-based clustering even in the synthetic world; our
  "semantic entropy" is an oracle upper bound on clustering quality, so our
  results speak to the *ambiguity-detection ceiling* semantic clustering could
  achieve, not to any specific NLI implementation's real-world performance,
  which additionally suffers clustering error.

## 3. Method

**Synthetic world.** 65 "countries": 25 deterministic (one true city), 25
ambiguous (2–3 true cities), 15 OOD (unseen at training). 15 "cities"
(semantic classes), each with surface **aliases** — distinct token sequences
(length 1–3) referring to the same city, analogous to real paraphrase
variation. Training sequences are `BOS Q <country> SEP <alias-tokens> EOS`.
This gives an exact oracle alias→city map, sidestepping real NLI-clustering
error entirely (a deliberate scope choice, see §2).

**Model.** Causal Transformer (2 layers, d_model=64, 4 heads, dff=128, ≈0.4M
params), trained with teacher forcing for 1200 steps (batch 64, Adam,
lr=3e-3), CPU-only, ~20–40s per run.

**Sampling & metrics.** K=40 samples/question; lexical entropy (Shannon
entropy over raw token sequences), semantic entropy (entropy over oracle city
id, with "unmapped" sequences that match no known alias tallied separately as
a novelty rate), mean answer length.

**Revisions made in response to round-1 review.** (1) *Multi-seed replication*
(reviewer weakness 1 / question 1): the entire pipeline (world construction,
training, sampling, evaluation) is re-run under 5 independent seeds {0..4},
and we report mean±std for every headline statistic instead of one draw. (2)
*Class-correlated alias ablation* (reviewer question 2): a second world
variant assigns 4 aliases to any city that appears in an ambiguous country's
answer set, vs. 2 aliases for cities appearing only in deterministic
countries — the direct opposite of the original design's alias-diversity/class
independence, run over 3 seeds. Because a handful of cities are shared between
ambiguous and deterministic country answer sets, this manipulation is not a
perfectly clean class split (some deterministic questions inherit a
high-alias-count city), which we flag as a limitation of the ablation itself,
not of the underlying test. (3) *Quantified OOD separability* (reviewer
weakness 4 / question 3): we compute AUROC for OOD-vs-in-distribution using
both semantic entropy and unmapped rate as scores, on the same evidentiary
footing as the ambiguity-detection AUROCs.

## 4. Results

Across all 8 total training runs (5 main-design seeds + 3 ablation seeds),
final loss plateaus at 0.83–1.02, consistent with the loss floor imposed by
genuine label noise (2 aliases × up to 3 valid cities). Code:
`experiment_v2.py`; raw results: `results_v2.json` (all in this directory,
seeds fixed, exactly reproducible); original single-seed artifacts
(`experiment.py`, `results.json`) retained for reference.

**(a) Ambiguity discrimination, 5 seeds, T=1.0, K=40.**

| Metric | AUROC (mean ± std) | Seeds where lexical ≥ semantic |
|---|---|---|
| Lexical entropy | **1.000 ± 0.000** | — |
| Oracle semantic entropy | 0.987 ± 0.011 | 5/5 |

The gap is small (≤0.03 AUROC) but directionally consistent across every
seed, not a single-draw artifact — addressing the reviewer's central
statistical concern.

**(b) Class-correlated alias ablation, 3 seeds.**

| Metric | AUROC (mean ± std) | Seeds where lexical ≥ semantic |
|---|---|---|
| Lexical entropy | 0.999 ± 0.001 | — |
| Oracle semantic entropy | 0.970 ± 0.017 | 3/3 |

Making alias diversity *positively* correlated with ambiguity class (the
opposite of the original independent design) narrows the semantic-entropy
disadvantage only marginally (0.987→0.970) and does not flip the ranking.
This is direct evidence against the concern that our null result is a
tautological consequence of alias count being fixed at exactly 2 — even when
we inject the class-correlation the original design lacked, semantic entropy
does not overtake lexical entropy in this setup (though the imperfect city-set
overlap noted in §3 means this ablation is suggestive, not definitive).

**(c) Length confound (deterministic questions only), 5 seeds.**

| Metric | Pearson r (mean ± std across seeds) |
|---|---|
| Lexical entropy | 0.29 ± 0.17 |
| Semantic entropy | −0.09 ± 0.21 |

This is a **revision of our original claim**. The single seed reported in our
first submission (r=−0.009 lexical, −0.058 semantic) understated the true
variability: with only 25 deterministic questions, per-seed Pearson r ranges
from ≈0.05 to ≈0.60 for lexical entropy across our 5 seeds. We no longer claim
a clean absence of length confound in this design; we instead report that the
confound's sign and magnitude are unstable at this sample size, which is
itself informative — it means single-seed length-correlation numbers (ours
originally, and potentially some in the literature) should not be trusted
without a variance estimate, echoing the reviewer's underlying concern.

**(d) Temperature ablation (single seed, as originally reported).** Lexical
entropy's AUROC edge over semantic entropy held at T∈{0.6, 1.0, 1.5}
(0.966/1.000/0.992 vs. 0.948/0.967/0.968); we did not have budget to re-run
this axis across 5 seeds as well as (a)–(c), so we present it with the
original caveat that it is a single-seed result.

**(e) Out-of-distribution detection, quantified (5 seeds).**

| Score | AUROC, OOD vs. in-distribution (mean ± std) |
|---|---|
| Semantic entropy | 0.888 ± 0.074 |
| Unmapped ("novel token") rate | 0.584 ± 0.156 |

This directly answers the reviewer's request for a quantitative OOD metric
analogous to the ambiguity-detection AUROCs. Semantic entropy is a good but
imperfect OOD detector — well above chance, but far from the ≈1.0 ceiling
reached on ambiguity detection — and the unmapped-rate proxy, while directionally
useful (mean entropy and unmapped rate both rise on OOD questions, as
originally reported), is considerably noisier (std 0.156, occasionally near
chance) and should not be treated as a drop-in replacement for full entropy
when quantitative reliability matters.

## 5. Discussion

Multi-seed replication strengthens the headline claim (lexical ≥ semantic
ambiguity detection) while weakening a secondary one (clean absence of length
confound). Both revisions matter for the same reason: with 25–65 synthetic
items per class, single-seed statistics in this kind of study carry real
sampling noise, and reporting only one draw — as our original submission
did — risks over- or under-stating an effect. The ambiguity-detection ranking
turned out to be robust to this noise; the length-correlation near-zero claim
did not.

The class-correlated alias ablation (§4b) was designed as a direct stress test
of our own "additive offset" explanation from the original submission: if
alias diversity were the only thing standing between lexical entropy and a
class-correlated confound, deliberately correlating alias count with ambiguity
class should have let semantic entropy close or reverse the gap. It narrowed
the gap (0.987→0.970) but did not reverse it, which is modest evidence that
the offset story is not purely an artifact of "exactly 2 aliases everywhere,"
though the imperfect city/class separation in this ablation (§3) means we
would want a cleaner variant — cities exclusively owned by one class — before
treating this as conclusive.

We still do not compare against a real NLI-based semantic clustering
implementation, even within this synthetic world; our "semantic entropy" is
an oracle upper bound, so a real implementation could only do worse here, not
better. This paper's scope is therefore narrower than "semantic entropy vs.
lexical entropy in practice" — it is "the best-case ceiling for semantic
clustering vs. lexical entropy, under exact ground truth." We flag this
explicitly rather than let the title imply a broader claim than the evidence
supports.

**Limitations.** 5 seeds for the main comparison and OOD metric, 3 for the
alias ablation, 1 for the temperature ablation (compute budget, not principle,
was the constraint — each run is CPU-only and takes 20–45s, so more seeds are
straightforward future work); a 0.4M-parameter model trained for 1200 steps
is not representative of frontier LLM scale; the alias mechanism remains a
simplified stand-in for real paraphrase diversity; K=40 samples bounds
entropy-estimate precision; no real NLI-clustering baseline is tested even in
this synthetic world.

## 6. Conclusion

Across 5 independent seeds, lexical (surface-form) sampling entropy matched or
slightly outperformed oracle semantic-clustering entropy at detecting genuine
ambiguity in a fully controlled synthetic QA world (AUROC 1.000±0.000 vs.
0.987±0.011), and this ranking survived a targeted ablation designed to break
it by making alias diversity class-correlated (0.999±0.001 vs. 0.970±0.017).
A length-entropy confound claim from our original single-seed submission did
not survive multi-seed replication — the correlation is noisy rather than
reliably absent — and we report the corrected, weaker version rather than the
cleaner original number. Entropy-based signals newly quantified here do
detect out-of-distribution questions well above chance (AUROC 0.888±0.074),
though a cheaper novelty-rate proxy is markedly noisier (0.584±0.156). We see
this as a small, honest, now variance-aware data point supporting more
controlled probing of *why* semantic clustering helps (or doesn't) in specific
regimes, rather than treating either "semantic > lexical" or "no length
confound" as mechanism-free givens. Code, logs, and raw results (both original
and revised) are provided for verification and extension — e.g., a real
NLI-clustering baseline, or a cleaner class-exclusive alias ablation.
