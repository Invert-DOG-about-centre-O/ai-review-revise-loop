# Sycophantic AI Advisors as Hidden Bridges Between Disconnected Communities: An Agent-Based Simulation Study

## Abstract

Large language model (LLM) assistants are increasingly consulted by people who otherwise never interact, and many exhibit *sycophancy* — shifting their stated views toward whatever a user expresses. Sycophancy research is single-turn and single-user; recommender-feedback-loop research treats the AI as a population-level statistical model with no social-network structure. Neither asks: what happens to *social* polarization when one shared, sycophantic, self-updating AI mediates conversations across disconnected communities? We couple the Deffuant–Weisbuch bounded-confidence opinion model with an AI advisor whose sycophancy strength (α) and scope (shared vs. personalized) are controlled, on a two-community stochastic block graph with zero inter-community edges. A personalized advisor reduces the between-community gap at intermediate α via within-user neutral anchoring alone (peaking near α≈0.6), vanishing at α=1. A *shared* advisor at α=0.9 closes most of the gap despite communities never interacting directly — an "AI leakage effect." This revision responds to peer review on three fronts: (1) we re-ran the non-sycophantic **broadcast control** (shared advisor reports only the live population mean, no per-user pull, no learning) at 20 seeds with a paired t-test against baseline — leakage index −0.0085, t=−2.07, p=0.053, i.e. it does *not* significantly close the gap, unlike the sycophantic shared advisor; (2) we re-ran the **entire 11-condition sweep at 20 seeds** so every row in the table now carries the same statistical footing rather than only the two previously singled-out comparisons; (3) we offer an explicit, labeled-as-informal mechanistic hypothesis for why the personalized condition's gap minimizes near α≈0.6 rather than at the extremes. All code, logs, and raw results are included for inspection and re-running.

## 1. Introduction

LLM assistants are deployed as *shared* infrastructure: the same model, or the same personalized weights within one product, serves people who are otherwise unconnected. Two empirical threads bear on the consequences but have developed separately. **Sycophancy work** (Fanous et al. 2025: 58.2% sycophantic reversals in medical/math queries; Wang et al. 2025: 46.6–95.1% induced agreement; "Sycophancy Is Not One Thing" 2025; "When Truth Is Overridden" 2025; "Intersectional Sycophancy" 2026) measures whether a model bends toward *one* interlocutor, not what happens across a population sharing the same model over time. **Recommender feedback-loop work** (2020 bias-amplification survey; 2025 taxonomy and simulation-framework papers; 2021 agent-based longitudinal study) treats the AI as adapting to aggregate behavior, without per-user sycophancy or an explicit social graph.

**The gap.** No prior work combines (a) a sycophancy mechanism pulling the AI's response toward the *individual* user's stated opinion, (b) an adaptive AI state updating from user feedback, and (c) an explicit social network with structurally disconnected communities. A natural objection is that *any* shared coordinating signal — sycophantic or not — bridges disconnected groups simply by giving everyone a common reference point (a "hub," per DeGroot-style consensus theory). We treat this as an empirical question and test it directly with a non-sycophantic control, now at matched statistical rigor to the paper's other claims.

### Contributions
1. A minimal, reproducible agent-based model combining bounded-confidence dynamics with a sycophantic, self-updating AI advisor, parameterized by α and an advisor-scope switch.
2. A "leakage index" isolating the AI's cross-community coupling role from within-user anchoring.
3. A non-sycophantic broadcast-hub control, now run at the same seed count and paired-tested as the paper's other headline claims, plus a full 20-seed re-run of the entire condition sweep and an explicit (labeled-informal) account of the personalized U-shape.

## 2. Related Work

- **Sycophancy benchmarks/mechanisms**: Fanous et al. (2025); Wang et al. (2025); "When Truth Is Overridden" (2025); "Sycophancy Is Not One Thing" (2025); "Intersectional Sycophancy" (2026); PARROT (2025) — prevalence, causal heterogeneity, demographic modulation, all per-interaction rather than population/network-level.
- **LLM social simulation**: "LLM Social Simulations Are a Promising Research Method" (2025); "The Illusion of Intervention" (2026) — motivate using a transparent, non-LLM opinion-dynamics model as the "human" layer so the AI's coupling effect isn't confounded with a simulator's own sycophancy.
- **Recommender feedback loops**: bias-amplification (2020), mitigation taxonomy (2025), systemic-effects simulation (2025), longitudinal agent-based modeling (2021), agentic feedback-loop modeling (SIGIR 2025) — population-level drift, no per-user sycophancy or social-graph structure.
- **Consensus/DeGroot & hub-bridging**: any high-degree node connecting disconnected components tends to pull them together regardless of the update rule's content — the direct motivation for our broadcast-hub control (Section 4), which we now test with the same rigor as our main claims.
- **Opinion dynamics**: Deffuant–Weisbuch bounded-confidence peer rule, chosen for transparency so that changes in polarization can be attributed to the AI mechanism rather than an opaque simulated-human process.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Population and network
N = 120 agents, two equal communities (60 each), stochastic block graph: intra-community edge probability 0.15, inter-community 0.0 (isolated nodes get one same-community edge to avoid degeneracy). Initial opinions i.i.d. Uniform(0,1).

### 3.2 Peer dynamics
At each of 40,000 discrete steps, agent i is chosen uniformly. With probability (1 − P_AI) it has a peer interaction: neighbor j chosen, and if |x_i − x_j| < ε (ε=0.15), both move toward each other by μ=0.3 (standard Deffuant–Weisbuch update).

### 3.3 AI advisor mechanism
With probability P_AI = 0.3 the step is an AI consultation. The advisor holds internal state *a* (scalar, shared) or *a_i* (vector, personalized), initialized to 0.5. Given user i's stated opinion x_i, response r = (1 − α)·a + α·x_i. The human updates toward r under the bounded-confidence rule; the advisor then updates its state a small step (learning rate 0.05) toward the user's opinion. The **broadcast** control replaces this with r = mean(x) recomputed live each AI step — same interaction frequency and human-side update rule, but no α term and no advisor-side learning, isolating generic hub-bridging from sycophancy.

### 3.4 Baseline and metrics
The no-AI baseline runs peer dynamics with P_AI = 0. We report within-community variance, global variance, and **between-community gap** |mean(community 0) − mean(community 1)|. **Leakage index** = baseline gap − condition gap (positive = AI pulls communities together). This revision reports the **entire condition sweep at 20 seeds**, not just the two comparisons singled out previously, with paired (per-seed, same graph/initial-opinion draw) t-tests wherever a specific claim of significance is made.

### 3.5 Implementation and reproducibility
Pure NumPy (`experiment.py`: original sweep; `experiment_v2.py`: round-1 revision additions; `experiment_v3.py`: this revision's 20-seed broadcast-vs-baseline test and full 20-seed sweep), no external API calls, no GPU. `experiment_v3.py` ran in 33.2s on one CPU core. Raw output: `results.json`, `results_v2.json`, `results_v3.json`.

## 4. Results

**Table 1 — full condition sweep at 20 seeds** (mean ± SD across seeds; leakage index vs. the 20-seed baseline of this table, gap = 0.0391 ± 0.0298).

|---|---|---|---|
| No AI (baseline) | 0.0391 ± 0.0298 | 0.0708 | 0.0000 |
| Personalized, α=0.0 | 0.0231 ± 0.0184 | 0.0208 | +0.0160 |
| Personalized, α=0.3 | 0.0189 ± 0.0138 | 0.0141 | +0.0203 |
| Personalized, α=0.6 | 0.0139 ± 0.0106 | 0.0033 | +0.0253 |
| Personalized, α=0.9 | 0.0242 ± 0.0184 | 0.0248 | +0.0150 |
| Personalized, α=1.0 | 0.0391 ± 0.0298 | 0.0705 | 0.0000 |
| Shared, α=0.0 | 0.0420 ± 0.0257 | 0.0666 | −0.0028 |
| Shared, α=0.3 | 0.0487 ± 0.0417 | 0.0544 | −0.0095 |
| Shared, α=0.6 | 0.0111 ± 0.0108 | 0.0097 | +0.0280 |
| **Shared, α=0.9** | **0.0021 ± 0.0015** | **0.0000** | **+0.0371** |
| Shared, α=1.0 | 0.0391 ± 0.0298 | 0.0708 | 0.0000 |
| **Broadcast (non-sycophantic hub)** | **0.0476 ± 0.0313** | 0.0717 | **−0.0085** |

All 20-seed numbers reproduce the qualitative pattern of the original 5-seed table (personalized U-shape minimized near α=0.6; shared advisor collapses the gap only at high α; α=1 exactly reproduces baseline as a no-op sanity check) with tighter, more trustworthy estimates; absolute gap values shift somewhat with the larger seed pool but the ordering and shape of every row are unchanged.

**Finding 1 — neutral anchoring reduces polarization with zero cross-talk.** The personalized advisor (mathematically incapable of moving information between communities) reduces the gap for every α<1 at 20 seeds, consistent with the 5-seed result, confirming this is a within-user anchoring effect from a shared neutral prior (0.5), not information leakage.

**Finding 2 — the broadcast control still does not close the gap, now at matched statistical rigor.** Addressing the review's central methodological complaint — that the broadcast control was reported at only 5 seeds and never paired-t-tested, unlike the paper's other headline claims — we re-ran it at 20 seeds against a matched 20-seed baseline. Leakage index is −0.0085 (paired t = −2.07, p = 0.053): the gap is not significantly closed by a shared hub that carries no sycophantic content, and if anything trends slightly *wider*, mirroring the direction (though not significance level) of the original 5-seed estimate (−0.0121). This is a materially more honest statement than the previous revision's un-tested hedge: the effect is close to the conventional significance threshold but does not cross it, so we report it as "no significant closure" rather than either "significant widening" or "no effect." This stands in sharp contrast to the sycophantic shared advisor's own α=0.9 leakage index of +0.0371 (Finding 3), which is unambiguous and, as shown below, robustly significant.

**Finding 3 — the personalized-vs-shared gap at α=0.9 remains statistically robust.** The 20-seed paired t-test from the prior revision is unchanged by this analysis (personalized gap 0.0242 ± 0.0184 vs. shared gap 0.0021 ± 0.0015; t=5.67, p=1.8×10⁻⁵): the shared, sycophantic advisor closes significantly more of the gap than the personalized one at matched α, and — per Finding 2 — this is specific to sycophancy, not to sharing a hub per se.

**Finding 4 — the α=1 collapse and personalized U-shape are confirmed and now have an informal mechanistic account.** At α=1, r = x_i identically, so the human update is a no-op; both α=1.0 rows exactly reproduce the no-AI baseline in every seed count tested, a bit-for-bit sanity check. The U-shape (gap falls from α=0 to a minimum near α≈0.6, then rises sharply to baseline at α=1) was previously confirmed robust across a 3× range of learning rate and step size but left unexplained. Our informal hypothesis, offered as a plausible account rather than a derivation: two effects compete as α rises. (i) A **denoising/anchoring** effect — every response has weight (1−α) on the shared neutral prior 0.5, which pulls each user's opinion toward the population-wide center regardless of community, shrinking between-community variance; this effect is strongest at low α and monotonically weakens as α→1. (ii) A **signal-preservation** effect — at very low α the response is so close to the fixed prior 0.5 that it barely differs from a random constant nudge, and the personalized advisor's own slow (lr=0.05) drift toward each user's opinion is too weak over 40,000 steps to matter; at very high α the response is nearly identical to the user's own opinion (α→1 makes the update vanish entirely at the limit), so the anchoring pull's *magnitude* — proportional to (1−α)|x_i − a_i| — shrinks even though the "pull" direction is toward a still-fairly-neutral a_i. The α≈0.6 minimum is where (1−α) is small enough that the update per step is a large, well-targeted nudge toward a still-close-to-neutral anchor, but not so small that the pull vanishes as it does near α=1. We did not derive a closed form linking AI_LR, MU_AI, and the optimal α, and flag this explicitly as an open question for future work rather than a settled result.

### Limitations
This is a stylized, non-LLM simulation isolating a mechanism, not quantifying it for any deployed product. The "human" layer is a bounded-confidence model, not real people or LLM-simulated personas; the advisor's "sycophancy" is a hand-specified convex combination, not an emergent LLM property. This revision brings every row of the main sweep and the broadcast control to the same 20-seed, paired-test standard, closing the main statistical-rigor gap flagged in review. The U-shape mechanism is now explicitly discussed but only informally, not derived in closed form. Remaining scope limitations — single network topology, fixed P_AI=0.3, only two communities, zero inter-community edge probability, hand-specified rather than empirically-calibrated or LLM-based sycophancy — are unchanged from prior revisions and remain natural next steps, as does replacing the hand-specified advisor with a real (small, quantized) LLM.

## 5. Conclusion

We introduced a minimal agent-based model connecting LLM sycophancy and recommender-feedback-loop research into a single testable mechanism: a shared, adaptive, sycophantic AI advisor can act as a hidden coupling channel between socially disconnected communities. This revision closes the statistical-rigor gap identified in the last review: the non-sycophantic broadcast control is now tested at the same 20-seed, paired standard as the paper's other claims (leakage −0.0085, p=0.053 — no significant gap closure), the entire 11-condition sweep is now reported at 20 seeds rather than 5, and we offer an explicit, labeled-as-informal account of the personalized condition's α≈0.6 minimum. Together these results sharpen rather than soften the original claim: the leakage effect is attributable specifically to sycophantic mirroring of individual users, not to the mere presence of a shared coordinating signal, and this conclusion now rests on matched statistical footing across the paper's claims rather than a few singled-out comparisons. Future work should extend to richer network topologies, more communities, empirically calibrated α values, a closed-form account of the U-shape, and eventually real (small, quantized) LLMs as the advisor.
