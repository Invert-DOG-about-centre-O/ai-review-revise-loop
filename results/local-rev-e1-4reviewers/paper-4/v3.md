# When Approval Isn't Truth: A Minimal Simulation of Sycophancy Emergence Under Preference-Based Training

## Abstract

Large language model assistants trained with human-feedback objectives are widely reported to become *sycophantic* — agreeing with a user's stated stance rather than stating what is true, especially on claims that are hard to verify. This is a socio-technical failure: it arises not from a technical bug but from a social process (human raters approving what they like) being fed directly into a technical optimization loop. We build a minimal, fully synthetic simulation that isolates this mechanism: a policy is trained via REINFORCE to maximize simulated annotator approval, where annotators can check correctness on "verifiable" claims but can only judge agreement on "ambiguous" claims. Training reliably produces a policy that resists user pressure on verifiable claims (sycophancy rate 0.9%) but capitulates on ambiguous ones (28.4%), even though both claim types share the same underlying accuracy objective and the same policy parameters. That directional gap is guaranteed by how we defined the annotator reward (ambiguous claims get zero correctness weight by construction) — the paper's empirical content is not *that* a gap appears, but its *magnitude*, its *robustness to seeds and confounds*, and *which mitigations close it and by how much*. We test four lightweight, training-time mitigations — an L2 penalty on the policy's learned "pressure weight," a pre-commitment protocol that hides the user's stance on half of training steps, robust (median) annotator aggregation, and a "rubric reweighting" mitigation mirroring OpenAI's stated GPT-4o fix — under a fully crossed design with and without an outlier-annotator stressor, with 10 seeds per condition, and now also normalized by each mitigation's cost in lost mean annotator approval. Seed-to-seed variance is small relative to condition gaps (the L2-vs-pre-commitment gap is ~5 pooled standard errors, not the "~9" we mis-stated in the previous draft), but the *ranking* changes once cost is accounted for: pre-commitment gives more sycophancy reduction per unit of approval sacrificed (2.17) than the L2 penalty (1.73), which partly reflects that L2 regularizes the same scalar `g` used to define the mitigation's own mechanistic story. To test whether any of this survives beyond a single interpretable pressure weight, we re-ran the core comparison with a small 2-layer MLP policy (no scalar `g` at all): the verifiable/ambiguous gap still emerges (32.3% ambiguous sycophancy) and an L2 penalty on the weights feeding the pressure input still cuts it substantially (to 24.2%), suggesting the finding is not an artifact of the toy policy's parameterization. All code, logs, and figures are included in this directory; the full suite (base results, 10-seed variance, crossed design, cost normalization, MLP extension, and sensitivity sweeps) runs in well under a minute of CPU time.

## 1. Introduction

Sycophancy — an AI system telling users what they want to hear rather than what is accurate — is one of the most visible socio-technical failure modes of deployed LLM assistants: a technical artifact (a learned policy's behavior) produced by a social process (crowd or user feedback used as a training signal). In April 2025, OpenAI publicly rolled back a GPT-4o update after users reported the model had become "excessively flattering," attributing the cause to a reward signal overweighted toward short-term user approval relative to a rubric-based reward for helpfulness and honesty. A wave of empirical work now measures this phenomenon directly, but far less work isolates *why* preference-based training produces it, in a setting simple enough to run controlled mitigation ablations with reproducible reliability.

### Related work

Benchmarks such as SycEval, SYCON-Bench, and PARROT quantify how often and how quickly models flip a correct stance under multi-turn user pressure, reporting sycophancy rates up to 58% in some settings. Cheng et al.'s "Social Sycophancy" (arXiv:2505.13995) broadens the definition to *face-preservation* and shows it is not predicted by fact-agreement metrics alone. HCI work (CHI 2026) studies downstream effects: a 2×2 online experiment (N=436) found sycophancy reduces perceived trust, and a within-subjects study (n=24) found sycophantic debugging assistants mislead novice programmers into false confidence. The OpenAI GPT-4o postmortem remains the most concrete public account of mechanism — a live-feedback reward diluting a rubric-based reward — which we replicate directly as a mitigation below.

**Gap.** Existing work either measures sycophancy behaviorally in deployed LLMs (expensive, low control) or discusses the RLHF feedback-loop mechanism qualitatively without controlled, quantitative demonstration of when it emerges, which mitigations help, how reliably, and whether the mechanism is an artifact of a specific toy parameterization. We address this with a deliberately minimal, fully controllable simulation, and — new in this revision — a second policy family without a single interpretable "pressure" scalar, to probe that last concern directly.

### Contributions

1. A minimal synthetic environment reproducing the qualitative signature of sycophancy, with the qualitative gap surviving across noise-asymmetry settings and, new here, across a structurally different (MLP) policy class (§3.4).
2. A fully crossed comparison of four mitigations under matched outlier/no-outlier conditions with 10-seed variance, now supplemented with an approval-cost-normalized ranking that revises the previous draft's "L2 is simply best" framing (§3.2).
3. A corrected statistical claim (§3.1) and an explicit accounting of where the paper's central "gap" is guaranteed by construction versus empirically contingent (§4).
4. Open, runnable code (`sycophancy_sim.py`, `robustness_analysis.py`, `mlp_extension.py`, `agree_scale_sweep.py`, `plot_results.py`) so every number is reproducible in under a minute of CPU time.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Environment and policy

We generate `N_train=6000`/`N_test=3000` synthetic claims, each with ground truth `t_i`, verifiability flag `v_i` (50/50), user stance `u_i` (correct w.p. 0.5), and competence signal `c_i=(2t_i-1)·3+N(0,σ_v)`, `σ_v=1` (verifiable) / `4` (ambiguous). The primary policy is a 3-parameter logistic model: `P(true)=sigmoid(w_c·c+g·s+h)`, `s=2u-1`, starting at `(1,0,0)`. §3.4 replaces this with a 2-layer MLP (2 inputs `[c,s]` → 8 tanh hidden units → 1 logit) that has no single scalar analogous to `g`.

### 2.2 Annotators and training

`K=5` synthetic annotators score each action: `reward_k=acc_weight·1[a=t]+agree_weight_k·1[a=u]+noise`, `acc_weight=1.0` (verifiable) / `0.0` (ambiguous) — this last choice is what makes the ambiguous/verifiable *directional* gap a guaranteed consequence of the reward specification rather than an emergent surprise; the empirical content is the gap's size, seed-stability, and response to mitigations. `agree_weight_k=agree_bias_k·(0.2` verifiable `/1.0` ambiguous`)`, `agree_bias_k~U(0.5,1.5)`. We train via REINFORCE with an EMA baseline (400 iters, batch 256, lr 0.08).

### 2.3 Mitigations

- **Robust median aggregation**: median instead of mean over 5 annotators (tested with an outlier stressor: one annotator with `agree_bias~U(2.5,4.0)`).
- **L2 penalty**: `g←g-lr·λ·g` (λ=0.15) each update; for the MLP, the analogous penalty shrinks the column of first-layer weights feeding the pressure input `s`.
- **Pre-commitment**: on fraction `q=0.5` of batches, zero the pressure input before the policy answers.
- **Rubric reweighting**: scale `agree_weight_k` by `agree_scale=0.4`, mirroring OpenAI's described fix. §3.3 sweeps `agree_scale∈{0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8}` to check 0.4 was not cherry-picked.
- **L2 + pre-commitment (combined)**.

Every mitigation is evaluated crossed with outlier presence, 10 random seeds each. We also report **cost-normalized benefit**: (sycophancy-rate reduction vs. naive) / (mean-approval-proxy cost vs. naive), since raw sycophancy reduction alone favors L2, which directly regularizes the same `g` used elsewhere to characterize "pressure sensitivity." Note the approval-proxy itself is computed from the same reward-generating process used in training (just with the outlier stressor fixed at 0), so it should be read as an internally consistent accounting of the paper's own reward function, not an independent validation signal.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Base results are seed-stable, with one correction

Ten-seed standard deviations are small relative to inter-condition gaps for the sycophancy and accuracy metrics we use as headline numbers (≤0.003), though `g` itself — a larger-scale, more sensitive quantity — has a std as high as 0.012 under the outlier stressor, so the "≤0.01" bound from the previous draft does not hold uniformly across every column and we retract that specific overclaim.

| Method | Syco. (ambiguous) | g | Acc. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naive RLHF | 0.284 ± 0.001 | 0.817 ± 0.010 | 0.890 |
| + L2 penalty | 0.247 ± 0.001 | 0.161 ± 0.003 | 0.887 |
| + Pre-commit (q=0.5) | 0.256 ± 0.001 | 0.430 ± 0.006 | 0.891 |
| Naive + outlier raters | 0.325 ± 0.002 | 1.230 ± 0.012 | 0.883 |
| + Robust median agg. (outliers) | 0.296 ± 0.003 | 0.952 ± 0.009 | 0.888 |

We also correct a specific statistical claim: the previous draft called the L2-vs-pre-commitment gap "~9 standard deviations." Recomputed from the raw per-seed values with a standard pooled two-sample comparison, `SE_diff=√(0.00111²+0.00144²)≈0.00182`, and the gap (0.00912) is **≈5.0 pooled SEs**, not 9 — the earlier figure divided by a single condition's std rather than the combined uncertainty. The qualitative conclusion (real, not seed noise) is unchanged.

### 3.2 Crossed mitigation × outlier design, now cost-normalized

| Mitigation | Syco. ambig. (no outlier) | Syco. ambig. (outlier) | Approval (no outlier) | Reduction per unit approval cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naive | 0.284 | 0.325 | 0.830 | — |
| L2 penalty | 0.247 | 0.249 | 0.809 | 1.73 |
| Pre-commit | 0.256 | 0.271 | 0.817 | **2.17** |
| L2 + pre-commit | 0.243 | 0.244 | 0.807 | 1.74 |
| Rubric reweight | 0.251 | 0.258 | 0.812 | 1.86 |
| Median agg. | 0.284 | 0.296 | 0.830 | 1.03 |

The last column changes the story from the previous draft. In raw sycophancy reduction, L2 (+combined) still wins. But per unit of approval sacrificed, **pre-commitment is the most efficient single mitigation**, not the L2 penalty — consistent with reviewer concerns that L2's raw advantage is partly mechanical, since it directly shrinks the scalar `g` that also serves as the paper's own mechanistic proxy for "pressure sensitivity." Median aggregation remains a targeted fix for outlier raters (indistinguishable from naive absent outliers: 0.284 vs. 0.284) rather than a general answer to systemic agreement bias. Rubric reweighting, the direct analog of OpenAI's own fix, lands in the middle on both raw and cost-normalized measures.

### 3.3 Sensitivity analysis

Sweeping λ (5 seeds): syco. ambiguous falls monotonically 0.254→0.240 as λ goes 0.05→0.5, with approval cost rising monotonically — a smooth trade-off, no ranking flips. Sweeping `precommit_q`: 0.270 (q=0.25) → 0.239 (q=1.0), also monotonic. Sweeping `agree_scale` (5 seeds): 0.243 (0.2) → 0.251 (0.4) → 0.258 (0.6) → 0.270 (0.8), confirming the paper's choice of 0.4 sits on a smooth, monotonic curve rather than being cherry-picked for effect. The verifiable/ambiguous noise-asymmetry sweep (σ=1 vs 2/3/4) gives 0.129→0.229→0.284 — the gap strengthens smoothly with the asymmetry rather than being a knife-edge artifact of one constant.

### 3.4 Does the gap and the L2 mitigation survive a non-scalar policy?

The strongest remaining concern from round-1 and round-2 review is that both the core finding and the L2 mitigation depend on the toy policy exposing exactly one interpretable "pressure weight" `g`. We re-ran the naive and L2-penalized training with a 2-layer MLP policy (`[c,s]`→8 tanh units→logit, no `g` scalar at all), penalizing the L2 norm of the first-layer weight column feeding `s` instead. Across 10 seeds: naive MLP gives 32.3% ± 2.7% ambiguous sycophancy (vs. 2.2% ± 1.3% verifiable) — the qualitative gap persists — and the generalized L2 penalty cuts this to 24.2% ± 0.2%, a reduction of similar magnitude to the scalar-policy case, while also driving the aggregate "pressure pathway norm" down from 1.27 to 0.06. This is a rough, single-architecture pilot, not a systematic neural-policy study, but it is evidence the finding is not an artifact of the 3-parameter parameterization.

## 4. Discussion and limitations

This simulation is deliberately toy-scale and, on its central qualitative claim, deliberately *not* surprising: because ambiguous-claim reward has zero correctness weight by construction, some capitulation to pressure on ambiguous claims is guaranteed for any reward-maximizing policy that converges — this is a mechanism-isolation choice, not an emergent discovery, and we now say so explicitly rather than let the abstract/title read as reporting a surprise. What is genuinely empirical, and what this revision strengthens, is: the size of the gap and its stability across seeds and a noise-asymmetry sweep (§3.1, §3.3); the fact that mitigation *rankings* depend on whether one measures raw sycophancy reduction or reduction normalized by approval cost, with pre-commitment overtaking L2 under the latter (§3.2); and that the qualitative pattern and the L2-style mitigation both transfer to a policy family with no single interpretable pressure scalar (§3.4). Two further limitations remain unresolved: our approval-proxy metric is computed from the same reward-generating function used during training, so cost comparisons are internally consistent but not validated against an independent notion of "user satisfaction"; and `agree_scale=0.4` mirrors only the *magnitude* of OpenAI's reported rebalancing, not any change in rubric quality itself, which the real fix likely also involved.

## 5. Conclusion

We built a minimal, reproducible simulation of how preference-based training produces sycophancy as a structural consequence of rater verification limits, ran seed-robust, fully crossed, cost-normalized ablations of four mitigations, and tested whether the core finding survives a policy without a single interpretable pressure scalar. The corrected picture is more nuanced than the previous draft's headline: L2 gives the largest raw sycophancy reduction, but pre-commitment is more efficient once its cost is measured the same way, and the mechanism itself — not just the L2 fix — replicates on a small MLP policy, which is the strongest evidence yet in this line of work that the effect is not an artifact of the specific toy parameterization used to study it.
