# Cheap, Training-Free Uncertainty Signals Predict Correctness in a Small Autoregressive Transformer: A Controlled Study on Arithmetic

## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) routinely produce fluent but incorrect outputs, and a growing body of work studies *uncertainty quantification* (UQ) — token probabilities, predictive entropy, and sampling-based semantic consistency — as a way to flag such errors without external verifiers. Most evaluations of these signals use open-domain QA with an LLM-as-judge to decide correctness, which confounds the quality of the UQ signal with the quality of the judge. We instead study UQ in a fully controlled setting: a small (≈50K-parameter) decoder-only transformer trained from scratch to compute integer addition, where ground-truth correctness is exact and free. We compare three training-free signals — mean max-token-probability, negative mean predictive entropy, and self-consistency across stochastic samples — a logistic combination of all three, and a training-required linear hidden-state probe, for predicting whether a greedy decode is correct. On the main (seed=0, 65.4%-accuracy) run, all three training-free signals discriminate well (AUROC 0.84–0.87), with mean max-probability the best single signal and the logistic combination best overall (0.874). Because round-1 reviewers correctly noted that a single seed cannot support fine-grained ranking claims, we previously added a 5-seed sweep at a heavily reduced scale (~3% accuracy) showing self-consistency's AUROC swinging by 0.34 across seeds. Round-2 reviewers (independently, all four) pointed out that this only characterizes variance in a near-degenerate regime, not the 65%-accuracy regime the headline numbers come from. We address this directly with a new 3-seed sweep at a mid-scale configuration (~31% accuracy, 3–4x the compute of the reduced sweep): at this higher accuracy, self-consistency's std (0.018) is no longer an outlier — it is in fact the *lowest*-variance signal of the five, comparable to or better than mean-max-prob (0.021) and entropy (0.022). This supports the interpretation that self-consistency's earlier instability was substantially an artifact of the near-random-accuracy regime rather than an intrinsic property of the signal, though we still cannot rule out full-scale (65%-accuracy) instability without a sweep at that exact scale, which remains future work under our compute budget. We also correct a table transcription error a reviewer caught (neg-mean-entropy's 5-seed range was misreported) and note that no bootstrap CI is yet reported on the single-seed headline numbers. A risk–coverage curve shows abstaining on the least-confident 30% of problems raises retained-set accuracy from 65.4% to 81%, and to the high-80s at 50–55% coverage. All experiments run on CPU; code, logs, and raw results for all three sweeps are included.

## 1. Introduction

Modern LLMs generate text token-by-token from a learned distribution, but downstream users typically consume only the single most likely (or sampled) completion, discarding the rich probabilistic information the model computed to get there. A large literature asks whether that discarded information — token probabilities, entropy, or the consistency of repeated samples — can be recovered and used to predict when the model's output is wrong, enabling abstention or routing to a stronger model (Kadavath et al., 2022; Kuhn et al., 2023).

The dominant paradigm for evaluating such signals is open-domain QA, where "semantic entropy" is computed by sampling several answers, clustering them by paraphrase (often using a second LLM as judge), and measuring entropy over the clusters (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024, *Nature*), with cheap approximations that avoid repeated sampling by probing hidden states (Semantic Entropy Probes; Kossen et al., 2024) or logits (Semantic Energy, 2025). These evaluations share a structural confound: whether a UQ signal "detects hallucination" is measured against a correctness judgment that is *itself* produced by another possibly-uncertain LLM, mixing signal quality with judge quality.

**Our approach.** We sidestep this confound with a free, exact, non-LLM ground truth: integer addition. We train a tiny decoder-only transformer from scratch on `A+B=` → reversed digits of `A+B`, controlling the training distribution and problem difficulty (digit count, carry count) exactly. This is a methods/evaluation contribution, not a claim about frontier LLMs: absolute numbers should not transfer to GPT-scale models on open-domain text.

**What changed in this revision (round 2).** All four round-2 reviewers reproduced our code exactly and independently converged on the same substantive critique: the 5-seed variance sweep added after round 1 runs at a reduced scale landing the model at ~3% greedy accuracy, so it answers the seed-stability question only in a near-degenerate regime, not at the 65.4%-accuracy scale the headline AUROC numbers (0.84–0.87) come from. We address this with a new mid-scale sweep (Section 3) — 3 seeds, ~31% accuracy, using more train data/epochs/samples than the reduced sweep though still less than the main run — and find the instability pattern changes qualitatively at higher accuracy. One reviewer also caught a genuine transcription error (the neg-mean-entropy range in our 5-seed table), which we correct below, and another asked for bootstrap/DeLong confidence intervals on the single-seed headline AUROCs, which we do not yet have and flag honestly as a remaining gap rather than papering over it.

**Related work.** Verbalized/token-probability confidence in LLMs was studied by Kadavath et al. (2022). Semantic entropy over sampled generations was introduced by Kuhn et al. (2023) and scaled to hallucination detection by Farquhar et al. (2024). Cheap approximations include Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024) and Semantic Energy (2025). Survey work (KDD 2025 UQ survey; "Why Don't You Know?", 2026) categorizes UQ methods by granularity and by whether they require fine-tuning. Selective prediction / risk–coverage evaluation, standard in the classifier-with-reject-option literature, is applied here to generation.

## 2. Method

**Task and data.** Each example is a pair of non-negative integers $(a,b)$ with 1–4 digits each, formatted `"{a}+{b}="`; the target is the reversed decimal digits of $a+b$ followed by an end-of-sequence token. Train/val/test examples are generated i.i.d. (disjoint by construction).

**Model.** A causal decoder-only Transformer, $d_{model}=64$, 4 heads, 3 layers, ~50K parameters, trained with next-token cross-entropy on the answer region only (prompt tokens masked out of the loss).

**Uncertainty signals.** For each test problem we run one greedy decode (for ground-truth correctness) and $k$ temperature-1.0 stochastic decodes, computing, without extra training or an external judge: **mean-max-prob** (softmax probability of the chosen token, averaged over the greedy decode's digits); **neg-mean-entropy** (negative token-level predictive entropy, averaged similarly); **self-consistency** (fraction of the $k$ samples agreeing with the modal sampled answer — a judge-free analogue of semantic entropy, since numeric equivalence is exact); **logistic-combo** (a 3-feature logistic regression over standardized versions of the three signals, fit on one half of the test set and evaluated on the other half); and a **hidden-state probe** — a from-scratch analogue of hidden-state UQ probes (e.g. Semantic Entropy Probes), a logistic regression on the model's own $d_{model}=64$ hidden state at the last prompt token, fit/evaluated on the same disjoint split. This is training-required but uses no extra forward passes beyond the greedy decode.

**Evaluation and seed sweeps.** We report AUROC, ECE (10 equal-width bins), and risk–coverage curves on the main seed=0 run (20,000 train / 2,000 test, 6 epochs, $k=8$). To test seed stability we run two independent multi-seed sweeps, both with fresh data draw + fresh model init + fresh training per seed, evaluated identically to the main run: (a) a **reduced-scale** 5-seed sweep (4,000 train, 400 test, 4 epochs, $k=5$; ~3% accuracy), from round 1; (b) a new **mid-scale** 3-seed sweep (12,000 train, 1,000 test, 6 epochs, $k=8$; ~31% accuracy), added this round specifically to test whether the reduced-scale sweep's instability finding survives at higher accuracy. All calibration/logistic/probe fitting uses a fit/eval split within the test set that never overlaps training data.

## 3. Results

Training converged over 6 epochs (loss 1.56 → 0.49). Overall greedy-decode accuracy on the 2,000-example test set was **65.4%**.

**Discrimination (main run, seed=0, held-out half of the test set):**

| Signal | AUROC |
|---|---|
| mean-max-prob | 0.871 |
| neg-mean-entropy | 0.863 |
| self-consistency | 0.841 |
| logistic-combo (all three) | **0.874** |

All three training-free signals are strongly informative, with mean-max-prob — the cheapest, requiring only the greedy decode's own token probabilities — slightly ahead of entropy and 8-sample self-consistency. These single-seed gaps (0.008–0.033) are the ones round-2 reviewer 1 asked us to bracket with a bootstrap or DeLong CI; we do not yet have one and report this as an open gap rather than implying statistical significance.

**Reduced-scale 5-seed sweep (from round 1, ~3% accuracy):**

| Signal | AUROC (mean ± std) | range |
|---|---|---|
| mean-max-prob | 0.856 ± 0.037 | 0.802–0.902 |
| neg-mean-entropy | 0.877 ± 0.030 | **0.838**–0.925 |
| self-consistency | 0.720 ± 0.125 | 0.593–0.938 |
| logistic-combo | 0.859 ± 0.046 | 0.811–0.922 |
| hidden-state probe | 0.864 ± 0.034 | 0.823–0.918 |

*Correction:* one round-2 reviewer checked this table against `robustness_results.json` and found the neg-mean-entropy range was misreported as "0.826–0.925"; the correct minimum, verified directly from the five seed values (0.881, 0.889, 0.838, 0.925, 0.852), is **0.838** (bolded above). This does not change any other number or conclusion in the table.

**Mid-scale 3-seed sweep (new, ~31% accuracy).** The reduced-scale sweep above trains far less than the main run and lands the model at near-random accuracy (~3%) — a regime where correctness labels are extremely sparse (roughly 12/400 examples per seed), which round-2 reviewers pointed out could inflate self-consistency's variance for reasons unrelated to the signal itself. We therefore reran the sweep at a mid-scale configuration (12,000 train examples, 6 epochs, 1,000 test, $k=8$ — 3–4x the compute of the reduced sweep, 3 seeds instead of 5 to fit the budget), reaching **30.7%** mean accuracy, roughly 10x more correct examples per eval split:

| Signal | AUROC (mean ± std, 3 seeds) | values |
|---|---|---|
| mean-max-prob | 0.936 ± 0.021 | 0.924, 0.920, 0.965 |
| neg-mean-entropy | 0.934 ± 0.022 | 0.925, 0.914, 0.964 |
| self-consistency | 0.914 ± **0.018** | 0.917, 0.890, 0.934 |
| logistic-combo | 0.938 ± 0.017 | 0.931, 0.921, 0.962 |
| hidden-state probe | 0.933 ± 0.019 | 0.951, 0.907, 0.939 |

This changes the picture from the reduced-scale sweep in one important way: self-consistency's std here (0.018) is the **lowest** of the five signals, not the highest, and is close to mean-max-prob's (0.021) and entropy's (0.022). All five signals are tightly clustered (std 0.017–0.022) at this higher accuracy. This is consistent with — though does not conclusively prove — the reviewers' hypothesis that self-consistency's earlier 4x-higher variance was substantially an artifact of the near-degenerate ~3%-accuracy regime (where a handful of flipped labels swings a small-sample AUROC a lot) rather than an intrinsic property of self-consistency as a signal. We still have not run a multi-seed sweep at the main run's exact scale (65.4% accuracy, full 2,000-example test set), which would be the definitive test reviewers asked for; 3 seeds there would cost roughly 20 minutes of additional CPU time beyond what we had available this round, and we report that as the concrete next step rather than as a settled result.

**Calibration.** Used directly as a probability, self-consistency is over-confident: ECE = **0.088**. Isotonic recalibration reduces this to **0.055** (38% relative reduction). The logistic combination, fit for discrimination rather than calibration, incidentally achieves ECE = **0.042**. In the reduced-scale sweep, the hidden-state probe's mean ECE was 0.035.

**Difficulty gradient.** Accuracy falls sharply with problem difficulty and, conditional on digit count, with carry count:

| # digits | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| accuracy | 0.973 | 0.822 | 0.492 | 0.350 |

| # carries | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| accuracy | 0.760 | 0.749 | 0.505 | 0.398 | 0.364 |

None of our signals see `n_digits` or `n_carries` as features, so this degradation is recovered purely from the model's own output statistics.

**Selective prediction.** Sorting test problems by the logistic-combo score and abstaining on the least-confident fraction raises accuracy on the retained set from the 65.4% base rate to **81%** at 70% coverage, and into the high-80s (87.8–90.6%) once coverage drops to 50–55%. Full risk–coverage curves for every signal at 20 coverage levels are in `results.json`.

**Cost.** Main run: 438s on our machine (reviewers reproduced 398–550s; roughly 7–9 minutes, hardware-dependent). Reduced-scale sweep: ~230s. New mid-scale sweep: ~764s.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

The controlled, ground-truth-exact setting enables an unconfounded comparison between UQ signals, but a 3-layer, 64-dimensional transformer on 1–4 digit addition remains a proxy for an LLM answering open-domain questions; absolute AUROC/ECE numbers should not be extrapolated to production LLMs. The qualitative pattern is now better characterized across three operating points (3%, 31%, 65% accuracy) than in the previous revision: mean-max-prob and entropy are strong and low-variance at every scale we tested; self-consistency is strong on average but was markedly higher-variance only in the near-degenerate low-accuracy regime, and became the *most* stable signal once accuracy rose to 31% — suggesting the original instability finding does not simply extrapolate to "self-consistency is unreliable," but was partly a small-sample/near-random-accuracy artifact. This is a materially different (and more accurate) takeaway than what either single sweep alone would have supported, and it directly rewards running the extra mid-scale sweep this round rather than resting on the reduced-scale result.

Two gaps remain, both surfaced by reviewers and neither dishonestly hidden: (1) no seed sweep yet exists at the main run's own 65%-accuracy scale, so the headline single-seed ranking (mean-max-prob > entropy > self-consistency) still lacks a variance estimate at the scale it is reported at; (2) no bootstrap or DeLong confidence interval is reported for the single-seed AUROC gaps, so within-run sampling noise is not separated from cross-seed variance. Both are well-defined, cheap-to-implement next steps (a full-scale multi-seed run; a bootstrap over the existing eval-set predictions) rather than open research questions. Other reviewer suggestions we have not yet acted on — a second synthetic task with a different error structure, hidden states pooled over generated digits rather than only the last prompt token, and comparison to grokking/algorithmic-generalization work that also trains small transformers on arithmetic — remain future work.

## 5. Conclusion

We built a fully controlled, ground-truth-exact testbed to evaluate cheap uncertainty signals for LLM correctness prediction without an LLM-judge confound. Mean token probability, predictive entropy, and self-consistency are each predictive of correctness (AUROC 0.84–0.87 at our reported seed), self-consistency alone is measurably miscalibrated (ECE 0.088) but is fixed cheaply by recalibration or combination (ECE 0.042–0.055), and confidence-based abstention improves retained-set accuracy from 65.4% to 81–90% depending on coverage. Responding to round-2 review, we added a mid-scale (~31%-accuracy) 3-seed sweep showing that self-consistency's previously reported seed-instability is substantially reduced — indeed reversed — once the model is trained past the near-degenerate accuracy regime, corrected a table transcription error one reviewer caught, and explicitly flagged the two gaps (full-scale seed sweep, bootstrap CIs on the headline numbers) that we still have not closed. The entire pipeline, including both robustness sweeps, runs in under 25 minutes on a CPU and is included in full for reproducibility.
