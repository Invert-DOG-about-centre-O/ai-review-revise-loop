# Cheap Single-Pass Signals Can Match — and Cheap Probes Can Recover — Expensive Sampling-Based Uncertainty in a Toy Language Model

## Abstract

Detecting when a language model's output is wrong is central to LLM
reliability. Two families of methods dominate: cheap single-pass signals
from one forward pass (max-softmax probability, predictive entropy), and
expensive sampling-based signals that draw multiple stochastic completions
and measure their agreement or diversity (self-consistency, semantic
entropy). We study this trade-off in a fully controlled, from-scratch toy
setting: a small causal transformer trained on synthetic two-digit addition,
decoded under both greedy and stochastic sampling. In our primary run
(n=700 test examples), (1) cheap max-probability is a *better* error
detector than 20-sample self-consistency or sampling entropy (AUROC 0.734
vs. 0.655/0.595); (2) a linear probe on cheap features recovers the
sampling-entropy signal (held-out R²=0.26) well enough that the probe's
prediction nearly matches the true 20-sample signal's downstream AUROC
(0.621 vs. 0.620) — though this closeness is a primary-run coincidence, not
a stable property across seeds; (3) self-consistency agreement is
well-discriminating but badly miscalibrated (ECE=0.331 vs. 0.064). Because a
single run cannot establish whether these gaps are signal or noise, we
re-ran the full pipeline for 5 independent seeds. Max-probability beats
self-consistency in **5/5 seeds** (mean gap 0.105, range 0.027-0.274); a
paired bootstrap over seeds puts the 95% CI on the mean gap at
**[0.048, 0.194]**, excluding zero (sign test p=0.031; p=0.0625 if the one
outlier seed is dropped, 4/4). The gap narrows but does not close as the
sampling budget grows (K=20→50: self-consistency AUROC 0.685→0.721, still
below maxprob's 0.773). New in this revision: clustering self-consistency
samples by edit distance instead of exact match — the experiment all four
round-2/3 reviewers independently asked for — does **not** close the gap
(agreement AUROC actually drops, 0.655→0.614; entropy AUROC rises slightly,
0.595→0.609, both still well below maxprob's 0.734), and a loss-curve
diagnosis of the one outlier seed shows it is a slow-converging trajectory,
not a divergent one (it reaches 98.8% accuracy given 2x the steps). We
release all training/eval code, the multi-seed, K-sweep, edit-distance, and
seed-diagnosis scripts, and raw logs.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models are increasingly deployed in settings where knowing
*when to trust an answer* matters as much as producing the answer. Two
broad families of uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods have emerged.
**Single-pass signals** — token probability, predictive entropy, verbalized
confidence — are cheap but known to be poorly calibrated (Xiong et al.
survey work, 2025-2026). **Sampling-based signals** — self-consistency,
semantic entropy, semantic energy — draw multiple completions and measure
agreement or the entropy of the induced meaning distribution. These tend to
be more reliable but multiply inference cost by 5-20x.

This cost has motivated *cheap proxies for expensive signals*. Semantic
Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024, arXiv:2406.15927) train a linear
classifier on a single forward pass's hidden states to predict semantic
entropy. Efficient Bayesian estimation of semantic entropy
(arXiv:2504.03579, 2025) similarly reduces the samples needed. A
2025-2026 survey (arXiv:2412.05563) catalogs UQ methods and flags the
cost/reliability trade-off as open. Semantic Energy (arXiv:2508.14496, 2025)
argues post-softmax semantic entropy misses information in raw internal
confidence.

**Gap.** Most empirical evidence for the cheap-vs-expensive trade-off comes
from large pretrained models on open-ended QA, where the "expensive" signal
itself requires an auxiliary NLI model or LLM judge to cluster samples into
semantic equivalence classes, making it hard to isolate whether a probe
recovers genuine uncertainty structure or exploits benchmark artifacts. We
ask: **does the same qualitative pattern appear in a minimal, fully
controlled, exactly-checkable toy task**, where ground-truth correctness and
answer clustering are trivial and the pipeline runs on a CPU in minutes?

## 2. Method

**Task.** Two-digit addition as fixed-length strings: input `"aa+bb="` (6
chars, a,b uniform in [0,99]), target `"sss"` (3-digit zero-padded sum).

**Model.** A causal, decoder-only Transformer from scratch (2 layers,
d_model=64, 4 heads, ff=128; ~130K params), character-level vocabulary of 12
tokens. Teacher forcing, cross-entropy on the 3 answer positions, AdamW,
lr=3e-3, batch 128. All data are drawn i.i.d. from the full 10,000-example
input space at each step/eval, so there is no train/test overlap.

**Uncertainty signals**, per test example from the greedy-decoded answer:
*cheap, single forward pass* — mean max-softmax probability (`maxprob`) and
mean predictive entropy (`pred_entropy`) across the 3 positions; *expensive,
K stochastic samples* (temperature 1.0) — self-consistency agreement with
the greedy answer (`sample_agree`) and Shannon entropy over distinct sampled
answer strings (`sample_entropy`), the toy analogue of semantic entropy with
exact-string-match clustering.

**Cheap probe.** A linear model (Ridge, alpha=1.0, standardized features) is
fit to predict `sample_entropy` from cheap features alone (`maxprob`,
`pred_entropy`, mean hidden state, 64-dim), on a 50/50 held-out split so
both R² and downstream AUROC are out-of-sample.

**Primary run.** 260 training steps, N_TEST=700, K=20, seed 0. This budget
was chosen because an earlier 1200-step run reached 100% accuracy, leaving
no errors to detect; §3.2 shows 260 steps is not a stable operating point.

**Multi-seed check.** `multiseed.py` repeats the *entire* pipeline (fresh
init, data, and sampling) for 5 seeds (0-4) at the same 260-step budget,
N_TEST=400, reporting the maxprob-minus-self-consistency gap summarized by a
paired bootstrap CI and exact sign test (`significance.py`).

**K-sweep.** `k_sweep.py` retrains the seed-0 model, draws K=50 samples
(N_TEST=200), and compares self-consistency AUROC at K=20 vs. all 50
(paired, same draws) against maxprob.

**Edit-distance clustering (new).** All four round-2/3 reviews independently
asked whether self-consistency's disadvantage is an artifact of exact-match
clustering starving it of a real paraphrase-equivalence signal. `editdist_
cluster.py` retrains the identical seed-0 pipeline and, for the same 20
samples per example, additionally greedily clusters samples using Hamming
distance ≤1 on the 3-digit string (an "off-by-one-digit" answer counts as
the same cluster) instead of exact match, recomputing agreement (fraction of
samples in the greedy answer's cluster) and entropy (over cluster-size
distribution).

**Seed-2 diagnosis (new).** Seed 2's 260-step accuracy (7.7%) was a
striking outlier flagged by 3/4 reviewers as possibly a training-instability
bug rather than legitimate variance. `seed2_diagnosis.py` logs the training
loss every 20 steps for all 5 seeds under identical settings, and separately
trains seed 2 for 520 steps (2x budget) to check whether it recovers.

**Evaluation.** AUROC (sign-flipped so higher = more confident) for
correct-vs-incorrect greedy answers; Expected Calibration Error (10 bins);
held-out R² and AUROC for the probe.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Primary run (seed 0, n=700)

Greedy-decode test accuracy: **81.1%** (133 incorrect, 567 correct).

| Signal | Type | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| maxprob | cheap, single-pass | **0.734** |
| neg. predictive entropy | cheap, single-pass | 0.618 |
| self-consistency agreement (K=20) | expensive, sampling | 0.655 |
| neg. sampling entropy (K=20) | expensive, sampling | 0.595 |

**Calibration (ECE, 10 bins):** `maxprob` is well calibrated (0.064);
`sample_agree` used directly as a confidence value is badly miscalibrated
(0.331) — samples agree with each other far more often than the greedy
answer is actually correct.

**Probe recovering the expensive signal (held-out n=350):** R²=0.261
predicting `sample_entropy` from cheap features. The probe's predicted
entropy gives AUROC=0.621, versus 0.620 for the true 20-sample signal on the
same held-out examples — a striking match, but §3.2 shows it is a
seed/split coincidence, not typical of the R²=0.09-0.42 range across seeds.
The cheap `maxprob` baseline still outperforms both (AUROC=0.750).

### 3.2 Multi-seed robustness check (5 seeds, n=400 each)

| Seed | Acc. (correct/400) | maxprob AUROC | self-consist. AUROC | gap | probe R² | true holdout AUROC | probe holdout AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.820 (328) | 0.755 | 0.678 | 0.077 | 0.260 | 0.577 | 0.588 |
| 1 | 0.750 (300) | 0.730 | 0.646 | 0.085 | 0.419 | 0.537 | 0.687 |
| 2 | 0.077 (31)  | 0.663 | 0.389 | 0.274 | 0.089 | 0.568 | 0.485 |
| 3 | 0.760 (304) | 0.679 | 0.653 | 0.027 | 0.201 | 0.566 | 0.587 |
| 4 | 0.880 (352) | 0.714 | 0.652 | 0.063 | 0.301 | 0.605 | 0.659 |
| **mean±std** | 0.658±0.294 | 0.708±0.033 | 0.603±0.108 | **0.105±0.097** | 0.254±0.109 | 0.571±0.022 | 0.601±0.070 |

Maxprob beats self-consistency in **5/5 seeds**. A paired bootstrap over the
5 seeds (10^5 resamples) gives a 95% CI on the mean gap of **[0.048,
0.194]**, with 0/100,000 resamples ≤0; an exact sign test gives **p=0.031**
(one-sided, 5/5). Dropping seed 2 — the extreme outlier discussed below —
and testing the remaining 4/4 gives p=0.0625, still significant at the
conventional one-sided 10% level despite the smaller n; the direction is
not driven by that one seed.

Probe R² is noisy (0.089-0.419) and on this smaller holdout, sometimes
exceeds the true expensive signal's own holdout AUROC (seeds 1, 3, 4) —
likely because the probe smooths out K=20 sampling noise. This confirms the
primary run's "near-exact match" (0.621 vs. 0.620) was a coincidence, not a
general property.

**Why seed 2?** Seed 2 reached only 7.7% accuracy (31/400) at the 260-step
budget, versus 75-88% for the other four. We diagnosed this by logging the
training loss curve for all 5 seeds every 20 steps (identical setup) and by
training seed 2 for 520 steps instead of 260. The loss curves show seed 2 is
not diverging or stuck in a qualitatively different basin — it descends
smoothly like the other seeds through step ~100 (loss ≈1.17, comparable to
seeds 0/1/3/4 at the same step) but then plateaus around 1.0-1.1 instead of
continuing to drop below 0.6, while the other four keep descending. Given
520 steps (2x budget), seed 2 reaches **98.8%** accuracy — it is a
slow-converging trajectory under a fixed step budget, not a broken or
divergent one. This is consistent with, and reinforces, the paper's other
finding that 260 steps is not a stable "~19% error" operating point:
different seeds simply cross the accuracy-vs-steps curve at different
points, and seed 2 happens to be the slowest of the five. We therefore treat
it as legitimate seed variance worth keeping in the 5/5 and bootstrap
figures (also supported by the p=0.0625 result when it is excluded), rather
than an artifact to discard, while flagging that at this specific step
budget its AUROC is built on a skewed 31-vs-369 split and should be read
with that caveat.

### 3.3 Does the gap close with more samples? (K-sweep, seed 0, n=200)

Self-consistency AUROC using the first K=20 of 50 draws: **0.685**; using
all K=50: **0.721**; maxprob on the same 200 examples: **0.773**. Going from
K=20→50 (2.5x the cost) narrows the gap from 0.088 to 0.053 but does not
close it, consistent with self-consistency approaching an asymptote below
maxprob's AUROC.

### 3.4 Does edit-distance clustering close the gap? (new)

All four round-2/3 reviews independently suggested the same experiment:
cluster self-consistency samples by edit distance rather than exact match,
since exact-match clustering may simply starve self-consistency of the
paraphrase-equivalence signal that motivates it on real LLMs. We implemented
this (`editdist_cluster.py`, same seed-0 model/data/samples as §3.1,
Hamming distance ≤1 on the 3-digit answer): agreement AUROC is **0.614**
(down from 0.655 exact-match) and entropy AUROC is **0.609** (up slightly
from 0.595), both still well below maxprob's 0.734 on the same examples.
Loosening clustering does not help agreement — an off-by-one-digit sample is
weak evidence the greedy answer is right, so folding it into "agreement"
adds noise — and only marginally helps entropy. This is direct evidence,
not just intuition, for the structural explanation in §4: maxprob is
computed on the very trajectory being scored, while self-consistency must
infer that trajectory's correctness indirectly from resamples, and no
reclustering choice we tried removes that disadvantage.

**Ablation note.** An initial 1200-step run reached 100% accuracy (AUROC
undefined); a first probe attempt with unscaled features and plain linear
regression gave negative R². We report both as motivation for the final
design (260 steps; Ridge on standardized features), per `run_log.txt`.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

This is a deliberately minimal, synthetic testbed — a 130K-parameter
character model on two-digit addition, not a large language model, and its
"errors" are near-miss arithmetic mistakes rather than open-ended factual
hallucinations. Arithmetic near-misses have little notion of semantic
equivalence distinct from string identity; §3.4's new edit-distance result
shows this is not merely a hypothesis but is empirically why sampling-based
signals underperform here — loosening the clustering criterion does not
close the gap, and if anything makes raw agreement noisier.

Runtime is not a stable number on this pipeline: this paper's earlier
"~110s" claim for `experiment.py` was stale, and independent re-measurements
by round-3 reviewers on their own machines ranged from ~140s to ~229s (our
own re-measurement gave ~229.5s). Rather than present a single "corrected"
point estimate with false precision, as one reviewer noted, we report this
as an order-of-magnitude figure: **on the order of 2-4 minutes on a single
CPU core**, varying with hardware/load. No accuracy, AUROC, or calibration
number depends on wall-clock time, and all reproduced bit-for-bit across
every independent rerun we are aware of.

The multi-seed check (§3.2), its bootstrap/sign-test analysis, and the new
seed-2 loss-curve diagnosis together address the most consistent concern
raised across reviews: without repeated runs, a diagnosis of the outlier
seed, and a formal test, an 8-27 point AUROC gap from a single seed is hard
to distinguish from noise or a training bug. Five seeds is still a small
sample for the gap's *magnitude* (bootstrap CI [0.048, 0.194]), but its
*sign* is now supported by a 5/5 sweep, p=0.031 (or p=0.0625 with the
outlier seed dropped), and a mechanistic account (§3.4) of *why* the gap
exists rather than just *that* it does. The K-sweep (§3.3) separately shows
raising K to 50 narrows but does not reverse the gap.

We view three results as robust enough to flag as hypotheses for pretrained
LLMs: (1) cheap single-pass probability should not be assumed inferior to
sampling-based UQ by default, and should be a baseline whenever expensive
sampling-based UQ is proposed — supported here across 5/5 seeds, at K=50,
and under looser clustering; (2) self-consistency agreement can be badly
miscalibrated even with reasonable discriminative power; (3) linear probes
on single-pass features can recover much of an expensive sampling signal's
discriminative power on average (R²≈0.25), though any single seed's
apparent "near-exact match" to the true signal's AUROC should not be
over-read, and on this toy task an even cheaper signal (maxprob) already
dominates both. We caveat all three as toy-task hypotheses, not
pretrained-LLM findings: the exact-match/edit-distance-clustering result is
suggestive but the genuinely hard part of semantic entropy — NLI-based
clustering of open-ended paraphrases — has no analogue here at all.

## 5. Conclusion

In a fully controlled, from-scratch toy LM trained on synthetic addition,
cheap single-pass max-probability was a better error detector than
20-sample self-consistency or sampling entropy — not just in a single run,
but in 5/5 independent seeded reruns (bootstrap 95% CI on the mean gap
[0.048, 0.194]; sign test p=0.031, p=0.0625 without the one outlier seed),
persisting (though narrowed) at K=50 samples and under edit-distance rather
than exact-match clustering — the experiment every reviewer asked for, now
run rather than argued from intuition. The apparent outlier seed (7.7%
accuracy) is diagnosed as slow convergence, not divergence: it reaches 98.8%
accuracy given 2x the training steps. Self-consistency agreement was
substantially miscalibrated despite reasonable AUROC, and a linear probe on
single-pass features recovered most of the expensive signal's discriminative
power on average, though not with the seed-0 precision the primary run's
headline number implied. The "~19% error" operating point is itself
seed-sensitive, and runtime is reported as an honest range (~2-4 min) rather
than a falsely precise point estimate. These patterns qualitatively echo
recent findings on pretrained LLMs in a setting that is trivial to reproduce
and extend. All code (`experiment.py`, `multiseed.py`, `k_sweep.py`,
`significance.py`, `editdist_cluster.py`, `seed2_diagnosis.py`), logs, and
raw results are included alongside this paper.
