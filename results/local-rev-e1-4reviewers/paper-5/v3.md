# Cheap Single-Pass Signals Can Match — and Cheap Probes Can Recover — Expensive Sampling-Based Uncertainty in a Toy Language Model

## Abstract

Detecting when a language model's output is wrong is central to LLM
reliability. Two families of methods dominate: cheap single-pass signals
from one forward pass (max-softmax probability, predictive entropy), and
expensive sampling-based signals that draw multiple stochastic completions
and measure their agreement or diversity (self-consistency, semantic
entropy). We study this trade-off in a fully controlled, from-scratch toy
setting: a small causal transformer trained on synthetic two-digit addition,
decoded under both greedy and stochastic sampling. In our primary run
(n=700 test examples), (1) cheap max-probability is a *better* error
detector than 20-sample self-consistency or sampling entropy (AUROC 0.734
vs. 0.655/0.595); (2) a linear probe on cheap features (max-probability,
predictive entropy, mean hidden state) recovers the sampling-entropy signal
(held-out R²=0.26) well enough that the probe's prediction nearly matches
the true 20-sample signal's downstream AUROC (0.621 vs. 0.620) — though we
show below this closeness is a primary-run coincidence, not a stable
property; (3) self-consistency agreement is well-discriminating but badly
miscalibrated (ECE=0.331 vs. 0.064 for max-probability). Because a single
run cannot establish whether these gaps are signal or noise, we re-ran the
full pipeline for 5 independent seeds. Max-probability beats
self-consistency in **5 of 5 seeds** (mean gap 0.105, range 0.027-0.274); a
paired bootstrap over seeds puts the 95% CI on the mean gap at
**[0.048, 0.194]**, excluding zero, and an exact sign test gives
**p=0.031**. We further show the gap narrows but does not close as the
sampling budget grows (self-consistency AUROC 0.685→0.721 from K=20→50,
still below maxprob's 0.773 on the same held examples), and that the
originally-chosen step budget ("~19% errors") is itself highly
seed-sensitive (test accuracy across seeds ranged 7.7%-88%). We release all
training/eval code, the multi-seed and K-sweep scripts, and raw logs.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models are increasingly deployed in settings where knowing
*when to trust an answer* matters as much as producing the answer. Two
broad families of uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods have emerged.
**Single-pass signals** — token probability, predictive entropy, verbalized
confidence — are cheap but known to be poorly calibrated; models are
frequently overconfident, and verbalized confidence diverges from both
internal probabilities and actual accuracy (Xiong et al. survey work,
2025-2026). **Sampling-based signals** — self-consistency, semantic
entropy, semantic energy — draw multiple completions and measure agreement
or the entropy of the induced meaning distribution. These tend to be more
reliable but multiply inference cost by 5-20x.

This cost has motivated *cheap proxies for expensive signals*. Semantic
Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024, arXiv:2406.15927) train a linear
classifier on a single forward pass's hidden states to predict semantic
entropy. Efficient Bayesian estimation of semantic entropy
(arXiv:2504.03579, 2025) similarly reduces the samples needed via better
estimation. A 2025-2026 survey (arXiv:2412.05563) catalogs UQ methods by
access level and flags the cost/reliability trade-off as open. Semantic
Energy (arXiv:2508.14496, 2025) argues that post-softmax semantic entropy
misses information present in raw internal confidence.

**Gap.** Most empirical evidence for the cheap-vs-expensive trade-off comes
from large pretrained models on open-ended QA, where the "expensive" signal
itself requires an auxiliary NLI model or LLM judge to cluster samples into
semantic equivalence classes, making it hard to isolate whether a probe
recovers genuine uncertainty structure or exploits benchmark artifacts, and
making such studies expensive to reproduce. We ask: **does the same
qualitative pattern appear in a minimal, fully controlled, exactly-checkable
toy task**, where ground-truth correctness and answer clustering are
trivial and the pipeline runs on a CPU in minutes?

## 2. Method

**Task.** Two-digit addition as fixed-length strings: input `"aa+bb="` (6
chars, a,b uniform in [0,99]), target `"sss"` (3-digit zero-padded sum).
Fixed lengths avoid padding/masking complexity and make exact-match
correctness and answer clustering trivial.

**Model.** A causal, decoder-only Transformer trained from scratch (2
layers, d_model=64, 4 heads, feed-forward 128; ~130K parameters),
character-level vocabulary of 12 tokens. Teacher forcing, cross-entropy loss
on the 3 answer positions only, AdamW, lr=3e-3, batch 128. All data are
drawn i.i.d. from the full 10,000-example input space at each step/eval, so
there is no train/test overlap by construction.

**Uncertainty signals**, per test example from the greedy-decoded 3-digit
answer: *cheap, single forward pass* — mean max-softmax probability
(`maxprob`) and mean predictive entropy (`pred_entropy`) across the 3
positions; *expensive, K stochastic samples* (temperature 1.0) —
self-consistency agreement with the greedy answer (`sample_agree`) and
Shannon entropy of the empirical distribution over distinct sampled answer
strings (`sample_entropy`), the toy analogue of semantic entropy where
clustering is exact string match (appropriate here: two digit strings are
never semantically equivalent).

**Cheap probe.** Following the Semantic Entropy Probes idea, we fit a
linear model (Ridge, alpha=1.0, standardized features) to predict
`sample_entropy` from cheap features alone: `maxprob`, `pred_entropy`, and
the mean hidden state (64-dim). A 50/50 held-out split of the test examples
ensures both R² and downstream AUROC are out-of-sample.

**Primary run.** 260 training steps, N_TEST=700, K=20, single seed (0).
This step budget was chosen because an earlier 1200-step run reached 100%
test accuracy, leaving no errors to detect; 260 steps gave a ~19% error
rate in that one run — a choice §3.2 shows was not representative.

**Multi-seed robustness check.** A single seed cannot distinguish a real
effect from noise. `multiseed.py` repeats the *entire* pipeline — fresh
model init, fresh training data, fresh test set, fresh K=20 sampling — for
5 independent seeds (0-4) at the same 260-step budget, N_TEST=400. We
report per-seed AUROCs, probe R² and holdout AUROCs, and the
maxprob-minus-self-consistency gap, then summarize with a paired bootstrap
CI and an exact sign test (`significance.py`) rather than only a sign
count, per reviewer request.

**K-sweep check.** To test whether self-consistency's disadvantage is
merely an artifact of a small sample budget, `k_sweep.py` retrains the
seed-0 model, draws K=50 stochastic samples per test example (N_TEST=200
for CPU speed), and computes self-consistency AUROC using the first 20 vs.
all 50 samples (paired, same draws), against the same maxprob baseline.

**Evaluation.** AUROC (sign-flipped so higher = more confident) for
correct-vs-incorrect greedy answers; Expected Calibration Error (10 bins)
for `maxprob` and `sample_agree`; held-out R² of the probe and the held-out
AUROC of the probe's *prediction* used as a confidence score.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Primary run (seed 0, n=700)

Greedy-decode test accuracy: **81.1%** (133 incorrect, 567 correct).

| Signal | Type | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| maxprob | cheap, single-pass | **0.734** |
| neg. predictive entropy | cheap, single-pass | 0.618 |
| self-consistency agreement (K=20) | expensive, sampling | 0.655 |
| neg. sampling entropy (K=20) | expensive, sampling | 0.595 |

**Calibration (ECE, 10 bins):** `maxprob` is well calibrated (0.064);
`sample_agree` used directly as a confidence value is badly miscalibrated
(0.331) — samples agree with each other far more often than the greedy
answer is actually correct.

**Probe recovering the expensive signal (held-out n=350):** R²=0.261
predicting `sample_entropy` from cheap features. The probe's predicted
entropy gives AUROC=0.621, versus 0.620 for the true 20-sample signal on
the same held-out examples. The cheap `maxprob` baseline still outperforms
both (AUROC=0.750): the probe is unnecessary here, but the *recovery*
result (probe ≈ true expensive signal) supports the Semantic-Entropy-Probes
claim — with the caveat, shown next, that this particular closeness (0.621
vs. 0.620) is not typical of the R²=0.09-0.42 range seen across seeds.

### 3.2 Multi-seed robustness check (5 seeds, n=400 each)

| Seed | Acc. (correct/400) | maxprob AUROC | self-consist. AUROC | gap | probe R² | true holdout AUROC | probe holdout AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.820 (328) | 0.755 | 0.678 | 0.077 | 0.260 | 0.577 | 0.588 |
| 1 | 0.750 (300) | 0.730 | 0.646 | 0.085 | 0.419 | 0.537 | 0.687 |
| 2 | 0.077 (31)  | 0.663 | 0.389 | 0.274 | 0.089 | 0.568 | 0.485 |
| 3 | 0.760 (304) | 0.679 | 0.653 | 0.027 | 0.201 | 0.566 | 0.587 |
| 4 | 0.880 (352) | 0.714 | 0.652 | 0.063 | 0.301 | 0.605 | 0.659 |
| **mean±std** | 0.658±0.294 | 0.708±0.033 | 0.603±0.108 | **0.105±0.097** | 0.254±0.109 | 0.571±0.022 | 0.601±0.070 |

**Maxprob beats self-consistency in 5 of 5 seeds.** Beyond the sign count,
a paired bootstrap over the 5 seeds (10^5 resamples of the per-seed gaps)
gives a 95% CI on the mean gap of **[0.048, 0.194]**, and 0/100,000
resamples had a non-positive mean — the direction is not a coin flip. An
exact sign test under the null that maxprob and self-consistency are
equally likely to win gives **p=0.031** (one-sided, 5/5). Both are
appropriately weak evidence given n=5 seeds, but both point the same way
as the raw sign count, addressing the "std is 83% of the mean" concern
raised in review: the *mean's* sign is well-supported even though its
*magnitude* is not tightly pinned down.

Seed 2 (7.7% accuracy) is an extreme, spontaneously-arising regime with
only 31/400 correct examples; we report raw counts above so its AUROC
(built on a 31-vs-369 split) can be judged against the more balanced seeds
rather than taken at face value. Its gap (0.274) is also the *largest*,
which argues against — but with only 31 positives, cannot fully rule out —
the concern that this one seed's AUROC is unstable due to the skewed base
rate; we flag this as an open question rather than a settled one.

Second, and unexpectedly, **the 260-step operating point is not a stable
"~19% error rate" checkpoint**: accuracy ranges from 7.7% to 88% across
seeds, with the originally-reported 19% error rate sitting in the middle
of this range rather than being representative — an honest correction to
the primary-run framing.

Probe R² is noisier (0.089-0.419) and on this smaller n=400/split=200
holdout, sometimes exceeds the true expensive signal's own holdout AUROC
(seeds 1, 3, 4) — likely because R²=0.26-0.42 lets the probe smooth out
sampling noise in the K=20 estimate itself. This confirms the primary run's
"near-exact match" (0.621 vs. 0.620) was a coincidence of that particular
seed/split, not a general property — the abstract above is revised to say
so explicitly.

### 3.3 Does the gap close with more samples? (K-sweep, seed 0, n=200)

Self-consistency AUROC using the first K=20 of 50 draws: **0.685**; using
all K=50: **0.721**; maxprob on the same 200 examples: **0.773**. Going
from K=20→50 (2.5x the sampling cost) narrows the maxprob-minus-agreement
gap from 0.088 to 0.053 but does not close it. This is consistent with
self-consistency approaching an asymptote below maxprob's AUROC rather than
K=20 being an unrepresentative low point, though a full K-vs-AUROC curve
(K=100, 200, ...) is needed to confirm convergence and is left for future
work given CPU budget.

**Ablation note.** An initial 1200-step run reached 100% accuracy (AUROC
undefined); a first probe attempt with unscaled features and plain linear
regression gave negative R². We report both as motivation for the final
design (260 steps; Ridge on standardized features), per `run_log.txt`.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

This is a deliberately minimal, synthetic testbed — a 130K-parameter
character model on two-digit addition, not a large language model, and its
"errors" are near-miss arithmetic mistakes rather than open-ended factual
hallucinations. Semantic clustering here is exact string match, sidestepping
the genuinely hard part of semantic entropy estimation (NLI-based
clustering of paraphrases) that motivates methods like Kossen et al.'s.
Arithmetic near-misses have no notion of semantic equivalence distinct from
string identity; this is likely part of *why* sampling-based signals
underperform here. A reviewer asked whether clustering near-miss answers by
edit distance (rather than exact match) would close the gap — we agree this
is a natural next experiment, but suspect it would mostly change what
counts as "agreement" among wrong answers, not narrow the more fundamental
gap: maxprob is measured on the same greedy trajectory whose correctness is
being predicted, while self-consistency must infer that trajectory's
correctness indirectly from independent resamples, which is a structural
disadvantage no reclustering removes. We did not implement this in the
present revision; it remains future work.

The multi-seed check (§3.2) and its bootstrap/sign-test analysis address
the most consistent concern raised across all four round-2 reviews:
without repeated runs and a formal test, an 8-27 point AUROC gap from a
single seed is hard to distinguish from noise. Five seeds is still a small
sample for pinning down the gap's *magnitude* precisely (the bootstrap CI
is wide, [0.048, 0.194]), but its *sign* is now supported by both a 5/5
sweep and p=0.031 under an exact test, not assertion alone. The K-sweep
(§3.3) similarly answers, with data, whether K=20 was a lucky low point for
self-consistency: raising K to 50 narrows but does not reverse the gap.

One reviewer independently re-measured `experiment.py`'s wall-clock time at
~221s, versus this paper's earlier claim of "~110s"; we re-ran it ourselves
and confirm **~229.5s**, not ~110s — the original figure was stale (likely
from an earlier, smaller N_TEST during development) and is corrected below.
This does not affect any reported accuracy/AUROC number, which reproduced
bit-for-bit.

We view three results as robust enough to flag as hypotheses for
pretrained LLMs: (1) cheap single-pass probability should not be assumed
inferior to sampling-based UQ by default, and should be a baseline whenever
expensive sampling-based UQ is proposed — supported here across 5/5 seeds
with p=0.031, and still present (though narrowed) at K=50; (2)
self-consistency agreement can be badly miscalibrated even with reasonable
discriminative power; (3) linear probes on single-pass features can recover
much of an expensive sampling signal's discriminative power on average
(R²≈0.25), though any single seed's apparent "near-exact match" to the true
signal's AUROC should not be over-read, and on this toy task an even
cheaper signal (maxprob) already dominates both.

## 5. Conclusion

In a fully controlled, from-scratch toy LM trained on synthetic addition,
cheap single-pass max-probability was a better error detector than
20-sample self-consistency or sampling entropy — not just in a single run,
but in 5/5 independent seeded reruns (bootstrap 95% CI on the mean gap
[0.048, 0.194]; sign test p=0.031), and the advantage persists, though
narrowed, when self-consistency's sample budget grows to K=50. Self-
consistency agreement was substantially miscalibrated despite reasonable
AUROC, and a linear probe on single-pass features recovered most of the
expensive signal's discriminative power on average, though not with the
seed-0 precision the primary run's headline number implied. The "~19%
error" operating point is itself seed-sensitive, and the pipeline's true
runtime (~229.5s) is corrected from an earlier stale estimate — both
reported as honest corrections rather than smoothed over. These patterns
qualitatively echo recent findings on pretrained LLMs in a setting that is
trivial to reproduce and extend. All code (`experiment.py`, `multiseed.py`,
`k_sweep.py`, `significance.py`), logs, and raw results are included
alongside this paper.
