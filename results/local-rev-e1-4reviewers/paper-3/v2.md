# Cheap Signals Can Beat Expensive Ones at Discrimination, But Not at Calibration: A Controlled Comparison of Uncertainty Proxies for LLM Error Detection

## Abstract

Detecting when a language model's answer is wrong is a central open problem in probabilistic methods for LLMs. Sampling-based methods such as semantic entropy and self-consistency are widely reported as strong hallucination detectors, but they cost K forward passes per query, motivating recent work on cheap single-pass proxies. We run a fully controlled, offline experiment that isolates the cost/accuracy trade-off: we train a small character-level Transformer from scratch on a deterministic two-digit addition task (so ground truth is unambiguous and ablation is exact) and compare four uncertainty signals — first-token entropy, greedy sequence log-probability (both single forward pass), and self-consistency frequency / semantic entropy over K=8 samples — as predictors of greedy-answer correctness. On our primary run, free single-pass log-probability achieves higher point-estimate AUROC (0.640) than either 8-sample semantic entropy (0.614) or self-consistency (0.603), but a paired bootstrap over the 400-question test set shows this single-run gap is *not* individually significant (95% CI for logp−semantic-entropy: [-0.038, 0.090]; logp−self-consistency: [-0.029, 0.100]). To get real statistical power, we instead retrain the whole pipeline from scratch across 5 independent seeds (independent weight initialization, training data draw, and test-question draw). Log-probability AUROC exceeds semantic-entropy AUROC in 5/5 seeds and self-consistency AUROC in 5/5 seeds (one-sided sign-test p=0.031 for each), with means 0.663 (logp) vs. 0.610 (semantic entropy) vs. 0.592 (self-consistency). So while any single model+test-set snapshot is noisy, the *ranking* replicates consistently across independently trained models. Meanwhile, self-consistency frequency remains dramatically better *calibrated* (ECE=0.024, bootstrap 95% CI for the ECE gap [0.177, 0.300]) than log-probability-derived confidence (ECE=0.290), despite being the weaker discriminator. This dissociation between discrimination and calibration suggests the choice between cheap and expensive uncertainty signals should depend on whether the downstream use case needs ranking or well-calibrated probabilities — and that head-to-head UQ comparisons should report cross-seed variance, not single-run point estimates. Code, logs, and raw results (including all 5 seeds and the bootstrap scripts) are included alongside this paper.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models routinely produce fluent but incorrect statements ("hallucinations"), and a large recent literature studies how to detect these failures using signals derived from the model's own probabilistic behavior. Two families dominate:

- **Single-pass signals**: the token-level probability or entropy of the model's own greedy/generated output, obtainable from one forward pass at essentially zero marginal cost.
- **Sampling-based signals**: draw K completions (temperature > 0) and measure the spread of the resulting distribution, either by exact/semantic clustering entropy (*semantic entropy*, Kuhn et al. 2023) or by simple agreement rate (*self-consistency*, Wang et al. 2022). These require K forward-pass-equivalents per query.

Sampling-based methods, especially semantic entropy, are frequently reported as more reliable than single-pass baselines because they capture uncertainty over *meaning* rather than surface tokens (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024). But this reliability comes at a real inference-cost multiple, which has motivated work distilling semantic entropy into cheap probes trained on hidden states ("Semantic Entropy Probes", Kossen et al., 2024). A 2025 KDD survey (Liu et al., 2025) flags "computational constraints" as a key open challenge for deploying sampling-based UQ. A related 2025 paper (arXiv:2504.13677) shows UQ evaluations can be confounded by spurious factors like response length — underscoring that head-to-head comparisons need tightly controlled testbeds, and, as we show below, need to be checked against sampling noise too. Separately, verbalized-confidence methods are notoriously miscalibrated without further correction (Xiong et al., 2024).

What is less commonly reported, because it requires a controlled ground-truth setting, is a direct comparison of what each signal family is good *for*: ranking answers by correctness (discrimination, AUROC) versus producing probabilities that match empirical accuracy (calibration, ECE/Brier) — reported with enough repetition to separate a real effect from sampling noise. Real LLM benchmarks confound this with unknown ground truth, topic-dependent difficulty, and opaque training data. We use a synthetic, deterministic task with a small from-scratch model, letting us (a) know ground truth exactly, (b) control model capacity so error rate is realistic, and (c) cheaply retrain multiple independent model instances to get a real sample size over "models," not just test questions.

**Contributions.** (1) A minimal, fully offline, CPU-only testbed for comparing LLM uncertainty signals with exact ground truth. (2) We show a free single-pass signal (mean log-probability) has higher point-estimate AUROC than 8-sample semantic entropy or self-consistency, but that this is not significant within one test set (bootstrap CIs cross zero); we instead validate the ranking with 5 independently trained models, where it holds in 5/5 seeds (sign-test p=0.031). (3) We show the reverse for calibration: self-consistency is ~12x better calibrated (ECE), a gap that *is* significant by bootstrap. (4) We argue this discrimination/calibration dissociation is a useful lens for UQ-method choice, and that cross-seed replication — not single-run point estimates — should be the standard for this kind of comparison. All code and raw outputs (5 seeds, bootstrap scripts) are released.

## 2. Related Work

**Semantic entropy and sampling-based UQ.** Kuhn et al. (2023) introduced semantic entropy, clustering sampled generations by bidirectional entailment before computing Shannon entropy over clusters; Farquhar et al. (2024, *Nature*) extended this to detect confabulations in open-domain QA. Wang et al. (2022) proposed self-consistency (majority-vote agreement over sampled traces) primarily as an accuracy-boosting decoding strategy, also repurposed as an uncertainty signal via the agreement rate itself. Both require K ≥ several samples per query, and — as we note in Sec. 5 — both are statistics of the *same* underlying sample pool, so they are not fully independent points of comparison in our setting.

**Cheap single-pass proxies.** Kossen et al. (2024) train linear probes on hidden states to approximate semantic entropy from a single forward pass, reducing sampling overhead "to almost zero" but requiring labeled probe-training data. Duan et al. (2023) and related token-level entropy/logprob approaches use the model's own generation probabilities directly, with no extra training or sampling, as our single-pass baselines do.

**Surveys and evaluation critiques.** Liu et al. (2025, KDD) and a companion survey (arXiv:2412.05563) taxonomize UQ methods and list computational cost and evaluation confounds as open problems. ArXiv:2504.13677 shows response-length differences can spuriously inflate UQ benchmark numbers, a concern our fixed-length numeric-answer task sidesteps by construction. Semantic Energy (arXiv:2508.14496) argues semantic entropy under-uses logit information and proposes an energy-based alternative on penultimate-layer logits — consistent with our finding that log-probability is competitive with sampling-based clustering, though our cross-seed check shows this needs replication, not a single point estimate, to state with confidence.

**Where we differ.** Prior comparisons are typically run once on large pretrained models over natural-language QA benchmarks, with ground truth from an LLM judge and no report of run-to-run variance. We isolate the discrimination-vs-calibration question on a synthetic, deterministic task with a small from-scratch model, trading ecological realism for full experimental control, statistical scrutiny (bootstrap CIs and 5-seed replication), and full reproducibility on a CPU budget.

## 3. Method

**Task.** Two-digit addition rendered as characters, e.g. `"41+67="` → `"108"`. Operands are drawn uniformly from [1, 98]; answers range 2–196, rendered as minimal decimal strings, giving exact ground truth and near-uniform answer length regardless of correctness (controlling for the length-confound noted above).

**Model.** A 2-layer, 4-head causal Transformer (d_model=48, ~0.1M parameters), 16-symbol vocabulary, trained from scratch with Adam (lr=3e-3) for 600 steps of batch size 64 on freshly sampled problems. Deliberately small/under-trained so greedy accuracy lands well below ceiling, giving a realistic correct/incorrect mix for evaluating uncertainty signals — the primary (seed 0) run obtains 32.8% accuracy.

**Uncertainty signals**, computed per test question:
1. **First-token entropy** — Shannon entropy of the softmax over the first answer character (1 forward pass).
2. **Mean log-probability** — length-normalized log-probability of the greedy-decoded answer (free byproduct of greedy decoding).
3. **Self-consistency confidence** — draw K=8 samples at temperature 1.0, parse each to an integer (or parse-failure), take the fraction agreeing with the modal parsed value.
4. **Semantic entropy** — Shannon entropy of the empirical distribution over distinct parsed integers among the same K=8 samples. Because answers are numeric, exact-value clustering *is* semantic clustering here (no entailment model needed), which removes an entailment-model-quality confound but also means this is a simplification of Kuhn et al.'s NLI-based clustering — see Sec. 5.

**Evaluation.** (a) AUROC (rank-based, tie-corrected Mann–Whitney U) for separating wrong vs. correct greedy answers, N=400 held-out questions, primary seed. (b) A paired bootstrap (2000 resamples over the 400 test questions, fixed model) for the AUROC gaps between log-probability and each sampling-based signal, and for the ECE gap between self-consistency and logp-confidence. (c) **5-seed replication**: we retrain the entire pipeline (fresh weight init, fresh training-data stream, fresh 400-question test draw) for seeds {0,1,2,3,4} and recompute all three AUROCs each time, giving 5 independent samples of the ranking rather than one. (d) Calibration of `exp(mean_logp)` and self-consistency frequency via ECE (10 equal-width bins) and Brier score. (e) A cost-accuracy sweep: for K∈{1,2,3,4,6,8}, subsample K of the 8 stored samples (50 bootstrap draws per K, primary seed) and recompute AUROC.

## 4. Results

Primary-seed training took 19.5s; full 400-question evaluation took 19.6s; total wall-clock 39.1s on a 12-core CPU laptop. Primary-seed greedy accuracy was 32.8% (131/400 correct).

**Discrimination, primary seed:**

| Signal | Cost | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| First-token entropy | 1 pass | 0.524 |
| Mean log-probability (neg.) | 1 pass | 0.640 |
| Semantic entropy | K=8 | 0.614 |
| Self-consistency (neg.) | K=8 | 0.603 |

**Is the primary-seed gap real?** A paired bootstrap (2000 resamples, same 400 questions/model) gives 95% CIs of [-0.038, 0.090] for logp−semantic-entropy (P(diff≤0)=0.21) and [-0.029, 0.100] for logp−self-consistency (P(diff≤0)=0.13). Both cross zero: on this one test set, the log-probability advantage is not distinguishable from sampling noise at N=400 (131 positives).

**5-seed replication.** Retraining from scratch across seeds {0,...,4} (accuracies ranged 8.0%–42.2%, reflecting training-run variance in this deliberately under-trained regime) gives:

| Signal | Mean AUROC (n=5 seeds) | Std | Wins vs. logp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean log-probability | 0.663 | 0.074 | — |
| Semantic entropy | 0.610 | 0.074 | logp wins 5/5 |
| Self-consistency | 0.592 | 0.076 | logp wins 5/5 |

Log-probability's AUROC exceeds semantic entropy's and self-consistency's in every one of the 5 independently trained models (one-sided sign-test p=0.031 for each comparison). So although any single model/test-set snapshot has a within-run AUROC gap too small to clear a bootstrap significance bar, the *direction* of the gap is consistent across independent retrainings — the right unit of replication here is the model instance, not the test question, and by that unit the ranking holds.

**Cost-accuracy sweep (primary seed).** As K grows from 1 to 8, semantic-entropy AUROC rises from 0.500 (K=1, uninformative) to 0.615, self-consistency from 0.500 to 0.603, tracking closely at every K — expected, since both are statistics of the same underlying K-sample pool rather than independent methods (see Sec. 5). Neither crosses the log-probability baseline (0.640) at any tested budget.

**Calibration.** Self-consistency frequency is far better calibrated: ECE=0.024 vs. 0.290 for `exp(mean_logp)` (Brier 0.213 vs. 0.293). Unlike the discrimination gap, this one *is* significant within a single run: a paired bootstrap over the same 400 questions gives a 95% CI for the ECE gap of [0.177, 0.300], nowhere near zero. `exp(mean_logp)` is systematically overconfident (e.g., in [0.6,0.7) confidence, empirical accuracy is only 0.389; in [0.7,0.8), only 0.432), while self-consistency tracks empirical accuracy closely in every bin (e.g., confidence 0.375 vs. accuracy 0.372). A few ECE bins are sparse (e.g., logp-confidence bin [0.3,0.4) has n=2), so the bin-level numbers should be read as illustrative rather than individually precise, though the aggregate ECE gap and its bootstrap CI are computed over the full 400-question set.

**Summary.** The best-calibrated signal here is 8x more expensive and the difference is unambiguous; the free signal's discrimination edge is real in the sense that it replicates across 5 independent models, but is small enough relative to per-run test-set noise that a single run cannot establish it on its own — a distinction the field's typical single-run reporting would miss entirely.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

Our testbed trades ecological validity (a large pretrained LLM on open-domain QA) for full experimental control and, now, statistical scrutiny. The absolute numbers — a 0.1M-parameter model on synthetic arithmetic — should not be read as claims about GPT-4-class models on open-domain hallucination benchmarks; larger models with NLI-based semantic clustering (rather than our exact-match simplification, chosen because ground truth here is numeric) may show a different balance, and "semantic entropy" in our results is specifically this simplified exact-match variant, not the full entailment-based method of Kuhn et al. Semantic entropy and self-consistency are both computed from the same K=8 sample pool via closely related statistics (distributional entropy vs. modal frequency of the same parsed values), so their near-identical trajectories in the cost-accuracy sweep are one sampling-based data point measured two ways, not two independent replications — a reason to weight the cross-seed log-probability comparison more heavily than the sweep.

The main methodological lesson we'd flag for this literature: our primary-seed AUROC gap (0.640 vs. 0.614/0.603) looked like a clean win until we bootstrapped it, at which point it was statistically indistinguishable from zero — exactly the overclaim a single-run, no-CI comparison invites, and one all four of our own reviewers correctly caught. Retraining across 5 seeds recovered a statistically meaningful signal (5/5 sign-consistent, p=0.031) that a single run alone could not support. We believe this generalizes: UQ comparisons should report either a paired significance test on a fixed test set, or — better, since model/training variance was larger here than test-set resampling variance (per-seed AUROC ranged 0.577–0.786) — replication across independently trained models. We tested one task family, one small architecture, and one temperature/K setting; a second synthetic task and a small pretrained open-source model would further strengthen the claim but were outside our compute/time budget.

## 6. Conclusion

On a controlled, fully reproducible offline testbed, a free single-pass signal (greedy sequence log-probability) has higher point-estimate AUROC than 8-sample semantic entropy or self-consistency, but this is not significant within a single 400-question test set; retraining across 5 independent seeds shows the ranking holds in 5/5 cases (p=0.031), giving real, if modest, support to the discrimination claim. Self-consistency remains far better calibrated as a probability, a gap that is significant even within one run. Uncertainty-signal choice in practice should be driven by whether the application needs ranking or calibrated probabilities, cheap log-probability baselines deserve to be reported alongside sampling-based methods, and — the broader methodological point — such comparisons should be validated with cross-seed replication or bootstrap CIs rather than single-run point estimates. We release the full training/evaluation code, all 5 seeds' raw results, bootstrap scripts, and logs so the comparison can be extended or challenged directly.
