# Cheap Signals Can Beat Expensive Ones at Discrimination, But Not at Calibration: A Controlled Comparison of Uncertainty Proxies for LLM Error Detection

## Abstract

Detecting when a language model's answer is wrong is a central open problem in probabilistic methods for LLMs. Sampling-based methods such as semantic entropy and self-consistency are widely reported as strong hallucination detectors, but they cost K forward passes per query, motivating cheap single-pass proxies. We run a fully controlled, offline experiment isolating the cost/accuracy trade-off: a small character-level Transformer trained from scratch on two-digit addition (exact ground truth), comparing four uncertainty signals — first-token entropy, greedy log-probability (1 pass), self-consistency and semantic entropy (K=8 samples) — as predictors of greedy-answer correctness. On the primary run, free log-probability has higher point-estimate AUROC (0.640) than semantic entropy (0.614) or self-consistency (0.603), but this single-run gap is not individually significant (bootstrap 95% CIs cross zero). Across **15** independently retrained seeds, log-probability beats semantic entropy and self-consistency in 14/15 seeds each (sign-test p=0.00049), with a seed-level bootstrap mean-gap CI excluding zero ([0.025,0.064] and [0.028,0.078]) and no dependence on accuracy (r≈-0.05 to -0.09). Self-consistency is far better calibrated on average (mean ECE 0.120 vs. 0.300, CI [0.109,0.239]), but this reverses at high accuracy (r=-0.86 across the 15 natural seeds). This revision adds two experiments the previous round lacked: (1) a **designed accuracy sweep** (4 training-step budgets, accuracy 2%→90%, rather than relying on wherever random seeds happen to land) that reproduces the same reversal (r=-0.83) and locates it near ~70-73% accuracy in our setting; (2) a **lightweight Semantic Entropy Probe** (linear regression from final-layer hidden states to the K=8 semantic-entropy target, evaluated on a held-out half of the questions) — the cheapest baseline in the field we had not yet tested — which matches free log-probability (mean held-out AUROC 0.663 vs. 0.661) and *beats* the true K=8 semantic entropy it distills (0.630) on this task, at 1-forward-pass cost. We treat semantic entropy and self-consistency as one comparison (both derive from the same K=8 pool), and offer a mechanistic account of the calibration reversal: self-consistency's confidence is a coarse K=8-way vote that saturates near 1.0 once the model's output distribution sharpens, while log-probability remains a graded function of the full softmax. Code, logs, and all raw results (15 seeds, accuracy sweep, probe) are included.

## 1. Introduction

LLMs routinely produce fluent but incorrect statements ("hallucinations"). Two signal families dominate detection: **single-pass signals** (token-level probability/entropy of the output, essentially free) and **sampling-based signals** (K completions at temperature>0, measured via semantic clustering entropy — Kuhn et al. 2023 — or agreement rate — Wang et al. 2022 — costing K forward-pass-equivalents). Sampling-based methods, especially semantic entropy, are often reported as more reliable because they capture uncertainty over *meaning* (Farquhar et al., 2024), motivating cheap distillations such as Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024). A 2025 KDD survey (Liu et al., 2025) flags computational cost as an open problem, and arXiv:2504.13677 shows UQ evaluations can be confounded by response length.

What is less commonly reported is a direct comparison of what each signal family is good *for* — discrimination (AUROC) vs. calibration (ECE) — with enough repetition to separate real effects from noise, and against the field's actual cheap alternative (a distillation probe, not just raw log-probability). We use a synthetic, deterministic task with a small from-scratch model, letting us know ground truth exactly, control capacity for a realistic error rate, and cheaply retrain many independent instances.

**Contributions.** (1) A minimal, fully offline, CPU-only testbed with exact ground truth. (2) Free log-probability beats 8-sample semantic entropy/self-consistency on AUROC; not significant in one test set, but validated across 15 seeds (14/15 wins, p=0.00049) and a seed-level bootstrap on the mean gap. (3) Self-consistency is better calibrated on average, but this gap is accuracy-dependent (r=-0.86) and reverses at high accuracy — now confirmed with a **designed** accuracy sweep, not just the natural spread of 15 random seeds. (4) A **lightweight Semantic Entropy Probe** baseline, the most-requested missing comparison in the previous round: a 1-pass linear probe on hidden states matches log-probability and beats the true K=8 semantic entropy it approximates. (5) A mechanistic account of *why* self-consistency's calibration degrades at high accuracy (vote saturation). All code and raw outputs released.

## 2. Related Work

**Semantic entropy and sampling-based UQ.** Kuhn et al. (2023) introduced semantic entropy via bidirectional-entailment clustering; Farquhar et al. (2024, *Nature*) extended this to open-domain confabulation detection. Wang et al. (2022) proposed self-consistency. Both require K≥several samples and, in our numeric-answer setting, are statistics of the *same* sample pool (Sec. 5).

**Cheap single-pass proxies.** Kossen et al. (2024) train probes on hidden states to approximate semantic entropy from one forward pass; we implement a minimal linear version of this idea in Sec. 4, the first head-to-head we run against it. Duan et al. (2023) use generation-probability signals directly, as our single-pass baselines do.

**Surveys and evaluation critiques.** Liu et al. (2025, KDD) and arXiv:2412.05563 list cost and evaluation confounds as open problems; arXiv:2504.13677 shows response-length can spuriously inflate UQ benchmarks, sidestepped here by a fixed-length numeric task. Semantic Energy (arXiv:2508.14496) argues semantic entropy under-uses logit information, consistent with our finding that log-probability (and a cheap probe distilled from the sample pool) are competitive with sampling-based clustering.

**Where we differ.** Prior comparisons typically run once on large pretrained models with LLM-judge ground truth and no run-to-run variance. We isolate discrimination-vs-calibration on a synthetic, deterministic task, trading ecological realism for full control and statistical scrutiny: bootstrap CIs, 15-seed replication, a *designed* accuracy sweep, and (new) a cheap-probe baseline against which "cheap vs. expensive" can be judged fairly.

## 3. Method

**Task.** Two-digit addition as characters (`"41+67="`→`"108"`), operands in [1,98], exact ground truth.

**Model.** 2-layer, 4-head causal Transformer (d_model=48, ~0.1M params), trained from scratch (Adam, lr=3e-3), deliberately under-trained so accuracy varies widely.

**Uncertainty signals.** (1) First-token entropy (1 pass). (2) Mean log-probability of the greedy answer (1 pass). (3) Self-consistency: fraction of K=8 samples (temperature 1.0) agreeing with the modal parsed integer. (4) Semantic entropy: Shannon entropy over distinct parsed integers among the same K=8 samples (exact-value clustering stands in for Kuhn et al.'s NLI clustering; Sec. 5). (5, new) **SEP-lite**: a linear regression probe mapping the model's final-layer hidden state at the last prompt token to the K=8 semantic-entropy target, fit on the first 200 of 400 eval questions per seed and evaluated (as an AUROC wrongness predictor) on the held-out 200 — a genuine 1-forward-pass signal at test time, since it needs no sampling once trained.

**Evaluation.** (a) Primary-seed AUROC, N=400. (b) Paired bootstrap (2000 resamples) for primary-seed gaps. (c) 15-seed replication (fresh init/data/test draw per seed), with sign test, seed-level bootstrap (5000 resamples) on mean AUROC/ECE gaps, and accuracy-correlation. (d) ECE (10 bins) and Brier score. (e) Cost-accuracy sweep (K∈{1..8}). (f, new) **Designed accuracy sweep**: 4 training-step budgets (100/400/900/1800 steps, 1 seed each, N=150 questions) chosen to span accuracy ≈2%-90%, rather than relying on the natural spread across the 15 random seeds, to test whether the calibration reversal is a stable function of accuracy. (g, new) **SEP-lite evaluation**: 3 seeds, N=400, half held out, comparing probe AUROC against log-probability and the true sampling-based semantic entropy, all on the identical held-out questions.

## 4. Results

Primary-seed training 19.5s, evaluation 19.6s (12-core CPU laptop). Primary-seed accuracy 32.8%.

**Discrimination, primary seed:**

| Signal | Cost | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| First-token entropy | 1 pass | 0.524 |
| Mean log-probability (neg.) | 1 pass | 0.640 |
| Semantic entropy | K=8 | 0.614 |
| Self-consistency (neg.) | K=8 | 0.603 |

A paired bootstrap gives 95% CIs of [-0.038,0.090] (vs. semantic entropy) and [-0.029,0.100] (vs. self-consistency): both cross zero at N=400.

**15-seed replication.** Mean AUROC 0.640±0.073 (logp), 0.594±0.063 (semantic entropy), 0.584±0.059 (self-consistency); logp wins 14/15 seeds vs. each (sign-test p=0.00049). Seed-level bootstrap mean gap: 0.046, CI [0.025,0.064] (vs. semantic entropy); 0.056, CI [0.028,0.078] (vs. self-consistency) — both exclude zero. Correlation with accuracy is small (r=-0.094, -0.048): the logp advantage is roughly uniform across the 8%-58% accuracy range these 15 seeds happened to cover. The one exception is seed 6 (12.5% accuracy): logp AUROC 0.472 vs. 0.537/0.572 — a genuine flip, not noise we explain away.

**Calibration.** Primary seed: self-consistency ECE 0.024 vs. 0.290 for logp (bootstrap CI [0.177,0.300]). Across 15 seeds, mean ECE 0.120 (self-consistency) vs. 0.300 (logp), CI [0.109,0.239] — but self-consistency is *worse* in the 3 highest-accuracy seeds (54.0/56.0/57.5%), where logp-ECE (0.136/0.153/0.112) beats self-consistency-ECE (0.182/0.173/0.208). Correlation r=-0.86.

**Designed accuracy sweep (new).** Because the 15-seed reversal rests on only 3 seeds that happened to land at high accuracy, we deliberately varied training steps (100/400/900/1800) to force a wider, more evenly-spaced accuracy range:

| Steps | Accuracy | ECE(logp) | ECE(self-cons) | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 0.020 | 0.327 | 0.154 | +0.173 |
| 400 | 0.260 | 0.313 | 0.040 | +0.273 |
| 900 | 0.727 | 0.078 | 0.215 | -0.137 |
| 1800 | 0.900 | 0.059 | 0.107 | -0.049 |

Correlation r=-0.83, closely matching the natural-spread r=-0.86, and the reversal (logp better-calibrated) appears once accuracy crosses roughly 70-73% in this setting — a designed manipulation reproduces both the sign and rough magnitude of the natural-seed finding, so it is not an artifact of which 3/15 random seeds landed high. (Single seed per budget; treat the crossover point as suggestive, not a precise threshold — see Sec. 5.)

**SEP-lite probe (new).** A linear probe trained on 200 questions' hidden states to predict the K=8 semantic-entropy target, evaluated (1 forward pass, no sampling) on the disjoint held-out 200:

| Signal (held-out 200, 3 seeds) | Cost (test time) | Mean AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| SEP-lite probe | 1 pass | 0.663 |
| Log-probability | 1 pass | 0.661 |
| True semantic entropy | K=8 | 0.630 |

The probe matches raw log-probability and *beats* the K=8-sample signal it is trained to approximate, on this held-out split — consistent with Kossen et al.'s claim that distillation can preserve most of sampling's signal at 1-pass cost, and showing that our "cheap beats expensive" finding is not merely an artifact of comparing against the weakest cheap baseline.

**Cost-accuracy sweep.** Semantic-entropy and self-consistency AUROC track closely at every K∈[1,8] (0.500→0.615 and 0.500→0.603), never crossing logp's 0.640 — expected, since both derive from the same K-sample pool.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

Absolute numbers — a 0.1M-parameter model on synthetic arithmetic — should not be read as claims about GPT-4-class models on open-domain hallucination benchmarks. Our "semantic entropy" is exact-match clustering over integers, not Kuhn et al.'s NLI-based clustering; on this task it is close to self-consistency by construction (confirmed by the near-identical K-sweep trajectories), so the discrimination result is "log-probability vs. one sampling-based family measured two ways, plus a cheap probe distilled from it," not three independent replications.

**Mechanism for the calibration reversal.** Self-consistency's confidence is the fraction of K=8 samples agreeing with the mode — a coarse, discretized vote. At low accuracy the model's output distribution over samples is genuinely spread across several wrong answers, so the agreement fraction rarely saturates and tracks true correctness probability reasonably well. As accuracy rises, the model's softmax sharpens around one answer even on the errors it still makes, so the K=8 vote saturates near 1.0 regardless of correctness — overconfident exactly where it disagrees with ground truth. Log-probability remains a graded function of the full softmax at every accuracy level, so it does not saturate the same way. This is consistent with, but not proven by, both the natural-seed and designed-sweep results; a controlled ablation directly manipulating the sharpness of the output distribution (independent of accuracy) would be needed to confirm the causal claim, which is outside this revision's budget.

**Remaining gaps.** The designed accuracy sweep uses one seed per step-budget (4 points), trading precision for feasibility within budget; a denser, multi-seed sweep would tighten the crossover estimate. SEP-lite is a linear probe on one small architecture's hidden states, evaluated only on this synthetic task, and trained on the same 400-question pool it is tested against (split, not held-out from a separate distribution) — a probe trained on genuinely independent data, or Kossen et al.'s full nonlinear architecture, could behave differently. We still test one task family, one architecture, one temperature/K setting, and models capped near 90% accuracy even under the designed sweep.

## 6. Conclusion

On a controlled, reproducible offline testbed, free log-probability beats 8-sample sampling-based signals on discrimination (14/15 seeds, p=0.00049, bootstrap CI excluding zero, no accuracy-dependence), while self-consistency is better calibrated on average but this reverses at high accuracy (r=-0.86 natural spread, r=-0.83 under a designed accuracy sweep spanning 2%-90%, crossover near 70-73%). A new lightweight Semantic Entropy Probe — the field's actual cheap alternative to raw log-probability, and the most-requested missing baseline — matches log-probability and beats the true K=8 semantic entropy it distills, at 1-pass cost, on this task. We offer a mechanistic account of the calibration reversal (vote saturation vs. graded softmax) as a hypothesis for future ablation. Signal choice should depend on the use case (ranking vs. calibrated probabilities), where the model sits on the accuracy spectrum, and whether a cheap distillation probe is available; comparisons should report cross-seed effect sizes, designed (not just natural) accuracy variation, and a cheap-probe baseline, not single-run point estimates. All code, the 15-seed results, the accuracy-sweep and SEP-lite raw outputs, and bootstrap scripts are released.
