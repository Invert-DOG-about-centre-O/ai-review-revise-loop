# Cheap Signals Can Beat Expensive Ones at Discrimination, But Not at Calibration: A Controlled Comparison of Uncertainty Proxies for LLM Error Detection

## Abstract

Detecting when a language model's answer is wrong is a central open problem in probabilistic methods for LLMs. Sampling-based methods such as semantic entropy and self-consistency are widely reported as strong hallucination detectors, but they cost K forward passes per query, motivating cheap single-pass proxies. We run a fully controlled, offline experiment isolating the cost/accuracy trade-off: a small character-level Transformer trained from scratch on two-digit addition (exact ground truth, exact ablation), comparing four uncertainty signals — first-token entropy, greedy sequence log-probability (single forward pass), and self-consistency frequency / semantic entropy over K=8 samples — as predictors of greedy-answer correctness. On a single primary run, free log-probability has higher point-estimate AUROC (0.640) than 8-sample semantic entropy (0.614) or self-consistency (0.603), but a paired bootstrap over the 400-question test set shows this single-run gap is *not* individually significant (95% CIs cross zero). To get real power we retrain the whole pipeline from scratch across **15** independent seeds (fresh weights, training data, and test questions). Log-probability's AUROC beats semantic entropy's in 14/15 seeds and self-consistency's in 14/15 seeds (one-sided sign-test p=0.00049); a seed-level bootstrap on the *mean* gap gives 95% CIs of [0.025, 0.064] (vs. semantic entropy) and [0.028, 0.078] (vs. self-consistency), both excluding zero — so unlike the n=5 replication in an earlier draft, we now have both a directional and a magnitude-based cross-seed result, and a larger n reveals one seed where the ranking genuinely flips (low-accuracy seed 6), which is informative in its own right. Because semantic entropy and self-consistency are both statistics of the same K=8 sample pool, we treat this as evidence for one comparison (logp vs. sampling), not two. Self-consistency remains markedly better calibrated on average (mean ECE 0.120 vs. 0.300 for log-probability-derived confidence across the 15 seeds, seed-level bootstrap CI for the gap [0.109, 0.239]), but this advantage is itself accuracy-dependent: it correlates strongly with model accuracy (r=-0.86) and *reverses* in the three highest-accuracy seeds, where log-probability is actually better calibrated. The discrimination gap shows no such dependence (r≈-0.05 to -0.09 with accuracy). This suggests the choice between cheap and expensive uncertainty signals should depend on both the downstream use case (ranking vs. calibrated probabilities) and where the model sits on the accuracy spectrum — and that head-to-head UQ comparisons should report cross-seed variance and effect sizes, not single-run point estimates or bare sign tests. Code, logs, and raw results (all 15 seeds, bootstrap scripts) are included alongside this paper.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models routinely produce fluent but incorrect statements ("hallucinations"), and a large literature studies detecting these failures via signals from the model's own probabilistic behavior. Two families dominate:

- **Single-pass signals**: token-level probability or entropy of the greedy/generated output, essentially free.
- **Sampling-based signals**: draw K completions (temperature > 0) and measure the spread, either via exact/semantic clustering entropy (*semantic entropy*, Kuhn et al. 2023) or simple agreement rate (*self-consistency*, Wang et al. 2022), costing K forward-pass-equivalents.

Sampling-based methods, especially semantic entropy, are frequently reported as more reliable because they capture uncertainty over *meaning* (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024), motivating cheap distillations ("Semantic Entropy Probes", Kossen et al., 2024). A 2025 KDD survey (Liu et al., 2025) flags computational cost as a key open problem, and a related 2025 paper (arXiv:2504.13677) shows UQ evaluations can be confounded by response length — underscoring the need for tightly controlled testbeds, checked against sampling noise.

What is less commonly reported, because it requires a controlled ground-truth setting, is a direct comparison of what each signal family is good *for* — discrimination (AUROC) versus calibration (ECE) — with enough repetition to separate real effects from sampling and training-run noise. Real LLM benchmarks confound this with unknown ground truth and opaque training data. We use a synthetic, deterministic task with a small from-scratch model, letting us (a) know ground truth exactly, (b) control capacity for a realistic error rate, and (c) cheaply retrain many independent model instances for a real sample size over *models*, not just test questions.

**Contributions.** (1) A minimal, fully offline, CPU-only testbed for comparing LLM uncertainty signals with exact ground truth. (2) A free single-pass signal (log-probability) beats 8-sample semantic entropy/self-consistency on point-estimate AUROC, but not significantly within one test set; validated across **15** independently trained models (14/15 wins, sign-test p=0.00049) *and* by a seed-level bootstrap on the mean AUROC gap (both 95% CIs exclude zero). (3) The reverse holds for calibration on average (self-consistency ~2.5x better ECE, seed-level CI excludes zero), but we show this calibration gap is not uniform: it shrinks and reverses at higher model accuracy (r=-0.86 with accuracy), while the discrimination gap does not depend on accuracy. (4) We argue for reporting cross-seed effect sizes (not just win counts) and accuracy-stratified results as the standard for this kind of comparison. All code and raw outputs (15 seeds, bootstrap scripts) are released.

## 2. Related Work

**Semantic entropy and sampling-based UQ.** Kuhn et al. (2023) introduced semantic entropy via bidirectional-entailment clustering of sampled generations; Farquhar et al. (2024, *Nature*) extended this to open-domain confabulation detection. Wang et al. (2022) proposed self-consistency (majority-vote agreement), also usable as an uncertainty signal via the agreement rate. Both require K ≥ several samples and, as we discuss in Sec. 5, are statistics of the *same* underlying sample pool in our setting — not fully independent comparison points.

**Cheap single-pass proxies.** Kossen et al. (2024) train linear probes on hidden states to approximate semantic entropy from one forward pass, requiring labeled probe-training data. Duan et al. (2023) and related token-level entropy/logprob approaches use generation probabilities directly, as our single-pass baselines do. We do not run these methods head-to-head in our testbed (Sec. 5).

**Surveys and evaluation critiques.** Liu et al. (2025, KDD) and a companion survey (arXiv:2412.05563) list computational cost and evaluation confounds as open problems. ArXiv:2504.13677 shows response-length differences can spuriously inflate UQ benchmarks, a concern our fixed-length numeric task sidesteps by construction. Semantic Energy (arXiv:2508.14496) argues semantic entropy under-uses logit information — consistent with our finding that log-probability is competitive with sampling-based clustering, though our cross-seed check shows this needs replication with effect sizes, not a sign test alone.

**Where we differ.** Prior comparisons are typically run once on large pretrained models over natural-language QA, with LLM-judge ground truth and no report of run-to-run variance. We isolate the discrimination-vs-calibration question on a synthetic, deterministic task with a small from-scratch model, trading ecological realism for full experimental control, statistical scrutiny (bootstrap CIs, 15-seed replication with effect sizes, and an accuracy-dependence check), and full reproducibility on a CPU budget.

## 3. Method

**Task.** Two-digit addition rendered as characters, e.g. `"41+67="` → `"108"`. Operands drawn uniformly from [1, 98]; answers range 2–196, minimal decimal strings, giving exact ground truth and near-uniform answer length regardless of correctness.

**Model.** A 2-layer, 4-head causal Transformer (d_model=48, ~0.1M parameters), 16-symbol vocabulary, trained from scratch with Adam (lr=3e-3), 600 steps, batch size 64, on freshly sampled problems. Deliberately small/under-trained so accuracy varies widely across seeds (8%–58%), giving a realistic and diverse correct/incorrect mix.

**Uncertainty signals**, computed per test question:
1. **First-token entropy** — Shannon entropy over the first answer character (1 forward pass).
2. **Mean log-probability** — length-normalized log-probability of the greedy-decoded answer.
3. **Self-consistency confidence** — K=8 samples at temperature 1.0, fraction agreeing with the modal parsed value.
4. **Semantic entropy** — Shannon entropy over distinct parsed integers among the same K=8 samples; exact-value clustering serves as semantic clustering here (numeric answers), a simplification of Kuhn et al.'s NLI-based method (Sec. 5).

**Evaluation.** (a) AUROC for wrong-vs-correct greedy answers, N=400, primary seed. (b) Paired bootstrap (2000 resamples over the 400 questions) for the primary-seed AUROC and ECE gaps. (c) **15-seed replication**: retrain the entire pipeline (fresh weight init, training stream, and 400-question test draw) for seeds 0–14, recording AUROC and ECE for all signals each time. We report both a sign test on win/loss counts and a bootstrap (5000 resamples over the 15 seed-level gaps) on the *mean* gap, giving an effect size with a CI, plus the Pearson correlation between per-seed accuracy and each gap to test accuracy-dependence. (d) Calibration via ECE (10 equal-width bins) and Brier score, both per-primary-seed and per-seed across all 15 retrainings. (e) A cost-accuracy sweep (K∈{1,...,8}, primary seed).

## 4. Results

Primary-seed training took 19.5s; full evaluation 19.6s; total 39.1s on a 12-core CPU laptop. Primary-seed greedy accuracy was 32.8%.

**Discrimination, primary seed:**

| Signal | Cost | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| First-token entropy | 1 pass | 0.524 |
| Mean log-probability (neg.) | 1 pass | 0.640 |
| Semantic entropy | K=8 | 0.614 |
| Self-consistency (neg.) | K=8 | 0.603 |

**Is the primary-seed gap real?** A paired bootstrap (2000 resamples) gives 95% CIs of [-0.038, 0.090] for logp−semantic-entropy and [-0.029, 0.100] for logp−self-consistency: both cross zero. At N=400 the single-run gap is not distinguishable from sampling noise.

**15-seed replication.** Retraining from scratch across seeds 0–14 (accuracy ranged 8.0%–57.5%) gives mean AUROC 0.640±0.073 (logp), 0.594±0.063 (semantic entropy), 0.584±0.059 (self-consistency). Log-probability wins in 14/15 seeds against each sampling-based signal (one-sided sign-test p=0.00049). The one exception, seed 6 (accuracy 12.5%), had logp AUROC 0.472 versus 0.537 (semantic entropy) and 0.572 (self-consistency) — a genuine ranking flip in a low-accuracy run, not noise we can explain away, and useful evidence that the effect is real-but-not-universal rather than deterministic.

Beyond the sign test, a seed-level bootstrap (5000 resamples over the 15 per-seed gaps) gives a mean AUROC gap of 0.046 (95% CI [0.025, 0.064]) vs. semantic entropy and 0.056 (95% CI [0.028, 0.078]) vs. self-consistency — both exclude zero, giving a magnitude, not just a direction, to the cross-seed effect. Since accuracy varies substantially across seeds, we also checked whether the discrimination gap tracks accuracy: correlation between per-seed accuracy and the AUROC gap is small (r=-0.094 vs. semantic entropy, r=-0.048 vs. self-consistency), so the logp advantage looks roughly uniform across the accuracy range we sampled, not an artifact of easier or harder training runs.

**Cost-accuracy sweep (primary seed).** As K grows 1→8, semantic-entropy AUROC rises 0.500→0.615, self-consistency 0.500→0.603, tracking closely at every K — expected, since both are statistics of the same K-sample pool (Sec. 5). Neither crosses the log-probability baseline (0.640) at any tested budget.

**Calibration.** On the primary seed, self-consistency is far better calibrated: ECE 0.024 vs. 0.290 for `exp(mean_logp)` (Brier 0.213 vs. 0.293), a gap confirmed by bootstrap (95% CI [0.177, 0.300]). Across all 15 seeds this holds *on average* — mean ECE 0.120 (self-consistency) vs. 0.300 (logp), seed-level bootstrap CI for the gap [0.109, 0.239] — but not uniformly: self-consistency was better calibrated in 12/15 seeds and *worse* in the 3 highest-accuracy seeds (54.0%, 56.0%, 57.5% accuracy), where logp-ECE (0.136, 0.153, 0.112) beat self-consistency-ECE (0.182, 0.173, 0.208). The correlation between per-seed accuracy and the ECE gap (ece_logp − ece_self-cons) is strongly negative, r=-0.86: as the model gets more accurate, self-consistency's calibration edge shrinks and eventually reverses. This is a genuinely new finding in this revision, not visible from the single-seed or 5-seed results, and it means the calibration recommendation should be read as accuracy-conditional rather than a blanket rule.

**Summary.** The discrimination advantage of log-probability is small per-seed but reproduces directionally (14/15, p=0.00049) and in magnitude (bootstrap CI excludes zero) across 15 independent retrainings, and does not depend on model accuracy. The calibration advantage of self-consistency is larger on average and also bootstrap-significant across seeds, but is accuracy-dependent and reverses at the high-accuracy end of our range — a distinction single-run or small-n reporting would miss entirely.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

Our testbed trades ecological validity for full experimental control and statistical scrutiny. Absolute numbers — a 0.1M-parameter model on synthetic arithmetic — should not be read as claims about GPT-4-class models on open-domain hallucination benchmarks; larger models with true NLI-based semantic clustering may show a different balance, and our "semantic entropy" is specifically the exact-match simplification, not Kuhn et al.'s full method. Semantic entropy and self-consistency are both computed from the same K=8 sample pool (distributional entropy vs. modal frequency of the same parsed values), so their near-identical AUROC trajectories in the cost-accuracy sweep, and their both losing to logp in 14/15 seeds, are one sampling-based data point measured two ways, not two independent replications — we weight the logp-vs-sampling comparison as a single result throughout, not two wins.

Two methodological lessons this revision surfaces. First, an earlier n=5 replication reported 5/5 sign-consistent wins and stopped there; expanding to 15 seeds both sharpened the effect (a seed-level bootstrap now gives an interval, not just a sign) and revealed a genuine counterexample (seed 6) that a smaller sample could easily have missed by chance — "replicates in n=15 with one informative exception" is a stronger claim than "replicates in n=5." Second, discrimination and calibration gaps need not behave alike across the same seeds: the AUROC gap was flat with respect to accuracy while the ECE gap was strongly accuracy-dependent (r=-0.86) and sign-reversing at the high end. A comparison run at a single accuracy level, as most single-checkpoint UQ evaluations effectively are, would see only one of these regimes and miss the interaction.

We still have not run a head-to-head against Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al., 2024) or Duan et al.'s token-level method — implementing SEPs requires a labeled probe-training procedure outside this revision's budget; this is the most direct remaining gap relative to our framing as a controlled comparison of the field's proxies. We also tested one task family, one small architecture, and one temperature/K setting; a second synthetic task and a small pretrained open-source model would further test whether the accuracy-dependence of calibration generalizes, but were outside our compute/time budget.

## 6. Conclusion

On a controlled, fully reproducible offline testbed, a free single-pass signal (log-probability) has higher point-estimate AUROC than 8-sample semantic entropy or self-consistency; this is not significant within a single 400-question test set, but across 15 independently retrained models it wins 14/15 times (sign-test p=0.00049) with a seed-level bootstrapped mean-gap CI that excludes zero and shows no dependence on model accuracy. Self-consistency is on average far better calibrated (mean ECE 0.120 vs. 0.300, seed-level CI excluding zero), but this advantage is itself accuracy-dependent and reverses at the highest-accuracy seeds we sampled — a nuance invisible without cross-seed replication. Uncertainty-signal choice should be driven by whether the application needs ranking or calibrated probabilities *and* by where the model sits on the accuracy spectrum; cheap log-probability baselines deserve to be reported alongside sampling-based methods; and such comparisons should be validated with cross-seed effect sizes and accuracy-stratified analysis, not single-run point estimates or bare win-count sign tests. We release the full training/evaluation code, all 15 seeds' raw results, bootstrap scripts, and logs so the comparison can be extended or challenged directly.
