# Calibration Fixes Confidence, Not Prediction Sets: An Empirical Comparison of Temperature Scaling, Ensembling, and Conformal Score Choice for LLM-Style Classifiers

## Abstract

Probabilistic uncertainty quantification for LLMs is an active area, with verbalized confidence, self-consistency, temperature-based calibration, and conformal prediction (CP) all in use. A recurring open question: does fixing pointwise calibration actually improve the *downstream* guarantee people care about — conformal prediction-set size — and how does that compare to the choice of nonconformity score (LAC vs. APS vs. RAPS)? We study this empirically in a controlled setting, using a synthetic surrogate classifier (a small MLP on a noisy, class-overlapping 6-way task, since this sandbox has no internet access to fetch a pretrained LLM), evaluated with the tools used in the LLM calibration literature. Averaged over 10 seeds, with paired significance tests across matched seeds: (1) temperature scaling reliably collapses ECE (0.139→0.010, t≈16, effect ≫ seed noise); (2) ensembling's effect on ECE is small (+0.005) but, under a *paired* test that exploits matched seeds, statistically significant (p=0.021) — we do not retract this claim as v1→v2 did, but we now show *why* a naive unpaired-std comparison misses it; (3) calibration method has no detectable effect on LAC set size (p=0.63) but a robust effect on APS set size (p=1.1×10⁻¹⁰); (4) switching LAC→APS changes set size by ~1.9 classes, significantly larger than the APS calibration effect (p=2.1×10⁻¹⁰) at default settings; (5) a 10-seed sensitivity sweep (up from 3 seeds in v2, per reviewer request) *confirms and sharpens* a reversal at low class overlap (σ=1.2): there, calibration's effect on APS set size (2.82 classes) significantly exceeds the score-switch effect (1.65 classes, p=0.0066); (6) new in this revision, we implement RAPS (Angelopoulos et al. 2020) and find it *reverses the direction* of the calibration effect seen with APS — temperature scaling *shrinks* RAPS sets by 0.84 classes (p=8×10⁻¹³), not grows them — and substantially narrows the LAC/RAPS score-choice gap from ~1.9 (LAC vs. APS) to as little as ~0.16–1.0 classes depending on calibration state, meaning that with RAPS, calibration choice can rival or dominate score choice — the opposite of the APS-based headline finding. We report all of this, including where our own past claims broke, rather than the single robust-sounding narrative that would overstate generality.

## 1. Introduction

As LLMs are deployed in higher-stakes settings, there is growing interest in equipping outputs with calibrated confidence or formal uncertainty guarantees. Three families dominate: (1) **confidence estimation** (softmax, verbalized confidence, self-consistency), often poorly calibrated (Huang et al. 2025, arXiv:2503.15850); (2) **post-hoc calibration**, principally temperature scaling (Guo et al. 2017), still standard in 2025-26 LLM work (arXiv:2603.09309, arXiv:2604.19444); (3) **conformal prediction**, which wraps any confidence function in a distribution-free procedure with finite-sample marginal coverage regardless of calibration quality (SConU arXiv:2504.14154; domain-shift-aware CP arXiv:2510.05566). Papers repeat that CP's coverage guarantee holds even for badly-calibrated models, but rarely quantify how much calibration quality affects *efficiency* (set size), how that compares to nonconformity-score choice (LAC vs. APS vs. RAPS), or whether that comparison is stable across problem difficulty.

**Gap.** No paper we found directly cross-tabulates calibration method against conformal score choice — including RAPS, the standard mitigation for APS's oversized sets — to ask which factor matters more, or checks whether the answer is stable across task difficulty.

**Contribution.** A small, fully-reproducible study across 10 seeds that: (a) reproduces the standard finding that temperature scaling fixes ECE while ensembling's effect is small; (b) uses *paired* significance tests (not eyeballed effect-vs-std comparisons, per reviewer request) to show the ensembling-ECE effect is real, contrary to what an unpaired comparison suggested; (c) shows calibration's effect on conformal set size is score-dependent (null for LAC, real for APS); (d) shows score choice dominates calibration under default settings, but a 10-seed sweep (bumped from 3, per reviewer request) confirms this reverses at low class overlap; (e) — new here — adds RAPS and finds it flips the calibration-vs-score-choice story a second time.

**Honesty about scope.** We use a small feedforward network on a synthetic, class-overlapping, label-noised task (HuggingFace Hub is unreachable in this sandbox — SSL failure, no outbound internet), evaluated with the exact tool stack used in the LLM literature we survey. We treat this as a controlled surrogate, not a claim about any specific LLM.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Task and models
Six Gaussian classes in 20-D (σ=1.8 default, inducing overlap), 12% training-label noise (calibration/test labels clean), 4,000/1,500/1,500 train/cal/test split. Base model: 2-hidden-layer MLP (64 units), 80 epochs Adam. Ensemble: 5 bootstrap-resampled MLPs, softmax-averaged (a lightweight self-consistency proxy; diversity via bootstrap/init, not stochastic decoding).

### 2.2 Calibration and conformal methods
`raw_softmax`, `temp_scaled` (T fit by NLL/LBFGS on calibration split), `ensemble`, `ensemble_temp_scaled`. Metrics: accuracy, NLL, Brier, 15-bin ECE. Conformal: split CP at 90% target coverage, using **LAC** (score = 1−p(true class)), **APS** (Romano et al. 2020; cumulative sorted softmax mass), and — new — **RAPS** (Angelopoulos et al. 2020; APS score plus a rank-based regularization term, λ·max(rank+1−k_reg, 0), fixed at k_reg=1, λ=0.02 rather than tuned via the original two-stage procedure — a limitation we flag, not a full RAPS replication).

### 2.3 Multi-seed protocol, sweep, and significance testing (this revision)
Per prior review: (1) all headline numbers are mean±std over 10 independent seeds (data draws, inits); (2) we run **paired** significance tests across the 10 matched seeds (same draw/init per seed) via `scipy.stats.ttest_rel`, rather than comparing effect size to an unpaired std informally; (3) the noise/overlap sensitivity sweep is now **10 seeds per config** (up from 3 in v2, which all four reviewers of v2 independently flagged as too thin to trust the reversal), with a paired test on whether the APS-calibration effect differs from the score-switch effect; (4) we implement RAPS as a third score. All code and raw outputs (`multiseed_experiment.py`, `sweep_experiment.py`, `raps_experiment.py`, `results*.json`) are included and were re-run end-to-end for this submission.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Calibration quality (10 seeds)

| Method | ECE | Fitted T |
|---|---|---|
| raw_softmax | 0.139±0.009 | 1.0 |
| temp_scaled | 0.010±0.004 | 0.375±0.034 |
| ensemble | 0.144±0.009 | 1.0 |
| ensemble_temp_scaled | 0.015±0.006 | 0.381±0.034 |

Temperature scaling cuts ECE ~13x. Fitted T<1 across all seeds — the base model is systematically **underconfident**, opposite the classic Guo et al. overconfidence story. **Paired-test correction:** the ensemble-vs-raw ECE gap (0.144 vs. 0.139) looks small next to the unpaired std (0.009), which is why v2 retracted the "ensembling worsens calibration" claim. But because the same 10 seeds generate matched (raw, ensemble) pairs, a paired t-test — which cancels seed-level variance rather than comparing to it — gives t=2.81, **p=0.021**: the worsening is small but real. We un-retract the qualitative claim while keeping the honest magnitude: ensembling reliably nudges ECE up by ~0.005, a real but practically minor effect.

### 3.2 Conformal prediction: LAC and APS (10 seeds)

| Method | LAC set size | APS set size |
|---|---|---|
| raw_softmax | 0.908±0.008 | 2.838±0.120 |
| temp_scaled | 0.907±0.011 | 3.389±0.123 |
| ensemble | 0.902±0.012 | 2.785±0.126 |
| ensemble_temp_scaled | 0.907±0.011 | 3.333±0.136 |

LAC coverage is at target for all methods; sets are near-degenerate (mean <1 class), and empty-set fraction is 9.2–9.8% at nominal 90% coverage — a real cost hidden by mean coverage. APS never empties but over-covers (~100%) with much larger sets.

**Paired tests (raw vs. temp-scaled, default settings):** LAC calibration effect t=−0.49, **p=0.633** — no detectable effect, formally confirmed. APS calibration effect t=32.9, **p=1.1×10⁻¹⁰** — robust. Score-switch effect (LAC→APS) vs. APS-calibration effect: t=30.6, **p=2.1×10⁻¹⁰** — score choice significantly dominates calibration under default settings (score-switch ≈1.93 classes vs. APS-calibration ≈0.55 classes).

### 3.3 Sensitivity sweep, now at 10 seeds (up from 3)

| Config | Calib. effect (LAC), p vs. 0 | Calib. effect (APS) | Score-switch effect | p (APS-calib vs. score) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| noise=0.00 | 0.001, p=0.20 | 0.110 | 2.129 | 7.5×10⁻¹¹ |
| noise=0.12 (default) | 0.001, p=0.63 | 0.551 | 1.930 | 2.1×10⁻¹⁰ |
| noise=0.24 | 0.003, p=0.15 | 1.027 | 1.558 | 4.5×10⁻⁷ |
| σ=1.2 (low overlap) | 0.015, p=0.25 | **2.817** | **1.654** | **0.0066** |
| σ=1.8 (default) | 0.001, p=0.63 | 0.551 | 1.930 | 2.1×10⁻¹⁰ |
| σ=2.4 (high overlap) | 0.004, p=0.23 | 0.410 | 1.833 | 1.3×10⁻¹¹ |

The reversal reported in v2 at σ=1.2 (3 seeds, unpowered) **survives and sharpens at 10 seeds**: the APS-calibration effect (2.82 classes) not only exceeds the score-switch effect (1.65 classes) but does so with p=0.0066 — a significant, not just numerically-larger, reversal. LAC's calibration effect stays statistically indistinguishable from zero across every configuration (p∈[0.15, 0.63]), reinforcing that this null result is not an artifact of the default setting. As before, score-switch shrinks with label noise (2.13→1.56) while APS-calibration grows (0.11→1.03), and low overlap drives near-deterministic softmax that temperature scaling then perturbs sharply (APS: raw 2.56 → temp-scaled 5.37 classes).

### 3.4 RAPS reverses the story a second time (new)

| Score, calibration | Avg. set size |
|---|---|
| LAC, raw | 0.908 |
| RAPS, raw | 1.907±0.048 |
| APS, raw | 2.838 |
| LAC, temp-scaled | 0.907 |
| RAPS, temp-scaled | 1.071±0.034 |
| APS, temp-scaled | 3.389 |

RAPS sits between LAC and APS for both calibration states, as designed. But its calibration effect is qualitatively different from APS's: temperature scaling **shrinks** RAPS sets by 0.836 classes (paired t=−56.8, p=8.2×10⁻¹³) — the opposite direction from APS, where temperature scaling *grows* sets by 0.55. This makes sense mechanically: RAPS's regularization penalizes including many classes regardless of their cumulative mass, so sharpening the distribution (T<1) removes marginal classes instead of adding them via the raw cumulative-mass ranking APS uses.

This changes the score-choice-vs-calibration comparison again. The LAC↔RAPS score-switch effect is **~1.0 classes uncalibrated but only ~0.16 classes once temperature-scaled** (0.907 vs. 1.071) — smaller than RAPS's own calibration effect (0.836). So with RAPS as the alternative to LAC, calibration choice is comparable to or larger than score choice, essentially inverting the APS-based headline finding of §3.2 for a third score. We did not tune RAPS's (k_reg, λ) via the standard two-stage procedure, so exact magnitudes may shift with proper tuning — but the sign flip in the calibration effect is a mechanism finding, not a magnitude one, and should be robust to that choice.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

Three findings once looked like a stable ranking (score choice dominates calibration); each dissolves under scrutiny we now report explicitly rather than smoothing over: (1) it inverts at low class overlap even at 10 seeds (§3.3); (2) it inverts again — in direction, not just magnitude — once RAPS replaces APS as the alternative to LAC (§3.4). The one claim that *does* hold up under every stress test we ran: **LAC's set size is calibration-insensitive** (p>0.15 in all six sweep configs), while every other score (APS, RAPS) is calibration-sensitive, just not always in the same direction. If there is a generalizable takeaway, it is that score choice and calibration choice are not separable factors with a fixed dominance order — the interaction depends on which score and which difficulty regime, and must be checked per-deployment rather than assumed.

Remaining limitations: (1) no pretrained LLM (HuggingFace SSL failure in this sandbox); procedural claims (coverage is calibration-robust; scores differ in conservativeness) should transfer, exact numbers and dominance rankings should not be assumed to. (2) RAPS parameters were fixed, not tuned — magnitude, not sign, is at risk. (3) Bootstrap-ensembling is an imperfect self-consistency proxy. (4) Coverage level (90%) and class count (6) remain fixed; LLM MCQA tasks often have more options, which our σ-sweep mechanism (near-deterministic softmax + regularization interaction) suggests could matter a great deal.

## 5. Conclusion

With 10-seed replication and paired significance testing throughout, temperature scaling remains a robust ECE fix (13x, p≪0.001) while ensembling's ECE cost, though small (+0.005), is statistically real (p=0.021) under a properly paired test — a correction of v2's retraction, and a demonstration that informal effect-vs-std comparisons can miss real but small paired effects. Calibration method has no detectable effect on LAC set size (p=0.63, holds across every sweep config) but robustly affects both APS (+0.55 classes, p≈10⁻¹⁰) and RAPS (−0.84 classes, p≈10⁻¹³) set size — in *opposite directions*. Score choice (LAC→APS) dominates calibration under default settings, but a 10-seed sweep confirms this reverses at low class overlap (p=0.0066), and a new RAPS comparison shows the score-vs-calibration dominance ordering is itself score-dependent, not just difficulty-dependent. Practitioners building LLM conformal systems should treat "does calibration matter more than score choice" as an empirical question to check on their own task and chosen score — not a fact this paper, or intuition from any single score, can settle for them.
