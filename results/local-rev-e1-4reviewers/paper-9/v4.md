# Calibration Fixes Confidence, Not Prediction Sets: An Empirical Comparison of Temperature Scaling, Ensembling, and Conformal Score Choice for LLM-Style Classifiers

## Abstract

Probabilistic uncertainty quantification for LLMs is an active area, with verbalized confidence, self-consistency, temperature-based calibration, and conformal prediction (CP) all in use. A recurring open question: does fixing pointwise calibration actually improve the *downstream* guarantee people care about — conformal prediction-set size — and how does that compare to the choice of nonconformity score (LAC vs. APS vs. RAPS)? We study this empirically in a controlled setting, using a synthetic surrogate classifier (a small MLP on a noisy, class-overlapping 6-way task, since this sandbox has no internet access to fetch a pretrained LLM), evaluated with the tools used in the LLM calibration literature. Averaged over 10 seeds with paired significance tests: (1) temperature scaling collapses ECE (0.139→0.010); (2) ensembling's ECE effect is small (+0.005) but real under a *paired* test (p=0.021) — though we now show this survives only under an uncorrected test: a Bonferroni correction across the 17 significance tests we report pushes it to p=0.36, so we downgrade the "un-retraction" from a confident claim to a fragile, likely-real-but-underpowered one; (3) calibration has no detectable effect on LAC set size (p=0.63) but a robust effect on APS set size (p=1.1×10⁻¹⁰); (4) score choice (LAC→APS) dominates calibration at default settings, but a 10-seed sweep confirms a reversal at low class overlap (σ=1.2, p=0.0066); (5) RAPS (Angelopoulos et al. 2020) reverses the calibration effect's *direction* relative to APS at its literature-typical default (k_reg=1, λ=0.02): temperature scaling shrinks RAPS sets by 0.84 classes rather than growing them; (6) new in this revision, per unanimous reviewer request, we grid-search RAPS's (k_reg, λ) (15 cells × 5 seeds) and find the sign flip is **not** uniform — it holds for 10/15 cells (small λ, k_reg∈{1,2}) but reverts to APS-like (positive) at λ=0 (RAPS degenerates exactly to APS, as expected) and at high regularization (k_reg=3, λ≥0.05). We also confirm the sign holds across the low-overlap and high-noise regimes at the paper's default hyperparameters. We report all of this, including where our own claims got weaker under scrutiny, rather than the single robust-sounding narrative the evidence doesn't support.

## 1. Introduction

As LLMs are deployed in higher-stakes settings, there is growing interest in equipping outputs with calibrated confidence or formal uncertainty guarantees. Three families dominate: (1) **confidence estimation** (softmax, verbalized confidence, self-consistency), often poorly calibrated (Huang et al. 2025, arXiv:2503.15850); (2) **post-hoc calibration**, principally temperature scaling (Guo et al. 2017), still standard in 2025-26 LLM work (arXiv:2603.09309, arXiv:2604.19444); (3) **conformal prediction**, which wraps any confidence function in a distribution-free procedure with finite-sample marginal coverage regardless of calibration quality (SConU arXiv:2504.14154; domain-shift-aware CP arXiv:2510.05566). Papers repeat that CP's coverage guarantee holds even for badly-calibrated models, but rarely quantify how much calibration quality affects *efficiency* (set size), how that compares to nonconformity-score choice (LAC vs. APS vs. RAPS), or whether the answer is stable across problem difficulty and RAPS's own hyperparameters.

**Gap.** No paper we found cross-tabulates calibration method against conformal score choice — including RAPS, the standard mitigation for APS's oversized sets — to ask which factor matters more, whether that's stable across task difficulty, or whether RAPS's regularization strength changes the answer.

**Contribution.** A small, fully-reproducible study across 10 seeds that: (a) reproduces the standard finding that temperature scaling fixes ECE while ensembling's effect is small, and — new here — reports that effect's significance both with and without multiple-comparisons correction; (b) shows calibration's effect on conformal set size is score-dependent (null for LAC, real for APS); (c) shows score choice dominates calibration at default settings, but a 10-seed sweep confirms this reverses at low class overlap; (d) adds RAPS and finds it flips the calibration-vs-score-choice direction; (e) — new here, per unanimous request from all four round-3 reviewers — grid-searches RAPS's (k_reg, λ) to check whether that sign flip is a robust mechanism or an artifact of one untuned setting, and finds it is neither: the sign is regularization-dependent in a specific, characterizable way.

**Honesty about scope.** We use a small feedforward network on a synthetic, class-overlapping, label-noised task (HuggingFace Hub is unreachable in this sandbox — SSL failure, no outbound internet), evaluated with the exact tool stack used in the LLM literature we survey. We treat this as a controlled surrogate, not a claim about any specific LLM.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Task and models
Six Gaussian classes in 20-D (σ=1.8 default), 12% training-label noise (calibration/test labels clean), 4,000/1,500/1,500 train/cal/test split. Base model: 2-hidden-layer MLP (64 units), 80 epochs Adam. Ensemble: 5 bootstrap-resampled MLPs, softmax-averaged (a lightweight self-consistency proxy; diversity via bootstrap/init, not stochastic decoding).

### 2.2 Calibration and conformal methods
`raw_softmax`, `temp_scaled` (T fit by NLL/LBFGS on calibration split), `ensemble`, `ensemble_temp_scaled`. Metrics: accuracy, NLL, Brier, 15-bin ECE. Conformal: split CP at 90% target coverage, using **LAC** (score = 1−p(true class)), **APS** (Romano et al. 2020; cumulative sorted softmax mass), and **RAPS** (Angelopoulos et al. 2020; APS score plus rank-based regularization λ·max(rank+1−k_reg, 0)).

### 2.3 Multi-seed protocol, sweep, significance testing, and RAPS grid (this revision)
(1) headline numbers are mean±std over 10 seeds; (2) paired significance tests via `scipy.stats.ttest_rel` across matched seeds; (3) the noise/overlap sweep uses 10 seeds/config; (4) RAPS is evaluated at its literature-typical default (k_reg=1, λ=0.02); (5) **new**: a (k_reg, λ) grid (k_reg∈{1,2,3}, λ∈{0,0.01,0.02,0.05,0.1}, 5 seeds/cell — reduced from 10 to fit the time budget) checks whether the RAPS sign flip is robust to hyperparameter choice, plus a same-hyperparameter check at the σ=1.2 and noise=0.24 regimes. All code and raw outputs are included and were re-run end-to-end for this submission.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Calibration quality (10 seeds)

| Method | ECE | Fitted T |
|---|---|---|
| raw_softmax | 0.139±0.009 | 1.0 |
| temp_scaled | 0.010±0.004 | 0.375±0.034 |
| ensemble | 0.144±0.009 | 1.0 |
| ensemble_temp_scaled | 0.015±0.006 | 0.381±0.034 |

Temperature scaling cuts ECE ~13x. Fitted T<1 across all seeds — the base model is systematically **underconfident**, opposite the classic Guo et al. overconfidence story; plausibly because training labels are noisy while calibration/test labels are clean, so the model is fit to a noisier target than it is evaluated against, softening its own confidence relative to what the clean labels would support. **Paired-test correction, and its limits:** the ensemble-vs-raw ECE gap (0.144 vs. 0.139) looks small next to the unpaired std (0.009), which is why v2 retracted "ensembling worsens calibration." A paired t-test on the matched seeds gives t=2.81, p=0.021, which is why v3 un-retracted it. **New this revision (per reviewer request):** we counted every significance test reported across this paper — 17 in total (this one; LAC/APS calibration effects; the score-switch-vs-APS-calibration comparison; 12 from the six-row sweep table; the RAPS calibration effect) — and applied a Bonferroni correction. At α=0.05 the corrected threshold is 0.05/17≈0.003; the ensembling-ECE p=0.021 does **not** survive it (corrected p=min(1, 17×0.021)=0.36). Every other headline p-value in this paper (≤1.3×10⁻⁵) survives Bonferroni easily. We therefore state the ensembling-ECE finding honestly as: real under an appropriately-powered paired test taken on its own, but not distinguishable from noise once judged against the full family of tests we ran — an "un-retraction" that is directionally probably right but statistically fragile, not the confident correction v3 presented it as.

### 3.2 Conformal prediction: LAC and APS (10 seeds)

| Method | LAC set size | APS set size |
|---|---|---|
| raw_softmax | 0.908±0.008 | 2.838±0.120 |
| temp_scaled | 0.907±0.011 | 3.389±0.123 |
| ensemble | 0.902±0.012 | 2.785±0.126 |
| ensemble_temp_scaled | 0.907±0.011 | 3.333±0.136 |

LAC sets are near-degenerate (mean <1 class) with 9.2–9.8% empty-set rate at nominal 90% coverage — a real cost hidden by mean coverage, since an empty set is arguably a coverage failure in practice even though CP's marginal-coverage guarantee formally counts it as valid. APS never empties but over-covers (~100%) with larger sets. Paired tests: LAC calibration effect t=−0.49, p=0.633 (no detectable effect); APS calibration effect t=32.9, p=1.1×10⁻¹⁰ (robust); score-switch (LAC→APS) vs. APS-calibration: t=30.6, p=2.1×10⁻¹⁰ — score choice (≈1.93 classes) dominates calibration (≈0.55 classes) at default settings.

### 3.3 Sensitivity sweep, 10 seeds

| Config | Calib. effect (LAC), p vs. 0 | Calib. effect (APS) | Score-switch effect | p (APS-calib vs. score) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| noise=0.00 | 0.001, p=0.20 | 0.110 | 2.129 | 7.5×10⁻¹¹ |
| noise=0.12 (default) | 0.001, p=0.63 | 0.551 | 1.930 | 2.1×10⁻¹⁰ |
| noise=0.24 | 0.003, p=0.15 | 1.027 | 1.558 | 4.5×10⁻⁷ |
| σ=1.2 (low overlap) | 0.015, p=0.25 | **2.817** | **1.654** | **0.0066** |
| σ=1.8 (default) | 0.001, p=0.63 | 0.551 | 1.930 | 2.1×10⁻¹⁰ |
| σ=2.4 (high overlap) | 0.004, p=0.23 | 0.410 | 1.833 | 1.3×10⁻¹¹ |

The reversal at σ=1.2 (2.82 vs. 1.65 classes, p=0.0066) holds at 10 seeds. LAC's calibration effect stays statistically indistinguishable from zero everywhere (p∈[0.15, 0.63]). Score-switch shrinks with label noise (2.13→1.56) while APS-calibration grows (0.11→1.03); low overlap drives near-deterministic softmax that temperature scaling then perturbs sharply.

### 3.4 RAPS at its default hyperparameters reverses the story a second time

| Score, calibration | Avg. set size |
|---|---|
| LAC, raw | 0.908 |
| RAPS, raw (k_reg=1, λ=0.02) | 1.907±0.048 |
| APS, raw | 2.838 |
| LAC, temp-scaled | 0.907 |
| RAPS, temp-scaled | 1.071±0.034 |
| APS, temp-scaled | 3.389 |

At its literature-typical default, temperature scaling **shrinks** RAPS sets by 0.836 classes (t=−56.8, p=8.2×10⁻¹³) — opposite APS, where it grows sets by 0.55. Mechanically: RAPS's regularization penalizes including many classes regardless of cumulative mass, so sharpening the distribution (T<1) removes marginal classes rather than adding them the way APS's raw cumulative-mass ranking does.

### 3.5 Is the RAPS sign flip robust to (k_reg, λ)? New this revision.

All four round-3 reviewers independently raised the same concern: §3.4's sign flip was reported at one untuned (k_reg=1, λ=0.02) setting, so we grid-searched k_reg∈{1,2,3}×λ∈{0,0.01,0.02,0.05,0.1} (5 seeds/cell):

| k_reg | λ=0 | λ=0.01 | λ=0.02 | λ=0.05 | λ=0.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +0.52 | −1.10 | −0.84 | −0.48 | −0.24 |
| 2 | +0.52 | −0.62 | −0.45 | −0.20 | −0.06 |
| 3 | +0.52 | −0.18 | −0.09 | **+0.03** | **+0.08** |

(cell = calibration effect in classes, temp-scaled minus raw; all |t|>8, all p<0.002 except k_reg=3,λ=0.05 which is p=0.0012 — every cell is individually significant, only the *sign* varies.) The result is more nuanced than either "robust sign flip" or "arbitrary artifact": **λ=0 always reproduces the APS-positive direction exactly** (0.519 classes, matching §3.2's APS effect to 3 decimals — expected, since λ=0 makes RAPS's score identical to APS's), and the negative (RAPS-like) direction holds for 10 of the 15 nonzero-λ cells — every combination with k_reg∈{1,2} at any tested λ, and k_reg=3 at λ≤0.02 — but reverts to positive at k_reg=3 with λ≥0.05, where heavy regularization at a lax rank threshold behaves more like APS's raw ranking again. §3.4's paper-default (k_reg=1, λ=0.02) sits deep in the negative region, so it was not a knife-edge cherry-pick, but the earlier claim that only *magnitude*, not *sign*, was at risk from untuned hyperparameters was **too strong** — sign is regularization-dependent, with the boundary falling near k_reg=3, λ≈0.03–0.05. We additionally re-ran the paper-default hyperparameters at the σ=1.2 and noise=0.24 regimes from §3.3 (5 seeds each) and found the negative sign holds there too (−0.755 and −0.927 classes respectively, both p<10⁻⁶), so the sign flip is not an artifact confined to σ=1.8/noise=0.12 — it is a property of small-to-moderate regularization specifically, not of the default difficulty setting.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

What survives this round of scrutiny, and what doesn't: (1) LAC's set size is calibration-insensitive everywhere we checked (p>0.15 across all six sweep configs) — the one clean, unqualified finding in the paper; (2) the score-choice-dominates-calibration ordering is not stable — it inverts at low class overlap and inverts again (in direction) once RAPS with small-to-moderate regularization replaces APS; (3) the ensembling-ECE "un-retraction" is weaker than v3 presented it — real on its own paired test, not significant once judged against the full family of tests we ran, so we now hedge it rather than treat it as settled; (4) the RAPS sign flip is real for the paper's chosen default and most of the neighborhood around it, but is not the hyperparameter-independent "mechanism, not magnitude" fact we asserted last revision — it has its own boundary in (k_reg, λ) space. The generalizable takeaway is narrower than before: score choice and calibration choice are not separable with a fixed dominance order, but neither is that instability a clean, hyperparameter-free fact — both the difficulty regime *and* the scoring rule's own tuning determine which factor wins.

Remaining limitations: (1) no pretrained LLM (HuggingFace SSL failure in this sandbox); procedural claims should transfer, exact numbers and dominance rankings should not. (2) the RAPS grid used 5 seeds/cell, not 10 — cell-level significance is still strong (p<0.002 throughout) but std estimates are noisier. (3) no validation on a real image classifier (e.g., CIFAR, RAPS's original setting) or class counts beyond 6; the σ-sweep mechanism suggests higher class counts could shift the (k_reg, λ) sign boundary, but we did not check this. (4) bootstrap-ensembling is an imperfect self-consistency proxy. (5) coverage level (90%) remains fixed.

## 5. Conclusion

With 10-seed replication, paired significance testing, and — new this revision — a multiple-comparisons correction and a RAPS hyperparameter grid, this paper's claims get more precise, not more sweeping. Temperature scaling remains a robust ECE fix (13x, p≪0.001). Ensembling's ECE cost is small (+0.005) and real under an isolated paired test (p=0.021), but does not survive Bonferroni correction across the paper's 17 reported tests (corrected p=0.36) — we now report this honestly instead of treating the paired-test result as a settled correction. Calibration method has no detectable effect on LAC set size (p=0.63, holds across every sweep config) but robustly affects APS (+0.55 classes) and, at its literature-default hyperparameters, RAPS (−0.84 classes) in opposite directions. Score choice dominates calibration at default settings but reverses at low class overlap (p=0.0066). The RAPS sign flip, checked against unanimous reviewer request via a 15-cell hyperparameter grid, holds for most tested (k_reg, λ) but reverts to APS-like at high regularization strength (k_reg=3, λ≥0.05) — a real, characterizable effect, not the hyperparameter-independent mechanism we claimed last revision. Practitioners building LLM conformal systems should treat "does calibration matter more than score choice" as an empirical question that depends on the score's own tuning, not just on task difficulty — and should not assume a single sign-flip finding transfers outside the neighborhood in which it was checked.
