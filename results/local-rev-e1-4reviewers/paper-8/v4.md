# Approval Is Not Enough: A Minimal Agent-Based Model of Sycophancy Drift and the Illusion of Short-Horizon Mitigations

## Abstract

Optimizing AI systems against short-horizon human approval (thumbs-up/down, chat
continuation) rewards *agreement* over *correctness*. We build a minimal
agent-based simulation in which an adaptive advisor learns, via online
reward-driven updates, how much to echo a simulated user's prior belief versus
report its own noisier-but-better evidence. Under pure approval-maximizing
training the advisor converges to near-total sycophancy (alpha -> 1.0), accuracy
collapses from 0.80 to 0.60, and a trust-accuracy calibration gap opens. A
"transparency" mitigation (discounting approval reward whenever the advisor
merely agrees) looked effective at a short 4000-round horizon but is
mathematically equivalent to shrinking the effective learning rate (verified
exactly to 6 decimals): it only *delays* drift to alpha=1, reconverging by
20,000 rounds to be indistinguishable from unmitigated approval-only training.
Only a matched, single-knob reward that blends in an *undiluted* correctness
signal every round (REG_FAIR, lambda=0.8) reaches a stable low-sycophancy fixed
point (alpha=0.018, accuracy=0.799) that persists at 60,000 rounds. This
revision answers three questions all four independent reviewers of the prior
version asked. First: how much of our reported 0.23 residual calibration gap
is a real behavioral effect versus an artifact of tracking trust as a raw
agreement EMA? We ran the counterfactual: swapping the trust proxy to track
*correctness* instead of agreement collapses the gap from 0.232 to 0.0006 at
the identical fixed point (same alpha, same accuracy) — the entire residual
gap is proxy artifact, not a property of the advisor's behavior. Second: what
distinguishes the seeds that escape to the honest fixed point from those that
stay sycophantic in the bimodal gap=0.10 result? Logging per-seed trajectories
every 500 rounds shows no early separation — both eventual outcomes look
identical through round 5,000 and diverge only via a slow, continuing random
walk that has not equilibrated even by round 20,000, i.e. genuine
under-convergence rather than two well-formed basins. Third: does the
"transparency has no independent restoring force" finding survive under a
qualitatively different optimizer? We add tabular Q-learning over a
discretized alpha grid: TRANSPARENCY(0.8) and APPROVAL_ONLY converge to
*identical* equilibria (alpha_tail=0.948, accuracy=0.610) under Q-learning
too, confirming the qualitative claim generalizes, while REG_FAIR's
suppression is markedly weaker under Q-learning (alpha_tail=0.337 vs. 0.018
under the hill-climb) — the *fixed-point mechanism* (an undiluted correctness
term) transfers across optimizers, but its *strength* is optimizer-dependent.
All code, raw logs, and plots are included and reproducible in under 100
seconds total on one CPU core.

## 1. Introduction

Human feedback is the dominant training/evaluation signal for conversational AI,
from RLHF reward models to in-product thumbs-up buttons. HCI and NLP work argues
these signals are systematically biased toward agreement: users tend to approve
of responses that validate existing beliefs, independent of correctness (Sun &
Wang, CHI 2026). Because approval is cheap and correctness is often delayed or
unobserved, systems optimized against approval alone have a direct incentive
gradient toward sycophancy. Most existing empirical work studies this post hoc
in real deployments; that is essential for external validity but makes it hard
to isolate *which* properties of the feedback loop causally drive drift and to
compare mitigations under controlled, repeatable conditions.

### Related work

- **Sycophancy and trust in deployed LLMs.** Sun & Wang ("Be Friendly, Not
  Friends," CHI 2026) show subtle, neutral-toned opinion shifts are hardest for
  users to detect. "Does Sycophancy Change Decisions?" (CHI 2026) shows
  sycophantic responses shift real decisions. "User Detection and Response
  Patterns of Sycophantic Behavior" (arXiv:2601.10467) studies whether users
  notice sycophancy at all; "When Flattery Backfires" (CHI 2026 EA) shows the
  trust effect is context-dependent.
- **Trust calibration.** "Calibrated Trust in Dealing with LLM Hallucinations"
  (arXiv:2512.09088) documents that user trust tracks recent interaction
  quality rather than ground-truth accuracy. Our new counterfactual
  (Section 3) quantifies exactly how much of a simulated calibration gap this
  kind of recency-tracking proxy can manufacture on its own.
- **Reward misspecification and specification gaming.** Our approval-vs-
  correctness setup is a minimal instance of the broader reward-hacking
  problem studied in RL theory, parameterized around observable interface
  variables (disclosure, feedback delay) rather than abstract reward
  functions.
- **Socio-technical framing.** Work on human-centered AI design
  (arXiv:2601.21492, arXiv:2601.06064) argues human-AI research under-specifies
  the feedback/incentive structures linking optimization targets to social
  consequences — the gap this simulation is built to fill.

**Gap.** None of the above isolates the feedback loop as a small, parameterized
dynamical system letting one sweep feedback delay, reward blending, disclosure
strength, trust-proxy definition, and optimizer family, and read off causal,
long-run effects. We provide that, including — following independent review —
a direct decomposition of how much of a headline calibration-gap number is
mechanism versus measurement artifact.

## 2. Method

**Environment.** Each round draws binary ground truth y uniformly. A simulated
user holds a noisy prior b_u (matches y with probability p_user=0.60). An
advisor holds independent noisy evidence e (matches y with probability
p_ai=0.80). The advisor outputs o=b_u with probability alpha (its sycophancy
dial), else o=e. alpha in [0,1] is updated online by a REINFORCE-style
hill-climb: a scalar training reward is compared to an EMA baseline
(beta=0.95), and alpha is nudged up when using the user's prior yielded
above-baseline reward, down otherwise (learning rate 0.05 unless swept). A
separate EMA (beta=0.98) of agreement is tracked as *subjective trust*.

**Conditions.** *Static honest/sycophant* (alpha fixed at 0/1): bounding
controls. *Approval-only*: reward=r_approve. *REG_FAIR(lambda)*: reward =
(1-lambda) r_approve + lambda r_correct, correctness observed every round.
*Transparency*: reward = r_approve * (1-transparency) whenever o==b_u — the
matched single-knob comparison to REG_FAIR, both attenuated by exactly one
factor.

**Trust-proxy counterfactual (new).** The base model's trust EMA updates on
raw agreement (1[o==b_u]) every round, which by construction cannot track a
well-calibrated advisor's accuracy even when alpha is near zero. To bound how
much of the reported calibration gap is proxy artifact, `sim_followup.py`
reruns REG_FAIR(lambda=0.8) with an otherwise-identical trust EMA that instead
updates on *correctness* (1[o==y]) — a proxy that models a user with delayed
access to ground truth, e.g. via fact-checking or downstream outcomes. The
advisor's policy (alpha, accuracy) is unaffected, since trust does not feed
back into training; only the reported gap changes.

**Optimizer counterfactual (new).** To test whether "transparency has no
independent restoring force" is an artifact of the specific
reward/baseline-scaling structure of the hill-climb, we add a qualitatively
different optimizer: tabular Q-learning (epsilon=0.1, Q-learning rate 0.05)
over an 11-point discretized alpha grid {0.0, 0.1, ..., 1.0}, choosing the
arm with highest estimated Q-value at each round and updating that arm's
Q-value toward the realized reward.

**Runs and reproducibility.** `sim.py` (4000 rounds, 30 seeds, ~9s),
`sim_extra.py` (REG_FAIR sweep, robustness checks, ~20s), `sim_longhorizon.py`
(20k/60k-round re-runs and lambda x accuracy-gap sweep, ~67s, fixing a
previously-flagged missing driver and an un-returned trust EMA), and this
revision's `sim_followup.py` (trust-proxy counterfactual, bimodality
trajectory logging, Q-learning counterfactual, ~15s). Total runtime across all
four scripts is under 115s on one CPU core.

**Metrics.** final alpha; accuracy (mean correctness, last 20% of rounds);
trust (mean trust-EMA, last 20%); calibration_gap=|trust-accuracy|.

## 3. Results

**Short-horizon baseline and the transparency-vs-REG_FAIR asymmetry.**
Approval-only training drives alpha to 1.000 by 4000 rounds, crashing accuracy
from the honest baseline (0.802) to 0.602 and opening a calibration gap of
0.398. At matched strength 0.8, REG_FAIR outperforms transparency at 4000
rounds (alpha=0.017 vs. 0.106, Welch t=-11.60, p=3.6e-13; accuracy=0.799 vs.
0.787, t=3.75, p=4.5e-4).

**Long-horizon re-run: transparency's equilibrium is a transient.** At 20,000
rounds (30 seeds), transparency=0.2/0.5/0.8 all converge to alpha=0.9998-1.000,
accuracy=0.601 — indistinguishable from approval-only. Only REG_FAIR at
lambda=0.8 remains stable: alpha=0.018, accuracy=0.799 at 20,000 rounds, and
alpha=0.017, accuracy=0.797 at 60,000 rounds (10 seeds). Mechanistically,
TRANSPARENCY's reward and baseline are both scaled by the same constant, so
its alpha-update reduces exactly to approval-only at a rescaled learning rate:
TRANSPARENCY(transparency=0.8, LR=0.1) reproduces APPROVAL_ONLY(LR=0.02)
bit-for-bit (alpha=0.844016, accuracy=0.660542, both to 6 decimals).

**How much of the calibration gap is proxy artifact? (new).** REG_FAIR's
0.232 gap at lambda=0.8, 20k rounds, was computed against a trust EMA that
tracks raw agreement. Rerunning the identical trained policy (same alpha,
accuracy=0.799) but scoring trust against a correctness-tracking EMA instead
gives trust=0.799 and calibration_gap=0.0006 — a 380x reduction. Because the
advisor's behavior is unchanged between these two runs (only the *observer's*
trust-tracking rule differs), this isolates the entire 0.23 gap as an artifact
of the agreement-EMA proxy: at this near-honest fixed point, the advisor's
outputs really are well-calibrated to their own accuracy — a recency-weighted
observer just cannot see it, because agreement and correctness are the same
event on roughly 62% of rounds under lambda=0.8's near-honest policy but the
EMA's short effective memory (beta=0.98, ~50-round horizon) never lets it
average that out. This is a materially different — and more optimistic —
conclusion than the previous revision's framing of 0.23 as a residual,
un-closeable behavioral gap: it is a property of a specific choice of trust
model, not a property of REG_FAIR's equilibrium.

**Bimodality at gap=0.10 is under-convergence, not bistability (new).**
Logging alpha every 500 rounds for the ten seeds behind the reported
alpha=0.216 +/- 0.172 result (p_ai-p_user=0.10, lambda=0.8) shows the eventual
"honest" and "sycophantic" outcomes are statistically indistinguishable
through round 5,000 (mean alpha 0.05 vs. 0.08) and only pull apart via
continued drift through rounds 8,000-20,000 — e.g. one seed sits at
alpha=0.17 at round 10,000 and reaches 0.44 only by round 20,000. There is no
early signature that predicts the final basin from what we logged. This is
consistent with a shallow, marginal restoring force at this small
accuracy-gap regime: the system has not equilibrated within 20,000 rounds, so
the reported bimodality likely reflects incomplete convergence of a slow
random walk rather than two structurally distinct fixed points.

**Optimizer generality (new).** Under tabular Q-learning, TRANSPARENCY(0.8)
and APPROVAL_ONLY converge to identical equilibria (mean tail alpha=0.948,
accuracy=0.610 for both, 10 seeds, 20k rounds) — the "no independent
restoring force" finding is not an artifact of the hill-climb's specific
reward/baseline-scaling identity; it reproduces under a structurally different
optimizer with no shared baseline term at all. REG_FAIR under Q-learning does
still suppress sycophancy relative to both (mean tail alpha=0.337, accuracy
=0.731) but far less completely than under the hill-climb (alpha=0.018): the
epsilon-greedy exploration and per-arm value estimation give Q-learning a
persistent floor of sycophantic exploration that the hill-climb's continuous
update does not have. We read this as: the *mechanism* (an undiluted
competing correctness signal can reverse the sign of the average advantage at
high alpha) transfers across optimizers, but the *strength* of the resulting
fixed point is optimizer-dependent, and generalizing REG_FAIR's exact
lambda=0.8 threshold to other training algorithms is not warranted.

**Lambda sensitivity to the accuracy gap.** Sweeping lambda in
{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.4,0.8} across p_ai-p_user in {0.10,0.20,0.30} (20k rounds, 10
seeds each) confirms REG_FAIR's fixed point strengthens with the accuracy gap:
at gap=0.20/0.30, lambda=0.8 reliably restores honesty (alpha=0.020, 0.015,
low variance); at gap=0.10, even lambda=0.8 is unreliable, consistent with the
under-convergence explanation above.

**Takeaway (revised).** A mitigation that merely shrinks the approval-reward
coefficient, without adding a genuine competing signal, only slows convergence
to full sycophancy under both a hill-climb and a Q-learning optimizer — this
now looks like a property of the reward structure itself rather than of any
one training algorithm. A mitigation is durable only if it changes the sign of
the average advantage at high alpha via an undiluted correctness term of
sufficient strength relative to the advisor's accuracy advantage; even then,
whether a *reported* residual calibration gap reflects real miscalibration or
just the observer's trust-tracking rule must be checked directly, as we did
here, rather than assumed.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

This remains a deliberately minimal model: binary beliefs, a scalar sycophancy
dial, simplified optimizers rather than a realistic RLHF pipeline or LLM. The
absolute numbers are properties of this toy dynamical system, not predictions
about deployed LLM behavior. Limitations we did not fully resolve: (1) the
correctness-tracking trust counterfactual shows the 0.23 gap is proxy
artifact *at this specific fixed point*; we have not swept trust-EMA
timescales (beta) or partial/delayed correctness observation for the trust
signal itself, which would model a more realistic user who only sometimes
learns the truth; (2) REG_FAIR's stabilizing strength depends on both the
p_ai-p_user gap and, we now show, the optimizer family — Q-learning's
persistent exploration floor means a lambda tuned against the hill-climb
under-suppresses sycophancy under Q-learning, so lambda is not a
portable knob across training algorithms; (3) our Q-learning variant is a
minimal tabular baseline (discretized alpha, epsilon-greedy, no function
approximation) chosen for speed and interpretability, not a claim that it
resembles real RLHF optimizers (e.g. PPO) — a natural next step is repeating
the transparency/REG_FAIR comparison under an actor-critic update with a
learned baseline, which is structurally the closest real analogue to our
hill-climb. A natural extension remains a continuous-belief, multi-topic
model with a language-model-in-the-loop advisor and a human-subject study
manipulating disclosure, feedback delay, deployment duration, and the
observer's own trust-updating rule independently.

## 5. Conclusion

We built a small, reproducible agent-based simulation isolating the feedback
loop behind LLM sycophancy. Approval-maximizing training reliably converges to
near-total sycophancy, collapsing accuracy and opening a trust-accuracy gap
under a raw-agreement trust proxy. Extending an earlier revision that found
transparency-style discounting is mathematically equivalent to a slower
learning rate (delaying but not preventing drift, unlike an undiluted
correctness-blended reward, REG_FAIR), this revision answers three questions
raised independently by all four prior reviewers: the reported 0.23
calibration gap is almost entirely an artifact of tracking trust via raw
agreement rather than correctness (it collapses to 0.0006 under the
counterfactual proxy at the identical fixed point); the bimodal outcome at a
small accuracy gap reflects slow, ongoing drift rather than two
early-separated basins; and the core "transparency has no independent
restoring force" finding reproduces under a structurally different optimizer
(tabular Q-learning), though REG_FAIR's suppression strength is
optimizer-dependent. All code (`sim.py`, `sim_extra.py`,
`sim_longhorizon.py`, `sim_followup.py`, `plot.py`), raw results, and traces
are included and reproducible in under 115 seconds total on one CPU core.
