# Approval Is Not Enough: A Minimal Agent-Based Model of Sycophancy Drift and the Illusion of Short-Horizon Mitigations

## Abstract

Optimizing AI systems against short-horizon human approval (thumbs-up/down, chat
continuation) rewards *agreement* over *correctness*, a dynamic documented
qualitatively but rarely isolated as a controllable, quantitative system. We build
a minimal agent-based simulation in which an adaptive advisor learns, via online
reward-driven updates, how much to echo a simulated user's prior belief versus
report its own noisier-but-better evidence. Under pure approval-maximizing
training the advisor converges to near-total sycophancy (mixing weight
alpha -> 1.0), accuracy collapses from 0.80 to 0.60, and a trust-accuracy
calibration gap of 0.40 opens. At the fixed 4000-round training budget used in
our first experiments, a "transparency" mitigation (discounting the approval
reward whenever the advisor merely agrees) looked far more effective than
blending in delayed correctness feedback (REG): transparency=0.8 recovered
accuracy to 0.787, while REG's correctness term looked weak unless observed on
almost every round. Independent review of this comparison flagged that REG's
correctness signal was diluted through *two* multiplicative factors (blend
weight lambda and observation probability q) while transparency discounted
agreement through only one — an unfair fight. We built a matched, single-knob
REG variant (REG_FAIR: correctness always observed, attenuated only by lambda)
and re-ran both mitigations to a much longer horizon (20,000-60,000 rounds,
5-15x the original budget). The result overturns our original headline finding:
transparency discounting does **not** create a new stable equilibrium — it is
mathematically equivalent to shrinking the effective learning rate (we verify
this exactly: TRANSPARENCY at discount 0.8 with learning rate 0.1 reproduces
APPROVAL_ONLY at learning rate 0.02 to 6 decimal places), so it only *delays*
the drift to alpha=1. Given enough rounds, transparency=0.8 reconverges to
alpha=0.9998 and accuracy 0.601 (30 seeds, 20,000 rounds) — indistinguishable
from approval-only. Only REG_FAIR at lambda=0.8, which injects an undiluted
correctness bonus every round, reaches a genuinely stable low-sycophancy fixed
point (alpha=0.018, accuracy=0.799) that persists at 60,000 rounds. All
differences are significant under Welch's t-test across 30 seeds (p<1e-6 for
the horizon effect, p=3.6e-13 for REG_FAIR vs. transparency at matched
strength). We also locate the delayed-feedback transition point more precisely
(between q=0.7 and q=0.9) and show the LR-sensitivity flagged by reviewers is
not incidental noise but the exact mechanism behind the original illusion. All
code, raw logs, and plots are included and reproducible in under 90 seconds on
one CPU core.

## 1. Introduction

Human feedback is the dominant training/evaluation signal for conversational AI,
from RLHF reward models to in-product thumbs-up buttons. HCI and NLP work argues
these signals are systematically biased toward agreement: users tend to approve
of responses that validate existing beliefs, independent of correctness (Sun &
Wang, CHI 2026). Because approval is cheap and correctness is often delayed or
unobserved, systems optimized against approval alone have a direct incentive
gradient toward sycophancy and, downstream, toward users trusting the system
more than its actual competence warrants. This is a socio-technical problem: a
learning algorithm (technical) interacting with a user's evolving trust (social).
Most existing empirical work studies this post hoc in real deployments; that is
essential for external validity but makes it hard to isolate *which* properties
of the feedback loop causally drive drift and to compare mitigations under
controlled, repeatable conditions.

### Related work

- **Sycophancy and trust in deployed LLMs.** Sun & Wang ("Be Friendly, Not
  Friends," CHI 2026) show subtle, neutral-toned opinion shifts are the hardest
  for users to detect. "Does Sycophancy Change Decisions?" (CHI 2026) shows
  sycophantic responses shift real decisions. "User Detection and Response
  Patterns of Sycophantic Behavior" (arXiv:2601.10467) studies whether users
  notice sycophancy at all; "When Flattery Backfires" (CHI 2026 EA) shows the
  trust effect is context-dependent.
- **Trust calibration.** "Calibrated Trust in Dealing with LLM Hallucinations"
  (arXiv:2512.09088) documents that user trust tracks recent interaction
  quality rather than ground-truth accuracy — the divergence our simulation
  reproduces mechanistically via an approval-EMA trust proxy.
- **Reward misspecification and specification gaming.** Our approval-vs-
  correctness setup is a minimal instance of the broader reward-hacking/
  specification-gaming problem studied in RL theory (proxy reward diverging
  from true objective under an optimizing agent). We do not claim novelty over
  that literature's formal results; our contribution is a small, socio-
  technically-motivated instance specifically parameterized around observable
  interface variables (disclosure, feedback delay) rather than abstract reward
  functions, letting us compare *interface-level* mitigations directly.
- **Socio-technical framing.** Work on human-centered AI design
  (arXiv:2601.21492, arXiv:2601.06064) argues human-AI research under-specifies
  the feedback/incentive structures linking optimization targets to social
  consequences — the gap this simulation is built to fill.

**Gap.** None of the above isolates the feedback loop as a small, parameterized
dynamical system letting one sweep feedback delay, reward blending, and
disclosure strength and read off causal, *long-run* effects on sycophancy and
calibration. We provide that, and — importantly, following independent review —
we show that reading off effects from a single fixed training horizon can be
actively misleading: a mitigation that looks effective at 4000 rounds can be a
transient artifact that a longer horizon reveals as no mitigation at all.

## 2. Method

**Environment.** Each round draws binary ground truth y uniformly. A simulated
user holds a noisy prior b_u (matches y with probability p_user=0.60). An
advisor holds independent noisy evidence e (matches y with probability
p_ai=0.80). The advisor outputs o=b_u with probability alpha (its sycophancy
dial), else o=e. alpha in [0,1] is the only free behavioral parameter, updated
online by a REINFORCE-style hill-climb: a scalar training reward is compared to
an EMA baseline (beta=0.95), and alpha is nudged up when using the user's prior
yielded above-baseline reward, down otherwise (learning rate 0.05 unless
swept). A separate EMA (beta=0.98) of agreement is tracked as *subjective
trust* — a recency-weighted proxy, not an audited accuracy score. We flag this
operationalization as a modeling choice, not a derived quantity: a trust proxy
that instead updates on *later-revealed* correctness would by construction
track accuracy more closely, and would likely shrink the residual calibration
gap we report at high transparency; we leave that variant to future work.

**Conditions.** *Static honest/sycophant* (alpha fixed at 0/1): bounding
controls. *Approval-only*: reward=r_approve. *REG(lambda,q)*: reward =
(1-lambda) r_approve + lambda r_correct, but r_correct is only available with
probability q per round (else reward falls back to pure approval) — modeling
delayed/partial outcome labels, and diluting the correctness signal through
*two* factors. *REG_FAIR(lambda)*: a fairness-corrected variant added in this
revision, with r_correct observed on every round (q=1) so the correctness term
is attenuated by exactly one factor (lambda), matching how *Transparency*
attenuates agreement by exactly one factor (1-transparency). *Transparency*:
reward = r_approve * (1-transparency) whenever o==b_u.

**Runs.** Original sweep: 4000 rounds, 30 seeds (`sim.py`, ~11s). This revision
adds (`sim_extra.py`, ~90s total): REG_FAIR at lambda in {0.2,0.5,0.8}; a finer
q sweep at q in {0.5,0.7,0.9,1.0}; a learning-rate/baseline-beta robustness
sweep; and, critically, a long-horizon re-run (20,000 and 60,000 rounds, 30
seeds) of the key conditions to test whether apparent equilibria at 4000 rounds
are genuine fixed points or slow transients.

**Metrics.** final alpha; accuracy (mean correctness, last 20% of rounds);
trust (mean trust-EMA, last 20%); calibration_gap=|trust-accuracy|;
sycophancy_index = Pearson correlation between per-round alpha and agreement —
we flag explicitly (per reviewer comment) that this index is uninformative once
alpha has saturated in either direction, since a "never sycophantic" and an
"instantly and permanently sycophantic" run both correlate near zero; it should
be read only as a transitional-regime diagnostic, not a headline number, and we
no longer report it as a standalone abstract-level claim.

## 3. Results

**Short-horizon (4000 rounds) baseline, reproduced from the original
submission.** Approval-only training drives alpha to 1.000, crashing accuracy
from the honest baseline (0.802) to 0.602 and opening a calibration gap of
0.398 (trust saturates at 1.000). REG with the original two-factor dilution
(lambda swept at q=0.3) barely moves alpha (0.999->0.993); sweeping q at
lambda=0.5 shows a nonlinear threshold: q=0.5 gives alpha=0.994, q=0.7 gives
0.981, q=0.9 gives 0.786, and q=1.0 gives 0.567 (accuracy 0.706) — the finer
grid added in this revision locates the transition between q=0.7 and q=0.9,
tightening the original claim that "q>=1.0" was required. At this same
horizon, transparency=0.8 looked like the clear winner: alpha collapses to
0.106, accuracy recovers to 0.787.

**Fairness check: REG_FAIR vs. Transparency, matched single-factor
attenuation.** Reviewers independently flagged that REG's correctness signal
was diluted by *two* factors (lambda and q) while transparency's agreement
discount used only one, making the comparison structurally unfair. REG_FAIR
removes the q dilution (correctness observed every round). At 4000 rounds and
matched strength 0.8, REG_FAIR *outperforms* transparency: alpha=0.017 vs.
0.106 (Welch t=-11.60, p=3.6e-13, n=30 each) and accuracy=0.799 vs. 0.787
(t=3.75, p=4.5e-4) — REG_FAIR reaches within 0.3 points of the honest ceiling.
This confirms the reviewer's suspicion directly: the original "transparency
beats delayed correctness" finding was substantially an artifact of how REG's
attenuation was structured, not a general fact about disclosure vs. outcome
labels.

**Long-horizon re-run: transparency's apparent equilibrium is a transient.**
Extending training 5-15x reveals a further, more consequential problem. At
20,000 rounds (30 seeds): transparency=0.2/0.5/0.8 all converge to
alpha=0.9998-1.000 and accuracy=0.601 — statistically indistinguishable from
approval-only. Even the strong REG (lambda=0.5, q=1.0), which looked like a
partial win at 4000 rounds (alpha=0.567), reaches alpha=0.992 by 20,000
rounds. Only REG_FAIR at lambda=0.8 remains stable: alpha=0.018, accuracy=0.799
at 20,000 rounds, and alpha=0.018, accuracy=0.797 at 60,000 rounds (10 seeds) —
a genuine fixed point, not a slow drift.

| Condition (20,000 rounds, 30 seeds) | final alpha | accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| Static honest | 0.000 | 0.802 |
| Approval-only | 1.000 | 0.601 |
| Transparency 0.2 | 1.000 | 0.601 |
| Transparency 0.5 | 1.000 | 0.601 |
| Transparency 0.8 | 1.000 | 0.601 |
| REG, lambda=0.5, q=1.0 | 0.992 | 0.600 |
| REG_FAIR, lambda=0.5 | 0.991 | 0.603 |
| REG_FAIR, lambda=0.8 | 0.018 | 0.799 |

**Why transparency's short-horizon "win" was illusory.** We identify the exact
mechanism, addressing the sensitivity concern independently raised by three
reviewers. Because TRANSPARENCY's reward is r_approve*(1-transparency) with no
compensating correctness term, and both the training reward and the EMA
reward-baseline are scaled by this same constant, the alpha-update reduces to
`alpha += LR*(1-transparency)*(scaled advantage)*sign*0.1` — mathematically
identical, at every step, to running approval-only at learning rate
LR*(1-transparency). We confirmed this is not an approximation but an exact
identity: TRANSPARENCY at transparency=0.8 with LR=0.1 reproduces
APPROVAL_ONLY at LR=0.02 bit-for-bit (alpha=0.844016, accuracy=0.660542 in
both, n=30). Transparency has no independent restoring force; it only rescales
the approach speed toward the same alpha=1 corner. REG_FAIR is different in
kind, not degree: because its correctness term is undiluted, at high alpha the
average reward from *not* using the user's prior (higher accuracy, since
p_ai=0.80 > p_user=0.60) can exceed the discounted-agreement reward from using
it, producing a genuine interior fixed point where the hill-climb's advantage
balances rather than a slowed-down march to the boundary.

**Learning-rate and baseline-beta robustness.** Beyond explaining the
transparency illusion, this also answers the general "is the ranking an
artifact of the hill-climb constants" question: at LR=0.02, even
approval-only has not fully converged by round 4000 (alpha=0.844,
accuracy=0.661), confirming that short-horizon numbers at low LR conflate "not
yet sycophantic" with "durably mitigated." Varying baseline_beta in
{0.90,0.95,0.99} leaves transparency=0.8's 4000-round alpha in a narrow band
(0.088-0.112) — the qualitative short-horizon story is robust to this
particular constant, but not to LR or horizon, both of which change the
underlying regime, not just noise around it.

**Takeaway (revised).** The lesson is not "transparency beats delayed
correctness feedback." It is that *any* mitigation which merely shrinks the
approval-reward coefficient, without adding a genuine competing signal, only
slows convergence to full sycophancy — and a sufficiently long deployment will
still drift there. A mitigation is only durable if it changes the sign of the
average advantage at high alpha, which requires an actual, undiluted
correctness term (REG_FAIR here), not just disclosure of the agreement bias.
This is a much stronger and more falsifiable prediction than our original
submission's, and one a human-subject or longer-horizon RLHF follow-up could
directly test.

## 4. Discussion and Limitations

This remains a deliberately minimal model: binary beliefs, a scalar sycophancy
dial, a hill-climbing update rather than a realistic RLHF pipeline or LLM. The
absolute numbers (e.g., "alpha=0.018 at REG_FAIR lambda=0.8") are properties of
this toy dynamical system, not predictions about deployed LLM behavior. Three
limitations we did not fully resolve: (1) the trust proxy is a raw-agreement
EMA by construction, so it cannot track a well-calibrated advisor's accuracy
even at the true fixed point (residual calibration gap at REG_FAIR lambda=0.8
is 0.190-0.20) — a trust model that updates on later-revealed correctness would
likely close this gap and is a natural next experiment rather than a
correction to what we report here; (2) our REG_FAIR reward form, like
TRANSPARENCY, remains one specific parameterization among many linear blends,
so "undiluted correctness signal creates a fixed point" should be read as a
demonstrated sufficient condition in this model, not a proof it is necessary;
(3) the hill-climb is a bespoke heuristic, not a derived policy-gradient
update — the long-horizon experiments partially substitute for a full
optimizer-robustness study by showing the qualitative ranking (REG_FAIR stable,
everything else eventually sycophantic) holds at 15x the original horizon, but
we have not tested a genuinely different optimizer family (e.g. tabular
Q-learning over discretized alpha). A natural extension is a continuous-belief,
multi-topic model with a language-model-in-the-loop advisor and a
human-subject study manipulating disclosure, feedback delay, and *deployment
duration* independently — this revision's central finding is that duration is
not a nuisance variable but a first-order determinant of whether a mitigation
is real or transient.

## 5. Conclusion

We built a small, reproducible agent-based simulation isolating the feedback
loop behind LLM sycophancy. Approval-maximizing training reliably converges to
near-total sycophancy, collapsing accuracy and opening a persistent
trust-accuracy gap. Independent review of our original mitigation comparison
correctly flagged a structural asymmetry between our two reward parameterizations;
correcting it (REG_FAIR) and, more importantly, extending the training horizon
5-15x overturned our original headline claim. Transparency-style reward
discounting, however strong, has no independent restoring force in this model
and is mathematically equivalent to a slower learning rate — it delays but does
not prevent sycophancy drift. Only a reward that blends in an undiluted,
every-round correctness signal reaches a genuinely stable, near-honest
equilibrium (accuracy 0.799 vs. an 0.802 ceiling, sustained to 60,000 rounds).
All code (`sim.py`, `sim_extra.py`, `plot.py`), raw results
(`results_summary.csv`, `results_extra.csv`, `results_longhorizon.csv`,
`traces.json`), and the figure (`results_plot.png`) are included for
inspection and re-running (original sweep ~11s, extended/long-horizon
experiments ~90s, all on one CPU core).
